Question 901

A patient who speaks limited English is being discharged with new wound care instructions and a friend offers to translate—what should the CMA do first?

Obtain a qualified medical interpreter and then use teach-back for the wound care steps; the trap is using family/friends, which increases errors and privacy risks.
Question 902

During a follow-up call, a patient can’t explain how they’re taking their blood pressure medication and repeatedly says, “Same as always,”—what should the CMA do next?

Ask the patient to read the label and describe the exact dose/timing (teach-back) and notify the provider of adherence confusion; the trap is documenting “patient understands” without verifying, which can hide under- or overdosing.
Question 903

A patient arrives for a procedure and says they never received the prep instructions; the provider is running behind—what should the CMA do first?

Pause and clarify the prep requirements with the provider/clinic protocol before proceeding, then re-educate the patient with teach-back; the trap is “pushing through” to stay on schedule, risking an unsafe or ineffective procedure.
Question 904

The provider ordered a referral for a diabetic eye exam; the patient says, “I don’t have transportation, so I’ll do it later”—what is the CMA’s best action?

Identify the barrier and coordinate resources (e.g., community transport options or closer in-network locations) and schedule follow-up outreach; the trap is simply handing a referral sheet without problem-solving, leading to missed preventive care.
Question 905

A patient returns after starting an antibiotic and says, “I stopped when I felt better after two days,”—what should the CMA do next?

Provide brief education on taking the medication as directed and use teach-back, then inform the provider because early discontinuation can cause treatment failure; the trap is simply advising “restart it” without provider involvement.
Question 906

A patient with hypertension was asked to return in 2 weeks for a recheck but left without scheduling and now calls 6 weeks later—what should the CMA do next?

Question 907

Question 908

Question 909

Question 910

Treat it as misdirected PHI, stop processing, notify the sender, and follow clinic HIPAA incident procedure; the trap is filing it “because the DOB matches,” which risks a privacy breach and chart contamination.
Question 911

Question 912

Question 914

A patient is given a referral for physical therapy but says they can’t get there because they don’t drive and buses are unreliable; what should the CMA do?

Identify and offer appropriate community resources (transportation assistance, ride programs, telehealth options if available) and communicate the barrier to the care team because access affects compliance, and the trap is labeling the patient “noncompliant” without addressing barriers.
Question 996

A patient was told to start a new medication and return in 2 weeks for a blood pressure check, but at follow-up scheduling they say, “I’ll just check it at the pharmacy,” what should the CMA do?

Reinforce why the ordered follow-up visit matters, schedule the check if possible, and document refusal/plan because provider-directed monitoring supports safety, and the trap is treating retail readings as an equivalent substitute without the provider’s agreement.
Question 997

A parent asks the CMA to explain the specialist referral to their teenage child in the waiting room with other patients nearby; what is the CMA’s best action?

Move the conversation to a private area and confirm permission to discuss details before explaining next steps because privacy is part of coordination, and the trap is sharing protected information in public even when the parent is present.
Question 998

A patient missed a follow-up appointment for diabetes management and says on the phone, “I’ve been busy; I’ll reschedule eventually,” what is the CMA’s best next step?

Attempt to reschedule during the call and document outreach per recall workflow because proactive recall supports continuity, and the trap is accepting “eventually” without a concrete plan or documentation.
Question 999

A referral to cardiology was placed two weeks ago, and the patient says they never received an appointment; the referral status shows “pending authorization,” what should the CMA do next?

Verify the referral/authorization status with the payer or referral coordinator per clinic process and update the patient on the next step because delays are often administrative, and the trap is telling the patient to call the specialist without confirming authorization requirements.
Question 1000

A patient calls asking for their biopsy results and says, “Just tell me if it’s cancer,” but the provider has not reviewed the report yet; what should the CMA do?

Explain you cannot interpret or release unreviewed results, route the report to the provider for review, and arrange the provider call-back because scope and accuracy matter, and the trap is paraphrasing results or offering reassurance without authorization.
