Question 3901

A 17-year-old arrives alone requesting contraception counseling and asks that their parent not be told; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Escalate to the provider/clinic manager and follow state/clinic minor-consent rules while maintaining privacy within legal limits because consent laws vary and the CMA cannot promise confidentiality; the trap is guaranteeing secrecy or automatically calling the parent.
Question 3902

At check-in, a patient says they recently moved and asks you to “just change my address” in the EHR while a family member starts answering questions for them; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Verify the patient’s identity using two identifiers and confirm the address change directly with the patient (not the family member) before updating the EHR, because accurate demographics affect billing/insurance and HIPAA privacy, and the common mistake is accepting third-party information without patient verification.
Question 3903

After giving printed after-visit instructions, the patient says, “Yeah, yeah, I understand,” but seems confused and keeps looking at the paper; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Use teach-back (“Tell me how you’ll take this medication/follow these steps”) to confirm understanding because assent is not comprehension; the trap is accepting “I understand” without verification.
Question 3904

A patient says, “I’m recording this visit,” and holds up a phone; the provider is not in the room yet—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Pause the rooming process and notify the provider/manager to follow clinic policy and state recording laws because consent for recording may be required; the trap is grabbing the phone or allowing recording without guidance.
Question 3905

While documenting a telephone message, the caller reports chest tightness and dizziness “right now” and asks for a same-day appointment; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Instruct the caller to hang up and call emergency services (or transfer per protocol) and notify the provider because this is a potential emergency and scheduling is not the priority; the trap is offering the next available slot or routing as routine.
Question 3906

A patient wants a copy of their full medical record and asks you to “just print it now,” but you’re not sure of the request process; what is the best CMA response?

Provide the patient the clinic’s records request process (written request/identity verification and routing to records/privacy officer) because releases must be tracked and authorized; the trap is printing and handing over records without verification.
Question 3907

In a busy waiting room, a CMA calls out, “Mr. Diaz, your STI results are ready—come back,” and the patient looks embarrassed; what is the best action to prevent this?

Use a neutral identifier (name only and/or first name with last initial) and escort privately without stating the reason for visit because minimum necessary privacy applies; the trap is announcing diagnoses or sensitive tests publicly.
Question 3908

At check-in, an adult patient insists you “just tell my spouse in the lobby what my lab results were last week” and points to them as proof; what should the CMA do NEXT to stay HIPAA-compliant and helpful?

Ask the patient to provide written/verifiable authorization (or confirm an existing release in the EHR) before disclosing any results, and instead offer to discuss results privately with the patient now; the trap is relying on verbal requests/pointing to a person in the waiting area, which is not sufficient permission for PHI disclosure.
Question 3909

A patient arrives for a specialist referral visit, but the referral authorization was never obtained and the patient drove two hours; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Contact the referral/insurance team immediately to attempt urgent authorization guidance and inform the patient of options because timely escalation may prevent a wasted visit and unexpected charges; the trap is sending the patient anyway without addressing authorization.
Question 3910

You made a typo in the chief complaint (entered “no pain” instead of “knee pain”) after the patient has left; what is the best way to correct it?

Enter a dated/time-stamped correction/addendum per EHR policy stating the error and the corrected information because legal records require integrity and traceability; the trap is deleting the original entry without an audit trail.
Question 3911

The provider performs an in-office procedure, and the patient later disputes the bill saying “I never agreed to that”; what documentation is most important to support proper billing and reduce legal risk?

A signed consent (when required) plus a clear procedure note with indication, what was done, and patient tolerance because billing must match documented services; the trap is relying on a verbal “okay” without documentation.
Question 3912

A patient’s insurance card is expired, and they insist “nothing changed—bill it like last time,” with a full waiting room; what is the best CMA next step?

Verify eligibility/coverage before the visit and explain potential self-pay responsibility because expired/changed coverage drives denials; the trap is proceeding without verification and creating avoidable claim rework.
Question 3913

You receive a denial stating “diagnosis does not support medical necessity,” and the provider asks what you should check first before resubmitting; what is the best CMA action?

Confirm the diagnosis code and procedure code are accurate and linked correctly to the visit documentation because mismatched or missing code linkage commonly triggers medical-necessity denials; the trap is resubmitting unchanged.
Question 3914

A parent requests information about their 22-year-old child’s visit because “I pay the bills,” and the patient is not present; what should the CMA do?

Do not disclose without the adult patient’s explicit authorization and offer to have the patient contact the office because HIPAA rights belong to the patient regardless of who pays; the trap is treating payment as permission.
Question 3915

Question 3916

When verifying insurance, you learn the patient’s plan requires a referral authorization, but the patient is already here for a specialist visit; what should the CMA do next?

Inform the patient and notify the supervisor/provider to determine options (obtain authorization, reschedule, or self-pay acknowledgment) because missing referrals often cause claim denial; the trap is proceeding without addressing authorization requirements.
Question 3994

A patient says, “My insurance will cover it,” but the eligibility check shows coverage terminated last month; what should the CMA do first?

Clarify the finding and discuss payment options or updated insurance information before services are rendered because eligibility affects billing and patient responsibility; the trap is assuming the patient’s statement overrides verification.
Question 3995

The schedule shows two patients with the same first and last name booked back-to-back, and one is due for an injection today; what should the CMA do first at check-in/rooming?

Use at least two identifiers (full name plus DOB) and confirm the ordered service before proceeding because look-alike names are a common patient-safety error; the trap is relying on the appointment time or face recognition.
Question 3996

A patient messages in the portal: “I’m having chest pressure—should I take another dose of my medication?” what should the CMA do first?

Escalate immediately per urgent symptom protocol (advise emergency evaluation/notify clinician right away) because medication advice and chest pain require prompt triage; the trap is replying with dosing guidance or delaying to “wait for the provider.”
Question 3997

A patient’s spouse calls asking for the patient’s lab results and says, “I’m listed as the emergency contact”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Verify whether the patient has authorized disclosure to that specific person (e.g., written release/portal proxy) and, if not, decline and offer to speak with the patient directly because emergency contact is not automatic permission; the trap is assuming relationship equals access.
Question 3998

You accidentally open the wrong patient’s EHR and see diagnoses before realizing the mistake; what is the best action?

Close the chart immediately and report the inappropriate access per clinic privacy policy because it may be a HIPAA breach requiring documentation/mitigation; the trap is “just closing it” and never reporting.
Question 3999

After entering vitals, you realize you documented 180/80 instead of 108/80 in the EHR; what is the correct way to fix it?

Make an addendum/correction per EHR policy with date/time and reason (do not delete or obscure the original entry) because medical records must show an audit trail; the trap is backdating or deleting the entry.
Question 4000

A provider verbally tells you to “add that the patient denies chest pain” after the visit, but the patient already left and you didn’t ask; what should the CMA do?

Do not document what you did not assess; instead document only what you know (or route a message for the provider to document) because charting unverified statements is falsification; the trap is writing it to “help the provider.”
