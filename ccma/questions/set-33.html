Question 3201

After taking vitals, you realize you documented the pulse as 18 instead of the respirations as 18 in the EHR; what is the correct action?

Correct the entry using the EHR’s approved correction method (add an amendment with date/time and reason) because accurate records are a legal document; the trap is deleting or “editing silently” without an audit trail.
Question 3202

A patient’s automated BP reads 164/102, but they’re talking and their legs are dangling; what is the best next step?

Recheck manually after proper positioning (seated, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level, appropriate cuff) because technique errors commonly cause false highs; the trap is documenting the first reading as “hypertension” without confirming.
Question 3203

A 4-year-old arrives for a visit and is uncooperative for BP; which action is best to obtain an accurate reading?

Use a pediatric cuff and a calm, seated approach (measure after the child settles, parent present) because correct cuff size and reducing agitation improves accuracy; the trap is using an adult cuff or taking BP while the child is crying and moving.
Question 3204

A patient receiving an injection suddenly develops hives and wheezing—what is the CMA’s best immediate action?

Stop the procedure, call for the provider/emergency response immediately, and position/monitor airway and vitals because this suggests anaphylaxis requiring rapid escalation; the trap is having the patient “wait it out” or offering water instead of activating emergency care.
Question 3205

You are about to enter an exam room and see a “Contact Precautions” sign for a draining wound; what PPE should you don before touching the patient or surfaces?

Don gown and gloves (and add mask/eye protection if splash risk) because contact precautions prevent transmission via direct/indirect contact; the trap is using only gloves or waiting to gown until after the exam starts.
Question 3206

A used needle is on the tray after an injection and the sharps container is across the room; what should the CMA do?

Engage the safety device and carry the syringe carefully to immediately dispose in a sharps container without recapping because delayed disposal increases needlestick risk; the trap is recapping “just for transport.”
Question 3207

While verifying the medication order, you notice the chart lists an allergy to latex and the clinic’s adhesive bandages contain latex; what should you do next?

Use latex-free supplies and alert the team to the latex allergy because preventing exposure is part of medication/procedure safety; the trap is assuming only medications (not supplies) trigger allergic reactions.
Question 3208

You prepare an IM injection and the patient says, “I always faint with shots”; what is the best action before administering?

Have the patient lie down (or seated with support) and assess for prior syncope precautions because positioning prevents injury from vasovagal episodes; the trap is proceeding with the patient standing to “get it over with.”
Question 3209

Question 3210

Question 3211

Question 3212

Question 3298

During intake, a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 84/52 with a regular pulse of 110; the patient is sitting upright on the exam table—what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a supine position with legs elevated and call for the provider/activate clinic emergency response as needed because this may be shock/syncope risk; the trap is continuing the intake or walking the patient to another room.
Question 3299

During intake in an urgent care clinic, a 62-year-old reports new dizziness and looks pale; you obtain vitals: BP 86/54, pulse 118, RR 22, SpO2 96%, and she says she “just needs to sit a minute”—what should the CMA do first?

Place the patient supine with legs elevated and immediately alert the provider/activate emergency response while staying with her, because hypotension with tachycardia suggests possible shock/syncope risk and the common mistake is having her complete paperwork or walk to an exam room before stabilizing and escalating.
Question 3300

In the waiting room, a visitor asks why a specific patient is in the clinic today—what should the CMA do?

Politely state you cannot share any patient information and offer to take a message because HIPAA prohibits disclosure without authorization; the trap is giving “harmless” details like confirming the patient is being seen.
