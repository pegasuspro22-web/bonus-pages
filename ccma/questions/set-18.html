Question 1701

A teen arrives alone for treatment, and the parent is not reachable; the patient asks for care; what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider/office manager to determine consent authority based on state law and clinic policy before proceeding (unless emergency) because minors’ consent rules vary; the trap is assuming all minors cannot consent or automatically treating without verifying.
Question 1702

A patient asks for a copy of their records “right now,” but the request is verbal at the front desk; what is the best next step?

Provide the clinic’s formal records request/authorization process (written request with identity verification) because releases must be documented and validated; the trap is printing the chart immediately without verifying identity and documenting the disclosure.
Question 1703

A patient reports they changed their address and insurance, and the front desk is busy; what should the CMA prioritize before the patient leaves?

Ensure demographics and insurance information are updated/flagged for update to prevent billing/claim denials and missed follow-up communications; the trap is postponing updates and then sending PHI/bills to the wrong address.
Question 1704

A patient refuses to sign a procedure consent form but says, “I still want it done—just don’t make me sign”; what should the CMA do?

Stop and notify the provider because the procedure should not proceed without valid informed consent documentation when required; the trap is witnessing a signature later or proceeding based on verbal agreement alone.
Question 1705

A patient leaves an angry voicemail with full name and date of birth asking for results; when returning the call, what is the best way to protect privacy?

Verify identity using at least two identifiers before discussing any PHI and avoid leaving detailed results on voicemail unless prior consent is documented because callbacks can reach the wrong person; the trap is assuming the voicemail proves who will answer.
Question 1706

A patient asks why they have a balance after insurance “paid,” and you see a copay and coinsurance on the EOB; what is the best CMA response?

Explain at a basic level that the copay/coinsurance/deductible are patient responsibilities per the plan and offer to route to billing for details because clear communication prevents escalation; the trap is promising the balance will be waived or giving complex coverage advice beyond your role.
Question 1707

A patient arrives for a referral visit, but the referral authorization is missing and the insurer requires it; what is the best CMA action?

Inform the patient and initiate the process to obtain authorization (contact referring office/insurer per workflow) before the visit proceeds to reduce denial risk; the trap is proceeding without alerting anyone and creating an avoidable patient bill.
Question 1708

A provider asks you to “delete that note so it won’t show up” after you documented the wrong medication lot number; what is the best action?

Add an addendum/correction that clearly states the error and the correct lot number (with date/time/initials per EHR) because legal documentation must remain intact and the trap is deleting/overwriting which can be seen as tampering.
Question 1709

At check-in, a patient asks for a printed copy of today’s visit summary, but their spouse who drove them says, “Just give it to me, I’m family”; what should you do first?

Verify the patient’s permission/authorized disclosure status before releasing anything because HIPAA allows sharing only with the patient or authorized individuals, and the trap is assuming “family” equals authorized.
Question 1710

On a phone call, a patient says, “I’m dizzy and my heart is racing,” then asks to schedule next week; what should the CMA do first?

Assess for urgent symptoms and direct the patient to immediate evaluation (alert provider/911 per severity) rather than routine scheduling because safety overrides appointment logistics; the trap is booking an appointment and ending the call without escalation.
Question 1711

During check-in, a patient asks to see “all my records right now,” and the provider is running behind while the front desk line is growing; what should the CMA do first to handle the request appropriately under HIPAA?

Verify the patient’s identity and route the request through the clinic’s designated medical records process (provide the access-request form and timeline) because patients have a right to access but staff must ensure proper authorization and documentation rather than printing/disclosing records on the spot.
Question 1712

A patient’s claim was denied for “incorrect patient date of birth,” and the patient is upset at the desk; what should the CMA do next?

Verify demographics against a valid ID and correct the registration/insurance record, then route for claim resubmission because many denials are data-entry related; the trap is blaming the insurer without checking basic identifiers.
Question 1713

A caller says, “I’m the patient’s employer and need to know if she was seen today,” and offers the patient’s date of birth; what should the CMA do?

Decline to confirm the visit and route the request to the release-of-information process because confirming attendance is PHI, and the trap is thinking a single identifier makes the disclosure permissible.
Question 1714

While rooming a patient, the CMA overhears another patient at the front desk ask, “Did my neighbor get his test results back yet?” and the receptionist starts to answer; what should the CMA do first?

Immediately interrupt and redirect the conversation to a private area and remind staff that disclosing any patient information without authorization violates HIPAA, because even confirming someone is a patient is protected and the common trap is “it’s okay if they know each other.”
Question 1715

Question 1716

A patient asks you to “code it as a wellness visit so insurance will pay” even though the visit is for chronic knee pain; what should the CMA do?

Explain that coding must reflect the documented services and route billing questions to the billing department/provider, because altering codes is fraud and the trap is thinking it’s harmless “insurance help.”
Question 1792

A patient’s spouse calls asking for the patient’s lab results and says, “I’m on the insurance, so you can tell me,” while the patient is not present; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the caller’s authorization in the record and, if not documented, decline to disclose and offer to take a message for the patient/provider because HIPAA requires patient permission and a common trap is assuming spouse/insurance coverage equals access.
Question 1793

A patient arrives for a minor office procedure and says, “I didn’t sign anything yet, but let’s start because I’m late”; what should the CMA do first?

Confirm the provider has obtained informed consent before proceeding and pause the workflow until it’s documented because consent is a provider responsibility but the CMA must ensure it’s in place and the trap is treating consent like a mere formality.
Question 1794

At check-in, a patient asks the CMA to print “everything in my chart today,” but the account shows they recently changed their last name and the photo ID on file is outdated; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the patient’s identity and update demographics with current government-issued identification (and confirm the request is for their own records) before releasing any printouts, because HIPAA requires reasonable identity verification and a common mistake is handing records to someone without confirming identity after a name change.
Question 1795

A coworker asks the CMA to look up their neighbor’s appointment time “so I can pick them up”; what is the best response?

Decline and do not access the chart because accessing records without a job-related need violates HIPAA and the trap is thinking “good intentions” make it permissible.
Question 1796

The CMA is entering a vaccine administration note and the EHR auto-populates today’s date, but the vaccine was given yesterday during a nurse visit; what is the best action?

Change the administration date/time to the actual date/time and document accurately because immunization records must be precise and the trap is leaving auto-filled values that create false documentation.
Question 1797

After a phone call, the CMA documents “Pt fine, no issues” but realizes the patient actually reported intermittent chest pressure with exertion; what should the CMA do now?

Immediately correct the documentation with an addendum reflecting the patient’s exact report and alert the provider promptly because accurate documentation supports safe triage and the trap is minimizing symptoms with vague wording.
Question 1798

A provider asks the CMA to “just sign my name” to a patient letter since the provider is running behind; what should the CMA do?

Refuse and request the provider sign it themselves because signing for someone else is falsification and the trap is thinking it’s acceptable if the provider verbally approved it.
Question 1799

A patient calls angry because they received a bill after their visit and demands you “fix it now” by changing the visit diagnosis in the EHR to match what their insurance covers; what should the CMA do next?

Decline to alter clinical documentation and route the issue to the provider (for documentation accuracy) and billing/insurance staff to review coding/claim details, because changing a diagnosis for coverage is falsification and a common trap is “helping” by editing records to force payment.
Question 1800

The CMA notices an incorrect medication dose was entered in the EHR medication list from the last visit; what is the best action?

Correct it using the EHR’s amendment/correction workflow with date/time and explanation (not deleting) and notify the provider as needed because records must show an audit trail and the trap is “erasing” the error.
