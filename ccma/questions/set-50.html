Question 4901

A patient with new inhaler instructions nods throughout teaching and says, “Yeah, I got it,” while glancing at the door; what should the CMA do next to reduce the chance of misuse?

Ask for teach-back and a quick return demonstration (e.g., “Show me how you’ll use it at home”), because agreement isn’t understanding and the common trap is stopping education after a yes/no confirmation.
Question 4902

A patient calls saying they never received their imaging referral order and the specialist won’t schedule without it; what should the CMA do next?

Verify the referral/order in the EHR, confirm insurance/authorization requirements, and resend/fax securely to the correct facility while documenting, because the trap is telling the patient to “call the specialist again” without closing the loop.
Question 4903

During rooming, a patient says they feel “a little dizzy,” and you notice they are pale and diaphoretic while standing at the scale, but they insist they’re fine and want to hurry to the exam room; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to sit or lie down immediately and obtain vital signs while alerting the provider/clinical lead, because suspected near-syncope requires preventing a fall and rapid assessment before continuing intake (a common mistake is walking them to the room or finishing paperwork first).
Question 4904

While rooming a teen for a sports physical, the parent asks to stay and answer all questions, and the teen looks uncomfortable; what should the CMA do next?

Follow clinic process to offer private time with the teen for sensitive history and explain confidentiality limits because accurate disclosure affects safety; the trap is defaulting to parental presence for all questions without considering patient comfort and privacy.
Question 4905

A provider verbally orders you to administer a vaccine, but the EHR order shows a different vaccine name than what the provider said; what is the best CMA action?

Stop and clarify with the provider and update/confirm the correct order before preparing the injection, because the trap is “going with what was said” and giving the wrong product.
Question 4906

A patient returns a positive FIT (stool test) for colon cancer screening and asks the CMA, “So do I have cancer?”; what is the best response?

Tell the patient the test is a screening that needs provider review and likely follow-up testing, then notify the provider per workflow, because the trap is interpreting/diagnosing results outside CMA scope.
Question 4907

At the front desk, a patient asks for their spouse’s lab results “because we share everything,” and the spouse is not present; what should the CMA do?

Question 4908

Pause and perform medication reconciliation by comparing the bottle/med list and alert the provider before any refills or instructions are given, because the trap is copying forward an incorrect dose from the record.
Question 4909

Question 4910

Question 4911

Question 4912

Question 4995

A provider orders a cardiology referral and the patient asks, “So am I supposed to call them or will they call me?”; what should the CMA do next?

Explain the referral process clearly (who will schedule, expected timeframe, and what the patient should do if not contacted) and provide written next steps, because unclear handoffs lead to failed referrals and the trap is giving vague reassurance.
Question 4996

A patient’s lab result is flagged critical in the EHR, but the patient’s voicemail greeting does not identify them; what should the CMA do first?

Notify the provider immediately and follow the clinic’s critical-result workflow while making a privacy-compliant contact attempt (no detailed voicemail), because timely escalation is required and the trap is leaving results on voicemail.
Question 4997

During discharge teaching, a patient says they will “double up” on a medication if they miss a dose to “catch up”; what should the CMA do best?

Reinforce that the patient should follow the prescribed directions and contact the clinic/pharmacy for missed-dose instructions, because doubling doses can be unsafe and the trap is offering medication-specific dosing changes outside CMA scope.
Question 4998

A referral is delayed because the specialist requires insurance preauthorization; what is the CMA’s most appropriate action?

Route the referral to the designated staff/workqueue for authorization and document the status for tracking, because preauthorization is an administrative step that must be coordinated and the trap is telling the patient the appointment is confirmed before approval.
Question 4999

After a visit, a patient calls and says they lost their written instructions and are unsure when to return; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the patient’s identity, then restate the provider’s return precautions and follow-up timing and resend instructions as allowed, because safety depends on clear return guidance and the trap is giving advice without confirming identity.
Question 5000

While processing a specialist referral, the patient states they cannot afford the visit and may skip it; what should the CMA do best?

Notify the provider/team and connect the patient with appropriate financial assistance or community resources per clinic process, because addressing barriers improves follow-through and the trap is pressuring the patient without offering options.
