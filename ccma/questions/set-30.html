Questions 2901–3000

Question 2901

A 4-year-old is crying and kicking during a temperature check and the parent asks for an oral temp “to be quick”; what should the CMA do?

Use a tympanic or temporal method per age/coop level because oral temps are unreliable/unsafe if the child can’t hold the probe properly, and the trap is forcing an oral reading for speed.
Question 2902

You enter an exam room and see used gauze with blood on the counter and no sharps present; what is the CMA’s best immediate action?

Perform hand hygiene and don gloves before touching anything because blood is a potential pathogen exposure, and the trap is “quickly cleaning” bare-handed to save time.
Question 2903

After removing gloves, you realize you forgot to clean the stethoscope diaphragm used on a patient with diarrhea; what is the best next step?

Perform hand hygiene and disinfect the stethoscope with an approved agent before the next patient because fomites spread infection, and the trap is thinking glove removal alone prevents cross-contamination.
Question 2904

You’re about to assist with an irrigation of a contaminated wound and the provider is setting up a sterile field; what is the CMA’s role regarding asepsis?

Maintain the sterile field by keeping hands/objects above waist and not reaching over it because breaking sterility risks infection, and the trap is adjusting supplies “real quick” across the field.
Question 2905

While taking a radial pulse, you notice it is irregularly irregular and about 140; what is the best action?

Count the apical pulse for a full minute and promptly report the finding to the provider because irregular tachycardia can be clinically significant, and the trap is documenting a 15-second estimate that misses irregularity.
Question 2906

A patient arrives for a routine injection but is sweating and breathing rapidly; you measure SpO₂ at 88% on room air—what should you do next?

Stop the planned procedure, place the patient upright, recheck the reading, and alert the provider immediately because hypoxemia is an urgent priority, and the trap is proceeding with the injection because it’s “scheduled.”
Question 2907

A patient with suspected influenza is coughing continuously at check-in; what should the CMA do first to reduce transmission?

Provide a mask and separate them from others promptly because source control interrupts the chain of infection, and the trap is completing paperwork first while they expose the waiting room.
Question 2908

During vitals, an adult’s oral temperature reads 101.8°F right after they drank hot coffee in the waiting room; what should the CMA do?

Wait about 15 minutes and retake the temp (or use an appropriate alternate route) because recent hot/cold intake skews oral readings, and the trap is reporting a false fever without confirming.
Question 2909

You prepared insulin and realize the dose in the syringe does not match the order; the patient is already in the room—what is the best action?

Discard the prepared syringe and restart the medication safety checks (right patient/drug/dose/route/time) because correcting at the source prevents harm, and the trap is “adjusting” the dose after it was drawn.
Question 2910

During a sterile dressing change, the patient reaches toward the open sterile supplies to “help”; what should the CMA do?

Politely stop the patient and protect/replace any contaminated sterile items because patient contact breaks sterility, and the trap is continuing the dressing change after contamination to avoid “wasting supplies.”
Question 2911

The provider orders an IM injection, and the patient says they take “blood thinners” but doesn’t know which one; what should the CMA do next?

Pause and clarify the medication/history with the patient and chart/notify the provider before administering because bleeding risk may change the plan, and the trap is giving the injection without assessing anticoagulant use.
Question 2912

A patient arrives for a tetanus booster and says they had a severe allergic reaction to a prior vaccine; what should the CMA do first?

Hold the injection and immediately notify the provider for assessment because prior anaphylaxis is a major safety contraindication to proceeding without evaluation, and the trap is relying on the schedule order alone.
Question 2913

After an injection, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint; what is the CMA’s best first action?

Assist the patient to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if tolerated) and stay with them because preventing syncope-related injury is immediate priority, and the trap is sending them to the front desk or grabbing forms first.
Question 2914

While drawing up a vaccine, the needle touches the countertop; there is no visible dirt—what should the CMA do?

Discard the needle and replace it because sterility is compromised even without visible contamination, and the trap is wiping it with alcohol and proceeding.
Question 2915

A patient needs a rectal suppository administered per provider order and asks to remain covered; what position is best for comfort and access?

Place the patient in Sims’ (left lateral) position with draping because it provides access while maintaining dignity, and the trap is using prone positioning that increases discomfort and exposure.
Question 2916

The medication label reads 250 mg/5 mL and the provider order is for 125 mg; how much should the CMA prepare?

Prepare 2.5 mL because that delivers 125 mg, and the trap is pulling 5 mL because it “matches the label volume.”
Question 2917

You are about to measure an adult’s respiratory rate and they start talking to you about their symptoms—what should you do to get the most accurate rate?

Count respirations unobtrusively (often while appearing to take the pulse) because awareness alters breathing pattern; the trap is asking them to “breathe normally” which changes the rate.
Question 2918

A patient rushes in short of breath and can only speak in 2–3 word phrases; where should you position them while you call for help?

Sit them upright in high Fowler’s to maximize lung expansion while you get the provider/911 as indicated; the trap is laying them flat which can worsen breathing.
Question 2919

During triage in an outpatient clinic, a seated patient reports sudden dizziness and looks pale; you obtain BP 86/54, pulse 124, and they say they “might pass out”—what should the CMA do first before repeating vitals or documenting?

Assist the patient to a safe supine position with legs elevated and call for the provider/activate emergency response while staying with the patient because these vitals suggest possible shock/syncope risk, and the common mistake is continuing routine intake (rechecking numbers or finishing paperwork) instead of stabilizing and escalating.
Question 2920

A patient’s automated BP reads 168/98, and they admit they just drank coffee and crossed their legs during the reading—what should the CMA do next?

Repeat the BP manually after 5 minutes with proper positioning (feet flat, arm at heart level, correct cuff) because technique can falsely elevate readings; the trap is documenting the first number as final.
Question 2921

A 5-year-old is crying and kicking during a pulse oximetry check and the reading shows 86% but the child looks pink and is talking—what should the CMA do next?

Reassess with a better signal (warm the hand, reposition probe, minimize motion) and then notify if still low because motion artifact commonly causes false lows; the trap is treating the number without verifying accuracy.
Question 2922

In the lobby, a patient collapses and is unresponsive; another staff member is calling the provider—what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/get AED) and start CPR if no pulse/normal breathing because early CPR/AED saves lives, and the trap is waiting for the provider or moving the patient before assessing.
Question 2923

During intake a 68-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 86/52, HR 118, RR 22, and cool clammy skin; the provider is running behind—what should the CMA do first?

Place the patient supine with legs elevated and alert the provider/activate clinic emergency response immediately because these are signs of shock; the trap is “rechecking later” or sending the patient back to the lobby.
Question 2924

A patient arrives for suture removal but the wound edges look separated with yellow drainage; what should the CMA do next?

Do not remove sutures and notify the provider promptly because dehiscence/infection requires evaluation, and the trap is removing sutures because “it’s on the schedule.”
Question 2925

You enter a room to collect a wound culture and see the patient coughing without a mask; what should you do first?

Provide the patient a mask and perform hand hygiene before starting because source control and standard precautions reduce spread; the trap is proceeding with the culture setup first.
Question 2926

You are preparing for an IM injection and realize the sharps container in the room is overfilled past the fill line—what should you do before giving the injection?

Replace/close the full container and bring in an empty sharps container because overfilled sharps increase needlestick risk; the trap is giving the shot and “dealing with it after.”
Question 2927

During a busy clinic session, you briefly set a clean dressing pack on the exam table where a used speculum was placed earlier—what is the best action?

Discard the pack and obtain a new one because the clean field is contaminated even if it “looks clean”; the trap is wiping the outside and using it anyway.
Question 2928

You prepared an injection and then the provider changes the order from 0.5 mL to 1 mL; what should you do with the prefilled syringe?

Discard it per medication safety policy and prepare a new dose exactly as ordered because altering a prepared syringe risks dosing errors and contamination; the trap is “topping off” or guessing the final volume.
Question 2929

While preparing a vaccine, you draw up the correct dose but notice the vial expiration date was last month—what should you do next?

Discard it and obtain an unexpired vial because expired vaccines may be ineffective and should not be administered; the trap is giving it anyway “since it’s only a month.”
Question 2930

After removing gloves you need to answer the room phone that is ringing—what is the correct next step?

Perform hand hygiene before touching the phone because glove removal does not equal clean hands; the trap is assuming “I had gloves on” is sufficient.
Question 2931

A patient with latex allergy needs a fingerstick glucose; what should the CMA do to prevent a reaction?

Use non-latex gloves and latex-free supplies because contact exposure can trigger reactions; the trap is focusing only on the lancet and forgetting gloves/tourniquets can contain latex.
Question 2932

The provider orders a tetanus booster, and the patient says they had a severe allergic reaction to a prior tetanus shot—what is the CMA’s best next step?

Hold the injection and immediately inform the provider because prior anaphylaxis is a critical safety contraindication to proceed without provider direction; the trap is administering because “it was ordered.”
Question 2933

You attach the AED to an unresponsive patient and it states “shock advised”; what should you do immediately before pressing the shock button?

Ensure everyone is clear and not touching the patient/bed, then deliver the shock because contact can injure rescuers and reduce effectiveness; the trap is shocking while someone is still providing care.
Question 2934

A patient’s first automated BP reads 178/112, they are talking comfortably and deny chest pain; what is the best next step?

Recheck manually after 5 minutes of rest with correct cuff size/arm position because technique errors commonly cause falsely high readings; the trap is immediately documenting it as “hypertensive crisis” without confirmation.
Question 2935

During a minor procedure, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while seated on the exam table—what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a supine position with legs elevated and stay with them because this supports perfusion and prevents a fall; the trap is leaving to “get water” or finishing the procedure.
Question 2936

After an IM injection, you notice a small drop of blood at the site and the patient is on a blood thinner—what should you do next?

Apply gentle pressure with gauze (do not massage) and reassess for continued bleeding because anticoagulants increase bruising/bleeding risk; the trap is rubbing the site which can worsen hematoma.
Question 2937

In the waiting room, a visitor collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/bring AED) and assess responsiveness/breathing to start CPR if needed because early activation and compressions save lives; the trap is searching for a pulse first and delaying action.
Question 2938

You are assisting with a simple wound dressing change and the provider steps out; the patient’s dressing is stuck to the wound—what should the CMA do next?

Moisten the dressing with sterile saline to loosen it before removal because pulling can damage tissue and restart bleeding; the trap is ripping it off quickly to “get it done.”
Question 2939

During intake a 58-year-old states “I feel lightheaded,” and you obtain BP 82/50, HR 122, skin pale/diaphoretic; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and call the provider/activate clinic emergency response because these are shock-like findings; a common trap is finishing the full intake or rechecking multiple times before acting.
Question 2940

A patient asks you to give their monthly IM medication, but the order in the chart is from 9 months ago and no current order is visible—what should you do next?

Do not administer and obtain a current provider order before preparing the medication because giving meds without a valid order is outside scope and unsafe; the trap is relying on patient report or old orders.
Question 2941

A patient arrives for a BP check wearing a thick sweatshirt and you’re running behind; what is the best action?

Place the cuff on a bare upper arm (or have them remove the sleeve) because clothing can alter readings; the trap is taking a “quick” BP over fabric and treating it as reliable.
Question 2942

In triage, a patient reports new severe headache and you note facial droop and slurred speech; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response/notify the provider immediately because stroke symptoms are time-sensitive; the trap is completing routine vitals and history before escalation.
Question 2943

You enter an exam room and notice dried blood on the exam table paper from the previous patient; what is the best next step before rooming the next patient?

Don gloves and clean/disinfect the surface with an appropriate EPA-registered disinfectant and correct contact time because this breaks the chain of infection; the trap is just changing paper without disinfecting.
Question 2944

A patient with suspected influenza is coughing in the waiting area without a mask; what should the CMA do first?

Provide a mask and separate/room the patient promptly while performing hand hygiene because source control reduces droplet spread; the trap is beginning paperwork first and leaving them unmasked.
Question 2945

While drawing up medication, your sterile needle touches the counter; what is the correct next action?

Discard the contaminated needle and replace it with a new sterile needle because sterility is broken; the trap is wiping it with alcohol, which does not restore sterility.
Question 2946

You remove gloves after assisting with a wound dressing and immediately need to adjust your own face shield; what should you do first?

Perform hand hygiene before touching your face PPE because hands may be contaminated after glove removal; the trap is assuming gloves alone prevented contamination.
Question 2947

A 6-year-old’s respirations are being counted and they start talking and giggling; what should the CMA do to get an accurate respiratory rate?

Count respirations unobtrusively (often right after pulse while keeping fingers on the wrist) because awareness changes breathing; the trap is telling the child to “breathe normally,” which rarely works.
Question 2948

While taking an oral temperature, the patient says they just drank hot coffee in the waiting room; what should you do next?

Wait about 15 minutes and retake (or use a different site per clinic protocol) because recent hot/cold intake skews oral temps; the trap is charting an inaccurate fever.
Question 2949

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint immediately after a vaccine; what should the CMA do first?

Assist them to a supine position with legs elevated and stay with them while calling for help as needed because vasovagal syncope can lead to injury; the trap is sending them to the lobby “to rest.”
Question 2950

During a dressing change for a small laceration, you realize you forgot to open the sterile gauze before removing the old dressing; what is the best next step?

Pause and set up supplies while maintaining clean technique and avoiding contamination of the wound because organization prevents breaks in asepsis; the trap is continuing and then reaching into drawers with contaminated gloves.
Question 2951

You are about to administer an IM vaccine and notice the patient’s name on the vial label does not match the patient in the room; what should you do next?

Stop and verify the right patient/right medication by checking two identifiers and the order before proceeding because label mix-ups cause wrong-patient errors; the trap is assuming the tray was set up correctly.
Question 2952

A patient returns with a bandage soaked through with blood after a minor procedure and says it “won’t stop”; what should the CMA do first?

Apply direct pressure with clean gauze and elevate the area while notifying the provider because controlling active bleeding is the immediate priority; the trap is removing the saturated dressing repeatedly, which can disrupt clotting.
Question 2953

After an IM injection, you recap the needle out of habit and feel it catch; what should you do immediately?

Stop and activate safety controls and dispose of the needle in a sharps container without recapping because recapping increases needlestick risk; the trap is trying to “carefully” recap to keep the room tidy.
Question 2954

You’re preparing to administer an IM injection and the patient is seated on the exam table and looks unsteady; what is the best positioning?

Have the patient seated with support or lying down with the target muscle relaxed because it reduces fall risk and improves injection safety; the trap is injecting while they’re standing to “save time.”
Question 2955

Just before giving an injection, the patient says they had hives after a similar shot years ago; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Hold the medication and notify the provider to clarify allergy history and orders because proceeding risks an adverse reaction; the trap is giving it anyway because “it’s ordered.”
Question 2956

A provider orders 0.5 mL of a medication, but the syringe you grabbed is a 3 mL syringe and the markings are hard to read; what is the best action?

Use an appropriately sized syringe (e.g., 1 mL) to measure accurately because large syringes increase dosing error risk; the trap is “eyeballing” between marks to save time.
Question 2957

The provider orders an IM vaccine and the patient states they had a severe allergic reaction to a prior dose; what should the CMA do?

Stop and notify the provider before preparing/administering, because allergy screening is part of safe medication administration and the trap is proceeding because “the order is in the chart.”
Question 2958

While rooming a patient, you note an SpO₂ of 89% on room air but the patient is talking comfortably; what should the CMA do next?

Reposition the probe/check waveform and reassess, then promptly report persistent low SpO₂ to the provider, because poor probe placement causes false lows and the trap is ignoring a true hypoxemia because the patient “looks fine.”
Question 2959

You are about to take a blood pressure and notice the patient’s sleeve is tight and bunched above the cuff; what should you do?

Ask the patient to remove the arm from the sleeve or roll it up loosely so the cuff is on bare skin, because constricting clothing falsely elevates readings and the trap is documenting an inaccurate BP as true.
Question 2960

During intake, a 64-year-old reports dizziness when standing; seated vitals are BP 128/78, HR 88, and when you help him stand his BP drops to 96/62 with HR 110—what should the CMA do next?

Have the patient sit or lie down immediately and notify the provider while rechecking vitals for stability, because this orthostatic drop suggests he may faint and fall; a common mistake is completing the rest of the intake while the patient remains standing.
Question 2961

During CPR in the clinic, the AED arrives and you’re about to place pads on an adult with a sweaty chest; what should you do next?

Quickly dry the chest and apply pads as shown, then follow AED prompts because moisture can interfere with pad adherence and shock delivery; the trap is delaying pad placement to continue compressions indefinitely without defibrillation when indicated.
Question 2962

In the waiting room, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what is the CMA’s first action?

Check responsiveness and breathing and call for help/activate EMS and get the AED because early activation and AED access saves time; the trap is searching for a pulse for too long before initiating the emergency sequence.
Question 2963

A patient arrives for a routine visit shivering with an oral temperature of 102.4°F and a new cough; what is the best next step before finishing intake questions?

Provide a mask, perform hand hygiene, and place the patient in an exam room using standard precautions, because source control reduces transmission and the trap is seating them in the waiting room while you “finish paperwork.”
Question 2964

During intake, a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain a BP of 82/54 with a regular pulse of 110; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient sit or lie down and recheck BP manually while alerting the provider/initiating clinic emergency protocol, because symptomatic hypotension is unstable and the trap is continuing routine intake or relying on a single machine reading.
Question 2965

You are about to administer a prescribed medication injection and the label on your prepared syringe does not match the MAR order; what is the best next step?

Do not administer—stop and resolve the discrepancy by rechecking the original order/medication label and confirming with the provider/pharmacy as appropriate, because mismatch signals a potential medication error and the trap is “guessing” which is correct.
Question 2966

A patient’s BP is 196/118 during a sports physical and they deny symptoms; what should the CMA do next?

Recheck manually after a few minutes of rest and promptly notify the provider of persistent severe hypertension, because it may require urgent evaluation and the trap is clearing the patient because they are “asymptomatic.”
Question 2967

You enter to give an injection and find the patient’s identity band is missing and another patient’s chart is open in the room; what should you do first?

Pause and verify the patient using two identifiers and correct the chart before any medication is given, because wrong-patient errors are high-risk and the trap is relying on room number or facial recognition.
Question 2968

A patient with diabetes becomes pale and sweaty in the waiting area and says, “I feel like I’m going to pass out”; what should the CMA do first?

Bring the patient to a safe position (sit/lie), assess responsiveness, and alert clinical staff immediately to evaluate for hypoglycemia/emergency, because preventing a fall and rapid escalation comes before routine questions and the trap is sending them back to wait.
Question 2969

A patient comes in for a wound check and you see you forgot to bring clean gloves into the room; what should you do before touching the dressing?

Perform hand hygiene and leave the patient area to retrieve gloves/supplies, then return and don gloves, because gloves are not optional for wound care and the trap is “just doing it quickly” with bare hands.
Question 2970

The provider asks you to “clean the wound and apply a new dressing” to an abrasion; what technique is most appropriate in an outpatient clinic?

Use clean technique with hand hygiene, clean gloves, and sterile dressing supplies as indicated, because most minor wound care is clean rather than sterile and the trap is breaking asepsis by reusing contaminated materials.
Question 2971

During intake you measure a respiratory rate of 8/min in an adult who is very drowsy; what should the CMA do first?

Assess responsiveness and airway/breathing and call for immediate clinical assistance (activate emergency response if needed), because bradypnea can be life-threatening and the trap is retaking vitals repeatedly instead of escalating.
Question 2972

While taking an oral temperature, the patient coughs directly toward you; what is the best immediate action?

Step back, perform hand hygiene, don appropriate PPE (at minimum mask/eye protection per exposure risk), and reattempt using proper infection control, because exposure prevention is immediate and the trap is continuing the procedure unprotected.
Question 2973

During intake, a 68-year-old sits down and says he feels “a little dizzy,” and you obtain BP 82/50, HR 118, RR 22; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and call the provider/activate clinic emergency response because the vitals suggest shock/hypotension; the trap is finishing the intake or letting the patient walk to the exam room.
Question 2974

The provider orders a subcutaneous injection and the patient has extensive bruising at the usual abdominal sites from anticoagulants; what should the CMA do?

Select an alternate appropriate site and rotate away from bruised areas (and confirm with the provider if site selection is unclear), because injecting into bruised tissue increases bleeding/absorption variability and the trap is using the same spot for convenience.
Question 2975

You remove gloves after cleaning an exam table and immediately reach for the door handle to leave; what should you do instead?

Perform hand hygiene after glove removal before touching environmental surfaces, because gloves can have microtears/contamination and the trap is assuming glove removal alone prevents spread.
Question 2976

You are about to take a tympanic temperature and notice visible ear drainage; what is the best action?

Use an alternate route per clinic protocol (e.g., oral/temporal) and perform hand hygiene/PPE as needed, because drainage can affect accuracy and infection risk, and the trap is forcing the probe into an ear with possible infection.
Question 2977

While preparing a vaccine, you accidentally touch the rubber stopper after swabbing it; what should you do?

Re-clean the stopper with alcohol and allow it to dry before inserting the needle, because you recontaminated the entry point and the trap is proceeding because “it was only a quick touch.”
Question 2978

A 24-year-old anxious patient’s first BP is 168/96 after rushing in from the parking lot; what is the best next step before reporting it as hypertension?

Recheck manually after the patient rests 5 minutes with proper cuff size and arm at heart level because technique/anxiety can falsely elevate readings; the trap is documenting a single rushed reading as definitive.
Question 2979

A patient is scheduled for an IM injection but is standing and appears unsteady; what is the safest positioning before administration?

Seat the patient with the arm supported (or have them lie down if history of fainting), because injections can trigger syncope and the trap is administering while standing, increasing fall risk.
Question 2980

A patient becomes unresponsive in the clinic hallway and you cannot feel a pulse; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/clinic code), begin CPR, and send someone for the AED, because early CPR/AED saves lives and the trap is searching for a provider before starting compressions.
Question 2981

A patient in a wheelchair needs orthostatic vital signs for dizziness; what is the correct sequencing?

Measure after 5 minutes supine, then sitting, then standing with safety support and document symptoms because proper timing and fall prevention matter; the trap is taking all readings back-to-back without rest or guarding.
Question 2982

A patient with a frequent cough is checking in at the front desk without a mask; what should the CMA do next?

Provide a mask, perform hand hygiene, and separate the patient from others (e.g., designated area/room) because source control interrupts droplet spread; the trap is waiting until the back office to address it.
Question 2983

You enter an exam room and see dried blood on the counter from the previous patient; what is the best immediate action?

Question 2984

Question 2985

You obtain an SpO₂ of 84% on a cold-fingered patient who is speaking in full sentences; what is the best next action?

Question 2986

Assist the patient to a seated or supine position immediately and call for help because preventing a fall is the priority; the trap is trying to finish the weight or walking them to the exam room.
Question 2988

Question 3000
