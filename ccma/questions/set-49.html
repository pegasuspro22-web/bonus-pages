Question 4801

While scheduling, a patient demands the first appointment Monday but the provider has a blocked time for procedures; what is the best response?

Offer the next appropriate open slot or place the patient on a waitlist per scheduling rules, because overriding templates can disrupt care and the trap is squeezing them in without considering clinic workflow and required resources.
Question 4802

A patient presents for a visit but says they switched plans last week and “coverage should be active,” yet eligibility check shows inactive; what should the CMA do next?

Inform the patient and follow the office’s financial policy (collect self-pay/deposit or reschedule/non-urgent hold) while offering to recheck details, because services may not be covered and the trap is guaranteeing coverage without verification.
Question 4803

A patient’s insurance card lists a different last name than the one on their driver’s license, and the patient says, “Just bill it anyway”; what should the CMA do first?

Question 4804

A patient asks on the phone, “Can you tell me what dose to take of my new medication? The bottle says one tablet but I’m not sure”; what should the CMA do next?

Question 4805

A patient calls angrily about a denied claim and asks you to “change the diagnosis code to get it paid”; what should the CMA do best?

Explain you cannot alter codes to obtain payment and route the issue to billing/provider for proper review, because changing codes without clinical basis is fraud; the trap is “helping” by selecting a payable code.

Question 4896

At check-in, a patient asks for a printed visit summary, and you notice another patient’s summary is sitting on the counter; what should the CMA do first?

Remove/secure the document immediately and follow the clinic’s privacy incident process because unattended PHI is a HIPAA risk; the trap is handing out papers quickly to “keep the line moving.”
Question 4897

A patient with low health literacy is starting an antibiotic and asks, “Can I stop it when I feel better?”; what should the CMA say/do?

Reinforce completing the full course as directed and use teach-back to confirm the plan, because stopping early is a common error that can cause treatment failure and resistance.
Question 4898

A patient calls stating they never received the specialist’s appointment after a referral placed 2 weeks ago; what should the CMA do first?

Verify referral status (sent/received, correct fax/address/authorization) and then coordinate with the specialist office while updating the patient because closed-loop referral prevents care gaps; the trap is telling the patient to “call the specialist” without checking transmission/approval issues.
Question 4899

During discharge teaching after a minor procedure, the patient says, “My daughter handles all my medical stuff,” and tries to leave before instructions; what is the best CMA action?

Provide the instructions directly to the patient using plain language and teach-back, and only include the daughter if the patient agrees/authorization is on file, because the trap is assuming a family member can receive information without permission.
Question 4900

At checkout, a patient with diabetes says they “sometimes skip” medication because they can’t afford it; what should the CMA do first?

Acknowledge the barrier and notify the provider/team while offering appropriate community/resources (e.g., assistance programs, pharmacy discount options per clinic process), because the trap is simply re-educating without addressing affordability.
