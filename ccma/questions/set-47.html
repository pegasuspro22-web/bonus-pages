Question 4601

You obtain a 12-lead EKG and the tracing shows a wandering baseline with heavy artifact; the patient is talking and shivering—what should you do next?

Instruct the patient to lie still and warm them as able, ensure electrodes have good skin contact, then repeat the EKG because motion/muscle tremor causes artifact; the trap is printing and handing off a poor-quality strip.
Question 4602

During venipuncture the patient reports tingling and sharp, radiating pain down the arm as blood begins to flow; what should the CMA do first?

Immediately release the tourniquet and remove the needle, apply pressure, and report per protocol because this can indicate nerve involvement, and the trap is “repositioning the needle” to keep the draw.
Question 4603

You collect a CBC and notice the lavender tube is only half filled and has a small clot when gently inverted; what should you do?

Recollect the specimen because underfilling and clotting can invalidate CBC results, and the trap is shaking the tube to “break up clots,” which also causes hemolysis.
Question 4604

The provider asks you to draw a peak medication level and the requisition specifies “draw exactly at 10:00”; the patient arrives at 10:25—what should you do next?

Notify the provider/lab and follow instructions rather than drawing immediately because timing affects interpretation, and the trap is drawing anyway and documenting “late” without escalation.
Question 4605

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint right after you remove the needle; what is the best next action in an outpatient clinic?

Assist the patient to a safe position (seated with head down or supine), monitor, and get help as needed because syncope can lead to injury, and the trap is letting them “walk it off” to the front desk.
Question 4606

You are running a CLIA-waived urine pregnancy test and the control fails; a patient is waiting for the result—what should you do next?

Do not report a result, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (check expiration/storage, run a new control, use a new kit) and document QC because failed controls invalidate testing; the trap is reporting the patient result anyway because the line “looks clear.”
Question 4607

After drawing a specimen, you label the tube at the counter using the schedule printout, but the patient has already left the room; what is the correct next step?

Treat it as an identification error and recollect with bedside labeling using two identifiers because unlabeled/mislabeled specimens are unacceptable, and the trap is “matching it later” from memory.
Question 4608

Midway through an EKG, you realize V1 and V2 are placed on the 4th intercostal space but swapped left-to-right; what is the best action?

Stop, correct the lead placement, and repeat the EKG because incorrect precordial placement can mimic or hide pathology, and the trap is leaving it and “telling the provider” without repeating.
Question 4609

A fingerstick glucose meter gives an “HI” reading on a symptomatic patient; what should the CMA do next?

Repeat per clinic protocol and immediately alert the provider/activate the urgent response plan while documenting because “HI” can indicate critical hyperglycemia, and the trap is only rechecking later or offering medical advice outside CMA scope.
Question 4610

A patient is taking photos of the computer screen at check-out where other patients’ names are visible on the schedule; what should the CMA do next?

Ask the patient to stop and reposition/cover the screen immediately because it exposes others’ PHI; the trap is focusing only on the patient’s own privacy and ignoring bystanders’ information.
Question 4611

A patient’s spouse calls asking for the visit summary and medication list “because I handle all their care,” and the chart has no release on file; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Verify whether the patient has authorized disclosure and, if not, politely decline and offer to speak with the patient directly because HIPAA requires permission; the trap is sharing because the caller sounds involved or knows identifiers.
Question 4612

A patient checks in and says they changed their address but asks you not to update it in the chart so a family member “won’t find out”; what is the best response?

Explain you must keep the record accurate and discuss safe contact preferences (phone, portal, alternate mailing) rather than leaving demographics incorrect because inaccurate records can harm care and billing; the trap is agreeing to keep incorrect data.
Question 4613

During intake, the patient says “I don’t want the flu shot,” but the provider later orders it and the patient is quiet; what should the CMA do before administering?

Reconfirm consent and understanding with the patient and allow questions because consent must be voluntary and current; the trap is assuming silence equals consent once an order exists.
Question 4614

A teen arrives alone for a routine sports physical and asks that their parent not be told about the visit details; what should the CMA do first?

Involve the provider/office lead to confirm state consent/privacy rules before promising confidentiality because minors’ rights vary by service and jurisdiction; the trap is guaranteeing secrecy without verifying legal authority.
Question 4615

A patient asks you to email their lab results to a personal email address because they “can’t access the portal”; what is the best action?

Follow the clinic’s secure communication process (portal/secure messaging or documented authorized release) rather than regular email because standard email may be unsecured; the trap is sending PHI by convenience without safeguards.
Question 4616

While rooming a patient, you overhear two staff discussing a celebrity patient’s lab results in the hallway outside exam rooms; what should the CMA do first?

Ask them to stop and move the conversation to a private area because PHI must be protected from incidental disclosure; the trap is assuming staff-to-staff talk is always permitted regardless of location.
Question 4617

After the provider leaves, a patient asks you to “add that I have chest pain” so insurance will cover the visit; what is the CMA’s best response?

Decline and inform the provider because adding false symptoms is fraudulent and clinically unsafe; the trap is treating it as a harmless coding workaround.
Question 4618

A patient calls for test results and says, “Just tell me if it’s cancer”; the result is posted but not reviewed by the provider yet; what should the CMA do?

Explain results must be reviewed and communicated per clinic process and route the message urgently if concerning, because interpreting or disclosing unreleased results can be harmful; the trap is reading the result verbatim and implying a diagnosis.
Question 4619

The front desk asks you to “just sign in” to their EHR account because yours is locked; what is the correct action?

Refuse and request proper access/reset because sharing logins violates security and accountability requirements; the trap is thinking it’s acceptable for speed during a busy clinic.
Question 4620

A provider gives a verbal order while walking away, and you’re not sure you heard the dosage correctly; what should the CMA do first?

Use read-back to verify the complete order before documenting/acting because misheard doses are a high-risk error; the trap is guessing and “fixing it later.”
Question 4621

During check-in, a patient asks to see the lab results of their adult daughter and says, “She told me it’s fine,” while holding the daughter’s insurance card; what should the CMA do next?

Politely decline and request the daughter’s written authorization (or speak directly with the daughter after verifying identity) before releasing any information, because HIPAA requires patient permission and the common mistake is treating a relative’s verbal claim or insurance card as consent.
Question 4622

You realize you documented a blood pressure in the wrong patient’s EHR 10 minutes ago; what should you do next?

Immediately follow the EHR correction process (late entry/addendum with date/time and explanation) and notify the appropriate supervisor because record integrity is legal documentation; the trap is deleting or overwriting to “hide” the error.
Question 4623

You are documenting a patient message and accidentally type it into the wrong date’s encounter; what is the best next step?

Move/correct it using the EHR’s late entry/addendum workflow to preserve an audit trail because misfiled documentation can mislead care decisions; the trap is copying/pasting without indicating the correction.
Question 4624

During check-in, a patient’s spouse insists on picking up the visit summary and lab results “because I’m the one who manages their care,” and the patient is still in the exam room; what should the CMA do next?

Verify the spouse’s authorization in the record and, if none is documented, do not release any information and instead obtain the patient’s explicit permission (or have the patient complete a release); HIPAA allows sharing only with the patient or an authorized representative, and the common trap is assuming family involvement equals permission.
Question 4625

A patient’s insurance card shows a different last name than the patient states, and the date of birth is hard to read; what is the CMA’s best next step before registration?

Confirm patient identity using at least two identifiers and update demographics/scan a clear card image because eligibility and billing depend on accurate data; the trap is guessing the DOB or assuming the name change doesn’t matter.
Question 4626

An insurance claim is denied for “prior authorization required,” and the patient calls the clinic upset; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Route to the appropriate staff to verify whether prior auth was obtained and help gather required documentation because denials are often process-related; the trap is telling the patient to “just pay” or promising coverage without verification.
Question 4627

The provider documents a procedure but the charge entry is missing, and billing asks you to “add the code you think fits”; what should the CMA do?

Do not select codes independently; instead, notify the provider/coding team to correct documentation and coding because coding must match provider documentation and scope; the trap is choosing a code to be helpful and creating compliance risk.
Question 4628

While rooming a teen with a parent present, the patient quietly asks about pregnancy testing and says “don’t tell my mom”—what should the CMA do first?

Ask the provider to speak with the patient privately and follow state/clinic policy on minor consent and confidentiality; promising secrecy or refusing outright are common traps.
Question 4629

A patient asks for a referral status and says they already saw the specialist but the clinic never received the report; what should the CMA do next?

Document the patient’s statement and follow up with the specialist office to request the consult note per process because care coordination depends on closing the loop; the trap is waiting for it to arrive automatically or documenting without initiating follow-up.
Question 4630

You leave a voicemail to remind a patient of tomorrow’s appointment, and their greeting includes their full name; what is the best message content?

Leave minimal information (clinic name, callback number, appointment time) without disclosing sensitive details because voicemail can be accessed by others; the trap is stating the reason for visit or test type.
Question 4631

A patient calls asking for her lab results, but the CMA cannot confirm her date of birth because she says “I don’t want to give that over the phone”—what is the best action?

Explain you must verify identity using approved identifiers and offer a call back after verification in person/through the patient portal; giving results without verification is a HIPAA violation.
Question 4632

A CMA realizes she charted “left ear pain” on the wrong patient with a similar name in the EHR; the provider has not signed the note yet—what should the CMA do next?

Notify the supervisor/provider and follow the EHR’s correction process (add an amended/corrected entry and clearly label the error); deleting or “quietly” changing the note is a common legal trap.
Question 4633

The CMA documents a vaccine lot number but later discovers one digit is wrong—what is the best way to fix it in the medical record?

Enter a dated correction/addendum that identifies the incorrect lot number and records the correct one without erasing the original entry; overwriting removes the audit trail and is a legal trap.
Question 4634

A patient wants a copy of the full medical record today and becomes upset when told it’s not immediately available—what should the CMA do next?

Provide the proper records request process (authorization, identity verification, and release per HIPAA/state timelines) and route to the records/designee; handing over the chart without authorization is the trap.
Question 4635

A coworker asks the CMA to share a patient’s diagnosis because “I’m covering her phone messages today”—what is the best response?

Confirm the coworker has a job-related need-to-know before sharing any PHI; assuming access because someone is “helping out” is a common HIPAA mistake.
Question 4636

The provider documents a procedure note but the diagnosis field is blank, and the claim is being prepared—what should the CMA do?

Flag the missing/unclear diagnosis for the provider to complete (medical necessity link) before billing; guessing a diagnosis code is the trap and is not within CMA authority.
Question 4637

Verify coverage/eligibility and update demographics/insurance in the system or refer to billing; assuming active coverage is a common cause of claim denial.
Question 4638

Do not process the refill independently; route the request through the provider per clinic policy and document the request appropriately, because acting as the prescriber is outside CMA scope.
Question 4639

A patient arrives late and demands to be seen, but the schedule is full and multiple patients are already roomed—what should the CMA do first?

Use clinic scheduling protocol (offer the next available slot, waitlist, or reschedule) and inform the provider/lead if needed; “squeezing in” without authorization disrupts care and is a common workflow error.
Question 4640

During check-in, a patient says their spouse will pick up “a copy of my labs” later today and asks you to leave the results at the front desk in an envelope; what should the CMA do next to stay HIPAA-compliant?

Explain you can only release records to the patient or a verified authorized recipient, then follow the clinic’s standard release process by confirming written authorization/identity before providing any results, because leaving labs for pickup without verified authorization is an improper disclosure.
Question 4641

Question 4648

Question 4698

The CMA documents a late entry for a phone call from yesterday; what is the correct documentation approach?

Label it as a “late entry” with the actual date/time of the original event and the current date/time of documentation, because making it look like it was charted yesterday (backdating) is a common legal trap.
Question 4699

A patient requests their entire record “right now” at the front desk; what should the CMA do?

Provide the release-of-information request process and timeline, verify identity, and route appropriately rather than handing over the chart immediately, because immediate access without verification/logging can cause a HIPAA breach.
Question 4700

During check-in, a patient asks to change their pharmacy and also requests a refill; what should the CMA do first?

Update the pharmacy information in the EHR and route the refill request to the provider per protocol, because the CMA should not authorize refills and an incorrect pharmacy entry can create a medication safety error.
