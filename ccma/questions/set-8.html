Question 701

At checkout, a patient says their insurance “should cover everything” and asks you to guarantee there will be no bill; what should the CMA say/do?

Explain that coverage depends on the plan and services, collect required copay/confirm benefits per process, and offer an estimate if available, because guarantees are inappropriate and the trap is promising “no cost” to keep the patient satisfied.
Question 702

The provider asks you to “just chart that the patient refused the vaccine” but the patient actually said they want to think about it and decide next visit; what should the CMA do?

Document the patient’s exact statement and any education provided, because records must be factual and the trap is using stronger language (“refused”) that could affect care and liability.
Question 703

You entered a patient’s phone number in the wrong chart and already saved it; what is the best documentation action?

Amend the incorrect chart entry following the EHR correction process and document the reason, then enter it in the correct chart, because accuracy and audit trails matter and the trap is copying/pasting between charts without verifying identifiers.
Question 704

A patient calls saying their portal shows an abnormal result, and they want you to interpret what it means; what should the CMA do?

Acknowledge the concern, avoid interpretation, and route the message per protocol for provider follow-up, because explaining clinical meaning is outside CMA scope and the trap is “reassuring” with your own interpretation.
Question 705

While rooming a patient, you realize you opened the wrong patient’s EHR and can see diagnoses on the screen; what is the best immediate action?

Close the chart immediately and open the correct chart after verifying two identifiers, because continuing to view or document in the wrong record creates a privacy breach and documentation error.
Question 706

A patient’s spouse asks at the front desk, “What were his lab results from yesterday?” while the patient is in the restroom; what should the CMA do first?

Ask to speak directly with the patient or obtain documented permission before sharing anything, because HIPAA allows disclosure only to authorized individuals and the trap is assuming a spouse automatically has access.
Question 707

You enter a vaccine lot number in the EHR and then notice you transposed two digits after signing the note; what should you do next?

Follow the EHR’s amendment/correction process (add an addendum with the correct lot and reason) rather than deleting or overwriting, because legal documentation requires an auditable trail and the trap is trying to “erase” the error.
Question 708

A provider tells you to “add that the patient denied chest pain” after the visit even though you did not ask; what is the best response?

Document only what you personally obtained/observed and notify the provider you cannot attest to information you didn’t collect, because adding unverified statements is falsification and the trap is “helping” the chart.
Question 709

At check-in, a patient says their employer is demanding proof of today’s visit and asks you to email “whatever you can” to their supervisor while the patient is standing at the front desk.

Obtain the patient’s written authorization specifying what may be released and to whom (or provide a patient-directed note/portal access instead) before sending anything, because HIPAA requires documented permission for disclosures to third parties and a common trap is assuming a verbal request at the desk is enough.
Question 710

A patient arrives late and demands to be seen immediately, raising their voice in the waiting area; what should the CMA do next?

Use calm, respectful de-escalation, move the discussion away from others, and explain scheduling options (wait, reschedule, or urgent evaluation if symptoms suggest) because professionalism and privacy matter and the trap is arguing publicly or disclosing other patients’ schedule details.
Question 711

A teen presents alone for a visit and asks that her parent not be told why she is here; what should the CMA do first?

Confirm with the provider/clinic policy whether the visit type allows confidential care under applicable law and document appropriately, because minors’ consent/privacy rules vary and the trap is promising confidentiality before verifying.
Question 712

A claim is denied for “incorrect patient information,” and you notice the date of birth in the registration screen doesn’t match the insurance card; what should the CMA do first?

Verify DOB with the patient and insurance card, correct the registration data, and notify billing to resubmit, because eligibility/identity errors commonly cause denials and the trap is resubmitting without fixing the root cause.
Question 713

During check-out, a patient’s friend offers to pick up the patient’s printed discharge instructions “because she’s parking the car”; what should the CMA do?

Confirm the patient’s permission (ideally written/verbal in the moment) before releasing documents, because printed instructions contain PHI and the trap is treating it like non-sensitive paperwork.
Question 714

A patient asks you to email their visit summary to a personal email address from your work account; what is the best action?

Use an approved secure patient portal or authorized release process instead of standard email, because unencrypted email can violate HIPAA and the trap is thinking the patient’s request makes it automatically permissible.
Question 715

A provider asks you to sign their name on a consent form because they are running behind; what is the CMA’s best response?

Refuse and have the provider obtain/sign the consent themselves (or sign only your own name as a witness if appropriate), because signing for someone else is fraud and the trap is viewing it as a time-saver.
Question 716

In the waiting room, a patient loudly asks, “Why can’t I get my STI results today?” and others are nearby; what should the CMA do first?

Move the conversation to a private area and speak quietly, because privacy must be protected even during routine communication and the trap is answering in public to be “helpful.”
Question 717

A patient arrives for a visit but the insurance card on file is expired; what should the CMA do first?

Request the current insurance card and verify eligibility before checkout/claim submission, because incorrect payer information commonly causes denials and the trap is “we’ll bill the old one and fix it later.”
Question 718

A patient becomes angry on the phone and demands, “Tell me what my insurance said about my claim denial”; what is the best first step before discussing details?

Verify the caller’s identity using established identifiers and confirm authorization if needed, because billing information is still PHI and the trap is assuming it’s “nonclinical” so verification isn’t necessary.
Question 719

A patient wants a copy of their full medical record “right now” at the front desk; what should the CMA do next?

Provide the records request/release-of-information process and route it to the designated staff/HIM workflow, because records release has legal time frames and identity verification requirements and the trap is handing out charts informally.
Question 720

You schedule a patient for a procedure visit, and their insurance plan requires prior authorization; what should the CMA do next?

Flag it for the authorization process before the appointment date and inform the patient of potential coverage limits, because lack of authorization can lead to denial and the trap is waiting until after the service is provided.
Question 721

During rooming, a patient says their employer needs proof they were seen today and asks you to email a copy of today’s visit note to the HR manager; what should the CMA do next?

Explain you can’t release the visit note without the patient’s written authorization and instead offer an appropriate verification (e.g., a work/school excuse or appointment confirmation) and route any release request to the designated staff, because emailing PHI to a third party without authorization is a HIPAA breach (common trap: assuming the patient’s verbal request is enough).
Question 722

A patient calls to schedule a follow-up and says, “Any time is fine,” but the provider’s order says follow up in 2 weeks due to abnormal results; what should the CMA do best?

Schedule within the ordered time frame and communicate urgency appropriately (and notify the clinical team if no slots are available), because delays can compromise care and the trap is offering the next routine opening without checking the order.
Question 723

While rooming a patient, you notice the provider’s note from yesterday is open on the exam room computer where the next patient can read it; what should the CMA do first?

Immediately close/lock the screen and turn it away from public view, because preventing unauthorized disclosure is the priority and the trap is “finishing rooming” before securing PHI.
Question 724

A patient insists the diagnosis code on their statement is wrong and asks you to change it; what should the CMA do?

Route the concern to the provider/coding-billing team for review rather than changing diagnoses, because CMAs don’t assign or alter diagnoses and the trap is thinking it’s a simple clerical fix.
Question 725

A patient says, “I don’t understand what the provider just told me,” and asks you to explain the treatment plan in detail; what is the best response?

Clarify using approved after-visit instructions and teach-back, then escalate questions requiring clinical interpretation to the provider, because CMAs can reinforce education but cannot diagnose or independently interpret plans and the trap is giving medical advice.
Question 726

You are documenting a phone call and accidentally type sensitive information into the wrong section of the EHR template; what is the best next action?

Correct it using the proper correction/addendum method and place it in the correct location while keeping the audit trail, because misfiled documentation can affect care and the trap is deleting content to hide the mistake.
Question 727

While scanning documents into the EHR, you notice two pages belong to a different patient; what should you do next?

Do not upload them to the wrong chart; instead, route for correct indexing and notify the appropriate person to prevent misfiling, because wrong-chart documents can harm care and create legal exposure and the trap is “scanning now, fixing later.”
Question 728

A patient refuses to sign the general consent-to-treat form and says, “Just take my vitals and I’ll decide later”; what should the CMA do first?

Pause non-urgent care and notify the provider/manager to address refusal and options, because proceeding without consent increases legal risk and the trap is assuming routine intake is always allowed without consent.
Question 729

A parent requests access to their 17-year-old’s visit notes through the patient portal; the visit was for reproductive health counseling; what should the CMA do next?

Escalate to the supervisor/privacy officer and follow state law/clinic policy on minor confidentiality before releasing any information, because minors may have protected confidentiality and the trap is releasing records solely because the parent requests them.
Question 730

A patient arrives late and demands to be seen; the schedule is fully booked and the provider is double-booked; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Check the clinic’s late-arrival policy, assess for urgent symptoms briefly, and offer the next appropriate option (reschedule/available slot/triage to provider if urgent), because safety comes before fairness and the trap is automatically refusing without screening for urgency.
Question 731

A patient asks you to email their immunization record to their personal Gmail “because it’s faster”; what should the CMA do?

Use the clinic’s approved secure method (portal, encrypted email, or printed/pickup with ID verification) or obtain documented acknowledgment of risk if policy allows, because standard email is not secure and the trap is prioritizing convenience over privacy.
Question 732

A coworker asks you to “just chart” a blood pressure you didn’t take so the visit can be closed out; what should the CMA do?

Refuse and report/seek guidance from a supervisor, because documenting care not provided is falsification and the trap is thinking it’s harmless “helping the workflow.”
Question 733

After documenting an injection in the EHR, you realize you entered the wrong lot number; what is the correct way to fix it?

Add an EHR correction/addendum per system policy with the correct lot number (do not delete or obscure the original entry), because records must preserve an audit trail and the trap is trying to “erase” the mistake.
Question 734

You answered the phone and the caller says, “I’m Dr. Lee’s office—send the last note and meds list now,” but provides no patient DOB; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the caller’s identity and the patient identifiers and confirm a valid purpose/authorization before sending any records, because HIPAA permits sharing for treatment only with proper verification and the trap is trusting the caller’s claim.
Question 735

Move the conversation to a private area or lower your voice and limit details, because financial information and health-related billing details are PHI and the trap is discussing balances at normal volume in public.
Question 736

Verify the patient’s identity and confirm permission to disclose to the spouse (or use a documented release) before sharing any results, because HIPAA requires authorization and the trap is assuming speakerphone implies consent.
Question 737

Schedule an office visit of appropriate length (not a quick nurse visit) and note both concerns, because multiple issues may require provider evaluation and the trap is booking a short slot meant only for single-purpose visits.
Question 738

Question 739

Question 740

Question 741

Notify the supervisor/privacy officer immediately and document the patient’s report per HIPAA breach-response procedures because disclosing PHI to an unauthorized party is a potential breach; the common trap is trying to “fix it” by calling the employer back or minimizing the incident without escalation and documentation.
Question 743

Question 792

While rooming a patient, the CMA notices the previous visit note in the EHR has the wrong medication dose entered under today’s date; what is the best action?

Create a corrected entry/addendum per EHR policy (date/time, reason, initials) without deleting the original because the legal record must remain auditable (common trap: “fixing” it by overwriting or deleting).
Question 793

A caller says they are from an insurance company and asks the CMA to confirm a patient’s diagnosis to “process the claim faster”; what is the best response?

Decline to disclose and route to the billing/records process with verification and minimum necessary information because identity and authorization must be confirmed before releasing PHI (common trap: assuming insurers always have automatic access by phone).
Question 794

A provider verbally tells the CMA, “Document that I reviewed the MRI images,” but the provider has not actually done so yet; what should the CMA do?

Document only what is known and true (e.g., “provider notified, review pending”) and prompt the provider to complete their own attestation because falsifying documentation is legally risky (common trap: charting future actions as completed).
Question 795

A patient shows up 40 minutes late for a 15-minute visit and insists they “must be seen today”; what should the CMA do first?

Acknowledge the concern, check the provider’s schedule and clinic late policy, and offer appropriate options (wait, reschedule, urgent triage if symptoms sound acute) because scheduling decisions must balance access and safety (common trap: automatically refusing without symptom screening).
Question 796

A patient requests to pay cash and asks the CMA not to bill insurance because they don’t want their spouse to find out; what should the CMA do?

Follow clinic policy to offer self-pay while explaining the limits (itemized receipts/portal may still show charges) and ensure documentation is accurate because patient preference doesn’t allow altering records or hiding services improperly (common trap: agreeing to “code it differently” to keep it private).
Question 797

The CMA receives a referral request from a specialist’s office asking for the patient’s entire chart “to save time”; what should the CMA send?

Send only the minimum necessary records relevant to the referral (per authorization/policy) because HIPAA requires limiting disclosure to what’s needed (common trap: faxing the whole chart for convenience).
Question 798

At the front desk, a patient says, “My employer needs proof I was here—can you tell them what I was seen for?”; what should the CMA do?

Offer a basic attendance note only (date/time) unless the patient signs a specific release for diagnosis details because disclosure of visit reason requires authorization (common trap: sharing “just a little” medical information).
Question 799

While verifying insurance, the CMA notices the patient’s name on the card doesn’t match the EHR (married name vs maiden name) and the date of birth matches; what should the CMA do next?

Confirm identity with approved identifiers and update demographics/scan documentation as allowed, then verify eligibility because mismatched names can cause claim denials (common trap: ignoring the mismatch since DOB matches).
Question 800

The CMA is entering a phone message: the patient reports “chest tightness when walking to the mailbox” and asks for an appointment next week; what should the CMA do first?

Escalate immediately per urgent-symptom protocol (alert provider/triage, advise emergency evaluation if directed) and document the exact words because potential emergencies should not be scheduled routinely (common trap: treating it as a normal scheduling request).
