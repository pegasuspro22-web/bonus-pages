Question 1901

A patient with newly prescribed metformin says, “I’ll just take it whenever I remember,” and is rushing to leave; what should the CMA do first to prevent a common medication error?

Use teach-back to confirm the patient can state when to take it and what to do if a dose is missed, because verifying understanding prevents incorrect self-dosing and the trap is giving a quick handout without confirming comprehension.
Question 1902

The clinic’s recall list shows a patient is overdue for a Pap test; when the CMA calls, the patient says they moved and “don’t do that test anymore”; what should the CMA do next?

Confirm identity, explain the reason for the preventive recall, offer to schedule or provide referral options, and document refusal with the reason, because closed-loop recall reduces missed screening and the trap is removing them from the list without documenting outreach and response.
Question 1903

A diabetic patient is scheduled for a fasting lipid panel tomorrow but says, “I ate breakfast today so I guess I’ll just skip the lab”; what is the CMA’s best action?

Clarify the timing (fasting applies before the blood draw, not the day prior), restate the fasting window and water/med guidance, and use teach-back, because correcting misunderstanding prevents unnecessary no-shows and the trap is letting the patient cancel based on incorrect assumptions.
Question 1904

The CMA calls a patient with a critical scheduling change and reaches the roommate who asks, “Is this about her test results?” what should the CMA do NEXT?

Provide only a neutral message to return the call and avoid confirming tests/results, because disclosure to an unauthorized person is a privacy breach and the trap is “just hinting” to get a faster callback.
Question 1905

A parent calls asking for their 17-year-old’s pregnancy test results and says, “I’m the guarantor so you have to tell me”; what should the CMA do?

Decline to release results without verifying legal authority and the patient’s consent per privacy rules and route the request appropriately, because HIPAA/state minor privacy protections may apply and the trap is confusing financial responsibility with permission to disclose.
Question 1906

While rooming a patient you see in the EHR health maintenance that their mammogram has been overdue for 18 months, and the patient says no one told them; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Flag the overdue screening for the provider per clinic workflow and offer to help schedule/print instructions, because it supports preventive care without independently ordering tests; the trap is telling the patient they “need” the test without provider direction.
Question 1907

While rooming a patient for an injection, the CMA notices the vaccine vial label does not match the EHR order but the patient says, “I always get the same one, just do it”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Stop and resolve the discrepancy by rechecking the provider order/lot and confirming the correct vaccine before preparing/administering, because the “five rights” prevent wrong-medication errors and the trap is relying on patient memory or routine.
Question 1908

A patient scheduled for an imaging referral arrives upset saying, “No one told me I needed prior authorization”; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the order and insurance requirements, then coordinate with the referral/authorization process (payer contact as directed) and update the patient on next steps, because preventing delays is the goal and the trap is sending the patient back to the imaging center without checking auth status.
Question 1909

A patient repeats the provider’s new inhaler directions incorrectly (“two puffs every hour if I feel tight”); what should the CMA do next?

Use teach-back again with plain language and then alert the provider/pharmacist if confusion persists, because incorrect dosing can be unsafe; the trap is simply handing the after-visit summary and assuming comprehension.
Question 1910

A 52-year-old leaves after an annual visit with an order for a screening colonoscopy and says, “I’ll call later to schedule,” while rushing to work; what should the CMA do before the patient exits?

Schedule the follow-up/referral or initiate the referral workflow before the patient leaves and give written next-step instructions, because “closing the loop” prevents missed screenings; the trap is assuming the patient will remember to call.
Question 1911

A patient misses a follow-up appointment for abnormal lab results; the patient portal shows the message was never opened—what should the CMA do next?

Initiate the clinic’s closed-loop follow-up (call using approved script/number, verify identity, arrange reschedule, and document contact attempts), because abnormal results require documented outreach and the trap is assuming portal messaging alone fulfills follow-up.
Question 1912

During rooming, a patient says they used their own epinephrine auto-injector 10 minutes ago for hives and throat tightness and now feels “a little better,” and they ask you to just take vitals quickly so they can leave—what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/alert the provider immediately) and keep the patient seated/monitored because symptoms can rebound after epinephrine; the trap is delaying escalation by finishing routine intake or assuming improvement means the emergency is over.

Question 1914

Question 1915

A patient with limited English proficiency is being given colonoscopy prep instructions, and their adult child offers to interpret; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Arrange a qualified medical interpreter and provide simple written instructions in the preferred language because accuracy and consent depend on clear communication; the trap is relying on a family member who may omit or change details.
Question 1995

A patient missed a follow-up appointment for abnormal screening results and hasn’t responded to one reminder call; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Follow the recall system by attempting the next approved contact method (e.g., second call/secure message/letter), document all attempts, and escalate per protocol because continuity requires persistent, trackable outreach; the trap is stopping after one attempt without documentation.
Question 1996

The provider orders a cardiology referral, and the patient asks, “So I just call them?”; what should the CMA do BEST?

Explain the referral process and next steps (who will schedule, expected timeframe, and what to do if not contacted), verify contact information, and document because referrals fail without clear coordination; the trap is assuming the patient understands the workflow.
Question 1997

A patient calls asking why they received a reminder for a mammogram and says they had it done at another clinic last month; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Obtain details and a release/permission as needed to request the outside report and update the record because recall systems depend on accurate data; the trap is turning off reminders without verifying documentation of the completed screening.
Question 1998

A patient is told to return if symptoms worsen, but at discharge they say, “I’m not sure what ‘worsen’ means”; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Reinforce the provider’s instructions using specific, plain-language red flags and when to call vs seek urgent care, then use teach-back because vague instructions reduce safety; the trap is repeating the same wording louder without clarifying.
Question 1999

A patient needs a specialist referral but says they have no transportation and may skip it; what should the CMA do BEST?

Identify and offer appropriate community resources (transportation services, ride programs, telehealth options if available) and document the barrier for the care team because access issues derail referrals; the trap is documenting “noncompliant” without addressing the barrier.
Question 2000

While rooming a 52-year-old, the CMA notices the health maintenance section shows colorectal screening overdue; what is the BEST action to support care coordination?

Flag it for the provider during the visit and ask about prior outside screening to update records because acting during the encounter improves completion; the trap is waiting until after the visit when the opportunity is lost.
