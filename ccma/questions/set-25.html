Question 2401

After giving a vaccine, the patient stands up and says they feel faint; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to sit or lie down and monitor, because preventing injury from syncope is the immediate priority and the trap is walking them to checkout or offering water while they remain standing.
Question 2402

During a dressing change, the sterile field becomes contaminated when the patient’s sleeve brushes the sterile drape; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and set up a new sterile field with new supplies, because once contaminated it cannot be “made sterile again,” and the trap is continuing to save time or supplies.
Question 2403

A provider orders an IM vaccine; you have the correct vial and syringe but the patient says they are “allergic to shots”; what should the CMA do first?

Clarify what reaction they have had and check the chart/notify the provider before administering, because true allergy/previous anaphylaxis changes the plan and the trap is proceeding because “it’s ordered.”
Question 2404

A patient arrives for a wound check; the outer dressing is saturated with bright red blood and it is actively seeping through; what should the CMA do first?

Apply direct pressure with a clean dressing/gloved hands and get the provider immediately, because uncontrolled bleeding is urgent and the trap is fully removing the dressing and “taking a look” before controlling bleeding.
Question 2405

You prepare an injection and the medication label is smudged so you can’t clearly read the concentration; what is the best next step?

Do not use it—obtain a clearly labeled medication and re-verify the order, because unclear labeling risks dosing/medication errors and the trap is guessing based on vial size or color.
Question 2406

While verifying the “rights” before an injection, the patient’s name matches the order but the date of birth does not; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and resolve the identification discrepancy using two identifiers before giving the medication, because wrong-patient errors occur despite partial matches and the trap is assuming it’s “close enough.”
Question 2407

A patient has visible dried blood on the exam table paper from the prior visit; what should the CMA do before seating the next patient?

Don appropriate PPE and disinfect the surface with an approved product for the required contact time, because blood is a potentially infectious material and the trap is just changing paper without proper disinfection.
Question 2408

You are cleaning up after a procedure and find a used needle on the tray; what is the safest action?

Immediately place it in a sharps container without recapping, because recapping increases needlestick risk and the trap is recapping “to be safe” before disposal.
Question 2409

In the waiting room, a patient collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first in an outpatient clinic?

Assess responsiveness and breathing, call for help/activate emergency response and get the AED, because early activation/AED use saves lives and the trap is searching for the chart or moving the patient unnecessarily before calling for help.
Question 2410

You are about to enter a room for a wound check on a patient with draining abscess and cough; what PPE is the best initial choice before you know the exact precautions?

Perform hand hygiene and don gloves plus a mask with eye protection (and add gown if splash risk) using Standard Precautions because drainage and coughing can expose mucous membranes; the trap is using only gloves.
Question 2411

During intake, a 54-year-old patient sits quietly but looks pale; you obtain vitals and the first blood pressure is 86/52 with a pulse of 118, and the patient says, “I feel dizzy when I stand”—what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient remain seated or supine, recheck the blood pressure manually to confirm, and immediately notify the provider/activate urgent assistance because possible hypotension/shock requires rapid escalation; the common trap is documenting it as an “outlier” or letting the patient walk to the exam room unassisted.
Question 2412

The provider orders an IM ceftriaxone injection, but the patient states they had an anaphylactic reaction to penicillin and “maybe cephalosporins too”; what should the CMA do before preparing the medication?

Stop and notify the provider for clarification before drawing up/administering because possible cross-reactivity and anaphylaxis history require reassessment; the trap is proceeding because “the order is signed.”
Question 2413

During intake for a 58-year-old, you obtain BP 182/110 with headache and blurred vision, and the patient says they drove themselves and “just need my refill”; what should the CMA do first?

Recheck BP manually after seating the patient quietly and confirm symptoms, then immediately alert the provider/activate urgent protocol because this may be hypertensive crisis; the trap is documenting it as “white coat” and rooming routinely.
Question 2414

A patient’s oral temperature reads 95.8°F right after they finished an ice-water bottle in the lobby; what is the best next step?

Wait ~15 minutes (or use an appropriate alternate site per clinic practice) and retake because recent cold intake falsely lowers oral temps; the trap is reporting hypothermia without validating the reading.
Question 2415

After removing gloves in the exam room, you realize you touched the outside of the glove with bare fingertips while pulling it off; what should you do immediately?

Perform hand hygiene right away because contamination can occur during doffing; the trap is thinking glove removal alone is “clean” and skipping hand hygiene.
Question 2416

While rooming a 74-year-old, you note a radial pulse that is irregularly irregular at 128 and the patient feels lightheaded; what should the CMA do next?

Have the patient sit or lie down, assess for additional symptoms, and notify the provider promptly because this may be unstable tachyarrhythmia; the trap is sending them back to the waiting room after “just documenting irregular pulse.”
Question 2417

A provider orders CBC, PT/INR, BMP, and a lactate from one venipuncture; the CMA prepares tubes—what is the correct order of draw to prevent additive carryover?

Draw blood cultures first (if ordered), then light blue, then serum (red/gold), then green, then lavender, then gray because this sequence reduces additive contamination; a common trap is drawing EDTA before chemistry and falsely elevating potassium/calcium changes.
Question 2418

A fasting lipid panel is ordered, but the patient says they drank a sugary coffee 20 minutes ago; what should the CMA do next?

Do not proceed as “fasting” without clarification—notify the provider/ordering clinician to reschedule or document non-fasting status because results may be inaccurate; a common trap is drawing anyway and labeling it fasting.
Question 2419

In the lobby, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive while a family member says, “He has a heart condition—do something!”; what should the CMA do first?

Check responsiveness and breathing and call for help/activate emergency response (have someone get the AED) and start CPR if no normal breathing because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is searching for history or pulse for too long before initiating care.
Question 2420

During venipuncture, blood flow stops after the first tube, and the CMA notices the bevel may be against the vein wall; what should the CMA do next?

Gently reposition the needle (slight pullback/angle adjustment) while keeping it stable because the bevel can occlude flow; a common trap is “digging” or probing, which increases hematoma risk.
Question 2421

Immediately after removing the needle, a patient reports dizziness and becomes pale in the phlebotomy chair; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Stop the draw, activate safety measures (lower head/raise legs or assist to supine), and notify the provider per clinic protocol because this suggests vasovagal syncope; a common trap is having the patient walk to “get air,” increasing fall risk.
Question 2422

During a dressing change, the provider asks you to “just use sterile technique,” but you open a sterile field and your glove brushes the edge of the sink; what should you do next?

Consider the glove contaminated, remove it, perform hand hygiene, and re-glove with sterile gloves because touching nonsterile surfaces breaks sterility; the trap is continuing since “only the cuff touched.”
Question 2423

While collecting a lavender-top tube, the CMA forgets to invert it and sets it down; what should the CMA do to best protect specimen integrity?

Invert the tube the recommended number of times as soon as noticed because EDTA must mix to prevent clotting; a common trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the sample.
Question 2424

A patient’s name and DOB on the requisition do not match the label already placed on the tube by a coworker; the patient is waiting—what should the CMA do first?

Stop the process and re-verify patient identity with two identifiers and relabel correctly at the bedside because mislabeling is a critical lab error; a common trap is “fixing it later,” which can lead to wrong-patient results.
Question 2425

While preparing for a 12-lead EKG, the CMA places V1 and V2 too high on the chest; why should the CMA correct this before running the test?

Reposition to the 4th intercostal space at the sternal borders because incorrect placement can mimic or hide ischemia/infarct patterns; a common trap is thinking “close enough” because the machine will “fix it.”
Question 2426

During a blood draw for a CMP and PT/INR, the CCMA fills the light blue citrate tube halfway because the vein collapses and then fills the serum tube completely; what should the CCMA do next to best ensure valid results?

Stop and recollect the PT/INR in a new light blue tube that can be filled to the required line (using proper order of draw), because an underfilled citrate tube alters the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio and can falsely prolong coagulation results; a common mistake is sending the partially filled tube anyway.
Question 2427

The provider orders a 12-lead EKG; after lead placement, the tracing shows a flat line in one limb lead but others appear normal—what should the CMA do first?

Check that specific electrode connection/skin contact and reattach or replace the electrode because a loose lead commonly causes a single-lead dropout; a common trap is interpreting it as asystole or a cardiac event without troubleshooting.
Question 2428

During an EKG, the baseline is wavy and irregular across multiple leads, and the patient is shivering; what should the CMA do next?

Warm/comfort the patient and ask them to relax and stay still, then repeat the tracing because muscle tremor causes artifact; a common trap is repeatedly repositioning leads when the issue is patient movement.
Question 2429

A patient with a hairy, oily chest needs an EKG and electrodes keep popping off; what is the best action?

Clean and dry the skin and use mild abrasion or clip hair as needed before reapplying electrodes because good skin contact prevents artifact; a common trap is pressing harder on the electrode without improving skin prep.
Question 2430

The CMA draws a gray-top tube for glucose but realizes it was underfilled; what is the best next step?

Recollect using a new tube and a new venipuncture if needed because incorrect fill can alter anticoagulant-to-blood ratio and affect results; a common trap is “topping off” from another tube, which contaminates additives.
Question 2431

After venipuncture, the puncture site begins swelling with bruising; what should the CMA do first?

Release the tourniquet (if still on), withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure because this is likely a hematoma; a common trap is continuing the draw to “finish the tubes,” worsening bleeding.
Question 2432

A patient’s antecubital area has a dialysis fistula on the left and an IV infusing on the right; where should the CMA attempt venipuncture?

Avoid both sites and escalate to the provider for an alternate plan (e.g., different site or trained phlebotomist) because fistulas and IV arms can compromise safety/accuracy; a common trap is drawing below an IV, which can dilute specimens.
Question 2433

A CLIA-waived rapid strep test is due, but the control results are out of range; what should the CMA do next?

Do not report patient results—repeat QC per manufacturer instructions and troubleshoot (new kit/reagents) and notify the supervisor if unresolved because invalid QC means invalid tests; a common trap is reporting “looks positive” despite QC failure.
Question 2434

A CMA performs a waived urine dipstick test but realizes the reagent strip bottle was left uncapped on the counter; what is the best next action?

Discard compromised strips and use a properly stored, in-date container because moisture/light can alter reagent performance; a common trap is using them anyway because the color change “still happens.”
Question 2435

A glucometer POCT result is unexpectedly low, but the patient has no symptoms and states they just washed hands after eating; what should the CMA do first?

Recheck using proper technique (new strip, ensure hands are clean/dry, confirm device coding/calibration if applicable) because contamination/technique can cause false readings; a common trap is treating the number without verifying accuracy.
Question 2436

A provider orders CBC, CMP, and PT/INR; you have a butterfly set and the light blue tube is the first tube needed—what is the BEST way to prevent an underfilled citrate tube?

Draw a discard tube first (non-additive) before filling the light blue to ensure proper fill ratio, because short draws skew PT/INR; the trap is assuming the butterfly won’t affect volume.
Question 2437

A patient becomes anxious during a blood draw and asks, “Are my results normal?” while the CMA is labeling tubes; what is the best response within CMA scope?

Acknowledge and explain you can’t interpret results, then tell them when/how the provider will review them because interpretation is not CMA scope; a common trap is reassuring with “it’s probably fine,” which can mislead or violate policy.
Question 2438

An EKG prints with a rate of 160 and the patient reports chest pressure and is diaphoretic; what should the CMA do next?

Stay with the patient and alert the provider/activate emergency response per clinic protocol because this is potentially unstable tachyarrhythmia; a common trap is filing the EKG first or telling the patient to “wait for the doctor” alone.
Question 2439

After collecting a CBC in a lavender-top tube, the CMA leaves it on the counter for pickup at end of day; what should the CMA do to best prevent inaccurate results?

Follow required transport/storage timing (prompt delivery and appropriate temperature) because delays can alter cell morphology and counts; a common trap is assuming all tubes are stable “all day” at room temperature.
Question 2440

During a fasting lipid draw, you collect a light blue sodium citrate tube first and then realize a lavender EDTA tube was supposed to be drawn before the blue; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Stop and redraw in the correct order of draw (blood cultures, light blue, serum, green, lavender, gray) because wrong additive carryover can alter coagulation results; the trap is “it’s already filled so just send it.”
Question 2441

Question 2442

Question 2443

Question 2444

Question 2445

Question 2448

Question 2449

Question 2450

Question 2451

The EKG tracing shows a wandering baseline and you see a loose chest lead; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Stop, re-prep the skin and reattach/secure the electrode, then repeat the tracing because poor contact causes artifact; the trap is printing and sending a poor-quality EKG that may be misread.
Question 2452

During limb lead placement, the patient has a left below-knee amputation; where should the CMA place the left leg (LL) electrode?

Place the LL electrode on the left lower torso (near the hip/abdomen) in a symmetric position because amputations require proximal placement to maintain a usable lead vector; the trap is skipping the lead or placing it on the prosthesis.
Question 2453

Question 2454

Question 2455

Stop excessive squeezing and recollect properly because heavy milking can hemolyze and contaminate with tissue fluid, affecting results; the trap is continuing to fill the tube to avoid a repeat stick.
Question 2456

Question 2457

Question 2458

Question 2459

Question 2462

Question 2463

Question 2465

Question 2494

A specimen for a CBC is placed on the counter unlabeled, and two other patients are waiting for labs; what should the CMA do immediately?

Label the tube in the presence of the patient using two identifiers before the patient leaves because delayed labeling risks wrong-patient errors; a trap is labeling later “when it’s quieter,” which is a major safety violation.
Question 2495

A patient’s EKG shows a heart rate of 38 with dizziness noted during intake; what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider immediately and continue monitoring per clinic protocol because symptomatic bradycardia may require urgent evaluation; a trap is documenting it as “baseline” and sending the patient back to the lobby.
Question 2496

The CMA draws blood from an arm with an active IV infusion because the other arm is “hard to stick”; what is the best response?

Stop and use an alternate site or follow facility protocol (often drawing below IV after pausing infusion only if permitted) because IV fluids can dilute or contaminate results; a trap is drawing above the IV, which increases erroneous lab values.
Question 2497

A urine dipstick bottle was left open during rooming, and now results seem inconsistent with the patient’s symptoms; what should the CMA do next?

Repeat testing with properly stored strips (and document/notify as needed) because exposure to air/moisture can degrade reagents and cause inaccurate results; a trap is trusting the first read and charting it without addressing storage error.
Question 2498

After a fingerstick for hemoglobin POCT, the CMA documents the value but forgets to record lot number and expiration date; what should the CMA do?

Add the required QC/traceability details promptly per CLIA-waived documentation standards because results must be traceable to reagents and device performance; a trap is assuming “CLIA-waived means no documentation.”
Question 2499

While preparing for venipuncture, the CMA cleanses the site with alcohol and immediately inserts the needle while the skin is still wet—what should the CMA have done?

Allow the antiseptic to air-dry completely to reduce stinging and contamination risk; a common trap is blowing or fanning the site, which re-contaminates the area.
Question 2500

During venipuncture for a coagulation study, the light blue tube stops filling at about half volume—what should the CMA do next?

Discard and redraw a new light blue tube to the fill line because citrate tubes require a 9:1 blood-to-additive ratio, and a common trap is sending an underfilled tube that yields inaccurate PT/INR results.
