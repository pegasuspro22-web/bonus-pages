Question 2201

During the “rights” check for an IM antibiotic, the patient says they are allergic to penicillin and the medication name sounds similar—what should the CMA do next?

Hold the medication and notify the provider immediately because allergy verification is critical before administration; the trap is proceeding because “the provider ordered it” or assuming it’s unrelated.
Question 2202

A provider requests you set up a sterile field for a minor procedure; you accidentally brush the sterile drape with your sleeve—what should you do next?

Stop and replace the contaminated sterile drape because sterility is broken by contact with nonsterile items; the trap is ignoring it because “it barely touched.”
Question 2203

You are about to administer a subcutaneous medication when the patient suddenly says, “I’m pregnant—I forgot to mention”—what is the best action?

Pause the administration and immediately inform the provider because pregnancy can change medication safety/ordering decisions; the trap is administering because “it’s already prepared” and out of the CMA’s scope to judge safety.
Question 2204

You remove an old dressing and note the wound has increased redness, warmth, and purulent drainage compared with last visit—what should the CMA do next?

Cover with a clean dressing and promptly report the change to the provider because it suggests infection requiring provider evaluation; the trap is applying ointment or advising antibiotics, which is outside CMA scope.
Question 2205

A patient with suspected scabies needs vitals and a brief intake; what PPE is most appropriate to don first?

Don gloves (and a gown if contact with clothing/skin is likely) because scabies spreads by direct contact and requires contact precautions; the trap is using only a surgical mask as if it were airborne.
Question 2206

A patient is due for an IM injection and asks you to “just give it in the same sore arm as last time so I don’t have two sore arms”—what should the CMA do?

Assess the preferred site and choose an appropriate, non-tender site with adequate muscle mass (and rotate sites when possible) because injecting into a sore area increases pain and may affect absorption; the trap is prioritizing convenience over safe site selection.
Question 2207

After an IM vaccine, the patient stands up quickly and looks unsteady; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient sit back down and observe (with fall precautions) because syncope can occur after injections and preventing injury is priority; the trap is letting them walk out because they “say they’re fine.”
Question 2208

During intake, a 67-year-old patient reports dizziness; you obtain vitals and note BP 84/52, pulse 118, and the patient looks pale—what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a supine position with legs elevated and immediately alert the provider/activate the clinic’s emergency response because this is symptomatic hypotension suggestive of shock; the common mistake is continuing the intake or having the patient stand/walk, which increases fall risk and delays care.

Question 2217

Question 2298

You enter an exam room and see dried blood on the exam table paper from the previous patient; the next patient is waiting—what should you do first?

Don gloves and clean/disinfect the surface with an appropriate disinfectant and contact time before rooming the next patient because turnover must break the chain of infection; the trap is just replacing paper without disinfecting.
Question 2299

While drawing up supplies for a procedure, you realize you forgot to remove your stethoscope after examining a patient with a draining wound—what is the best next step?

Disinfect the stethoscope (and perform hand hygiene) before using it on anyone else because equipment is a common fomite; the trap is assuming gloves prevented contamination of shared items.
Question 2300

A patient with a frequent cough is in the waiting area without a mask and approaches the desk to check in—what should the CMA do first?

Provide a mask and separate them from others (spatial distancing/rooming when possible) using standard and respiratory hygiene because source control prevents spread; the trap is completing paperwork first while they remain near others.
