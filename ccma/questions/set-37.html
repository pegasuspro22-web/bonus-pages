Question 3601

During a fasting CMP draw, the CMA collects a light blue tube, then a lavender, then realizes a gold SST was also ordered; what should the CMA do next before drawing the SST?

Stop and recollect the tubes in the correct order of draw (light blue then SST/gold then lavender) because incorrect order can cause additive carryover and inaccurate results; a common trap is “just adding the missed tube at the end.”
Question 3602

A patient’s requisition and labels are printed, but the patient steps away to the restroom before labeling; when should the CMA label the tubes?

Label the tubes in the patient’s presence immediately after collection using two identifiers because labeling later increases misidentification risk; the trap is labeling at the nurses’ station “to save time.”
Question 3603

After venipuncture, the CMA notices a rapidly swelling, painful site at the puncture area; what is the best immediate action?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure because this suggests a hematoma/infiltration; the trap is continuing to draw or massaging the area.
Question 3604

The CMA draws a light blue coag tube that is only half full; what should the CMA do?

Recollect a new light blue tube filled to the required line because coag testing requires a precise blood-to-anticoagulant ratio; the trap is topping it off from another tube or submitting it underfilled.
Question 3605

During a 12-lead EKG, the tracing shows consistent wandering baseline in all leads while the patient is shivering; what should the CMA do next?

Warm/comfort the patient and minimize muscle movement, then repeat the EKG because muscle tremor causes artifact; the trap is printing and sending the noisy strip as diagnostic.
Question 3606

A patient arrives for an outpatient EKG, and the CMA notices the skin is diaphoretic and covered with lotion; what should the CMA do before applying electrodes?

Clean and dry the skin (and lightly abrade if needed) to improve electrode contact because moisture/oils cause artifact; the trap is placing electrodes immediately and blaming the machine for “bad tracing.”
Question 3607

The CMA must collect a urine culture specimen and the patient hands in a cup filled to the top but says it was not midstream; what should the CMA do?

Request a new clean-catch midstream specimen with proper instructions because contamination can cause false-positive culture results; the trap is accepting it because “there’s plenty of urine.”
Question 3608

After placing limb leads, the CMA sees lead II is inverted and the printout flags “lead reversal suspected”; what is the best next step?

Verify correct limb lead placement and reconnect leads, then repeat the EKG because lead reversal can mimic abnormal rhythms/axis; the trap is documenting it as a true cardiac change.
Question 3609

A patient reports chest pressure and shortness of breath while the CMA is setting up the EKG; what should the CMA do first?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (alert provider/911 per protocol) and obtain vitals while preparing for immediate EKG because symptoms may indicate acute coronary syndrome; the trap is completing routine registration before escalating.
Question 3610

A patient on anticoagulants has persistent oozing after the draw despite brief pressure; what should the CMA do next?

Maintain firm pressure longer and apply a pressure dressing, and notify the provider per clinic protocol because bleeding risk is higher; the trap is sending the patient out with only a small bandage.
Question 3611

A CMP specimen in an SST is left on the counter for an hour after spinning, and the serum is still sitting on the cells; what is the best action?

Separate/aliquot the serum per protocol (or recollect if stability is exceeded) because prolonged contact with cells can change analytes like potassium and glucose; the trap is sending it unseparated because it was centrifuged.
Question 3612

During a draw, blood stops flowing after initially filling part of the tube; the patient’s arm is bent and the tourniquet has been on for over a minute; what should the CMA do next?

Ask the patient to straighten the arm and release/reapply the tourniquet as needed because prolonged tourniquet time and arm position can impede flow and alter results; the trap is repeatedly probing with the needle.
Question 3613

The CMA opens a new box of glucose test strips and is busy, so plans to run QC “later”; what is the best action?

Run quality control per manufacturer/clinic policy before patient testing because new lots can produce inaccurate results without verification; the trap is assuming new strips are automatically reliable.
Question 3614

The provider asks the CMA whether an EKG shows “ST elevation” and if the patient is having an MI; what is the appropriate response?

State that interpreting EKGs is outside the CMA scope and promptly relay the tracing and symptoms to the provider because diagnosis must be made by a licensed clinician; the trap is offering an interpretation to “be helpful.”
Question 3615

After filling a light blue citrate tube for PT/INR, the CMA notices it is only about 70% full; what should the CMA do next?

Recollect a properly filled light blue tube, because citrate tubes require the correct blood-to-additive ratio and “sending it anyway” can falsely prolong clotting results.
Question 3616

A CLIA-waived rapid strep test is negative, but the internal control line did not appear; what should the CMA do next?

Treat the test as invalid, repeat with a new kit/sample per instructions and QC requirements, and notify the provider if still invalid because controls verify accuracy; the trap is reporting “negative” based on the test line alone.
Question 3617

During venipuncture the CMA accidentally palpates the cleansed site again after applying alcohol; what is the best next step?

Clean the site again and allow it to dry before sticking, because re-touching contaminates the site and proceeding anyway is a common mistake that increases infection risk.
Question 3618

A provider orders PT/INR, CBC, and a BMP to be drawn at the same visit; what is the correct order of draw for these tubes?

Draw light blue first, then lavender, then green (or gold/SST if used for chemistry), because incorrect order can cause additive carryover (a common trap is drawing lavender before light blue, affecting coag results).
Question 3619

A patient’s fingerstick glucose on POCT reads 38 mg/dL, but the patient looks well and says they just washed hands after using hand lotion; what should the CMA do next?

Recheck using proper technique (clean, dry finger; consider a different site) and immediately notify the provider of critical results per protocol because contamination can skew readings but hypoglycemia is time-sensitive; the trap is dismissing the value as “machine error” without confirmation or escalation.
Question 3620

A fasting CMP is ordered and the CMA prepares to draw blood; which action must the CMA do first before applying the tourniquet if the patient has the same first and last name as another patient on the schedule?

Verify identity using two identifiers (e.g., full name and DOB) against the order and wristband if present, because misidentification is a major lab error and “confirming by room number or face” is a common trap.
Question 3621

During a fasting lipid panel and CMP draw, you fill a light blue tube first because it was closest, then remember the order of draw; what should you do NEXT before sending the specimens to the lab?

Discard the incorrectly collected light blue tube and redraw using the correct order of draw (blood culture if ordered, then light blue before serum/SST) because drawing after other tubes can contaminate the citrate tube and alter coagulation results; a common mistake is keeping it and “noting it,” which doesn’t fix the pre-analytical error.
Question 3622

After venipuncture, the CMA notices continued oozing at the site and the patient reports taking a blood thinner; what should the CMA do next?

Maintain firm pressure longer and apply a pressure dressing as needed, because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk and “a quick bandage” can lead to re-bleeding/hematoma.
Question 3623

A patient is on a “no needle” preference but agrees to testing; the first venipuncture attempt misses and a hematoma starts forming; what should the CMA do first?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure immediately, because continuing the draw worsens the hematoma (trap: trying to “fish” for the vein).
Question 3624

A patient is collecting a clean-catch urine sample but asks if they can fill the cup to the top “to make sure there’s enough”; what should the CMA instruct?

Collect midstream urine to the indicated fill line and avoid touching the inside of the cup, because overfilling/contamination can affect culture results (trap: “more is always better”).
Question 3625

A provider orders a basic metabolic panel and a lactate; the CMA draws both but then places the lactate tube in the regular pickup bin; what is the best corrective action?

Follow special handling—typically keep lactate on ice and send promptly per lab protocol—because improper transport alters results and “all tubes can wait for routine pickup” is a common error.
Question 3626

The CMA collects a CBC in a lavender tube and sets it on the counter to label later; what is the best next step?

Label the tube immediately at the patient’s side with required identifiers, because delayed labeling risks mislabeling (trap: labeling at the nurses’ station).
Question 3627

While drawing blood, the patient says they feel dizzy and looks pale; what is the CMA’s best immediate action?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, help the patient to a safe position (recline or supine) and monitor, because preventing a fall is priority and “finishing the tube first” is unsafe.
Question 3628

A urine culture is ordered but the patient hands the CMA an unlabeled specimen cup from the restroom; what should the CMA do first?

Confirm the specimen belongs to the patient using two identifiers and label it immediately in their presence, because accepting an unlabeled specimen without verification is a major identification trap.
Question 3629

The EKG printout shows excessive interference and the CMA notices a lead wire is loose at the electrode; what is the best next action?

Reattach/replace the electrode and secure the lead, then repeat the EKG, because poor contact creates artifact and “sending a noisy strip to the provider” delays care.
Question 3630

After completing an EKG, the CMA sees ST-segment elevation on the machine’s interpretive message; what should the CMA do next?

Immediately notify the provider/activate clinic urgent response per protocol and document the time, because CMAs don’t interpret/diagnose but must escalate potential acute findings (trap: filing the result for later review).
Question 3631

A patient scheduled for routine labs reports a history of mastectomy with lymph node removal on the right side; what is the best site selection?

Avoid venipuncture in the affected-side arm and use the other arm if possible, because lymph removal increases lymphedema/infection risk and “it’s fine if the vein looks good” is a common trap.
Question 3632

Immediately after drawing an SST (gold) tube, the CMA shakes it vigorously to mix; what is the best next step?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times (do not shake) and recollect if hemolysis is suspected, because shaking can hemolyze and distort chemistry results (trap: treating tubes like “mix by shaking”).
Question 3633

During daily QC for a point-of-care glucometer, the reading is out of the acceptable control range; what is the best next action before testing patients?

Do not use the meter for patient testing; repeat QC and troubleshoot (strip lot, expiration, cleaning, calibration) and notify the supervisor per protocol, because using a failed-QC device can produce unsafe patient results (trap: “it’s only slightly off”).
Question 3634

A patient with chest discomfort is placed for an EKG and the CMA notes the limb electrodes were placed on the wrists/ankles over heavy lotion; what is the best correction before recording?

Clean/dry the skin and place electrodes on appropriate sites (often distal limbs or torso per protocol) with good contact, because lotion reduces adhesion/conduction and “pressing harder” won’t fix poor signal.
Question 3635

While performing a 12-lead EKG, the tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is shivering in a cold room; what should the CMA do next?

Warm and relax the patient (blanket, reduce shivering) and ensure they are still before repeating, because muscle tremor causes artifact (trap: immediately reprinting without fixing the cause).
Question 3636

A CLIA-waived rapid strep test is running and the control line fails to appear; what should the CMA do next?

Treat the test as invalid, repeat with a new device/specimen as appropriate, and follow QC/troubleshooting steps, because reporting a result without a valid control is a common safety error.
Question 3637

You verify a patient’s identity and notice the lab requisition has no date of birth and the patient’s name is common; what should you do next before drawing blood?

Stop and resolve the order by confirming two identifiers with the patient and the ordering source/EHR before collecting, because drawing on an incomplete/ambiguous order risks wrong-patient results (trap: “I’ll just label carefully after the draw”).
Question 3638

You must collect blood cultures, PT/INR (light blue), CMP (serum), and CBC (lavender); what order of draw should you use?

Blood cultures first, then light blue, then serum tube, then lavender, because order of draw prevents additive carryover that can alter coagulation and hematology results (trap: drawing lavender before coag).
Question 3639

You are collecting a basic metabolic panel and the patient has a tight tourniquet on for over a minute while you search for a vein; what is the best action?

Release the tourniquet and reapply only when ready to puncture, because prolonged tourniquet time can hemoconcentrate and skew results (trap: leaving it on “to keep the vein up”).
Question 3640

A patient becomes pale and says “I’m going to faint” during the draw while seated on the exam table; what should the CMA do first?

Remove the tourniquet and needle and assist the patient to a safe position (recline/legs elevated) and call for help per clinic protocol, because preventing a fall is the priority (trap: trying to “finish the tube quickly”).
Question 3641

After filling a lavender-top tube for a CBC, you notice the blood looks “stringy” and may clot; what should you do immediately?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times right away, because EDTA must mix promptly to prevent clots that can invalidate the CBC (trap: shaking vigorously, which causes hemolysis).
Question 3642

During a routine venipuncture, blood flow stops after you’ve partially filled the first tube and the patient is calm; what is the best next step?

Gently adjust the needle angle or reposition the bevel and ask the patient to relax the fist without pumping, because minor needle position issues are common and pumping can hemolyze the sample (trap: immediately switching sites without trying a safe adjustment).
Question 3643

After a venipuncture, the site begins swelling and bruising while the tube is still filling; what should you do next?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, and apply firm pressure with elevation, because this indicates a developing hematoma and continuing worsens bleeding (trap: just tightening the tourniquet or continuing to fill tubes).
Question 3644

You drew a serum tube and forgot to label it at the bedside; you’re now at the lab counter with two unlabelled tubes and one patient in the room; what should you do?

Treat the specimen as unlabeled and follow clinic policy (typically discard and recollect with bedside labeling), because you cannot ensure correct patient identification after leaving the bedside (trap: guessing based on memory or room number).
Question 3645

A provider orders a fasting lipid panel but the patient mentions they ate breakfast an hour ago; what should the CMA do next?

Do not collect the specimen yet; notify the provider and reschedule or obtain instructions, because collecting a non-fasting sample may produce misleading results (trap: drawing anyway and “noting it later”).
Question 3646

An outpatient EKG prints with “leads off” and you notice the limb electrodes were placed on the patient’s lower legs over socks; what is the best next step?

Re-prep skin and place electrodes on clean, dry skin (no clothing) in correct limb positions, because poor contact causes lead-off alarms and inaccurate tracings (trap: hitting “print” again without fixing contact).
Question 3647

When removing the needle safety cap before venipuncture, the needle touches the countertop; what should you do?

Discard the needle and replace with a new sterile needle, because contamination increases infection risk and cannot be corrected by wiping (trap: cleaning it with an alcohol pad and proceeding).
Question 3648

You’re about to collect a specimen for a therapeutic drug level and the patient says they took the medication “just now in the car”; what should you do next?

Pause and clarify timing requirements on the order (peak vs trough) and notify the provider if timing is incorrect before drawing, because timing errors can lead to wrong clinical decisions (trap: drawing immediately without verifying timing).
Question 3649

For a capillary fingerstick glucose on an adult, the patient’s hands are cold and you get a poor blood drop; what is the best next step?

Warm the hand and allow it to hang down briefly before reattempting, because warming improves capillary flow and reduces the need for excessive squeezing (trap: “milking” the finger, which can dilute the sample with tissue fluid).
Question 3650

While collecting a urine specimen for culture, the patient hands you a cup that is filled to the brim with no lid and says it’s “clean catch”; what should you do?

Reject and recollect with correct instructions and a secure lid, because contamination and leakage compromise culture accuracy and safety (trap: accepting it to avoid “inconveniencing” the patient).
Question 3651

A patient’s PT/INR sample in a light-blue tube is only half full after the draw; what is the best action?

Recollect the specimen to ensure the tube is filled to the required line, because incorrect blood-to-anticoagulant ratio can falsely prolong or shorten results (trap: sending it “since there’s some blood”).
Question 3652

During a 12-lead EKG, the tracing shows a jagged baseline and the patient is shivering in a cold room; what should you do first to troubleshoot?

Warm the patient and reduce muscle movement (blanket, relax arms, ensure comfortable position) before repeating, because muscle tremor artifact mimics rhythm problems (trap: moving leads randomly without addressing the cause).
Question 3653

During a fasting lipid panel draw, you collect a light blue tube first because it “fills fastest,” then notice the requisition also includes a CMP in a serum tube; what is the best immediate action?

Stop and redraw using the correct order of draw (blood cultures if ordered, then light blue, then serum, then heparin, then EDTA, then glycolytic) because drawing blue before serum can contaminate additives and alter results, and the trap is thinking order only matters when multiple sites are used.
Question 3654

A provider asks you to perform a rapid strep test, but the kit’s external control documentation shows QC was not run for the new lot number; what should you do first?

Run and document the required QC before testing patient samples, because CLIA-waived tests still require QC to ensure valid results (trap: testing the patient first to “save time”).
Question 3655

While filling a light blue (citrate) tube for PT/INR, the tube stops short and is only half full; what should the CMA do next?

Discard and recollect a new light blue tube to the fill line because coag tests require a strict 9:1 blood-to-additive ratio, and the trap is “topping off” or sending an underfilled tube.
Question 3656

Your glucometer displays an error code during a busy clinic session and you’re unsure what it means; what is the best next step?

Stop patient testing and follow the device troubleshooting steps (manual/posted guide) and notify the supervisor if unresolved, because using a malfunctioning device risks incorrect results and unsafe decisions (trap: borrowing a reading from another patient’s device or guessing).
Question 3657

You obtain an EKG on a patient with chest pressure and the machine interpretation flags “possible acute MI”; what should the CMA do next?

Immediately notify the provider/activate the clinic’s urgent response while keeping the patient monitored and at rest, because CMAs don’t interpret/diagnose but must escalate critical alerts promptly (trap: trying to “confirm” by re-reading the strip yourself).
Question 3658

Draw the lavender (EDTA) before the gray (fluoride/oxalate) because EDTA tubes come earlier than glycolytic inhibitor tubes in the order of draw, and the trap is choosing gray first because it’s “more urgent for glucose.”

Question 3666

Allow the specimen to clot (per lab timeframe) and then centrifuge if required before sending because premature spinning or transport can affect serum quality, and the trap is immediately centrifuging or leaving it unprocessed too long.

Stop and redraw using the correct order of draw because additive carryover from EDTA can falsely alter chemistry results, and the trap is “it’s fine if the tubes are labeled correctly.”
Question 3695

During a routine venipuncture for CBC and CMP, you accidentally collect the lavender-top EDTA tube before the light green-top heparin tube, and you notice the lavender tube was filled and gently inverted; what should you do next to best protect specimen accuracy?

Stop and recollect using a new venipuncture with the correct order of draw (light green before lavender), because drawing EDTA first can contaminate subsequent tubes and alter chemistry results; the common trap is sending the samples anyway since the tubes look properly filled and mixed.
Question 3696

You are performing a CLIA-waived rapid strep test and the control line does not appear; what is the best next step?

Do not report the result, repeat the test with a new device and verify storage/expiration, because a failed control indicates an invalid test (trap: reporting based on a faint test area without a control).
Question 3697

A patient asks you to tell them what their abnormal lab value “means” while you’re processing the specimen; what is the best response?

Tell them you can’t interpret results but you will ensure the provider receives them and can discuss meaning and next steps, because CMAs must stay within scope and avoid diagnosing (trap: explaining “it means you have…” can cause harm and liability).
Question 3698

You draw a light blue tube for PT/INR but it fills only halfway because blood flow stops; what should you do NEXT?

Discard it and recollect a fully filled tube because the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio must be correct, and the trap is sending it anyway “since some blood is present.”
Question 3699

A patient is dehydrated and you consider drawing from an IV arm that is currently infusing normal saline; what should you do BEST?

Choose a different site/arm because IV fluids dilute and alter results, and the trap is drawing “below the IV” without proper clearance and documentation.
Question 3700

A provider orders PT/INR and CMP, and you have only one venipuncture attempt to minimize patient anxiety; what is the BEST tube sequence to collect?

Collect the light blue tube first, then the serum tube (red/gold), then the light green tube because coagulation testing is most sensitive to contamination and the trap is putting serum before citrate “to fill others first.”
