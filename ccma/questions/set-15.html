Question 1401

You are applying a dressing to a minor laceration; what is the correct sequence to reduce infection risk?

Perform hand hygiene, don clean gloves, cleanse the wound as directed, then apply the sterile dressing because cleaning before covering reduces contamination and the trap is placing a dressing over an uncleaned wound.
Question 1402

A patient is due for an injection, but the chart shows a documented latex allergy and the room has latex gloves on the counter; what should you do first?

Remove latex supplies and use latex-free gloves/equipment, confirming vaccine components as needed, because exposure can trigger a reaction and the trap is thinking “gloves won’t touch the patient” so it doesn’t matter.
Question 1403

You draw up an injectable medication and then realize the patient’s identity hasn’t been verified yet; what should you do next?

Pause and verify the patient using two identifiers before administering (and relabel/reprepare if there’s any doubt about mix-ups) because right patient is a core medication right and the trap is assuming the room assignment guarantees identity.
Question 1404

A patient needs a pelvic exam; how should you position them and what should you do to support safety and dignity?

Place them in lithotomy with proper draping and explain each step before assisting, because positioning and privacy reduce anxiety and improve cooperation and the trap is rushing positioning without draping or consent to proceed.
Question 1405

When giving an IM injection, you’re about to recap the needle “just to walk it to the sharps”; what should you do instead?

Do not recap; activate the safety device and discard immediately into a sharps container because recapping causes needlesticks and the trap is thinking a quick recap is safer for transport.
Question 1406

A patient reports dizziness immediately after a vaccination while standing at the checkout desk; what should the CMA do first?

Assist them to sit or lie down and monitor while calling for clinical help because syncope/fall prevention is immediate priority and the trap is offering water and letting them remain standing.
Question 1407

You accidentally stick yourself with a used lancet after a glucose test; what is the first action?

Wash the area immediately with soap and water and report the exposure per clinic protocol because prompt cleansing and reporting are time-sensitive and the trap is waiting until the end of the shift to “see if it’s a big deal.”
Question 1408

You are preparing an IM vaccine and notice the syringe label doesn’t match the order even though the vial looks similar; what is the best next step?

Stop and verify the medication against the order and the vial’s name/strength/expiration before proceeding because look-alike products cause errors and the trap is relying on appearance or a partially read label.
Question 1409

In the lobby, a visitor tells you a patient “just collapsed and isn’t breathing”; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per clinic policy) and begin CPR/AED as soon as available because early activation and compressions save lives and the trap is searching for the provider first instead of starting lifesaving steps.
Question 1410

You enter the room wearing gloves only to remove a saturated wound dressing, and the patient begins coughing repeatedly—what PPE change is best?

Add a surgical mask with eye protection (and keep gloves) because splash/droplet exposure is possible during wound care with coughing; the trap is thinking gloves alone meet Standard Precautions when mucous membrane exposure risk increases.
Question 1411

You are about to administer a medication and notice the MAR lists 0.5 mL but the syringe is drawn to 0.8 mL—what should you do next?

Stop and resolve the discrepancy by rechecking the order and recalculating/remeasuring before giving anything because the “right dose” is a core safety check; the trap is assuming the prefilled syringe amount is correct and administering anyway.
Question 1412

The provider orders an IM injection, and the patient says they have a severe latex allergy—what should the CMA do before preparing supplies?

Verify all supplies (gloves, vial stoppers, syringe/needle packaging, bandage) are latex-free and alert the provider if alternatives are needed because preventing exposure is the priority; the trap is assuming nonsterile gloves are the only latex source.
Question 1413

During rooming, an adult patient’s BP is 186/112 and they report a new severe headache; they are here for a routine refill visit—what should the CMA do first?

Recheck BP manually after a brief rest and immediately alert the provider with symptoms and readings because this may be hypertensive crisis; the trap is documenting it and continuing intake as “white coat” without prompt escalation.
Question 1414

While obtaining vitals, a patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while standing on the scale—what is the best next action?

Assist them to a seated or supine position (legs elevated if tolerated) and stay with them, then obtain vitals and notify the provider because fall prevention comes before finishing measurements; the trap is insisting they “hold on” to complete height/weight.
Question 1415

After drawing up an IM vaccine, the patient suddenly asks to use the restroom and you step out holding the uncapped syringe—what is the safest action?

Discard the prepared syringe and prepare a new dose before administration because leaving with/transporting an uncapped or unattended prepared medication risks contamination and mix-ups; the trap is “saving the dose” to avoid waste.
Question 1416

You obtain an oral temperature of 101.8°F on a patient who just drank hot coffee in the waiting room—what should you do next?

Wait about 15 minutes and retake using an appropriate route/device (or use tympanic/temporal per clinic practice) because recent hot liquids can falsely elevate oral temps; the trap is charting the first reading as true fever without confirming.
Question 1417

During venipuncture for a CBC and BMP, the CMA fills the lavender tube first because it was closest on the tray; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Stop and redraw using the correct order of draw because drawing EDTA (lavender) before a serum/chemistry tube can contaminate the sample and alter results; the trap is “it’s already collected” but accuracy requires recollection.
Question 1418

A fasting lipid panel specimen sits on the counter for 45 minutes after collection because the clinic got busy; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Follow the test’s handling requirements and contact the lab/supervisor to determine if recollection is needed because delayed processing can compromise stability; the trap is assuming all blood is stable at room temperature.
Question 1419

In the lobby, a visitor says, “Is my sister done yet? She’s in room 3 for an STI test,” and they ask you for her results—what should the CMA do?

Decline to confirm information and direct the visitor to the patient or authorized contact process because HIPAA prohibits sharing even presence/test details without permission; the trap is giving “just a status update” because it seems harmless.
Question 1420

A provider orders PT/INR, CMP, and CBC; the CMA prepares light blue, gold SST, and lavender tubes—what is the correct order of draw?

Draw light blue first, then gold/SST, then lavender because coagulation tubes must be collected before additive tubes that can affect clotting tests; the trap is placing SST before citrate.
Question 1421

After drawing a lavender tube, the CMA shakes it vigorously to “mix it fast”; what is the best corrective action?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times to mix additive without hemolyzing the specimen; the trap is shaking, which can cause hemolysis and inaccurate labs.
Question 1422

A patient arrives for a minor procedure and asks to watch while you set up the sterile field, reaching toward the drape to “help”—what is the best response?

Politely ask them to keep hands away and reposition them for safety (and re-establish sterility if contamination occurs) because patient movement can contaminate the field; the trap is allowing “help” to save time and then proceeding after contamination.
Question 1423

A patient’s name on the label is correct but the date of birth is missing; the specimen is already in the transport bag—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Do not send the specimen; relabel correctly at the patient’s side using two identifiers because mislabeling is a patient-safety event; the trap is fixing labels away from the patient, which breaks identification integrity.
Question 1424

While collecting a light blue tube for PT/INR, the tube stops filling early and is only half full; what should the CMA do?

Discard and recollect a properly filled tube because citrate tubes require the correct blood-to-additive ratio for accurate coagulation results; the trap is “send it anyway” which can falsely prolong or shorten results.
Question 1425

A rapid strep test is CLIA-waived, but the CMA realizes the control was not run for a new kit lot; the patient is waiting—what is the best next step?

Run the required controls before reporting patient results because lot verification is part of quality assurance even for waived tests; the trap is thinking CLIA-waived means “no QC needed.”
Question 1426

A patient’s antecubital veins are hard to palpate and the CMA is tempted to “fish” with the needle; what should the CMA do BEST?

Stop and reassess (reposition arm, warm site, try a different vein or obtain assistance) because probing increases pain, hematoma risk, and nerve injury; the trap is repeated redirection to salvage a missed attempt.
Question 1427

The provider orders a blood culture, and the CMA has collected the tubes but has not cleaned the site with antiseptic yet; what should the CMA do?

Discard and restart with proper skin antisepsis before collection because cultures are highly contamination-sensitive; the trap is thinking you can “clean after” or rely on alcohol alone without proper technique.
Question 1428

A urine dipstick POCT was performed, but the bottle of reagent strips has been left uncapped on the counter for an hour; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Repeat the test with properly stored strips (and document the issue) because moisture/light exposure can invalidate reagent chemistry; the trap is accepting the first result because it “looked normal.”
Question 1429

The clinic’s CLIA-waived glucose meter displays “QC failed” before patient testing; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Do not test patients and perform troubleshooting/QC per manufacturer instructions (repeat control, check strips, calibration, expiration) because failed QC means patient results are unreliable; the trap is proceeding to “save time.”
Question 1430

After venipuncture, a hematoma begins forming at the site while the tube is still filling; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Release the tourniquet, remove the needle, and apply firm pressure because continued blood flow into tissue worsens hematoma; the trap is keeping the needle in to complete the collection.
Question 1431

A patient on anticoagulant therapy has persistent oozing after the needle is removed; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Maintain firm pressure longer and reassess before bandaging, escalating per clinic protocol if bleeding won’t stop because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk; the trap is applying a bandage too soon and letting the patient leave.
Question 1432

During a draw, the patient says, “I feel dizzy,” becomes pale, and starts to slump in the chair; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, apply pressure, and assist the patient into a safe position (reclined with legs elevated if possible) to prevent syncope injury; the trap is trying to “finish the tube” despite symptoms.
Question 1433

A patient’s EKG has a wandering baseline and the CMA notices the patient is talking and the bed is not locked; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Instruct the patient to remain still and quiet and stabilize the environment (lock bed, ensure comfortable position) because movement causes artifact that can mimic dysrhythmias; the trap is assuming it’s a true rhythm change.
Question 1434

After placing chest leads for an EKG, the CMA realizes V1 and V2 were placed too high near the clavicles; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Reposition V1/V2 to the 4th intercostal space at the sternal borders and repeat the EKG because incorrect placement can mimic ischemia or axis changes; the trap is keeping the tracing because “it’s readable.”
Question 1435

The EKG printout shows consistent 60-cycle interference, and the CMA sees a nearby power cord draped over the lead wires; what should the CMA do BEST?

Separate/untangle lead wires from electrical cords and ensure good skin contact because electrical interference commonly causes this artifact; the trap is turning up filter settings instead of fixing the source.
Question 1436

While filling a light blue sodium citrate tube for PT/INR, blood flow stops and the tube is only half full; what should the CMA do next?

Discard that tube and recollect a fully filled light blue tube because underfilling changes the blood-to-additive ratio and invalidates results (trap: “topping off” from another tube or accepting it “since it clotted fine”).
Question 1437

A fecal occult blood POCT card is developed, but the sample window is smeared with too much stool and the result is hard to read; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Repeat the test with a proper thin smear and follow kit timing exactly because excess specimen can invalidate readability and accuracy; the trap is guessing the result or over-interpreting a compromised test.
Question 1438

While setting up a 12-lead EKG, the CMA places limb electrodes on the patient’s wrists and ankles, but the tracing shows heavy motion artifact from tremor; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Reposition limb electrodes to the torso (per facility practice) and have the patient relax/keep still to reduce artifact while maintaining correct lead orientation; the trap is repeating the EKG without addressing artifact source.
Question 1439

A provider asks the CMA to interpret the EKG as “normal” so the patient can leave; the tracing prints with an automated message “possible acute MI”; what should the CMA do?

Immediately notify the provider with the tracing and patient status because CMAs do not interpret/clear EKGs and possible critical findings require prompt escalation; the trap is reassuring the patient or documenting an interpretation.
Question 1440

A provider orders CBC, PT/INR, BMP, and a lactic acid, and you have one venipuncture attempt to get all tubes; what order of draw should you use?

Draw blood cultures first (if ordered), then light blue, then serum (red/gold), then green, then lavender, then gray because correct order prevents additive carryover (common trap: drawing lavender before light blue can falsely prolong coag results).
Question 1441

A patient’s wristband is missing, and they verbally confirm their name and date of birth; what is the best way to label the tubes?

Label at the patient’s side immediately after the draw using two identifiers matched to the order because bedside labeling reduces mislabeling errors (trap: labeling later at the workstation from memory).
Question 1442

A patient is fasting for labs and asks if they can sip coffee while you prepare the draw; what should the CMA do?

Ask what the provider ordered and instruct the patient to avoid anything but water until the draw is complete because coffee can break fasting requirements and alter results (trap: allowing “just a little” without verifying test prep).
Question 1443

The only specimen you receive for a potassium level is visibly hemolyzed (pink/red serum) after a difficult draw; what should the CMA do next?

Notify the lab/provider per clinic process and recollect if requested because hemolysis can falsely elevate potassium and compromise accuracy (trap: sending it anyway to “see what it says”).
Question 1444

A patient becomes pale and says, “I’m going to pass out,” while seated upright in the phlebotomy chair; what should you do first?

Stop the draw and assist the patient to a safe reclined position (legs elevated if possible) to prevent injury from syncope (trap: offering water or continuing the draw while they remain upright).
Question 1445

You’re preparing for venipuncture and the patient says they are on “blood thinners”; after removing the needle, how should you adjust aftercare?

Hold firm pressure longer than usual and ensure bleeding has stopped before bandaging because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk (trap: using the usual brief pressure time and letting them leave with continued oozing).
Question 1446

You drew a lavender tube for CBC and set it on the counter without mixing while you label other tubes; what should you do?

Gently invert the lavender tube the recommended number of times as soon as possible because EDTA tubes must be mixed to prevent microclots (trap: shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze, or assuming mixing isn’t needed).
Question 1447

You have difficulty advancing the needle and see no blood return; what should you do to avoid damaging the vein?

Withdraw the needle slightly and reassess angle/position, then stop and attempt a new site if unsuccessful because “digging” increases hematoma/nerve injury risk (trap: probing repeatedly under the skin).
Question 1448

Release the tourniquet, remove the needle, and apply firm pressure because this suggests a hematoma/infiltration and continuing worsens tissue injury (trap: finishing the draw to “get all tubes” despite swelling).
Question 1449

You place limb electrodes on the patient’s torso to reduce artifact, and the provider asks why the EKG looks different than prior studies; what is the best response/action?

Question 1450

Question 1451

Question 1452

Treat it as an invalid test and repeat with a new device/specimen as appropriate because a missing control means the result can’t be trusted (trap: reporting “negative” because the test line is absent).
Question 1453

You run daily quality control on a POCT analyzer and the control is out of range; what is the best next action?

Stop patient testing, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (new control/strip, verify lot/expiration, clean device), and document/notify as required because reporting patient results without passing QC is unsafe (trap: rerunning patient samples and assuming it will “average out”).
Question 1454

Question 1455

Question 1456

Question 1499

After drawing a light-blue tube for PT/INR, the CMA notices it is underfilled—what should the CMA do next?

Redraw the specimen to the proper fill line, because citrate tubes require a correct blood-to-additive ratio for accurate coagulation results; a common trap is “topping off” from another tube or accepting a short draw.
Question 1500

During venipuncture, blood stops flowing and the tube is only partially filled while the needle is still in the vein—what should the CMA do next?

Gently reposition the needle (slightly withdraw/adjust angle) and ensure the tube is fully seated before restarting, because the bevel may be against the vein wall; a common trap is “digging” aggressively or removing the needle without first checking tube engagement.
