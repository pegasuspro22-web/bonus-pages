Question 401

While preparing an injection, you realize you forgot to verify two patient identifiers and the patient is chatting casually; what should you do next?

Pause and verify two identifiers (e.g., name and DOB) against the order before administering because identification errors are high-risk and preventable; the trap is assuming you “know the patient.”
Question 402

You find an unresponsive adult in the clinic hallway, no normal breathing, and another staff member is present; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/bring AED) and begin CPR immediately because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is searching for a pulse for an extended time or going to notify the provider first.
Question 403

During a dressing change teaching, the patient says they will reuse the same gauze for a day “to save money”; what is the best CMA response?

Instruct to use clean, dry dressing supplies each change and discuss low-cost options/refill planning with the team because reused gauze increases infection risk; the trap is accepting the plan without correcting unsafe technique.
Question 404

During intake, a 54-year-old patient says they feel “lightheaded,” and you obtain BP 84/52 with HR 118 while they are seated; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and immediately notify the provider/activate clinic emergency response as needed because this is potential shock; the trap is rechecking multiple times or walking the patient to another room before stabilizing them.
Question 405

You draw up 1.0 mL for an IM vaccine and notice the label on the vial does not match the order, but the patient is already prepped; what is the best action?

Stop and resolve the discrepancy by rechecking the order and medication label/lot before administering because right medication is critical even if it delays; the trap is giving it “since it’s probably the same vaccine.”
Question 406

A patient returns from the restroom pale and diaphoretic, says they feel faint, and begins to slump in the chair; what should the CMA do first?

Assist them to a safe position (supine or seated with head down) and call for help because preventing a fall and improving perfusion is immediate priority; the trap is walking them to an exam room unassisted.
Question 407

In the lobby, a patient loudly asks if their neighbor “tested positive for something,” and others can hear; what should the CMA do?

Redirect the conversation to a private area and state you cannot discuss any patient information because HIPAA requires privacy; the trap is answering indirectly or confirming through body language.
Question 408

A patient asks you to “just give the shot in the same arm as last time,” but the chart notes a severe local reaction in that arm after the previous injection; what should you do?

Select an appropriate alternate site per injection guidelines and inform the provider as needed because prior severe reactions warrant site caution; the trap is prioritizing patient preference over documented safety concerns.
Question 409

A patient’s initial BP is 168/96, but they were talking and their feet were dangling during the measurement; what should the CMA do next?

Reposition with back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level, allow a brief rest, and repeat BP because technique errors can falsely elevate readings; the trap is reporting an inaccurately high BP without correcting conditions.
Question 410

After giving an IM injection, you realize the needle briefly touched the countertop before contacting the patient’s skin; what is the best action?

Stop and discard the needle/syringe in sharps and prepare a new dose/supplies because sterility has been compromised; the trap is thinking “it looks clean” and proceeding, increasing infection risk.
Question 411

You are taking an oral temperature on a patient who just drank hot coffee in the waiting area; what is the best next step?

Wait about 15 minutes and then retake the temperature (or use an alternate approved route) because hot beverages falsely elevate oral readings; the trap is charting the first value as fever without controlling the variable.
Question 412

A 6-year-old comes for a vaccine and is crying and twisting; what is the safest positioning the CMA should use before the injection?

Use a secure seated position with the parent/guardian holding the child’s arms and torso (and stabilizing the leg if needed) because minimizing sudden movement prevents needle injury; the trap is attempting the injection while the child is unrestrained “to be quick.”
Question 413

A patient with a draining wound arrives without a mask and is coughing; what should the CMA do first at check-in before rooming?

Provide a mask, perform hand hygiene, and separate/room the patient promptly following standard precautions because source control reduces transmission; the trap is continuing normal intake in a crowded waiting area.
Question 414

You remove gloves after cleaning a small blood spill, and then your phone rings; what should you do before answering?

Perform hand hygiene before touching the phone because removing gloves does not eliminate contamination risk; the trap is assuming gloves alone prevent transfer and skipping hand hygiene.
Question 415

During rooming, a 58-year-old becomes pale and says he feels faint while you are checking his blood pressure standing for orthostatic vitals; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the orthostatic measurement and assist the patient to a safe position (sit or lie supine with legs elevated) and call for the provider/clinic emergency help, because preventing a fall and addressing possible syncope takes priority over completing vitals (a common trap is trying to “finish the set” before stabilizing the patient).
Question 416

While measuring respirations, the patient becomes aware and starts breathing faster; what is the best technique to obtain an accurate rate?

Count respirations unobtrusively (often immediately after pulse while appearing to continue counting) because awareness alters breathing patterns; the trap is telling the patient “I’m counting your breaths,” which changes the rate.
Question 417

The provider orders an IM medication, and the vial label reads 100 mg/mL; the order is 50 mg; what should the CMA draw up?

Draw up 0.5 mL because dose (50 mg) ÷ concentration (100 mg/mL) = 0.5 mL; the trap is drawing 50 mL or 1 mL by confusing mg with mL.
Question 418

You are about to administer a medication injection and the patient says, “I’m allergic to that—my throat swelled last time,” but the chart allergy list is blank; what should you do next?

Hold the medication and immediately notify the provider to clarify/confirm the allergy before administration because anaphylaxis risk outweighs timing; the trap is proceeding because “it’s not documented.”
Question 419

During a wound check, the patient’s dressing is stuck to the wound bed and begins to pull at tissue when you remove it; what is the best next step?

Moisten the dressing with sterile saline or clean water (per protocol) to loosen it before removal because tearing tissue increases bleeding and infection risk; the trap is forcefully pulling to “get it off quickly.”
Question 420

While rooming a patient, you see a small puddle on the floor near the scale that could be body fluid; what should the CMA do first?

Block access to prevent slips/exposure and clean the spill using appropriate disinfectant and PPE per standard precautions because safety and containment come before continuing intake; the trap is stepping around it and rooming more patients.
Question 421

The provider asks you to give a vaccine from a multi-dose vial, but you notice the beyond-use date after first puncture was yesterday; what is the best action?

Do not use the vial and obtain a valid vaccine supply (and report wastage per procedure) because expired multi-dose vials can be unsafe/ineffective; the trap is using it “since it’s only one day” to avoid waste.
Question 422

You are changing a simple dressing on a superficial abrasion; what level of technique is appropriate in an outpatient clinic?

Use medical asepsis (clean technique) with hand hygiene and clean gloves because most simple dressings don’t require sterile field; the trap is using sterile technique unnecessarily or, worse, skipping clean technique because it’s “minor.”
Question 423

A patient receives an IM injection and then says they feel dizzy and look pale while standing at the checkout window; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a safe seated or supine position and assess responsiveness, then notify the provider because syncope risk includes falls; the trap is offering water and having them “walk it off.”
Question 424

A provider requests you give an injection, but the order in the chart is missing the medication dose; what should the CMA do next?

Clarify the incomplete order with the provider before preparing/administering because the CMA cannot guess a dose; the trap is using a “usual dose” from memory.
Question 425

While taking an adult’s BP you notice the cuff overlaps significantly and the reading is 168/102, but the patient’s arm is large; what should you do next?

Repeat the BP with the correct cuff size before escalating because too-small cuffs falsely elevate readings; the trap is reporting a hypertensive crisis based on a measurement error.
Question 426

During intake a 58-year-old reports chest pressure and you obtain vitals showing BP 88/56, HR 122, RR 24, skin cool/clammy; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (alert provider/call 911 per clinic protocol) and keep the patient seated/supine; these are shock-like findings and the trap is continuing routine intake or having the patient walk to an exam room.
Question 427

During rooming, a 52-year-old patient says he feels “a little dizzy” when you stand him for weight; you obtain vitals and the BP is 86/54 with a pulse of 118, and he insists he can still walk to the exam room—what should the CMA do first?

Sit or lay the patient down (supine with legs elevated if tolerated), stay with him, and promptly notify the provider/activate emergency response if symptoms worsen because suspected hypotension requires fall-prevention and rapid escalation; a common mistake is letting him ambulate or finishing the intake before addressing safety.
Question 428

Stop and notify the provider because the patient has the right to refuse and you must not administer without consent; the trap is trying to “convince quickly” and giving it anyway because it’s ordered.
Question 429

While taking vitals, a patient suddenly becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per clinic process) and begin CPR with chest compressions because early CPR and rapid activation save lives; the trap is searching for a pulse for too long or leaving the patient to find the provider.
Question 430

A patient stands up after vitals and becomes dizzy and pale; what is the CMA’s best immediate action?

Assist the patient to a safe seated or supine position and reassess vital signs, because preventing a fall is priority; the trap is walking the patient to checkout or leaving them standing while you “get the provider.”
Question 431

You obtain a pulse oximeter reading of 82% on a patient who is talking in full sentences and has cold fingers with dark nail polish; what should you do next?

Troubleshoot and repeat (warm the hand, remove polish, reposition probe) before notifying urgently, because poor perfusion/artifact causes false lows; the trap is assuming the number is accurate without assessing signal quality.
Question 432

An AED arrives while you are performing CPR on an adult; what should the CMA do next?

Continue compressions while another staff member applies the pads and follow AED prompts, pausing only when instructed to analyze/shock because minimizing interruptions improves outcomes; the trap is stopping CPR for extended periods while setting up the AED.
Question 433

You are asked to give an IM vaccine, but the patient’s name and DOB on the vial’s label do not match the chart even though the vaccine type is correct; what should you do first?

Hold the administration and resolve the discrepancy by re-verifying the patient and the labeled product, because wrong-patient errors are preventable; the trap is giving it because “it’s the right vaccine.”
Question 434

Question 435

Assist the patient to a safe position (sit or supine) and assess airway/breathing/circulation while staying with them, because preventing a fall and stabilizing the patient is the immediate priority; a common mistake is trying to “just finish vitals” or walking away to get help before the patient is secured.
Question 436

Question 437

Question 438

Question 439

During intake, an adult’s radial pulse is irregular and you count 72 for 15 seconds; what is the best next step?

Question 440

Perform hand hygiene immediately after glove removal before touching shared equipment, because gloves are not a substitute for hand hygiene; the trap is assuming glove use alone prevents transmission.
Question 441

Question 442

Question 443

Question 498

You have prepared an IM injection but realize the patient’s name on the syringe label doesn’t match the patient in the room—what is the best action?

Do not administer; stop and resolve the identification/labeling discrepancy and prepare a correctly labeled dose, because wrong-patient medication errors are high harm and the trap is assuming the schedule is correct.
Question 499

A patient is due for an IM vaccine but is taking a blood thinner and has a large bruise at a prior injection site—what should the CMA do best before giving the shot?

Notify the provider and choose an appropriate site/technique (e.g., firm pressure afterward) per guidance, because anticoagulation increases hematoma risk and the trap is using the bruised area or skipping follow-up instructions.
Question 500

While giving a subcutaneous injection, you feel unexpected resistance and the patient reports sharp burning pain—what should you do next?

Stop the injection, withdraw the needle, apply gentle pressure, and report to the provider, because continuing may worsen tissue injury and the trap is “pushing through” to finish the dose.
