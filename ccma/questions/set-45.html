Question 4401

The clinic recall report shows a patient is 6 months overdue for a colon cancer screening and has not responded to one postcard; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Follow the practice recall workflow by attempting direct contact (phone/portal) and documenting the outreach attempt and outcome, because recall systems require trackable steps; the trap is sending repeated reminders without documentation or escalation.
Question 4402

A patient returns to the front desk confused and says, “The doctor said something about labs, but I don’t know what to do next,” and the provider is with another patient; what should the CMA do first?

Review the written orders/after-visit summary and give clear, simple next-step instructions (and schedule the lab if needed), because reinforcing provider instructions is within CMA scope; the trap is improvising medical advice or guessing what was ordered.
Question 4403

A patient calls two days after a visit saying, “No one told me my test results,” and the chart shows results were reviewed by the provider yesterday; what should the CMA do next?

Follow the clinic’s results-notification protocol by confirming patient identity, relaying the provider-approved message, and documenting the communication, because test-result follow-up must be closed-loop; the trap is giving personal interpretation of results.
Question 4404

After medication teaching, the patient nods and says “Sure, I got it,” but keeps looking at the paper with a puzzled expression; what is the best way for the CMA to confirm understanding?

Use teach-back (“In your own words, how will you take this?”) and clarify gaps, because teach-back checks comprehension without shaming; the trap is accepting yes/no agreement as understanding.
Question 4405

During discharge education, a patient says they can’t read the printed instructions well and forgot their glasses; what should the CMA do best?

Provide verbal instructions in plain language and offer larger-print or simplified written materials, then use teach-back, because health literacy barriers reduce adherence; the trap is rushing or only handing paperwork.
Question 4406

Question 4492

After an office visit for new hypertension, a patient says, “I’m not sure what I’m supposed to do next,” and starts to leave without scheduling the provider-requested 2-week follow-up; what should the CMA do BEST to support continuity of care?

Pause the checkout, use teach-back to confirm the patient can state the medication and follow-up plan in their own words, provide simple written instructions, and schedule the 2-week follow-up before the patient leaves, because closing the loop prevents missed follow-up and a common mistake is assuming verbal instructions alone are understood or that the patient will call later.
Question 4493

A patient is checking out after a visit and says, “The doctor told me to see cardiology but I’ll call later,” and you notice no referral order was entered; what should the CMA do next to prevent a missed referral?

Notify the provider/ordering team member immediately to confirm and place the referral order, then help schedule or initiate the referral before the patient leaves and review the next steps in plain language, because assuming the patient will “call later” is a common way referrals fail and the loop isn’t closed.
Question 4494

A patient returns to the clinic stating, “The pharmacist said my new prescription needs prior authorization,” and is upset; what should the CMA do next?

Collect the pharmacy/insurance details and route the prior-authorization request to the designated staff/provider for processing while setting expectations on timing, because CMAs support workflow but don’t approve meds; the trap is promising immediate approval or changing the medication plan.
Question 4495

A patient asks for community help to quit smoking and says, “I don’t want to pay for a program,” what should the CMA do next?

Provide information/referral to free evidence-based resources (e.g., quitline/community programs) and document the education and patient preference, because matching resources to barriers improves follow-through; the trap is only advising “just stop” without actionable support.
Question 4496

After a visit for new hypertension, the provider explains a DASH-style diet and home BP log, but the patient says, “I’m not good with medical stuff—just tell me what to do,” and their adult child starts answering for them; what should the CMA do NEXT to support adherence?

Ask the patient (not the family member) to explain in their own words the diet changes and how they’ll record BP, then clarify using simple language and provide written instructions, because teach-back confirms understanding and the trap is accepting “yes/I understand” or letting a companion speak for the patient.
Question 4497

A specialist office requests additional documentation for a referral, including the most recent visit note and imaging report; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the requested items are authorized for release and send the necessary documents via the approved secure method, because incomplete referrals delay care; the trap is sending partial records or using unsecured email.
Question 4498

A patient with uncontrolled hypertension frequently no-shows and says, “I forget appointments,” what is the CMA’s best next step to support adherence?

Offer reminder options (texts/calls/portal), confirm the best contact method, and schedule the next visit before the patient leaves with written details, because reminder systems reduce no-shows; the trap is labeling the patient “noncompliant” without offering supports.
Question 4499

A patient’s screening mammogram result was “abnormal—needs diagnostic imaging,” and the EHR shows no scheduled follow-up 2 weeks later; the patient missed a prior appointment and is hard to reach—what is the CMA’s BEST next step to close the loop?

Follow the clinic’s recall workflow by attempting patient contact using approved methods, documenting each attempt and updating the provider/ordering team for escalation if unreachable, because abnormal results require documented follow-up and the trap is assuming “radiology will call” or leaving it untracked.
Question 4500

During a follow-up call about imaging results, a patient asks the CMA, “Is it cancer?” and the provider has not documented an interpretation plan yet; what should the CMA do?

Escalate to the provider for patient notification/interpretation and tell the patient the provider will review and contact them, because CMAs must not interpret diagnostic results; the trap is reading the report to the patient as an explanation.
