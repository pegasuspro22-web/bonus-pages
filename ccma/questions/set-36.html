Question 3501

A urine culture is ordered, and the patient hands the CMA an unlabeled specimen cup collected in the restroom; what should the CMA do next?

Confirm two identifiers and label the container immediately in front of the patient, then place it in the proper transport bag; the trap is labeling at the nurses’ station, increasing mix-ups.
Question 3502

Mid-draw, the patient becomes pale and says, “I feel like I’m going to pass out”; what should the CMA do next?

Immediately remove the needle, activate safety, help the patient into a safe position (recline with legs elevated if possible), and notify the provider per clinic protocol; continuing the draw is a safety error.
Question 3503

After a venipuncture, a large bruise starts forming and the patient reports sharp pain at the site; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Stop the draw if still in progress, remove the needle, and apply firm pressure and a cold pack; the trap is “digging” for the vein, which increases hematoma and nerve injury risk.
Question 3504

During venipuncture, blood flow stops after the first tube and the CMA notices the tourniquet has been on for a prolonged time while troubleshooting; what should the CMA do next?

Release the tourniquet and reassess before attempting again because prolonged tourniquet time can cause hemoconcentration and inaccurate results; the trap is leaving it on while fishing for flow.
Question 3505

A patient arrives with a 24-hour urine jug at room temperature and says it sat in the car for two hours; what is the best CMA action?

Ask about required storage (usually refrigerated) and notify the lab/provider that storage requirements may have been violated before processing; the trap is accepting and sending without documenting compromised integrity.
Question 3506

For a CLIA-waived rapid strep test, the CMA is about to run the cartridge but notices the kit’s expiration date was last week; what should the CMA do?

Do not use the expired kit, obtain an in-date test, and document per QC policy; the trap is “just running it” because waived tests still require valid reagents for accurate results.
Question 3507

A fasting glucose is ordered as a point-of-care fingerstick, but the patient’s hands are visibly soiled from work; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient wash hands with soap and water and dry thoroughly before the fingerstick; relying only on an alcohol swab is a common trap that can contaminate the sample and skew results.
Question 3508

The CMA draws a lavender tube for CBC and sets it upright on the counter without mixing; 10 minutes later they remember; what should the CMA do?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times as soon as possible and note if clotting is suspected; the common mistake is shaking vigorously, which hemolyzes the sample.
Question 3509

During a routine venipuncture for a CBC, CMP, and PT/INR, the CMA fills the light blue tube first but notices the tube only filled about halfway and the patient is stable; what should the CMA do NEXT to prevent an inaccurate coagulation result?

Discard that underfilled light blue tube and recollect a new light blue tube to the proper fill line before sending, because citrate tubes require a correct blood-to-additive ratio and a common mistake is “topping off” or submitting a short draw that can falsely prolong PT/INR.
Question 3510

After performing a waived urine dipstick, the CMA reads the results 10 minutes later while rooming the next patient; what should the CMA do?

Repeat the test with a new strip and read within the manufacturer’s time window; the trap is charting late-read colors, which can create false positives/negatives.
Question 3511

An EKG printout shows “LEAD OFF” and the tracing is flat in one lead; what should the CMA do first?

Check and reattach the indicated electrode/lead wire connection and ensure good skin contact; the trap is restarting the machine without fixing the physical connection.
Question 3512

A patient with a right-arm PICC line needs outpatient lab work, and the CMA is preparing for venipuncture; what is the best action?

Avoid venipuncture in the PICC arm and select the other arm or consult the nurse/provider for alternate collection; the trap is drawing near a vascular access device, increasing complication risk.
Question 3513

While running an EKG, the tracing shows a shaky baseline across multiple leads and the patient is visibly shivering; what should the CMA do next?

Warm and reassure the patient and minimize muscle movement, then repeat the tracing if needed; the trap is assuming it’s a cardiac rhythm problem and printing without troubleshooting artifact.
Question 3514

A provider orders a 12-lead EKG, and the CMA notices the patient’s chest is diaphoretic and lotion is present; what should the CMA do before placing electrodes?

Clean and dry the skin (and lightly abrade if needed) to improve electrode contact; the trap is placing electrodes over moisture/lotion, which increases artifact.
Question 3515

During EKG setup, the patient asks, “Does this mean I’m having a heart attack?”; what is the best CMA response?

Explain the test records the heart’s electrical activity and the provider will interpret results, then proceed promptly; the trap is giving interpretation or reassurance beyond scope.
Question 3516

After collecting a specimen for a send-out test, the CMA realizes the requisition has the correct name but the wrong medical record number; what should the CMA do next?

Stop processing and correct the paperwork using proper verification and facility procedure before sending; the trap is “fixing it later,” which can cause rejected specimens or misfiled results.
Question 3517

A patient on the schedule states they have a history of mastectomy with lymph node removal on the left; the CMA is preparing for venipuncture and the right arm has a visible IV from an infusion clinic; what should the CMA do next?

Consult the provider for an appropriate site (often avoiding the mastectomy side and avoiding draws above/near an IV) because site selection affects lymphedema and specimen dilution; the trap is choosing the “best vein” without considering restrictions.
Question 3518

A provider orders PT/INR and the CMA collects a light blue tube that is only half full; what is the best next action?

Recollect the specimen ensuring the light blue tube is filled to the indicated line because underfilling changes the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio; the trap is sending it anyway and getting inaccurate coagulation results.
Question 3519

During venipuncture, blood flow stops after the first tube and the CMA considers “fishing” the needle to restart flow; what should the CMA do instead?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, apply pressure, and attempt a new draw at a different site because probing increases hematoma/nerve injury risk; the trap is manipulating the needle to salvage the stick.
Question 3520

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint immediately after the needle is inserted; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, apply pressure, and position the patient safely (supine with legs elevated if possible) because syncope risk makes safety the priority; the trap is “finish quickly” to avoid wasting supplies.
Question 3521

Immediately after drawing blood cultures, the CMA realizes they labeled the bottles at the nurses’ station instead of in the room; what is the best next action?

Treat the specimens as mislabeled and follow the lab’s relabel/recollect process because labeling must occur in the patient’s presence to ensure identification integrity; the trap is adding the label later because you “know it’s theirs.”
Question 3522

After removing the needle, the CMA sees rapid swelling at the puncture site; what is the best immediate action?

Apply firm pressure and elevate the arm, then document/notify per protocol because this suggests a hematoma and needs prompt control; the trap is placing a bandage only and letting the patient leave.
Question 3523

During an outpatient phlebotomy, you draw a light blue (citrate) tube first but it only fills halfway because blood flow stops; the patient is stable and the provider needs PT/INR—what should the CMA do next?

Discard the underfilled light blue tube and redraw a new light blue tube that fills to the marked line because citrate tubes require the correct blood-to-additive ratio for accurate coagulation results, and the common mistake is sending an underfilled tube that can falsely prolong clotting times.
Question 3524

A fasting glucose is ordered and the patient’s tube sits on the counter for an hour before processing; what should the CMA do next?

Follow lab protocol for delayed processing (often recollect or ensure appropriate glycolysis inhibitor tube/handling) because glucose can drop if not handled correctly; the trap is assuming refrigeration alone fixes pre-analytic error.
Question 3525

After a fingerstick, the CMA strongly “milks” the finger to fill the microcollection device; what is the best correction for next time?

Use gentle pressure and wipe away the first drop rather than forcefully squeezing because excessive milking causes hemolysis and tissue-fluid contamination; the trap is thinking more pressure equals a better sample.
Question 3526

While obtaining an EKG, the machine displays “leads off” and the patient is anxious, asking if something is wrong with their heart; what should the CMA do next?

Reassure the patient it’s a contact issue, check cable connections/electrode adherence, and restart once corrected because this is usually equipment/patient interface—not a diagnosis; the trap is alarming the patient or discussing interpretation.
Question 3527

A capillary fingerstick is planned for POCT hemoglobin, and the patient’s hands are cold; what should the CMA do before puncture?

Warm the hand/finger and ensure good circulation before puncture because poor perfusion leads to inadequate/hemolyzed samples; the trap is squeezing hard to force blood, which dilutes with tissue fluid.
Question 3528

An EKG tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is shivering in the room; what should the CMA do first?

Address the patient’s comfort (provide warmth, have them relax/keep still) and then repeat the EKG because muscle tremor mimics artifact; the trap is interpreting it as an arrhythmia and escalating before troubleshooting.
Question 3529

The provider orders a clean-catch urine, and the patient asks if they can collect it after wiping once quickly; what should the CMA do next?

Re-educate on proper clean-catch steps (cleanse appropriately, start stream, collect midstream without touching inside of cup) because poor technique contaminates culture results; the trap is rushing collection to keep clinic flow moving.
Question 3530

While drawing blood for a CMP and CBC, the CMA fills the lavender tube first and then the light green tube; what should the CMA do next to protect specimen accuracy?

Label and send the specimens but notify the provider/lab that the order of draw was incorrect and a redraw may be needed, because EDTA carryover from lavender can falsely alter chemistry results (common mistake is trying to “fix” it by pouring blood into another tube or ignoring the error).
Question 3531

A urine culture is ordered and the patient hands the CMA a specimen cup filled to the brim with unlabeled urine; what should the CMA do first?

Verify patient identity and label the specimen immediately in the patient’s presence (then assess if recollection is needed for sterile technique) because unlabeled specimens risk misidentification; the trap is sending it “since it’s fresh.”
Question 3532

During a fasting lipid fingerstick POCT, the CMA’s first drop of blood touches the patient’s skin and then the test strip while the patient asks to “just run it anyway”; what should the CMA do next?

Discard that strip, wipe away the first drop, obtain a new drop, and retest with a new strip because contaminated/hemolyzed first-drop samples can cause inaccurate results, and the common mistake is trying to “save” the test after the strip has been contaminated.
Question 3533

The CMA opens a new box of test strips for a CLIA-waived strep test and plans to start testing immediately; what should the CMA do first?

Perform and document required QC for the new lot (and verify expiration/storage) before patient testing because lot changes can affect accuracy; the trap is assuming waived testing never needs QC.
Question 3534

While drawing a fasting lipid panel, you notice the tourniquet has been on for over 1 minute because you were searching for a vein; what should you do FIRST?

Release the tourniquet and allow circulation to normalize before reapplying because prolonged tourniquet time can falsely elevate analytes; the trap is proceeding to draw “quickly” anyway.
Question 3535

During a venipuncture, blood flow slows and the patient’s fist is repeatedly pumping; what should the CMA do NEXT to obtain an accurate sample?

Ask the patient to relax the hand and release fist-pumping because it can hemoconcentrate and alter results; the trap is encouraging pumping, which can worsen accuracy and flow.
Question 3536

A provider orders a CBC, BMP, and PT/INR on a patient, and you have to draw all specimens in one venipuncture; what tube should you fill first?

Draw the light blue citrate tube first because coagulation testing is most sensitive to additive carryover; a common trap is starting with serum or EDTA and contaminating the PT/INR.
Question 3537

After completing a CLIA-waived urine dipstick, the CMA documents results but forgets to record the reagent strip lot number and expiration; what is the best next action?

Add the missing QC/lot documentation per protocol as soon as identified because traceability is required for quality audits and result validity; the trap is thinking only the numeric result matters and QC details are optional.
Question 3538

In POCT glucose testing, the control solution result is out of the acceptable range but the patient is waiting; what should the CMA do next?

Do not report patient results, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (check strip lot/expiration, storage, meter cleaning, rerun controls) and repeat QC before testing because out-of-range controls invalidate results; the trap is “running the patient anyway” to save time.
Question 3539

You collected a lavender-top tube for CBC but forgot to invert it and it clots in the lab; what is the BEST corrective action?

Redraw the specimen because a clotted EDTA sample is unusable for CBC; the trap is trying to “shake it” later, which can’t undo clotting and may hemolyze.
Question 3540

A provider asks the CMA if the EKG “looks like atrial fibrillation” and whether to start treatment; what is the appropriate response?

State you cannot interpret or recommend treatment, and promptly give the tracing to the provider while reporting any urgent symptoms observed because interpretation/prescribing is outside CMA scope; the trap is offering a diagnosis based on pattern recognition.
Question 3541

You labeled tubes after leaving the exam room because you were interrupted, and now you’re not 100% sure which patient they belong to; what is the BEST action?

Question 3542

Question 3543

Question 3596

A new shipment of urine dipsticks arrives; what is the CMA’s BEST action before using them on patients?

Verify expiration date, storage requirements, and perform required quality control with control materials per manufacturer/clinic policy, because new lots can perform differently and the trap is using them immediately because they’re “factory sealed.”
Question 3597

A provider orders PT/INR and CBC, and the CMA has already drawn the lavender tube first; what is the best action?

Notify the supervisor/ordering provider and recollect with a new venipuncture using the correct order (light blue before lavender) because EDTA contamination can falsely prolong coag tests; the trap is sending it anyway “since blood is blood.”
Question 3598

During a routine draw, the patient says, “I feel dizzy,” becomes pale, and slumps in the chair; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle safely, and assist the patient to a safe position (legs elevated if possible) because vasovagal syncope can lead to injury; the trap is trying to “finish the tube” before helping.
Question 3599

While preparing for venipuncture, the CMA cleanses the site and then palpates again with an ungloved finger to “confirm the vein”; what should the CMA do?

Re-cleanse the site and avoid re-palpating the prepped area (or palpate with a clean gloved finger before final cleanse) because recontamination increases infection risk; the trap is proceeding without re-cleaning.
Question 3600

Immediately after filling an EDTA (lavender) tube, the CMA sets it upright without mixing; what is the best next step?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times right away to mix additive and prevent clotting; the trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the specimen.
