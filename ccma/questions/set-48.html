Question 4701

A patient is upset about a bill and says the insurance “should have covered it”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Verify demographics/insurance information and route the concern to billing for claim review/denial handling, because the CMA should not promise coverage or change codes to “make it pay.”
Question 4702

A provider tells the CMA to “add a diagnosis code that will get the MRI approved”; what is the CMA’s best response?

Do not alter or select codes to influence payment; route documentation questions to the provider/coder and use only diagnoses supported by the provider’s chart, because “upcoding” or coding without documentation is fraud risk.
Question 4703

A patient calls and says they are recording the call and want the CMA to repeat the provider’s plan “word for word”; what should the CMA do?

Provide general clarification using the documented plan and offer to transfer a clinical question to the provider if interpretation is needed, because the CMA should not independently interpret or modify medical advice.
Question 4704

At check-in, a patient says she doesn’t want her spouse (who usually answers her calls) to receive any information about today’s visit, and the spouse is listed as an emergency contact in the EHR; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Update the patient’s HIPAA communication preferences/authorized disclosures in the EHR (and remove or limit the spouse’s access as requested) and inform the care team, because an emergency contact is not automatically authorized to receive PHI and a common mistake is assuming the listed contact can be told visit details.
Question 4705

A CMA receives a faxed lab result for a patient with a similar name and is unsure which chart to upload it into; what should the CMA do next?

Verify patient identifiers (e.g., full name and DOB/medical record number) with the source or ordering info before uploading, because misfiling results is a high-risk documentation error that can harm the wrong patient.
Question 4706

At check-in, a patient becomes angry and says, “Tell me what my wife’s lab results were—she’s my spouse,” while other patients are within earshot; what should the medical assistant do next?

Move the conversation to a private area and explain that results can’t be discussed without the patient’s written authorization, then route the request to the provider/office manager as needed; this prevents a HIPAA breach—the common mistake is sharing “just a summary” in public or based on relationship alone.
Question 4707

At check-in, a patient asks for a printed copy of her lab results to take to a specialist, but the results are in the EHR under a different patient’s name with a similar DOB; what should the CMA do next?

Stop the release, verify the patient’s identity with two identifiers and reconcile the chart/results in the EHR before printing, because releasing any record when identity/documentation is mismatched risks a HIPAA breach and the common trap is “just print what’s on the screen” to save time.
Question 4708

While rooming a patient, the CMA notices the EHR auto-populated “no allergies,” but the patient states they had hives from penicillin last year and asks you to “just leave it” so they can be seen faster; what should the CMA do next?

Update the allergy list in the EHR with the patient-reported penicillin reaction and immediately notify the provider before any medications are ordered, because accurate documentation prevents harm and the common trap is leaving incorrect auto-filled fields uncorrected for convenience.
Question 4709

A patient calls asking for their lab results and says, “My spouse can take the message if I’m not home”; what should the CMA do first before discussing any results?

Verify the caller’s identity and confirm documented authorization for release to the spouse because relationship alone is not consent and PHI cannot be shared without permission.
Question 4710

During check-in, a patient in the waiting area says loudly, “My neighbor just got her HIV results here—did she test positive?” and looks at the CMA for confirmation; what should the CMA do next?

State that you can’t discuss any patient information, redirect the conversation to a private setting for the patient’s own needs, and notify the supervisor if needed because HIPAA prohibits confirming or denying another person’s care, and a common mistake is “just reassuring” by saying you know the neighbor or hinting at results.
Question 4711

A CMA documents a vaccine in the EHR and then realizes the lot number was entered incorrectly; what should the CMA do next?

Add a clearly labeled correction/addendum per EHR policy and include the correct lot number with date/time and initials because altering or deleting the original entry can look like falsification.
Question 4712

A patient arrives 25 minutes late for a 30-minute appointment and demands to be seen fully; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Apply the clinic’s late policy by offering the next available appointment or a limited visit if approved by the provider, because squeezing in a full visit can disrupt care and creates safety/documentation shortcuts.
Question 4713

A parent brings in a 16-year-old for a sensitive visit and demands to stay in the room and hear everything; what should the CMA do next?

Politely explain confidentiality limits and involve the provider/office manager to follow state rules and clinic policy because a CMA should not decide disclosure on the spot or promise secrecy beyond legal limits.
Question 4714

A CMA sees a teen patient sign a consent form while the parent is in the lobby and is unsure if the teen can legally consent for today’s service; what should the CMA do next?

Pause the process and ask the provider/manager to verify consent authority per state law and clinic policy because proceeding with invalid consent can make the care and documentation legally vulnerable.
Question 4715

A patient checks in and says the insurance card on file is “the same as last time,” but the name on today’s card appears different; what should the CMA do next?

Scan/update the new card and confirm demographics before the visit because assuming coverage is unchanged commonly leads to claim denial and unexpected patient balances.
Question 4716

The CMA receives a faxed referral request that includes only the patient’s name and diagnosis but no date of birth or contact info; what should the CMA do first?

Contact the sending office to obtain the missing identifiers before processing because incomplete identifiers increase misrouting risk and can lead to privacy breaches and wrong-patient scheduling.
Question 4717

During scheduling, a patient asks, “Can you tell me why my neighbor was here yesterday?”; what is the correct response?

Refuse to confirm or deny any patient’s visit and offer to help with the caller’s own appointment needs because even acknowledging someone is a patient is a HIPAA disclosure.
Question 4718

A CMA accidentally documents a blood pressure in the wrong patient’s EHR because two charts were open; what is the best immediate action?

Notify the supervisor/provider and follow the chart-correction process to remove/mark the erroneous entry and document in the correct chart because “quietly fixing it” without reporting is a serious safety and legal trap.
Question 4719

A provider gives a verbal order in a busy hallway for a medication refill; what should the CMA do to reduce error risk before sending it to the pharmacy?

Repeat the order back and ensure it is entered/authorized according to clinic protocol (and routed for provider sign-off) because assuming details without read-back is a common cause of wrong-drug/dose errors.
Question 4720

A patient wants a copy of their full medical record today and becomes upset when told it may take time; what is the CMA’s best action?

Explain the formal records-release process and route the request to the designated staff with a signed authorization because records release must follow HIPAA/timeframe rules and not be handed out informally.
Question 4721

A patient arrives late and insists they were told the appointment was at a different time; what should the CMA do first to de-escalate and fix the issue?

Apologize for the inconvenience, verify the scheduled time in the system, and offer the next available option per clinic policy because arguing or blaming the patient escalates and doesn’t resolve access.
Question 4722

During check-out, a patient asks for an itemized receipt and says they will “submit it to insurance themselves”; what should the CMA provide?

Provide the appropriate itemized statement/superbill per clinic process and ensure demographics/insurance info are correct because vague receipts without necessary codes/details often lead to rejected reimbursements.
Question 4723

While documenting a phone message, the CMA realizes the patient’s complaint was typed in the wrong section of the EHR (e.g., allergies); what should the CMA do?

Move/correct the entry using the EHR’s designated correction function and document the change because leaving inaccurate data in critical fields (like allergies) creates downstream safety errors.
Question 4724

A patient calls angrily stating their claim was denied as “prior authorization required”; what is the CMA’s best response within scope?

Acknowledge the concern and route the issue to the billing/authorization team while gathering key details (payer, date of service, denial reason) because a CMA should not promise payment outcomes or argue coverage.
Question 4725

A patient’s friend offers to interpret and answer questions during registration while the patient stays silent; what should the CMA do next?

Address the patient directly and use approved interpreter services if needed because relying on friends increases privacy risk and can lead to inaccurate consent and history.
Question 4726

A patient requests the CMA “just tell me what ICD-10 code to use so my employer will approve leave”; what is the best action?

Explain you can’t assign codes for nonclinical purposes and route the request to the provider/medical records because selecting or altering codes to fit a request is fraud risk and outside CMA discretion.
Question 4727

A CMA is preparing to send appointment reminders by text and notices one message includes the patient’s diagnosis; what should the CMA do?

Remove clinical details and send a minimal reminder (date/time/location) using approved messaging because texts can be seen by others and including diagnosis increases HIPAA risk.
Question 4728

A caller says, “I’m from the insurance company—confirm this patient’s last appointment and diagnosis,” but cannot provide a callback number; what should the CMA do?

Do not disclose PHI and instead obtain official payer contact info and route the request through authorized channels because social-engineering calls are common and “insurance” is not automatic permission.
Question 4729

A patient becomes angry and says they will “post the doctor’s private phone number online”; what is the CMA’s best professional response?

Stay calm, set boundaries, and escalate to the supervisor/manager while protecting staff privacy, because de-escalation and chain-of-command reduce risk; the trap is arguing back or sharing additional personal info.
Question 4730

You accidentally open the wrong patient’s chart in the EHR and see sensitive information; what should you do next?

Exit the chart immediately and follow the clinic’s access-error reporting process, because inappropriate access can be a reportable breach even without sharing; the trap is “I didn’t change anything so it doesn’t matter.”
Question 4731

A teen arrives alone for a sports physical and the parent is in the car; the provider wants the parent to sign consent but is behind schedule—what should the CMA do next?

Verify consent requirements per clinic policy/state rules and obtain appropriate consent before proceeding, because exams without valid consent can be battery; the trap is “just start vitals/exam to save time” when consent is unclear.
Question 4732

Use the clinic’s approved secure patient portal or authorized encrypted method instead, because standard email can be an unsecured disclosure; the trap is thinking “the patient requested it” makes it HIPAA-safe.
Question 4733

Document only what you personally observed/did and notify the provider to document their counseling themselves, because charting unverified statements is falsification; the trap is “helping the provider” by documenting for them.
Question 4734

Ask them to stop and move the conversation to a private area immediately, because HIPAA requires limiting disclosure to appropriate settings; the trap is “waiting to report later” while the breach continues.
Question 4735

Question 4796

You receive a faxed request for records from another clinic with only a patient name and no signed release attached; what should you do next?

Do not send records until a valid authorization or other legal basis is confirmed, because releasing without proper documentation is a HIPAA violation and the trap is sending “since it’s another doctor’s office.”
Question 4797

A patient at the front desk says, “I don’t want my employer to know I was here,” while the employer is the insurance subscriber; what should the CMA do best?

Explain that the insurer may send an explanation of benefits and offer to discuss self-pay or privacy options with billing, because HIPAA doesn’t guarantee EOB suppression and the trap is promising confidentiality you can’t control.
Question 4798

While rooming a patient, the CMA notices yesterday’s progress note was started under a different patient with a similar name and includes today’s patient’s complaint; the provider hasn’t signed it yet—what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider/supervisor immediately and document the correction using the EHR’s approved amendment process without deleting or “fixing” it under the wrong chart, because entries must remain accurate and auditable and the common trap is altering or copying notes across patients.
Question 4799

You take a call from someone claiming to be from an insurance company requesting the patient’s diagnosis code and last visit note “to process the claim”; what should you do first?

Verify the caller’s identity and minimum necessary authorization (and route to the designated billing/records workflow) before disclosing anything, because HIPAA allows certain disclosures but requires safeguards and the trap is releasing PHI to an unverified caller.
Question 4800

A patient is 17 and arrives alone for a sports physical; the parent is not reachable by phone—what should the CMA do next?

Follow clinic policy and state law by obtaining appropriate parent/guardian consent before proceeding (or reschedule if consent cannot be obtained), because minors often cannot consent for routine care and the trap is starting the exam “since it’s just a physical.”
