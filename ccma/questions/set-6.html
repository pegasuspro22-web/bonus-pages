Question 501

For a patient who feels faint during a fingerstick glucose check, what position is best to place them in first?

Supine with legs elevated if possible, because it supports cerebral perfusion and prevents falls, and the trap is keeping them seated “to finish the test.”
Question 502

During a dressing change, you accidentally touch the sterile gauze pad with your ungloved hand—what is the correct next step?

Discard the contaminated gauze and obtain a new sterile pad before continuing, because sterility is broken once touched and the trap is using it anyway since it “looks clean.”
Question 503

You are asked to remove sutures from a laceration; the wound edges are slightly separated with yellow drainage—what should you do next?

Do not remove the sutures; cover the area and notify the provider immediately, because dehiscence/infection needs evaluation and the trap is proceeding because the order said “suture removal.”
Question 504

The provider orders IM ketorolac; the vial label reads 30 mg/mL and the order is for 15 mg—what volume should the CMA prepare, and what safety step should happen next?

Draw up 0.5 mL and have the dose verified per clinic policy before administration, because dose-volume conversion errors are common and the trap is drawing 1 mL because it “looks standard.”
Question 505

After intake, a teen presents for a sports physical with a parent; the teen quietly asks you not to share a sensitive concern with the parent—what should the CMA do best?

Explain you’ll protect privacy within clinic policy and state law and involve the provider to discuss confidentiality and consent boundaries, because minors’ privacy rules vary and the trap is promising absolute secrecy or automatically telling the parent.
Question 506

A 6-year-old is anxious and squirming while you attempt an oral temperature; what is the best next step to obtain a safe, accurate vital sign?

Switch to a tympanic or temporal method per clinic policy and ensure correct probe placement because movement increases oral reading error and injury risk; the trap is forcing the oral method and recording an unreliable value.
Question 507

A patient arrives for a routine visit wearing a puffy coat and talking on the phone; what should the CMA do before measuring blood pressure for accuracy?

Have the patient sit quietly with feet flat, arm supported at heart level, and remove bulky clothing from the arm because improper positioning and clothing falsely elevate readings; the trap is taking BP over a sleeve while the patient is talking.
Question 508

You turn on the AED and it says “analyzing—do not touch the patient,” but another staff member is still holding the patient’s shoulder—what should you do?

Instruct everyone to clear and ensure no one is touching the patient before analysis/shock, because contact can interfere with analysis and risk shock to rescuers and the trap is ignoring the warning to “save time.”
Question 509

A patient suddenly becomes unresponsive in the waiting room; what should the CMA do first?

Assess responsiveness and breathing and activate emergency response (call for help/EMS and get the AED) while starting CPR if not breathing normally, because time to CPR/AED is critical and the trap is searching for the chart or provider first.
Question 510

While obtaining vitals, a patient says they feel faint as you stand them to measure weight; what is the best immediate action?

Assist them back to a seated or supine position and assess responsiveness before continuing because preventing a fall is priority; the trap is insisting they finish the weight to “complete vitals.”
Question 511

You recheck a pulse oximetry reading of 88% on a patient with cold hands but no distress; what should the CMA do next?

Warm the hand/reposition the probe and verify waveform, then reassess and notify the provider if it remains low because poor perfusion causes false lows; the trap is documenting 88% as true hypoxemia without troubleshooting.
Question 512

During intake, a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 84/52 and pulse 124; the provider is running behind—what should the CMA do first?

Keep the patient seated or supine and immediately alert the provider/activate clinic emergency protocol because hypotension with tachycardia suggests possible shock; the trap is continuing the intake or sending the patient to the waiting room.
Question 513

You place a patient in supine for an exam and they become short of breath; what should the CMA do first?

Raise the head of the exam table to Fowler’s position and reassess because positioning can quickly improve breathing; the trap is leaving them flat while you “go get the provider.”
Question 514

A patient with a known latex allergy is scheduled for an injection and the only gloves in the room are powdered latex; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and obtain non-latex gloves/supplies before proceeding because avoiding exposure prevents an allergic reaction; the trap is “being careful” but using latex anyway.
Question 515

A patient with a draining wound arrives without a dressing and sits in the waiting area; what should the CMA do first to reduce transmission risk?

Provide a clean dressing/covering and move them to an exam room as soon as possible because source control interrupts the chain of infection; the trap is leaving the wound uncovered while you finish check-in.
Question 516

You are preparing to administer an IM vaccine and the patient asks to use the restroom first; what should the CMA do with the drawn-up syringe?

Discard it and prepare a new dose when the patient returns because unattended prepared medication risks mix-ups and contamination; the trap is setting the labeled syringe on the counter “just for a minute.”
Question 517

Right before an injection, the patient states they had a severe reaction to a similar medication years ago but “can’t remember which one”; what is the best next step?

Hold the medication and notify the provider to verify allergy history before administration because preventing anaphylaxis is priority; the trap is proceeding because the allergy list in the chart looks incomplete.
Question 518

After cleaning an exam room, you notice dried blood on the counter near where supplies are stored; what is the best action?

Don appropriate PPE and disinfect with an EPA-registered disinfectant using proper contact time because blood requires standard precautions and correct dwell time; the trap is wiping quickly with alcohol and immediately setting clean supplies there.
Question 519

A coworker removes gloves after a fingerstick but skips hand hygiene because “I wore gloves”; what should the CMA do?

Perform hand hygiene and remind/educate that gloves do not replace hand hygiene because contamination can occur during glove removal; the trap is assuming glove use alone prevents transmission.
Question 520

The provider orders 0.5 mL of a medication, but the vial concentration and your calculation indicate the drawn volume would be 5 mL; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and clarify the order with the provider before preparing/administering because the dose appears unsafe/unreasonable; the trap is assuming a large volume is correct and giving it anyway.
Question 521

While changing a dressing, you accidentally touch the sterile gauze to the patient’s clothing; what should the CMA do?

Discard the contaminated gauze and replace it with a new sterile dressing because sterility is broken once it touches a nonsterile surface; the trap is using it anyway because “it still looks clean.”
Question 522

A patient arrives for suture removal but the wound edges are red, warm, and draining; what should the CMA do next?

Do not remove sutures independently and promptly notify the provider because signs suggest infection/dehiscence risk and requires clinical evaluation; the trap is proceeding with removal because it’s “scheduled.”
Question 523

In the lobby, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what is the CMA’s first action?

Check responsiveness and breathing and activate emergency response (call 911/bring AED per clinic protocol) because rapid recognition and activation precede CPR/AED use; the trap is immediately searching for a pulse and delaying action.
Question 524

You are about to give a subcutaneous injection and notice the medication label looks similar to another drug stored beside it; what should the CMA do to follow medication rights?

Compare the medication label to the order and patient identifiers again (right drug/right dose/right route/right time/right patient) before administering because look-alike packaging causes errors; the trap is relying on where it was stored as “proof.”
Question 525

You apply an AED and it announces “shock advised” while another staff member is still touching the patient; what should the CMA do next?

Loudly state “clear,” ensure no one is touching the patient, then deliver the shock because contact during shock risks injury and reduces effectiveness; the trap is pressing shock quickly without visually confirming everyone is clear.
Question 526

During intake, a 58-year-old reports “crushing chest pressure” and you obtain BP 88/54, pulse 124, RR 24, SpO₂ 90% on room air; what should the CMA do first?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (call for help/EMS per protocol) and keep the patient seated/resting while alerting the provider because these are unstable vitals suggestive of shock/cardiac emergency; the trap is continuing the full intake or having the patient walk to an exam room.
Question 527

A patient becomes pale and sweaty immediately after a vaccination and says, “I’m going to pass out”; what should the CMA do first?

Assist them to a supine position with legs elevated and stay with them while calling for help because vasovagal syncope can lead to injury; the trap is walking them to the lobby for water.
Question 528

The provider requests help positioning a patient for a rectal exam in an outpatient room; what position should the CMA use?

Assist the patient into Sims’ (left lateral) position for best access and comfort because it aligns anatomy and preserves modesty; the trap is using supine, which is less appropriate and increases discomfort.
Question 529

A patient arrives coughing with fever and asks to sit in the crowded waiting area while you “finish the chart”—what should the CMA do next?

Provide a mask, perform hand hygiene, and promptly separate/room the patient (or place at distance) using standard precautions because early source control breaks transmission; the trap is delaying PPE/actions until after registration.
Question 530

A provider orders PT/INR, CMP, and CBC, and the CMA has light blue, gold, and lavender tubes ready; what is the correct order of draw?

Draw light blue first, then gold, then lavender, because coagulation testing must be collected before serum/EDTA tubes and a common trap is drawing lavender early which can contaminate and alter clotting results.
Question 531

After removing gloves used for a wound dressing change, your hands look clean and you’re about to type in the EHR—what is the correct action?

Perform hand hygiene immediately after glove removal because gloves are not a substitute and contamination can occur during doffing; the trap is touching the keyboard/phone first and spreading pathogens.
Question 532

You are preparing an IM vaccine and the patient states they have a history of anaphylaxis to a prior dose; the order is already in the chart—what should the CMA do?

Stop and verify with the provider before administering because a reported severe allergy is a safety stop despite an existing order; the trap is proceeding because “the provider ordered it” or because the patient is due today.
Question 533

While drawing up medication for an injection, you notice the vial’s label concentration does not match what you expected from the order (e.g., different mg/mL) but you haven’t injected yet—what is the best next step?

Pause and re-clarify the order/concentration with the provider/pharmacy reference and re-calculate before giving the dose because concentration mismatches are a common cause of dosing errors; the trap is “making it work” by guessing or adjusting after the injection.
Question 534

While preparing to draw blood, the CMA notices the requisition says “fasting lipid panel,” but the patient mentions they had breakfast 30 minutes ago; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and clarify with the ordering provider or follow clinic protocol to reschedule/annotate before drawing, because collecting a non-fasting specimen can invalidate results and a common trap is drawing anyway and “just noting it later.”
Question 535

You are rooming a patient and recheck an initial BP of 168/102; the repeat after 5 minutes is 166/100, and the patient denies symptoms—what is the best next step?

Notify the provider and document both readings, method, and arm used because persistent severe hypertension needs provider evaluation even without symptoms; the trap is charting only the “better” number or giving lifestyle counseling as if that resolves the immediate finding.
Question 536

A patient becomes unresponsive and pulseless in the exam room while the provider is with another patient—what should the CMA do first?

Start CPR immediately and have someone call 911 and bring the AED because early compressions and rapid defibrillation are the priority in cardiac arrest; the trap is searching for vitals/history first or waiting for the provider to arrive.
Question 537

A patient takes a daily anticoagulant and continues oozing after the draw despite brief pressure; what is the best action?

Apply firm pressure longer and use a pressure dressing as needed, then reassess before discharge, because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk and a common trap is sending them out with minimal pressure once the tube is filled.
Question 538

A CMA draws blood and realizes they forgot to cleanse the site with antiseptic first; what is the best next step?

Discard the specimen and perform a new venipuncture with proper skin antisepsis, because contamination risk is increased and a common trap is sending the sample anyway since “it looks fine.”
Question 539

The patient’s name on the tube label is “Jon Smyth,” but the patient states their legal name is “John Smith”; what should the CMA do before sending the specimen?

Stop and resolve identification using two identifiers and relabel per protocol in the patient’s presence, because mislabeled specimens are rejected and a common trap is “fixing it later” away from the patient.
Question 540

After venipuncture, the puncture site begins to swell and bruise rapidly; what should the CMA do next?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure with the arm straight, because this suggests a hematoma and a common trap is bending the elbow, which can worsen bleeding and bruising.
Question 541

After collecting a lavender-top tube for CBC, the CMA sets it on the counter and sees small clots later; what should have been done immediately after collection?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times right away, because anticoagulant tubes must be mixed to prevent clotting and a common trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the sample.
Question 542

During phlebotomy, the CMA cannot palpate a vein well and considers using the same arm with an IV infusion running; what should the CMA do?

Choose an alternate site/arm and avoid drawing above an active IV when possible, because IV fluids can dilute or contaminate the specimen and a common trap is using the easiest visible vein despite accuracy issues.
Question 543

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint during a blood draw; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the tourniquet and needle, and assist the patient to a safe position (supine or head-down as appropriate), because preventing a fall is priority and a common trap is trying to “finish the tube” before addressing syncope.
Question 544

During venipuncture, blood stops flowing into an evacuated tube after initially filling; what should the CMA do first?

Reposition the needle slightly and check tube engagement before re-sticking, because the bevel may be against the vein wall and a common trap is immediately removing the needle and attempting multiple sticks unnecessarily.
Question 545

A provider asks the CMA to interpret an EKG as “normal” for a patient complaint of chest pressure; what is the appropriate response?

State that interpretation is provider responsibility and promptly ensure the EKG is obtained and given to the provider for review, because CMAs do not diagnose and a common trap is offering reassurance based on limited training.
Question 546

During EKG setup, the CMA places the limb electrodes on the wrists and ankles and gets a lot of motion artifact as the patient shifts; what is the best adjustment?

Place limb electrodes on the torso (per facility practice) to reduce motion artifact while maintaining correct lead orientation, because distal placement increases movement interference and a common trap is repeatedly reprinting without correcting placement.
Question 547

A urine specimen for culture is brought to the front desk unlabeled, and the patient has already left; what should the CMA do?

Reject the specimen and request a recollect with proper labeling, because unlabeled specimens cannot be verified and a common trap is labeling it based on the appointment schedule.
Question 548

The CMA completes venipuncture and notices the tourniquet has been on for over 2 minutes while searching for a vein; what is the best next step before another attempt?

Release the tourniquet and allow circulation to normalize before reapplying for a new attempt, because prolonged stasis can alter results and a common trap is keeping it on while continuing to troubleshoot.
Question 549

The CMA is preparing a 12-lead EKG and the patient is shivering and talking on the phone; the tracing shows jagged baseline; what should the CMA do first?

Address artifact by warming and positioning the patient and asking them to remain still/stop talking before repeating the tracing, because muscle movement mimics abnormal rhythms and a common trap is printing and sending a poor-quality strip.
Question 550

A provider requests blood cultures and a CMP from the same venipuncture; what should the CMA do first to reduce contamination risk?

Collect blood cultures first using strict aseptic technique before other tubes, because preventing skin flora contamination is critical and a common trap is drawing routine tubes first and then “adding cultures.”
Question 551

A patient’s EKG shows a flat line in one lead, but the patient is alert and talking; what should the CMA do next?

Check that electrode and lead wire connection and replace the electrode if needed, because this is most likely a loose lead artifact and a common trap is treating it as true asystole without troubleshooting.
Question 552

While obtaining a capillary fingerstick glucose, the CMA squeezes the fingertip repeatedly to get more blood; what should the CMA do instead?

Wipe away the first drop and allow free-flowing blood with minimal pressure, because excessive squeezing can dilute with tissue fluid and a common trap is “milking” the finger to speed collection.
Question 553

A provider orders a fasting lipid panel, but the patient admits they ate breakfast an hour ago and asks you to “just draw it now”; what should the CMA do?

Clarify and follow clinic protocol—typically reschedule or notify the provider before drawing because the result may be inaccurate; the trap is drawing without documenting/communicating the non-fasting status.
Question 554

A CLIA-waived rapid strep test is negative, but the control indicator did not change as expected; what should the CMA do?

Do not report the result and repeat the test with a new kit and proper QC, because an invalid control means the test is unreliable and a common trap is documenting “negative” anyway due to symptoms.
Question 555

You are about to draw blood and the patient says they fainted during their last blood draw; what is the best preparation before you start?

Have the patient lie down (or recline) and ensure safety measures (call light, observation) because prior syncope increases risk; the trap is proceeding with the patient seated and unattended.
Question 556

During an outpatient venipuncture, the patient says “I feel dizzy” and becomes pale while you’re filling the first tube; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the tourniquet and needle, and assist the patient to a safe position (recline/legs elevated) because preventing syncope injury is priority; the trap is trying to “just finish the tubes” before addressing symptoms.
Question 557

After drawing a lavender-top tube for a CBC, you notice you forgot to label it and another patient has just been roomed; what is the best next step?

Do not guess—discard and recollect with proper labeling at the patient’s side because unlabeled specimens are unacceptable for patient safety; the trap is labeling later from memory.
Question 558

While collecting a basic metabolic panel, you vigorously shake the green-top tube to “mix it faster”; what should you do next when you notice foaming?

Notify the lab/your supervisor and recollect if needed because vigorous shaking can hemolyze the sample and alter results; the trap is sending it anyway hoping it “runs fine.”
Question 559

You have an order for blood cultures, PT/INR, CMP, and CBC in the same visit; what is the correct collection sequence the CMA should follow?

Collect blood cultures first, then light blue (citrate), then serum/SST, then lavender (EDTA) because order of draw prevents additive carryover; the trap is drawing CBC before PT and contaminating coag studies.
Question 560

At the start of the day, the CMA runs glucometer quality control and the control value is out of range; what should the CMA do next?

Stop patient testing, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (new strip lot, control solution, calibration/cleaning) and document, because out-of-range QC invalidates patient results and a common trap is testing patients first and “fixing QC later.”
Question 561

During a venipuncture for a CMP and CBC, you draw the tubes in the wrong order and fill the lavender (EDTA) before the serum separator tube; what should the CMA do next to best protect specimen integrity?

Stop and recollect using the correct order of draw with new tubes and a new venipuncture site if possible, because EDTA carryover can falsely alter chemistry results (common trap: just sending the specimens anyway or swapping labels, which does not fix contamination).
Question 562

An SST tube is drawn for a chemistry panel, but it was placed in the rack and never inverted; what is the best next step?

Gently invert it the recommended number of times as soon as noticed and document/communicate if delayed because proper mixing affects clot activation/serum quality; the trap is shaking it hard to “catch up.”
Question 563

You placed limb leads for an EKG and the machine flags “lead reversal suspected”; what should the CMA do next?

Question 564

Question 565

Question 566

Warm the site and let the arm hang down to improve capillary flow because squeezing/cold fingers can dilute the sample; the trap is “milking” the finger to get enough blood.
Question 567

Question 568

Question 569

Question 570

Question 571

Stop the draw, remove the needle, apply pressure, and position the patient safely (supine with legs elevated if possible) because preventing injury from syncope is the priority; the common trap is trying to “finish the tubes” before addressing the reaction.
Question 572

A coworker hands you opened POCT reagent strips that were left uncapped on the counter; what should you do before using them on a patient?

Question 573

Question 574

Question 575

Treat it as an invalid test, repeat with a new device/sample per instructions, and document QC issues because results can’t be reported without a valid control; the trap is reporting “negative” because no test line appeared.
Question 576

Question 577

Hold firm pressure longer than usual and confirm bleeding has stopped before bandaging because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk; the trap is using the standard brief pressure time and letting the patient leave still oozing.
Question 578

Question 579

After drawing a lavender-top tube for CBC, you notice the tube was not mixed and small clots are forming; what is the best action?

Reject and recollect the specimen (following facility process) because clotted EDTA samples can falsely alter counts and cannot be fixed by shaking later; the trap is vigorously shaking to “break up” clots, which also causes hemolysis.
Question 580

Follow lab policy for delayed processing (often recollect) because prolonged contact with cells can change analytes like potassium/glucose; the trap is spinning “whenever you remember” and assuming results are still valid.
Question 581

Release the tourniquet (if still on), withdraw the needle if present, and apply firm pressure/ice as directed because this indicates hematoma formation; the trap is continuing to probe or immediately bandaging without sustained pressure.
Question 582

A patient’s hand is clenched and pumping during the draw to “make the vein pop,” and you are collecting potassium; what should the CMA instruct?

Question 583

A patient with a history of mastectomy on the right side needs routine labs; the left arm has an IV from a prior visit dressing still in place; what should the CMA do?

Question 584

A provider orders PT/INR and you fill a light blue tube only halfway because the vein stopped flowing; what should the CMA do next?

Recollect with a properly filled light blue tube because underfilling changes the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio and can falsely prolong clotting times; the trap is sending it anyway since “some blood is better than none.”
Question 585

Verify patient identity, label the specimen immediately in the patient’s presence, and assess if collection was midstream/clean-catch—recollect if technique is incorrect because culture results are highly contamination-sensitive; the trap is labeling later at the desk or accepting a non-clean-catch for culture.
Question 586

Stop and recollect the specimens using the correct order of draw (green before lavender) with properly filled tubes, because additive carryover and underfilling can alter results (a common mistake is sending the mislabeled/poor-quality draw “to see if the lab can run it”).

Question 599

You draw a gold SST for a lipid panel and immediately place it in the centrifuge without letting it sit; what should you do next?

Stop and allow the SST to clot for the recommended time before centrifuging because premature spinning can create fibrin strands and incorrect results; the trap is assuming all serum tubes can be spun right away.
Question 600

You complete an outpatient EKG and the machine interpretation flags “acute MI,” but the patient is stable and waiting; what should the CMA do next?

Immediately alert the provider and follow urgent escalation protocol because machine reads can indicate a time-sensitive emergency and the CMA should not interpret or dismiss it; the trap is assuming it’s “just artifact” and filing it for later review.
