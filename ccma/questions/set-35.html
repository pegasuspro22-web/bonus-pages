Question 3401

During venipuncture for a CMP and CBC, the CMA draws the lavender-top EDTA tube first and then the serum separator tube; what should the CMA do next to protect specimen integrity and patient safety?

Stop and redraw both tubes in the correct order of draw (serum/SST before EDTA) using new tubes and proper mixing because EDTA carryover can falsely alter chemistry results; the common mistake is “sending it anyway” since the blood was already collected.
Question 3402

For a potassium test, the patient repeatedly pumps their fist to help you find a vein; what is the best instruction?

Ask the patient to relax the hand and avoid pumping because fist pumping can falsely elevate potassium and the trap is encouraging vigorous pumping to “make the vein pop.”
Question 3403

During venipuncture for a CMP and PT/INR, the patient says, “I feel hot and dizzy,” and becomes pale while the needle is still in the arm; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, activate safety, and assist the patient to a safe position (recline/legs elevated) because this is likely a vasovagal reaction and the trap is trying to “finish the tube” and risking a fall/injury.
Question 3404

While collecting a fasting lipid panel, you apply the tourniquet and the patient’s veins are difficult; two minutes pass as you search—what should you do?

Release the tourniquet and reapply when ready to stick because prolonged tourniquet time can hemoconcentrate results and the trap is leaving it on “until I find the vein.”
Question 3405

The clinic receives a new lot number of rapid strep tests, and patients are already waiting; what is the best next step before using the new kit?

Perform and document required QC for the new lot before patient testing because lot changes can alter performance, and the trap is using it immediately because it is “CLIA-waived.”
Question 3406

A CLIA-waived glucose meter displays a “QC FAIL” message at the start of clinic; what should the CMA do next?

Do not test patients—repeat QC per manufacturer instructions and remove the device from service if it still fails because patient results are unreliable, and the trap is running patient tests “since it’s just a screening.”
Question 3407

During a 12-lead EKG, the tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is shivering in a cold room; what should the CMA do first to troubleshoot?

Warm and reposition the patient to reduce muscle tremor artifact because shivering commonly causes baseline instability, and the trap is repeatedly reprinting without fixing the cause.
Question 3408

A patient’s first blood culture set is ordered, but the CMA cleans the site and then palpates the vein again without gloves; what is the best next action?

Re-clean/disinfect the site and use proper aseptic technique because re-palpation contaminates the site and can cause false-positive cultures; the trap is proceeding because “it still looks clean.”
Question 3409

After removing the needle, a hematoma begins forming at the venipuncture site; what should the CMA do next?

Apply firm direct pressure (longer than usual) and elevate as appropriate because continued bleeding into tissue worsens the hematoma, and the trap is placing a bandage without adequate pressure.
Question 3410

You attempt venipuncture and the patient reports a sharp, electric pain radiating down the arm; what should you do immediately?

Stop the procedure and remove the needle because this may indicate nerve involvement, and the trap is “repositioning” the needle and risking nerve injury.
Question 3411

You collect a urine sample for a CLIA-waived pregnancy test, but the cup is unlabeled and two patients with similar names are waiting; what should you do first?

Stop processing and verify identity by re-collecting/confirming with two identifiers before testing because POCT errors are often identification errors, and the trap is testing first and “fixing the label later.”
Question 3412

A patient reports they are on warfarin and asks you to “use the same arm as always,” but you see a large bruise from a recent draw on that arm; what is the best site decision?

Choose an alternate site/arm and avoid the bruised area because anticoagulated patients bruise/bleed more easily, and the trap is using the bruised site for convenience and causing hematoma.
Question 3413

A patient scheduled for an EKG has a hairy chest and electrodes won’t adhere; what is the best action?

Clip (not shave) excess hair where electrodes will be placed and clean/dry the skin because poor adhesion causes artifact, and the trap is placing electrodes over hair and accepting a poor tracing.
Question 3414

During venipuncture, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, apply pressure, and assist the patient to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if possible) because preventing syncope-related injury is the priority (trap: trying to “finish the last tube” first).
Question 3415

An EKG printout has heavy jagged artifact and you notice a lead wire is loosely connected; what should the CMA do next?

Reconnect/secure the lead and ensure good skin contact, then repeat the EKG because loose leads create artifact and can mimic dysrhythmias; the trap is sending the unreadable strip to the provider.
Question 3416

While preparing an outpatient EKG, the patient asks if the test can be done through their shirt for privacy; what should the CMA do?

Explain that electrodes must be placed on clean, dry skin on the chest and provide draping to maintain privacy because correct placement is required for accuracy, and the trap is compromising lead contact to avoid an awkward moment.
Question 3417

While drawing a light blue tube for PT/INR, the tube only fills halfway before flow stops; what should the CMA do next?

Discard that underfilled light blue tube and recollect with a full draw because incorrect blood-to-anticoagulant ratio makes results unreliable (trap: “topping off” from another tube is unacceptable).
Question 3418

A fasting lipid panel is ordered, and the patient says they ate breakfast an hour ago; what should the CMA do first?

Pause the draw and notify the provider or follow clinic protocol to reschedule because eating can invalidate the test (trap: drawing anyway and “noting it” still risks wrong clinical decisions).
Question 3419

A provider glances at a newly completed EKG and asks the CMA, “Does this show an MI?” and the patient is listening; what is the best response?

State you can’t interpret or diagnose, promptly give the tracing to the provider, and focus on patient reassurance because interpretation is outside CMA scope, and the trap is offering an opinion that could mislead or alarm the patient.
Question 3420

A CMA is collecting a CBC and a basic metabolic panel and has light blue, red, and lavender tubes on the tray; what is the best order of draw?

Draw light blue first, then red, then lavender because citrate tubes must be filled before EDTA tubes to prevent additive carryover (common trap: drawing lavender before light blue can falsely alter coag results).
Question 3421

A throat swab rapid strep test is being performed, but the patient gagged and the swab touched the tongue; what is the best next step?

Discard the swab and recollect properly from tonsillar pillars/posterior pharynx because saliva/tongue contact can cause false results (trap: running the test anyway to avoid repeating discomfort).
Question 3422

While preparing to draw blood, the CMA realizes the labels were printed for a different patient with a similar name; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and re-verify patient identity with two identifiers and print correct labels because mislabeling is a critical lab error (trap: crossing out the wrong name or relabeling after the draw without verification).
Question 3423

A patient’s arm is very cold and veins are hard to palpate; what is the best action before attempting venipuncture?

Warm the site (e.g., warm compress) and reassess because heat increases blood flow and vein dilation (trap: applying excessive tourniquet time, which increases hemoconcentration and discomfort).
Question 3424

A serum chemistry sample has visible hemolysis noted by the lab; which collection mistake is most likely and what should the CMA do next time?

Avoid forceful syringe transfer/shaking and allow antiseptic to dry because these can hemolyze blood; recollect using correct technique since hemolysis can falsely elevate analytes (trap: thinking hemolysis “doesn’t matter” for chemistry).
Question 3425

After venipuncture, a hematoma begins forming at the site while the tube is still filling; what should the CMA do immediately?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure because continued suction worsens bleeding into tissue (trap: keeping the needle in and “readjusting” to save the draw).
Question 3426

The provider orders a blood culture and a CMP; which should the CMA collect first and why?

Collect blood cultures first using sterile technique because they must be uncontaminated and drawn before other additives (trap: drawing chemistry tubes first increases contamination risk and invalid results).
Question 3427

After drawing a lavender tube, the CMA sets it on the counter without mixing and continues with paperwork; what should the CMA do when realizing this within a minute?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times immediately because EDTA specimens can clot if not mixed, leading to rejected CBCs (trap: shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze and alter results).
Question 3428

A urine specimen for culture is ordered, and the patient hands the CMA a cup filled from the collection hat after voiding; what should the CMA do next?

Explain the need for a clean-catch midstream specimen and have the patient recollect because initial-stream urine and nonsterile collection can contaminate culture (trap: accepting any urine because “it’s enough volume”).
Question 3429

During a 12-lead EKG, the right and left arm leads were accidentally reversed; what is the best next step once discovered?

Correct the lead placement and repeat the EKG because limb lead reversal can mimic ischemia or axis changes (trap: sending it “with a note,” risking misinterpretation and wrong treatment).
Question 3430

A provider asks the CMA to “tell me if this EKG is a STEMI”; what is the most appropriate response?

State that interpretation is outside the CMA scope and immediately alert the provider of any urgent symptoms/abnormal tracing concerns while ensuring the EKG is promptly delivered because CMAs obtain and relay tests, not diagnose (trap: offering an interpretation that could delay care).
Question 3431

A CMA performed a rapid influenza POCT but read the result at 25 minutes instead of the specified time window; what is the best action?

Repeat the test with a new kit and read within the manufacturer’s time frame because late readings can cause false results (trap: documenting the late-read result as definitive).
Question 3432

A specimen is collected for a send-out test, but the requisition is missing the ordering provider and collection time; what should the CMA do before sending it?

Hold the specimen and obtain the missing required information, then complete documentation because incomplete requisitions can cause rejection or misattribution (trap: sending it anyway and hoping the lab “figures it out”).
Question 3433

An outpatient EKG printout shows heavy baseline wandering, and the patient is talking and shifting; what should the CMA do first?

Ask the patient to lie still and relax, then repeat the EKG because motion artifact commonly causes wandering baseline (trap: immediately adjusting machine settings or interpreting it as a rhythm change).
Question 3434

Question 3435

Do not test patients, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (e.g., repeat QC with new strip/control, check expiration), and notify the supervisor if unresolved because patient results are unreliable without passing QC (trap: running patient tests “just this once”).
Question 3436

During a routine venipuncture for a fasting lipid panel and CBC, the CMA fills the lavender tube first and then the light green tube; what should the CMA do next to best protect specimen accuracy?

Stop and recollect using the correct order of draw (light green heparin before lavender EDTA) and label/transport properly, because drawing EDTA first can carry over anticoagulant and distort chemistry results—a common mistake is sending the specimens anyway and assuming mixing will fix it.
Question 3437

A patient becomes pale and says they feel “hot and dizzy” right after the blood draw while seated on the exam table; what should you do first?

Assist the patient to a safe position (lying down or head lowered), stay with them, and call for help per clinic protocol because this is likely vasovagal syncope and fall prevention is the priority, a common trap is leaving to “get water” first.
Question 3438

Apply firm direct pressure and keep the arm straight/elevated because this suggests a hematoma and prompt pressure limits bleeding, a common mistake is bending the elbow which worsens bruising.
Question 3439

You collected a lavender-top tube for a CBC but forgot to invert it and it sat upright for several minutes; what is the best next action before sending it to the lab?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times immediately and check for clots because EDTA tubes must be mixed to prevent clotting and redraws, a common trap is shaking vigorously which hemolyzes the sample.
Question 3440

While drawing a BMP, you notice the patient repeatedly pumping their fist to “help the vein”; what should you do next?

Ask the patient to relax the hand and avoid fist pumping because it can alter analytes (e.g., potassium) and contributes to hemolysis, a common trap is allowing vigorous pumping to continue to keep the vein prominent.
Question 3441

After applying the tourniquet and locating a vein, a phone call distracts you and the tourniquet remains on for over a minute; what is the best action before proceeding?

Release the tourniquet, allow a brief rest, then reapply right before the venipuncture because prolonged tourniquet time can falsely concentrate results and increase hemolysis risk, a common trap is continuing “to avoid losing the vein.”
Question 3442

During a morning draw you need a CBC, BMP, PT/INR, and a fasting glucose from the same patient; in what order should the tubes be filled to prevent additive carryover?

Draw light blue (citrate) first, then serum (red/gold), then green (heparin), then lavender (EDTA), then gray (fluoride) because correct order of draw prevents anticoagulants (especially EDTA) from contaminating chemistry/coag results, a common trap when grabbing lavender early.
Question 3443

Collect the blood cultures first using aseptic technique before other tubes because contamination risk is highest and additive carryover from other tubes can affect culture integrity, a common mistake when “saving cultures for last.”

Question 3496

While drawing blood, the CMA sees the tube is only half-filled for a sodium citrate (light blue) specimen but the draw otherwise went smoothly; what is the best action?

Redraw the light blue tube to the required fill line because the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio must be exact; a common mistake is sending an underfilled coag tube that yields inaccurate clotting times.
Question 3497

A throat swab for rapid influenza POCT was collected, but the test requires a nasopharyngeal swab per manufacturer instructions; what should the CMA do?

Do not run the test and recollect using the correct specimen type because improper collection can cause false negatives and invalid results, the trap is running it anyway “since it’s the same area.”
Question 3498

A patient with a pacemaker comes for an EKG; the CMA is unsure if pacer spikes will appear—what should the CMA do BEST?

Perform the EKG as ordered and document the presence of a pacemaker (and any symptoms) because CMAs obtain/record but do not interpret, the trap is delaying the test to “confirm interpretation.”
Question 3499

A provider orders PT/INR, CMP, and CBC, and the CMA has light blue, gold, and lavender tubes ready; in what order should the CMA draw them?

Draw light blue first, then gold, then lavender; incorrect order can cause additive carryover that alters coagulation or hematology results.
Question 3500

During venipuncture for a CBC and BMP, the CMA accidentally draws the lavender (EDTA) tube before the green heparin tube and is about to label and send both; what is the best next action?

Stop and redraw the BMP using a new collection set and correct order of draw because EDTA carryover can falsely elevate potassium and alter chemistry results; a common mistake is sending the specimen anyway since it “filled correctly,” which risks inaccurate lab values.
