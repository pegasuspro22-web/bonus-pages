Question 3001

In the lobby, an adult collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first?

Verify unresponsiveness, call for help/activate EMS and get the AED, and begin CPR if no normal breathing/pulse per BLS training because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is checking vitals for a prolonged time before starting CPR.
Question 3002

During intake, a 58-year-old reports chest pressure and looks pale; you obtain vitals and the BP is 86/50 with a weak pulse—what should you do first?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (notify provider/call 911 per protocol) and place the patient supine; hypotension with chest pressure is unstable, and the trap is finishing the intake or repeating vitals instead of escalating immediately.
Question 3003

After a patient stands up from the exam table, they become dizzy and start to sway—what should you do first?

Assist them back to a seated or supine position and assess for immediate safety (call for help if needed); preventing a fall is the priority, and the trap is continuing intake questions while the patient is unstable.
Question 3004

During wound care teaching, a patient says they will reuse the same gauze at home to save money; what is the CMA’s best response?

Instruct them to use clean, single-use dressings and explain that reusing gauze increases infection risk, and offer to involve the care team for supply options; the trap is ignoring the statement because it’s “their choice.”
Question 3005

While removing gloves after a wound dressing change, your bare finger touches the contaminated glove exterior—what should you do next?

Perform hand hygiene immediately and then continue with clean tasks; glove removal errors contaminate hands, and the trap is assuming gloves “protected you” and skipping hand hygiene.
Question 3006

You are about to room a patient with a productive cough and fever who is not wearing a mask—what should you do first?

Provide a mask and perform hand hygiene before escorting them, using standard precautions; source control reduces transmission, and the trap is bringing them through the clinic unmasked or relying only on your own PPE.
Question 3007

A patient asks you to “just recap the needle for safety” after you give an injection because they’re nervous seeing it—what is the best response?

Do not recap; engage the safety device and dispose immediately in sharps; recapping is a common needle-stick cause, and the trap is trying to reassure the patient by doing an unsafe practice.
Question 3008

A patient needs contact precautions and you’re wearing gloves and a gown; your goggles are fogging and you want to adjust them—what should you do?

Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene, adjust goggles, then don new gloves; touching your face/eye protection with contaminated gloves spreads pathogens, and the trap is adjusting PPE with dirty gloves.
Question 3009

You’re about to administer an IM medication and the patient says, “I’m not sure what this is for”—what is the best next step within CMA scope?

Pause and verify the patient’s understanding by reviewing the medication name/purpose as directed and involve the provider for questions beyond your scope; informed participation improves safety, and the trap is giving it quickly to stay on schedule.
Question 3010

Stop and verify the medication against the order (right medication/right dose) with the vial and provider/pharmacy before administering; look-alike/sound-alike errors are high risk, and the trap is assuming it’s “close enough.”
Question 3011

Replace/secure a new sharps container before giving the injection; overfilled containers increase needle-stick risk, and the trap is “making it fit” or carrying the used needle elsewhere to dispose of later.
Question 3012

Question 3013

Question 3100

During intake, an adult patient reports dizziness when standing and you obtain BP 86/54, HR 118, RR 22; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient sit or lie down and recheck vitals while alerting the provider immediately, because these are possible shock/orthostatic findings and the trap is letting the patient walk back to the lobby or continuing the intake.
