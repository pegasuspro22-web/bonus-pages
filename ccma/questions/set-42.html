Question 4101

You overhear two staff discussing a celebrity patient’s diagnosis in the hallway where other patients can hear; what should you do FIRST?

Stop the conversation and move it to a private area, then remind/notify appropriate leadership because “they didn’t use the name” is not a safe excuse if the patient is identifiable.
Question 4102

You accidentally send a visit summary to the wrong fax number and realize it contained diagnoses and medications; what should you do FIRST?

Immediately notify your supervisor/privacy officer and follow the clinic’s breach/mitigation process (attempt retrieval, document) because quietly “fixing it later” delays required reporting and increases harm.
Question 4103

A patient arrives for an intrauterine device placement and says, “I don’t want this anymore,” but the consent form is already signed from check-in; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Stop the process and alert the provider because consent can be withdrawn at any time, and proceeding because “the form is signed” is the trap.
Question 4104

During a busy clinic day, a provider asks you to “just sign for me” on an electronic note to save time; what is the BEST response?

Refuse and escalate to the supervisor/provider that you cannot authenticate documentation for someone else because signing for another person is fraud even if the content is correct.
Question 4105

While rooming a patient, the CMA hears another patient at the front desk speaking loudly about “my positive STI test” and sees a visitor standing nearby; what should the CMA do FIRST to stay HIPAA-compliant?

Politely intervene by moving the conversation to a private area and lowering the voice (or asking front desk to do so) because protected health information must be discussed only where it cannot be overheard, and the common mistake is continuing the discussion in public because “they brought it up.”
Question 4106

A patient is checking out and asks you to email their imaging report to their work email for convenience; what should you do BEST?

Use only the clinic’s approved secure patient portal or encrypted process and explain standard options because regular email is a common HIPAA pitfall even with patient request.
Question 4107

A patient asks, “Can you tell my employer why I was here today?” and hands you a blank employer form; what should you do NEXT?

Have the patient sign an appropriate release and route the form to the provider for medical content because CMAs should not disclose PHI without authorization or independently interpret/justify medical details.
Question 4108

A caller says, “I’m the patient’s daughter—tell me what the biopsy showed,” and you see no release on file; what should you do NEXT?

Explain you can’t disclose without documented authorization and offer to speak directly with the patient or have the patient complete a release because confirming details “just to help” is still a privacy breach.
Question 4109

While documenting vitals, you realize you entered the blood pressure into the temperature field and already signed the intake note; what should you do BEST?

Add a corrected addendum/late entry per EHR policy referencing the error and entering the correct data because overwriting the wrong value without an audit trail is the common documentation trap.
Question 4110

A claim for an office visit is denied for “invalid subscriber ID,” and the patient is upset; what should you do NEXT as the CMA at the desk?

Re-verify demographics/insurance details with the patient and route correction to billing for resubmission because debating medical necessity won’t fix an eligibility/ID denial.
Question 4111

You receive an EHR task: “Please call patient with normal results,” but the note does not specify which test or which date; what should you do NEXT?

Clarify with the ordering provider or review the specific result and documented provider instructions before calling because giving vague “everything’s fine” without verifying can misinform the patient.
Question 4112

During check-in, a patient loudly asks why her coworker “was here yesterday for STD testing,” and the CMA can see that coworker’s chart open in the EHR from the prior visit; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Close the coworker’s chart and state that you can’t discuss any patient’s visits or results, then redirect to the current patient’s check-in needs because accessing/disclosing another patient’s information violates HIPAA even if the person is known to the patient (common trap: “confirming” or explaining to be helpful).
Question 4113

The provider adds a procedure to today’s visit after the patient already checked out; what is the BEST administrative step to prevent billing errors?

Immediately update the encounter/documentation and notify billing to capture the added service because missing charges are a common trap when the workflow ends too early.
Question 4114

A provider verbally tells you to “document the patient refused the vaccine” even though you watched the vaccine being given; what is the BEST action?

Document what actually occurred and immediately clarify the discrepancy with the provider because charting something untrue—“to match the provider”—creates legal risk and patient safety issues.
Question 4115

At the front desk, a patient wants to update their address but you’re not sure if it’s the patient or a look-alike; what should you do FIRST?

Verify identity using two identifiers before changing demographics because incorrect demographic updates can misroute results/bills and cause HIPAA disclosures.
Question 4116

A patient wants the earliest appointment and asks you to “just book it,” but the slot is labeled for annual physicals and the patient is coming for acute abdominal pain; what should you do BEST?

Triage scheduling by using the correct visit type and escalate urgent symptoms per protocol (offer same-day sick visit/urgent care guidance) because forcing mismatched slots can delay care and disrupt billing/coding.
Question 4117

You realize you accidentally entered a blood pressure under the wrong patient with a similar name in the EHR; what should you do first?

Immediately notify your supervisor/provider and follow the EHR correction process to remove/annotate the wrong-patient entry, because leaving it uncorrected risks treatment errors and “just re-entering it correctly” doesn’t fix the misfiled data.
Question 4118

A provider tells you, “Just copy my last progress note forward and I’ll tweak it later,” but the patient’s ROS has changed; what is the best CMA action?

Document only what was obtained today and flag the provider, because copy-forward can propagate inaccurate information and a common trap is charting items you didn’t actually assess.
Question 4119

At checkout, a patient asks for a printed after-visit summary, but the printer is at the front desk where other patients can see the pages coming out; what should the CMA do BEST to stay HIPAA-compliant?

Hand the summary directly to the patient in a way that prevents others from viewing it (e.g., retrieve it immediately and use a cover sheet or move to a more private spot), because protected health information must not be left visible or unattended—a common mistake is letting documents sit in a shared printer tray “just for a minute.”
Question 4120

A patient asks for a printed copy of their full record at checkout and says they’re in a hurry; what should you do next?

Provide the proper release-of-information process (verify identity and route the request to the designated records/ROI workflow), because handing over records on the spot without verification/documentation is a common privacy breach.
Question 4121

A coworker asks you to share your EHR login “just for a minute” because theirs is locked; what should you do?

Refuse and direct them to IT/supervisor to regain access, because sharing credentials violates HIPAA/security and the trap is thinking short-term convenience is acceptable.
Question 4122

In the waiting room, a patient loudly asks, “Why did my STD test come back positive?” and other patients can hear; what is the best response?

Move the conversation to a private area and speak quietly with minimum necessary information, because discussing results in public violates HIPAA and the trap is continuing the conversation to be “helpful.”
Question 4123

On the phone, a patient starts describing chest pressure and says they “don’t want to bother anyone,” asking for a refill instead; what should you do FIRST?

Stop the refill discussion and instruct them to seek emergency care immediately (activate clinic emergency protocol) because treating it as a routine call is a dangerous communication trap.
Question 4124

A patient with limited English nods “yes” to instructions for a prep and signs the form, but you suspect they don’t understand; what should you do NEXT?

Use a qualified medical interpreter (not a family member) and re-teach in their language before proceeding because a signed form without comprehension is not valid informed consent.
Question 4125

A patient’s spouse calls demanding lab results and says, “I’m on the account and I pay the bills”; what should you do?

Verify whether the spouse is authorized per documented release and, if not, decline and offer to speak with the patient directly, because financial responsibility does not equal permission to disclose PHI.
Question 4126

A teen arrives alone for a visit and wants the parent kept out of the room; what should you do first as the CMA?

Follow clinic policy and state law by alerting the provider and confirming consent/privacy rules before sharing any information, because assuming either full confidentiality or full parental access is a common legal trap.
Question 4127

A patient is scheduled for a minor in-office procedure and says, “I already signed something last year—can we skip it?”; what should you do next?

Ensure the current procedure-specific informed consent is completed before proceeding and notify the provider if it is missing, because consent must match the procedure and time, and the trap is relying on an old or generic signature.
Question 4128

At check-in, the patient’s name and date of birth in the scheduling system don’t match the insurance card, but the patient says, “It’s fine—just use whatever is in the computer”; what should you do first?

Stop and verify/correct demographics and insurance information before the visit is processed, because mismatches lead to claim denials and misfiled records, and the trap is proceeding to keep flow moving.
Question 4129

During a phone call, a patient says, “My insurance denied my claim—can you change the diagnosis code so they’ll pay?”; what should you do?

Decline and refer the issue to billing/provider for legitimate review, because changing codes to obtain payment is fraud and the trap is “helping” by altering documentation.
Question 4130

A patient calls saying they were in a car accident and want you to email their chart to their attorney today; what should you do?

Explain that records release requires a signed authorization and route to the ROI process (or legal department) rather than emailing PHI, because unsecured email and rushed disclosures are common HIPAA pitfalls.
Question 4131

During check-in, a patient’s spouse asks to see today’s lab results “because I handle all the bills,” and the patient is in the restroom and hasn’t given any written permission; what should the CMA do next?

Politely decline to disclose results and explain that protected health information can only be shared with the patient or an authorized person, then offer to discuss once the patient returns and confirms permission—common trap is sharing “just labs” because a spouse is present or pays bills, which still violates HIPAA.
Question 4132

A patient states they plan to harm their ex-partner and provides details; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Notify the provider/supervisor immediately and follow the clinic’s duty-to-warn/duty-to-protect protocol, because safety overrides routine confidentiality and the trap is treating it as ordinary venting.
Question 4133

While documenting intake, you realize you forgot to ask one required screening question and the patient has already left; what should you do?

Document what was actually obtained and notify the provider/perform follow-up per protocol rather than guessing, because backfilling answers you didn’t ask is falsification and creates legal risk.
Question 4134

After vaccine counseling, a parent says they “don’t do shots” but is willing to read; what is the CMA’s best action within scope?

Provide the VIS and use plain-language teach-back about benefits/side effects and what to monitor, because informed decisions require understanding and the trap is arguing rather than educating and documenting.
Question 4135

During discharge teaching for a colon cancer screening FIT kit, the patient nods but keeps asking “So I just mail the tube back?”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Ask the patient to explain the steps in their own words and correct gaps, because teach-back detects confusion and the trap is repeating instructions louder instead of verifying comprehension.
Question 4136

A patient arrives 25 minutes late for a 30-minute appointment and insists, “I only need a quick refill,” while the provider’s schedule is full; what is the best CMA action?

Follow scheduling policy by checking with the provider/front office to determine reschedule vs modified visit rather than promising they’ll be seen, because overcommitting creates safety and workflow problems.
Question 4137

A patient with new metformin starts says, “I’ll stop it when my glucose is normal,” and reaches for the door after the visit; what should the CMA do first?

Use teach-back to confirm the patient understands how to take it and when to call the clinic, because misunderstanding chronic meds is common and the trap is letting the patient leave with unchecked assumptions.
Question 4138

The provider asks you to “add a quick note” under their name in the EHR since they are running behind; what should you do?

Document only under your own credentials and within CMA scope, because entering documentation as the provider is misrepresentation and the trap is thinking it’s acceptable if the provider requested it.
Question 4139

A patient leaves a voicemail with their full name and says they’re changing pharmacies; what is the best way to document and route this message?

Enter a dated/time-stamped telephone encounter in the EHR with the patient’s stated information and route it to the provider/pharmacy team per workflow, because undocumented verbal messages are a common source of medication errors.
Question 4140

A patient with asthma is given a new inhaler and says, “I’ll figure it out at home”; what should the CMA do next?

Have the patient demonstrate inhaler technique (return demonstration) and correct errors, because technique drives effectiveness and the trap is assuming understanding based on verbal agreement.
Question 4141

A patient with low health literacy is instructed to take an antibiotic “twice daily” and responds, “So, two pills at breakfast?”; what should the CMA do?

Clarify dosing schedule with teach-back using time-based examples (e.g., morning and evening) and verify tablet count per dose, because “twice daily” is misinterpreted and the trap is not correcting a dangerous dosing error.
Question 4142

A patient refuses a referral to cardiology due to cost concerns and asks, “Can’t you just skip it?”; what should the CMA do next?

Acknowledge the barrier and connect them to financial assistance/community resources and notify the provider of refusal/barrier, because coordination supports adherence and the trap is pressuring the patient or silently dropping the referral.
Question 4143

At checkout, a patient says they didn’t understand the provider’s plan and asks the CMA, “Do I really need the follow-up in 2 weeks if I feel better?”; what is the best response?

Reinforce the prescribed follow-up timing using teach-back and offer to clarify questions with the provider, because follow-up is part of the plan and the trap is giving personal advice or changing the interval.
Question 4144

Coordinate scheduling using the patient’s constraints and offer community/clinic resources (ride assistance, later appointments) while documenting barriers, because addressing access improves completion and the trap is booking any slot and labeling the patient “noncompliant.”
Question 4145

Question 4146

Question 4150

Question 4152

Use teach-back to set realistic, provider-aligned goals (balanced changes, follow-up plan) and offer nutrition/community resources if available, because extreme plans reduce adherence and the trap is leaving misconceptions uncorrected because they sound “motivated.”
Question 4153

A patient reports a prior fainting episode after vaccination but is here for another vaccine today; what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient seated/lying down and ensure observation procedures are followed, because syncope risk is preventable and the trap is vaccinating while the patient stands at the counter.
Question 4154

The provider orders an IM injection, and the CMA finds two vials with similar labels in the med drawer; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Stop and perform the medication “rights” check against the order (drug, dose, route, time, patient, expiration) and clarify any mismatch before drawing up, because look-alike meds cause errors and the trap is assuming the vial is correct due to location.
Question 4155

Follow HIPAA by not leaving detailed results on voicemail unless authorized and instead use approved identity-verified methods (portal, call-back), because voicemail can be accessed by others and the trap is relying on patient permission without documented authorization.
Question 4156

Question 4157

Question 4160

Question 4197

A patient is unsure whether to stop blood thinners before a scheduled outpatient procedure and asks you at check-out; what is the CMA’s BEST action?

Escalate to the provider (or follow written protocol) and avoid giving medication-holding advice yourself because advising changes to prescribed meds is outside CMA scope and high-risk.
Question 4198

A patient with limited English agrees to colon cancer screening instructions while a family member “translates”; what should the CMA do FIRST to prevent a consent/understanding error?

Use a qualified medical interpreter and then use teach-back because family translation can omit or alter instructions and risks inaccurate understanding.
Question 4199

You call a patient with test results and a person answering says, “I’m her spouse—just tell me”; what should the CMA do BEST?

Verify approved release/authorization and patient identity before sharing any results because disclosing without permission is a common HIPAA violation trap.
Question 4200

The provider asks you to “make sure she follows up,” and the patient has uncontrolled diabetes but no next visit scheduled; what should the CMA do BEST before the patient leaves?

Schedule the follow-up appointment (or set a documented recall/task if scheduling isn’t possible) because relying on the patient to remember later is a common cause of missed follow-ups.
