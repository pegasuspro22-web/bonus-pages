Question 4301

A patient asks, “Can you tell my spouse my pregnancy test result?” while the spouse waits at the front desk; what should the CMA do?

Do not disclose and instead obtain the patient’s explicit permission per HIPAA (or have the patient share directly) because sharing results with family without authorization is a common legal trap.
Question 4302

During discharge teaching after a minor procedure, the patient repeats instructions incorrectly about when to remove the bandage; what should the CMA do next?

Re-teach in simpler steps and re-check with teach-back because handing the printed instructions only (without confirming understanding) is a common trap that causes at-home care errors.
Question 4303

A patient is due for a colon cancer screening and says, “I feel fine, so I don’t need it,” during an annual visit; what should the CMA do next?

Provide brief preventive screening education and use teach-back to confirm understanding of asymptomatic screening purpose because waiting for symptoms is a common trap that delays detection.
Question 4304

A patient is referred to physical therapy but says, “I can’t afford it and I don’t have transportation”; what is the CMA’s best action within scope?

Notify the provider and connect the patient with clinic-approved community resources/financial assistance or care coordination options because ignoring barriers and simply repeating the referral is a common trap that leads to noncompliance.
Question 4305

While rooming, you notice the patient’s preventive screening history shows a Pap test overdue by 2 years; the visit is for an ankle sprain; what should you do?

Flag it for the provider and/or initiate the clinic’s preventive-care prompt (offer scheduling/education if appropriate) because opportunistic reminders improve preventive care—the trap is ignoring it because it’s “not today’s complaint.”
Question 4396

A patient repeatedly no-shows for follow-up and tells you they have no reliable transportation; what should the CMA do next?

Document the barrier and connect the patient with transportation/community resources or care coordination per clinic protocol because addressing social barriers supports continuity—the trap is labeling the patient “noncompliant” without exploring the obstacle.
Question 4397

A patient’s abnormal test result requires repeat testing in 2 weeks; the provider wants to ensure it isn’t missed; what should the CMA do to support closing the loop?

Schedule the repeat test before the patient leaves (or create a timed recall/task with documented responsibility) and provide written date/time and instructions because proactive scheduling reduces missed follow-up—the trap is relying on the patient to remember to call back.
Question 4398

A patient checks out after an annual exam and says, “I’ll call later to schedule my mammogram,” while the provider note says it’s due now; what should the CMA do next?

Schedule the mammogram (or initiate the scheduling workflow) before the patient leaves and document the plan, because closing the loop prevents missed preventive care; the trap is assuming the patient will “call later” and the screening will happen.
Question 4399

A patient returns upset, saying, “I took my medicine wrong because the instructions were confusing,” and you see they were given only verbal directions at the last visit; what is the best CMA action now?

Alert the provider about the potential medication error and provide the provider-approved written instructions with teach-back to confirm correct use because safety and documentation require escalation—the trap is quietly correcting it without notifying the provider or documenting the issue.
Question 4400

A patient with limited English agrees to a new inhaler technique teaching, and their teen child offers to interpret; what should the CMA do next?

Request a qualified medical interpreter and then teach using the interpreter, because accuracy and privacy are critical; the trap is relying on family members who may omit or change information.
