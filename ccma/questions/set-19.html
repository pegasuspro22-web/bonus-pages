Questions 1801–1900

Question 1801

A patient leaves a voicemail with their full name, date of birth, and a request to “call back with my biopsy results”; what should the CMA do when returning the call?

Verify the patient’s identity with at least two identifiers and ensure you’re speaking to the patient (or authorized person) before discussing any results because voicemail details don’t confirm who answers and the trap is disclosing sensitive information to whoever picks up.
Question 1802

The provider documents a level of service that seems higher than what occurred, and the CMA is asked to submit the claim; what is the best next step?

Flag the discrepancy to the billing lead/provider for clarification before submission because claims must match documentation and the trap is “billing what the provider picked” even when it conflicts with the record.
Question 1803

At checkout, a patient asks the CMA to “change the diagnosis code so insurance will pay” for a noncovered service; what should the CMA do?

Decline and explain coding must reflect the provider’s documented diagnosis and service because altering codes is fraud and the trap is thinking it’s acceptable if the patient requests it.
Question 1804

The CMA receives a faxed request for records from an outside clinic with no patient signature attached; what should the CMA do next?

Do not send records and route it to the medical records/privacy process to obtain valid authorization or confirm a permitted disclosure because most disclosures require authorization and the trap is assuming a faxed letterhead is enough.
Question 1805

A patient at check-in says they changed jobs and their insurance “should still work” but can’t provide the new card, and they want to be seen quickly for a scheduled follow-up; what should the CMA do next to prevent billing and documentation errors?

Confirm and update demographics and insurance details in the EHR and obtain the best available insurance information (payer name/member ID if known) or mark as self-pay pending verification, because creating/keeping outdated coverage or guessing policy numbers leads to claim denials and inaccurate records.
Question 1806

A patient calls angry because they received a bill for a visit their insurance “should have covered,” and they demand you change the diagnosis code so it will be paid; what should the CMA do next?

Explain you cannot alter diagnosis codes, document the call, and route the issue to the billing staff/provider to verify coding/coverage and submit a corrected claim if appropriate, because changing codes to obtain payment is fraud and the common trap is “just fixing it” to satisfy the patient.
Question 1807

A patient asks the CMA on the phone, “Should I stop my blood thinner before my dental extraction tomorrow?”; what is the best response?

Tell the patient you cannot give medication-stop instructions and promptly route the question to the provider for guidance because medication changes require provider direction and the trap is offering personal advice to be helpful.
Question 1808

A patient repeatedly no-shows and then calls demanding an urgent same-day slot, stating, “Just squeeze me in anywhere”; what is the best scheduling action?

Follow the clinic’s scheduling protocol (triage urgency to clinical staff if needed, then book appropriately) rather than overriding rules because fairness and patient safety require consistent processes and the trap is giving clinical priority based solely on insistence.
Question 1809

A teen arrives for a sports physical and the parent demands to read the visit note at checkout; the teen asks staff not to share details; what should the CMA do next?

Pause disclosure and refer to the provider/office privacy process to determine minor consent/privacy rights for that service before releasing records, because HIPAA and state minor-consent rules may limit parental access and the trap is releasing by default.
Question 1810

During rooming, a patient quietly states the provider has been “sending inappropriate texts” and asks you not to tell anyone; what should the CMA do first?

Report the concern immediately to the supervisor/manager per clinic policy (and document the report objectively), because this is a potential safety and legal issue and the trap is promising confidentiality or trying to investigate personally.
Question 1811

A patient no-shows for a follow-up appointment for abnormal lab results, and the provider wants proof the office attempted contact; what documentation is most appropriate?

Document each outreach attempt with date/time, method, number/email used, message left or not, and any patient response, because this supports continuity of care and the trap is charting a vague note like “patient notified” without details.
Question 1812

At check-in, a patient asks you to “tell my boss I’m in the office today for a medical issue” and provides the boss’s phone number while the employer is waiting on the line; what should the CMA do NEXT to stay HIPAA-compliant and still be helpful?

Ask the patient to complete a written authorization (or have the patient personally make the call) before confirming any visit details because disclosure to an employer without documented permission is not allowed even if the patient verbally requests it, and the common trap is assuming implied consent covers non-care-related third parties.
Question 1813

A patient is upset about a long wait and begins yelling at the front desk while other patients watch; what should the CMA do first?

Use calm, professional communication and move the conversation to a more private area if possible because de-escalation and privacy reduce conflict and the trap is arguing publicly or discussing details where others can hear.
Question 1814

A patient’s spouse at the front desk asks for the lab results “because I’m on the account,” and the patient is not present; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the spouse’s identity and confirm documented authorization (or a signed release) before disclosing anything, because HIPAA requires permission to share PHI and a common trap is assuming marital status or “being on the account” equals access.
Question 1815

A provider verbally orders the CMA to “add that the patient denies chest pain” even though the CMA did not ask and the patient did not say it; what is the best response?

Do not document information you did not obtain; clarify with the provider and document only what was actually stated/observed (or have the provider enter their own statement), because falsifying documentation is a legal/ethical violation and the trap is “helping” the chart look complete.
Question 1816

Authenticate the caller using required identifiers (and call-back procedures if needed) before discussing any PHI, because phone requests are high-risk for misidentification and the trap is sharing details based on voice recognition.
Question 1817

After inhaler technique teaching, the patient says, “I’ll just spray it in my mouth like perfume”; what should the CMA do next?

Re-demonstrate correct technique and have the patient return-demonstrate because skills require practice; the trap is giving only verbal instructions without confirming technique.
Question 1818

Question 1819

Question 1820

Question 1821

Question 1822

Question 1823

Question 1824

Question 1825

Question 1895

The provider orders a referral and the patient states they have no transportation and can’t miss work; what is the CMA’s BEST next step?

Identify barriers and connect the patient to appropriate community/clinic resources (transportation assistance, flexible scheduling, telehealth options if appropriate) while documenting the plan, because addressing barriers improves follow-through and the trap is labeling the patient “noncompliant.”
Question 1896

A patient asks, “Can I cut my extended-release tablet in half to save money?” what should the CMA do?

Advise not to split extended-release medication and route the question to the provider/pharmacist for alternatives, because splitting can cause dose dumping and the trap is giving medication-change advice outside scope.
Question 1897

A patient with hypertension is given home BP log instructions and replies, “I don’t have a cuff and I can’t afford one”; what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider and connect the patient with community/clinic resources (low-cost cuffs, pharmacy checks, loaner programs) and document the barrier, because addressing access improves follow-through and the trap is repeating instructions without solving the barrier.
Question 1898

At checkout, a patient declines a recommended follow-up and says they can’t afford it; what is the CMA’s BEST next action?

Explore cost barriers and offer appropriate options (financial assistance info, sliding scale/resources, discussion with billing) and document refusal with education provided, because cost drives missed care and the trap is documenting “noncompliant” without addressing barriers.
Question 1899

After an asthma follow-up visit, the provider instructs the patient to demonstrate inhaler technique, but the patient nods and says, “I already know”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Ask the patient to show how they will use the inhaler and then correct technique as needed, because demonstration/teach-back reveals misuse and the trap is accepting verbal reassurance as proof of correct use.
Question 1900

A patient prescribed an antibiotic says, “I’ll stop once I feel better so I don’t get used to it”; what should the CMA say/do to reduce the highest-risk misunderstanding?

Use clear education with teach-back that they must take it exactly as prescribed and complete the course unless the provider stops it (and call for severe side effects), because early stopping risks treatment failure and the trap is giving vague advice like “take as needed.”
