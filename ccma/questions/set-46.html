Question 4501

A patient has a mastectomy on the left side with lymph node removal and needs blood drawn; both arms appear usable—what is the best site selection?

Use the right arm (avoid the affected side) to reduce lymphedema/infection risk; the trap is choosing the left because veins “look better” despite contraindication.
Question 4502

After drawing a lavender-top tube, the CMA notices the tube was shaken vigorously to “mix it faster”—what is the best next action?

Redraw the specimen if possible and document the reason because vigorous shaking can hemolyze and invalidate results; the common trap is sending it and blaming the lab for a rejected/abnormal sample.
Question 4503

A provider orders CBC, PT/INR, BMP, and lactate; the CMA prepares to draw and sees light blue, gold, green, and lavender tubes—what is the correct order of draw?

Draw light blue first, then gold, then green, then lavender; coagulation tubes must be collected before serum/other additives to prevent additive carryover, and a common trap is placing lavender (EDTA) before light blue which can falsely prolong coag results.
Question 4504

A fasting lipid panel is ordered, but the patient says they drank coffee with cream 1 hour ago; what should the CMA do first?

Stop and clarify with the ordering provider/office protocol before drawing because recent caloric intake can skew results, and the trap is drawing anyway “to avoid rescheduling” and producing misleading data.
Question 4505

A patient on anticoagulants is bleeding longer than expected after the draw; the site has steady oozing—what should the CMA do?

Maintain firm pressure longer and reassess before bandaging, and notify the provider if bleeding does not stop because anticoagulants increase bleeding risk; the trap is letting the patient leave with only a loose bandage.
Question 4506

A hematoma begins forming while blood is still flowing into the tube—what should the CMA do next?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure for several minutes because continued collection worsens tissue bleeding; the trap is applying a bandage quickly without sustained pressure.
Question 4507

For a timed drug-level lab, the requisition states “draw exactly at 1400,” but the patient arrives at 1415—what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider/lab and document the actual draw time before collecting because timing affects interpretation; the trap is writing 1400 on the tube to match the order.
Question 4508

Do not label or send the specimen; follow the facility’s error process and redraw with correct identifiers because mislabeled specimens are a major patient-safety event, and the trap is “fixing” labels from memory.
Question 4509

During phlebotomy, a patient becomes pale, diaphoretic, and says they feel faint—what should the CMA do first?

Remove the tourniquet and needle, activate safety, and assist the patient to a safe position (recline/supine) while calling for help as needed; the trap is trying to “finish the last tube” despite syncope risk.
Question 4510

After venipuncture, the patient reports tingling and sharp pain radiating down the arm—what is the best immediate action?

Stop the draw and remove the needle, then apply pressure and report to the provider because this may indicate nerve involvement; the trap is continuing and causing further injury.
Question 4511

A urine culture specimen is collected but sits on the counter at room temperature for an hour while the clinic gets busy—what is the best action?

Send to the lab promptly or refrigerate per protocol and document the delay because bacterial overgrowth can alter results; the trap is sending it without noting the handling issue.
Question 4512

A patient’s arm has an active IV infusion in the right forearm, and the provider wants a BMP today—what should the CMA do first?

Choose the opposite arm if available and avoid drawing above an active IV because fluids can dilute/alter results; the trap is drawing proximal to the IV because it’s convenient.
Question 4513

During EKG setup, the machine prints “LEAD OFF” and the tracing is flat in one limb lead—what should the CMA do next?

Check that the indicated electrode is attached to clean, dry skin and the lead wire is securely connected because a disconnected electrode is most common; the trap is repositioning chest leads randomly.
Question 4514

The CMA is performing a 12-lead EKG and sees a wandering baseline artifact across multiple leads while the patient talks and shivers—what should the CMA do first?

Ask the patient to remain still and warm them (e.g., blanket) before repeating because muscle tremor/movement causes artifact; the trap is troubleshooting electrodes first when patient motion is the main cause.
Question 4515

During a blood draw you collect a light blue (citrate) tube first and notice it filled only halfway; the patient is stable and you have not removed the needle yet—what should the CMA do NEXT to ensure an accurate coagulation result?

Draw a new light blue tube and fill it to the required volume before ending the venipuncture because citrate tubes must be correctly filled to maintain the 9:1 blood-to-additive ratio; a common mistake is sending an underfilled tube, which can falsely prolong PT/INR/aPTT and lead to specimen rejection.
Question 4516

Right after starting a point-of-care glucose test, the meter displays an expired strip lot code—what should the CMA do next?

Stop and repeat with unexpired strips (and correct coding if required) because expired reagents can give inaccurate results; the trap is recording the number “since it printed.”
Question 4517

After drawing a light blue tube for PT/INR, you notice it is only half full; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and recollect a properly filled blue-top tube because citrate tubes must be filled to the line to maintain the 9:1 blood-to-additive ratio; the trap is sending it anyway, which can produce invalid clotting times.
Question 4518

During daily quality control, both high and low controls for a POCT analyzer are out of range—what should the CMA do next?

Stop patient testing and troubleshoot (reagents, expiration, calibration, storage conditions) and report per protocol because out-of-range controls invalidate patient results; the trap is rerunning patient samples anyway and “fixing QC later.”
Question 4519

The provider asks the CMA to “interpret the EKG and tell me if it’s an MI,” and the CMA notes ST elevation on the printout—what is the best response?

Immediately alert the provider of the abnormal finding without diagnosing because interpretation is provider scope and ST changes can be emergent; the trap is reassuring the patient or documenting “MI” as a diagnosis.
Question 4520

During a morning draw you must collect blood cultures, a light blue citrate tube, a serum SST, a green heparin tube, and a lavender EDTA tube; what order should the CMA draw them?

Draw blood cultures first, then light blue, then SST (serum), then green, then lavender, because order of draw prevents additive carryover; the trap is drawing EDTA before serum and causing falsely high potassium/low calcium results.
Question 4521

A patient becomes pale and says, “I feel like I’m going to pass out,” midway through venipuncture; what should the CMA do first?

Remove the needle and help the patient into a safe position (recline with legs elevated if possible) because preventing a fall is the priority; the trap is trying to “finish the tube” before addressing syncope risk.
Question 4522

A CMA is collecting a CBC in a lavender tube and sees small clots after leaving it upright on the counter; what should the CMA do next?

Recollect the specimen because clotted EDTA blood invalidates a CBC, and document the recollection reason; the trap is trying to “break up” clots by shaking, which does not correct the sample.
Question 4523

Question 4524

A provider orders a “STAT potassium,” and the CMA is about to forcefully shake the green-top tube to “mix it faster”; what is the best action?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times (do not shake) because vigorous shaking can hemolyze the specimen and falsely elevate potassium; the trap is thinking more agitation improves mixing.
Question 4525

While drawing from an antecubital site, the CMA palpates a strong pulse and sees bright red, rapidly filling blood in the tube; what should the CMA do immediately?

Remove the needle, apply firm pressure, and notify the provider because an arterial puncture is possible; the trap is continuing the draw and increasing bleeding/hematoma risk.
Question 4526

Question 4527

After venipuncture, the patient has swelling and bruising forming around the site; what is the best immediate CMA action?

Question 4528

During a venipuncture, a CMA collects a light blue coagulation tube first but notices it is only half full and the vacuum has stopped; the patient is still stable and the order includes a CBC and BMP—what should the CMA do next?

Discard the underfilled light blue tube and recollect a new light blue tube to the fill line before proceeding with the other tubes because citrate tubes require a correct blood-to-additive ratio for accurate coagulation results; the common mistake is “topping off” from another tube or sending a short-draw specimen.
Question 4529

Question 4530

Question 4531

Question 4597

After a fingerstick, the CMA squeezes hard to obtain more blood for a hemoglobin POCT cartridge; what is the best correction?

Use gentle pressure only and, if needed, repeat the stick with proper technique, because excessive squeezing dilutes with interstitial fluid and can alter results (trap: “more pressure equals better sample”).
Question 4598

The CMA runs a CLIA-waived rapid strep test, but the control line fails to appear; what should the CMA do next?

Treat the result as invalid, repeat with a new test kit and proper timing, and document the QC failure, because no control means the test didn’t function (trap: reporting “negative” anyway).
Question 4599

A urine specimen for culture is collected in a clean cup but sits at room temperature for two hours waiting pickup; what should the CMA do?

Refrigerate per specimen requirements promptly or recollect if stability is exceeded, because bacterial overgrowth can invalidate culture results (trap: assuming urine is stable on the counter).
Question 4600

You have orders for PT/INR, CMP, and CBC and all are drawn from one venipuncture; what is the correct order of draw for the tubes you should use?

Draw light blue (citrate) first, then serum (red/gold), then lavender (EDTA) because this prevents additive contamination; the trap is placing EDTA before serum and causing falsely abnormal chemistries.
