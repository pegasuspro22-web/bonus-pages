Question 601

When obtaining a capillary fingerstick for glucose, you squeeze the fingertip repeatedly to get more blood; what should you do instead?

Use a gentle “milk” motion after wiping the first drop because excessive squeezing dilutes the specimen with tissue fluid and skews results; the trap is thinking more pressure equals a better sample.
Question 602

A patient on the phlebotomy chair suddenly reports tingling shooting down the arm when you insert the needle; what should you do next?

Stop the draw and remove the needle immediately, then apply pressure and report per protocol because this may indicate nerve involvement; the trap is “repositioning” and risking nerve injury.
Question 603

You cannot palpate a vein in the antecubital area and the patient suggests using the same arm with an IV line running; what should the CMA do?

Choose an alternate site/arm or seek assistance rather than drawing above an active IV because IV fluids can dilute specimens and alter results; the trap is using the IV arm “just this once” for convenience.
Question 604

During an outpatient EKG, the tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is shivering in a cold room; what should the CMA do first?

Warm/cover the patient and ask them to relax and remain still, then repeat the tracing because muscle tremor commonly causes artifact; the trap is immediately troubleshooting the machine or repositioning all leads without addressing patient factors.
Question 605

After collecting a lavender EDTA tube, the CMA sets it upright without mixing because the patient is impatient; what should be done?

Gently invert the tube the required number of times immediately because inadequate mixing can cause microclots and inaccurate CBC results; the trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the sample.
Question 606

A urine culture is ordered and the patient hands you an unlabeled cup from the restroom sink; what is the best action?

Do not accept it and have the patient provide a new properly labeled specimen after confirming identifiers because sink-side specimens risk mix-ups and contamination; the trap is labeling it afterward based on who is “probably” in the room.
Question 607

Mid-draw, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint; what should the CMA do first?

Immediately remove the tourniquet and needle, apply pressure, and assist the patient to a safe position (reclined/supine) because preventing injury from syncope is priority; the trap is trying to “finish the tube” before addressing safety.
Question 608

After venipuncture, a hematoma begins swelling at the site while blood is still flowing; what is the best next step?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure because continued bleeding into tissue worsens the hematoma; the trap is redirecting the needle or continuing the draw.
Question 609

An EKG printout shows a flatline in one lead but normal complexes in the others; what is the best next step?

Check that specific electrode/lead wire connection and skin contact, then repeat the EKG because a loose lead commonly causes a single-lead failure; the trap is reporting as asystole or a clinical emergency without verifying artifact.
Question 610

A CLIA-waived urine dipstick bottle was left uncapped overnight and a provider requests immediate testing; what is the best action?

Discard and replace the strips (and run QC if required) because exposure to air/moisture degrades reagents and invalidates results; the trap is using them to avoid delay and documenting “normal” findings.
Question 611

During venipuncture, blood stops flowing into a vacuum tube after a good initial flash and the patient’s arm is still—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Reposition the needle slightly (pull back a millimeter or adjust angle) while keeping it stable because the bevel may be against the vein wall; the trap is “digging” or fishing, which increases hematoma risk.
Question 612

A provider orders CBC, BMP, PT/INR, and a lactate; the CMA has a butterfly set and tubes on the tray—what order of draw should the CMA use?

Draw blood cultures first (if ordered), then light blue, then serum (red/gold), then green, then lavender, then gray because correct order prevents additive carryover (common trap: drawing lavender before light blue can falsely prolong coag results).
Question 613

You are preparing for a 12-lead EKG on a patient with a hairy chest and oily skin; what should you do to improve electrode adherence?

Clean and dry the skin and clip hair as needed before applying electrodes because poor contact causes artifact and repeat testing; the trap is placing electrodes over hair/oil and blaming the machine for noise.
Question 614

While performing a 12-lead EKG, the patient asks, “Does this mean I’m having a heart attack?” after seeing the monitor; how should the CMA respond?

Explain that the EKG records heart electrical activity and the provider will interpret the results, and promptly relay symptoms/concerns if present because interpretation is outside CMA scope; the trap is reassuring or diagnosing based on the tracing.
Question 615

A patient’s point-of-care hemoglobin result seems inconsistent with their prior value, but the patient feels fine; what should the CMA do next?

Repeat the test following proper technique and QC checks and report the discrepancy per protocol because POCT errors (sample/strip/device) are common; the trap is charting the unexpected value without verification or acting as if it is a diagnosis.
Question 616

Your clinic’s CLIA-waived strep test control result is out of range at the start of the day; what should the CMA do next?

Do not test patients, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (new reagents/controls, storage checks), and notify the supervisor if unresolved because reporting patient results with failed QC is unsafe; the trap is running patients anyway “since it usually works.”
Question 617

Immediately after a blood draw, a patient becomes pale and says, “I feel dizzy,” with eyes rolling upward—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, activate safety, and assist the patient to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if possible) because preventing injury is priority; the trap is trying to finish filling the tube.
Question 618

After drawing a lavender-top tube for CBC, the CMA is about to place it in the rack—what should the CMA do to the tube FIRST?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times immediately because EDTA must mix to prevent clotting; the trap is vigorous shaking, which can hemolyze the sample.
Question 619

While collecting a CMP and CBC, the CMA realizes the SST tube was filled only halfway and already inverted once—what is the BEST action?

Discard and recollect the SST to the proper fill because incorrect blood-to-additive ratio can alter results; the trap is “topping off” from another tube, which is contamination and not allowed.
Question 620

A CMA collects a throat swab for rapid strep at POCT but sets it down unlabeled because the EHR printer jammed—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Do not test it; re-collect with proper patient identification and labeling because unlabeled specimens are rejected for safety; the trap is labeling from memory after the fact.
Question 621

A provider orders a timed 2-hour postprandial glucose and the patient arrives early asking to be drawn now—what should the CMA do?

Verify the exact timing from the patient’s meal start/end and schedule the draw for the ordered time because timed tests require accuracy; the trap is drawing early “to help the schedule,” which invalidates the result.
Question 622

A patient’s requisition says fasting lipid panel, but the patient reports eating breakfast an hour ago; the waiting room is full—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Pause the draw and notify the provider or follow clinic protocol to reschedule because a non-fasting specimen can invalidate results; the trap is collecting anyway and “noting it” after, which may waste a redraw.
Question 623

A urine specimen for culture sits at room temperature on the counter for over an hour before transport—what should the CMA do?

Refrigerate promptly and follow lab policy for acceptability or recollect if required because bacterial overgrowth can alter culture results; the trap is sending it unrefrigerated and hoping the lab “fixes it.”
Question 624

A patient’s arm develops swelling and bruising during venipuncture—what is the CMA’s BEST next step?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure for at least several minutes because this is likely a hematoma; the trap is continuing to draw or immediately applying a bandage without pressure.
Question 625

A CLIA-waived rapid influenza test kit was left out past its room-temperature stability time, but controls “usually pass”—what should the CMA do?

Discard the compromised kit and use an in-date, properly stored kit because storage errors can produce false results; the trap is running the test anyway because it “might be fine.”
Question 626

The CMA completes a 12-lead EKG but sees a wandering baseline in multiple leads while the patient is shivering—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Warm and relax the patient (blanket, reduce muscle tension) and repeat the tracing because muscle artifact commonly causes baseline wander; the trap is repositioning all electrodes without addressing tremor.
Question 627

During EKG setup, the CMA notices the patient has a hairy chest and electrodes keep lifting—what is the BEST next step?

Clean and dry the skin and clip/shave small patches as needed for electrode contact because poor adhesion causes artifact; the trap is pressing harder or adding gel without improving skin prep.
Question 628

An EKG prints “leads off” and Lead II is flat, but the patient denies symptoms—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Check cable connections and electrode placement/adhesion for the affected lead and rerun the EKG because this is usually a technical issue; the trap is documenting it as an abnormal rhythm.
Question 629

While placing precordial leads, the CMA is unsure of V1 and V2 landmarks and the provider is waiting—what should the CMA do BEST?

Pause and re-identify landmarks (4th intercostal space at right and left sternal border) before recording because incorrect placement can mimic pathology; the trap is guessing to save time, creating a misleading EKG.
Question 630

While performing a fingerstick glucose, the CMA prepares to puncture the center of the fingertip for “more blood”—what is the BEST correction?

Use the lateral side of the fingertip and wipe away the first drop because it improves blood flow and reduces tissue-fluid contamination; the trap is squeezing hard, which can falsely lower results.
Question 631

A patient says they took aspirin today and “bleed easily,” and after venipuncture the site continues oozing—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Maintain direct pressure longer and reassess before bandaging because prolonged bleeding requires extended pressure; the trap is applying a small adhesive bandage quickly and sending the patient out.
Question 632

A patient with a mastectomy on the right side and an active IV on the left needs routine labs—what should the CMA do BEST?

Notify the provider and follow clinic guidance to select the safest alternative site (often avoiding the mastectomy side and not drawing above an IV) because both sides have risks; the trap is drawing from the IV arm “below the IV” without authorization.
Question 633

A urine pregnancy POCT is negative, but the patient insists she is pregnant and reports a missed period and nausea—what should the CMA do BEST within scope?

Report the result to the provider and document timing/collection details because early pregnancy or dilute urine can cause false negatives; the trap is reassuring the patient definitively or advising treatment based on the POCT alone.
Question 634

A provider orders PT/INR, CMP, and CBC; the CMA has light blue, red/gold, and lavender tubes ready—what is the correct order of draw?

Draw light blue first, then serum tube (red/gold), then lavender because coagulation tubes must be collected before tubes that can contaminate with additives; a common trap is drawing lavender before blue and skewing clotting results.
Question 635

During venipuncture for a CBC and CMP, the patient says, “I’m going to pass out,” becomes pale, and slumps in the chair—what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, and assist the patient into a safe position (recline with legs elevated) because syncope is an immediate safety risk; a common trap is trying to “finish the last tube” and causing a fall injury.
Question 636

Immediately after removing the needle, a hematoma begins to form at the venipuncture site—what should the CMA do next?

Apply firm direct pressure and elevate the arm, then document and select a different site for any redraw because continued bleeding causes tissue damage; a common trap is placing a bandage only and letting the patient leave.
Question 637

A patient with small veins has a slow blood flow with a butterfly set and the CMA switches collection tubes mid-draw—what should the CMA do to prevent inaccurate coag results when a light blue tube is among the orders?

Use a discard tube before filling the light blue tube because butterfly tubing contains air that can underfill the citrate tube; a common trap is skipping the discard and ending up with a short draw.
Question 638

During a fasting CMP and CBC draw, the CMA accidentally collects the light blue citrate tube after the lavender EDTA tube and notices a small amount of lavender additive may have backflowed into the blue tube; what is the best next step before sending specimens to the lab?

Discard and recollect the light blue tube using the correct order of draw (blood cultures, light blue, serum, green, lavender, gray) because EDTA contamination can alter coagulation results, and the common mistake is “sending it anyway” since it was only a small backflow.
Question 639

After collecting a light blue tube for PT/INR, the CMA notices the tube is only half full—what is the best next action?

Recollect the specimen with a properly filled light blue tube because an underfilled citrate tube alters the blood-to-additive ratio and invalidates results; a common trap is sending it anyway “so the lab can decide.”
Question 640

The CMA draws blood from the arm with an IV infusing normal saline because the other arm is difficult—what is the best action?

Avoid drawing above/near an active IV and use the other arm or notify the provider for guidance because IV fluid can dilute the specimen; a common trap is assuming “saline won’t affect labs.”
Question 641

While running a 12-lead EKG, the tracing shows a wavy baseline and the patient is shivering from a cold room—what should the CMA do next?

Warm the patient (blanket/adjust room) and ask them to lie still, then repeat the EKG because muscle tremor mimics artifact; a common trap is documenting it as an abnormal rhythm without troubleshooting.
Question 642

A patient arrives for an EKG and asks if the test will “tell me if I’m having a heart attack right now”—what is the most appropriate CMA response?

Explain that the EKG records the heart’s electrical activity and the provider will interpret results, because the CMA should not diagnose; a common trap is reassuring or interpreting ST changes to the patient.
Question 643

The CMA draws a CBC in a lavender tube and sets it down without mixing; 10 minutes later they remember—what is the best action?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times and inspect for clots, recollecting if clotted because EDTA tubes require immediate mixing; a common trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the sample.
Question 644

A urine culture is ordered, and the patient hands the CMA a specimen cup filled to the brim with no label—what is the best next step?

Label the specimen in the patient’s presence using two identifiers and confirm it was collected midstream clean-catch, because unlabeled/uncertain specimens must not be processed; a common trap is labeling it later from memory.
Question 645

During a CLIA-waived fingerstick glucose check, the meter displays “QC FAIL” but the patient is waiting for results so the provider can adjust the plan; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and do not report a patient result, repeat quality control with the correct control solution/expiration and technique (and troubleshoot or remove the meter from use if it still fails) because failed QC means results are unreliable—a common trap is rerunning the patient sample anyway to “save time.”
Question 646

A throat swab for rapid strep and culture is collected, but the courier pickup is delayed—how should the CMA handle the specimen?

Place the swab in the correct transport medium and store/transport per test requirements promptly because improper storage can reduce organism viability; a common trap is leaving it on the counter “until pickup.”
Question 647

The EKG printout shows “lead off” and a flat line in one lead—what is the best immediate action?

Check electrode placement and cable connections for that lead and reattach/replace the electrode because a disconnected lead produces false readings; a common trap is sending the strip as-is to the provider.
Question 648

While drawing a fasting lipid panel, the patient repeatedly opens and closes their fist to “help the blood come out”—what should the CMA do?

Ask the patient to relax the hand and avoid fist pumping because it can falsely elevate certain analytes (e.g., potassium, lactate); a common trap is coaching vigorous pumping to speed the draw.
Question 649

A CLIA-waived urine pregnancy test shows a faint second line within the allowed read time—what should the CMA do next?

Treat it as positive and follow clinic protocol for reporting/documentation because any visible test line within the time window is positive; a common trap is calling it negative because it’s faint.
Question 650

At the start of the day, QC for a CLIA-waived rapid strep test is out of range—what should the CMA do next?

Stop patient testing, troubleshoot per manufacturer instructions (new kit, controls, storage), and notify the supervisor if unresolved because out-of-range QC means patient results are unreliable; a common trap is repeating the patient test and ignoring QC failure.
Question 651

After collecting a CMP, the CMA realizes the tube was labeled with the correct patient name but the wrong date of birth—what is the best next action?

Do not alter the label; recollect and label correctly in the patient’s presence because mismatched identifiers can cause wrong-patient results; a common trap is crossing out and rewriting identifiers on the tube.
Question 652

A patient on warfarin arrives for a scheduled PT/INR draw but says they took an extra dose by mistake last night and feels dizzy—what should the CMA do first?

Perform a quick safety assessment (vitals) and immediately alert the provider before proceeding because symptoms plus anticoagulant dosing error may require urgent evaluation; a common trap is drawing the lab first and delaying escalation.
Question 653

A provider orders PT/INR, CBC, and a BMP, and you have to draw all tests from one venipuncture; what is the correct order of draw for these tubes?

Draw light blue (citrate) first, then lavender (EDTA), then green or gold depending on BMP tube type (heparin or serum) because coagulation tubes must be filled before EDTA contamination; a common trap is drawing lavender before light blue.
Question 654

During venipuncture for a fasting CMP, the patient says they feel “warm and dizzy” and their skin looks pale; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, and help the patient into a safe position (supine with legs elevated) because preventing syncope-related injury is the priority; a common trap is trying to “finish the tube” before addressing the reaction.
Question 655

The CMA runs a CLIA-waived glucose meter test and realizes the control solution has been left at an extreme temperature overnight—what is the best action?

Do not use that control solution; obtain in-date, properly stored controls and rerun QC because compromised controls invalidate patient results; a common trap is running the patient test anyway to “save time.”
Question 656

During EKG setup, the CMA accidentally switches the right arm and left arm limb leads—what is the best next step before printing the final tracing?

Correct the lead placement and repeat the EKG because misplacement can mimic ischemia or axis changes; a common trap is keeping the tracing and “noting the mistake” instead of obtaining an accurate test.
Question 657

A patient has a mastectomy on the left side and an IV running in the right arm; which site is best for routine venipuncture?

Use a hand vein (or another site per scope/policy) avoiding the mastectomy side and the IV arm because both can compromise sample integrity or patient safety; a common trap is drawing “below the IV” without considering alternatives.
Question 658

You draw a light blue tube for PT/INR but it only fills halfway; what should the CMA do?

Redraw to obtain a full, correctly filled citrate tube because underfilling alters the blood-to-additive ratio and can falsely prolong clotting results; a common trap is sending it because “some blood is better than none.”
Question 659

While collecting blood cultures, you realize you palpated the site after cleansing with chlorhexidine; what is the best next action?

Re-cleanse the site and allow it to dry before re-palpating only with sterile technique because re-contamination can cause false-positive cultures; a common trap is proceeding because “it was just a quick touch.”
Question 660

A patient is on warfarin and you are collecting a capillary sample for a CLIA-waived INR device; what is the best action before testing?

Verify the test strip lot/expiration and that QC is current/acceptable for the day because waived testing still requires quality checks to ensure accuracy; a common trap is skipping QC because it’s “point-of-care.”
Question 661

You used an SST (gold-top) tube for a lipid panel and immediately place it in the centrifuge; what is the best corrective action?

Remove it and allow it to clot for the recommended time before centrifuging because spinning too soon can cause fibrin strands and inaccurate results; a common trap is rushing to “get it done faster.”
Question 662

During a draw, a hematoma starts forming at the puncture site; what should the CMA do next?

Release the tourniquet, withdraw the needle, and apply firm pressure for several minutes because continued blood leakage can worsen the hematoma; a common trap is keeping the needle in place to “try a different angle.”
Question 663

During a routine venipuncture you successfully fill a light blue (citrate) tube but the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while you are about to collect the next tube; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, activate safety, and assist the patient into a safe position (seated with head down or supine) while monitoring and alerting the provider as needed, because preventing injury from syncope takes priority over completing the order of draw—a common mistake is trying to quickly finish the remaining tubes.
Question 664

After drawing a lavender-top tube for a CBC, the blood looks clotted when you gently tilt it; what should the CMA do next?

Redraw the specimen and properly invert the tube the required number of times because a clotted EDTA sample is unacceptable and will invalidate results; a common trap is sending it anyway “to see if the lab can run it.”
Question 665

During an office EKG, the patient suddenly reports chest pressure and looks diaphoretic; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the test and activate the clinic’s emergency response (notify provider/911 per protocol) while keeping the patient at rest because this is a possible acute cardiac event; a common trap is finishing the tracing “for more information” before escalating.
Question 666

An EKG prints with the calibration mark missing/incorrect (not the standard 10 mm/mV); what should the CMA do before handing it to the provider?

Verify and correct calibration settings and repeat the EKG because incorrect calibration can mislead interpretation; a common trap is sending it because “the rhythm is still visible.”
Question 667

While placing limb leads for an EKG, the patient’s wrists are covered in lotion and the stickers won’t adhere; what should the CMA do?

Clean and dry the skin (remove lotion) before applying leads because oil prevents adhesion and causes poor signal; a common trap is relocating leads incorrectly just to “make them stick.”
Question 668

You perform a CLIA-waived rapid strep test and the control line does not appear; what is the best next action?

Treat the test as invalid, repeat with a new device/specimen per instructions, and document the QC failure because results cannot be reported without a valid control; a common trap is reporting based on the test line alone.
Question 669

During a fingerstick, you find yourself “milking” the finger repeatedly to get enough blood; what is the best next step?

Stop excessive squeezing and obtain a new puncture at an appropriate site/depth because milking can hemolyze or dilute the sample with tissue fluid; a common trap is continuing to squeeze harder to fill the strip.
Question 670

After performing a fingerstick glucose, the first drop of blood is watery because the patient’s finger was just cleaned; what should the CMA do?

Wipe away the first drop and use the next drop for the test because residual alcohol/fluids can dilute the specimen; a common trap is using the first drop to avoid re-squeezing.
Question 671

The EKG tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is talking and moving their arms; what is the best immediate intervention?

Instruct the patient to lie still, relax, and not talk during the recording because muscle movement causes artifact; a common trap is troubleshooting the machine first when the issue is patient motion.
Question 672

You are preparing a 12-lead EKG and the patient has a hairy chest causing electrodes to lift; what should the CMA do next?

Clip/shave small areas where electrodes will be placed and clean/dry the skin because poor contact causes artifact; a common trap is pressing harder on the electrodes and accepting a noisy tracing.
Question 673

During a fasting lipid panel draw, you collect blood cultures first (per protocol) and then need a coagulation test (light blue) and a CBC (lavender); what tube should you fill next?

Fill the light blue tube next because citrate tubes must be collected before EDTA to prevent additive carryover that can falsely alter coagulation results (a common trap is grabbing lavender next because it’s “most common”).
Question 674

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint during a blood draw while seated upright in a high chair; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Remove the tourniquet and needle and assist the patient into a safe supine position (or lower the chair with legs elevated) because syncope risk and fall prevention are the priority (trap: offering water first or leaving them sitting).
Question 675

During a fasting lipid panel draw, you collect a light blue tube for PT/INR first and it fills only halfway before blood flow stops; the patient is stable and the requisition still shows PT/INR required—what should the CMA do next?

Discard the underfilled light blue tube and recollect a new properly filled light blue tube before drawing other tubes because citrate tubes require the correct blood-to-additive ratio for accurate coagulation results, and the common mistake is trying to “top off” the tube or sending a short draw that will be rejected or produce false results.
Question 676

A provider ordered PT/INR, and after drawing a light blue tube you notice it is only half full; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Redraw the specimen with a properly filled light blue tube because underfilling changes the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio and can invalidate results (trap: “topping off” from another tube or sending it anyway).
Question 677

The refrigerator temperature log for stored test reagents shows it was out of range overnight; what should the CMA do next?

Quarantine the affected reagents and notify the supervisor/lead per protocol before using them because improper storage can invalidate patient results; a common trap is using them “just this once” if they look normal.
Question 678

Immediately after venipuncture, a patient develops a rapidly swelling, painful area at the site; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop the draw and remove the needle, then apply firm pressure and an ice pack as directed because this is consistent with a hematoma and continuing the draw worsens bleeding (trap: “finish the tube” before addressing the swelling).
Question 679

A urine specimen for pregnancy testing arrives unlabeled, and the front desk says they’re “pretty sure” it belongs to the patient in room 2; what should the CMA do?

Reject the specimen and recollect with proper labeling in the patient’s presence because an unlabeled sample is a patient identification error; a common trap is labeling it afterward based on assumption to avoid re-collection.
Question 680

While drawing a basic metabolic panel, you vigorously shake the SST to “mix it faster”; later the lab reports hemolysis—what is the BEST prevention step for next time?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times rather than shaking because agitation causes hemolysis and can skew analytes like potassium (trap: thinking shaking improves mixing).
Question 681

A POCT glucometer gives an error code and the result won’t display; the patient is waiting and asks for their number; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Troubleshoot per manufacturer steps (check strip lot/expiration, insert correctly, confirm adequate sample) and repeat with a new strip, escalating if it persists because reporting no result is safer than guessing or using an errored reading (trap: estimating based on symptoms or prior readings).
Question 682

Before running a CLIA-waived rapid strep test, you notice the control log shows no documentation of today’s quality control check; what should the CMA do BEST?

Run and document required QC (or follow kit instructions on control frequency) before reporting patient results because unverified QC makes results unreliable and noncompliant (trap: running the patient test first “to save time”).
Question 683

You’re about to label a urine culture cup and the patient says, “I already wrote my last name on it—just send it”; what should the CMA do?

Label it with two identifiers (e.g., full name and DOB), date/time, and your initials in the patient’s presence because incomplete labeling risks misidentification and rejection (trap: accepting patient-only labeling).
Question 684

After printing an EKG, the device flags “possible acute MI”; what should the CMA do NEXT in an outpatient clinic?

Immediately notify the provider/activate the clinic’s emergency process while keeping the patient monitored and seated/supine because CMAs do not interpret but must escalate critical alerts promptly (trap: reassuring the patient or redoing the EKG repeatedly before notifying).
Question 685

You are setting up a 12-lead EKG and the machine prints a wavy baseline in all leads while the patient is shivering; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Warm and reposition the patient to reduce muscle tremor and ask them to remain still because shivering causes somatic artifact across leads (trap: repeatedly reprinting without correcting the cause).
Question 686

A patient asks for a printed copy of today’s visit summary, but you notice another patient’s name on the top of the first page; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the handoff immediately, secure the document, and reprint the correct summary, because releasing any PHI to the wrong person is a HIPAA breach and the trap is “just crossing out the name” and still giving it out.
Question 687

A patient hands you a sputum specimen that was collected at home “yesterday morning” and kept on the kitchen counter; what should the CMA do BEST?

Do not send it; explain it’s not acceptable due to improper storage/time and arrange recollection per instructions because delayed, unrefrigerated specimens can overgrow contaminants and invalidate culture (trap: sending it to “let the lab decide”).
Question 688

Midway through a 12-lead EKG, you notice the limb leads were placed on the wrists/ankles over lotion and one keeps loosening; what is the BEST next step?

Pause and re-prep the skin (clean/dry, lightly abrade if needed) and secure electrodes before continuing because poor contact causes artifact and misinterpretation (trap: “just hold it down” and finish).
Question 689

A patient arrives for a procedure and says they did not understand the consent form they signed at check-in; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the process and notify the provider to re-explain and re-confirm informed consent before proceeding, because consent requires understanding and the trap is trying to “explain it yourself” beyond CMA scope.
Question 690

A patient’s employer drops off a form requesting detailed visit notes for “work clearance,” and the patient isn’t present; what should the CMA do next?

Request a valid, signed authorization specifying what can be released before sending any records, because third parties need explicit permission and the trap is assuming “work clearance” automatically allows full notes.
Question 691

A patient asks you to email their immunization record to their work email “right now”; what should the CMA do?

Follow clinic-approved secure release method (patient portal, encrypted email/fax, or printed copy after ID verification) and document the release, because standard email may be insecure and the trap is sending PHI to a convenient address without safeguards.
Question 692

At check-in, a patient starts discussing their STI treatment loudly at the front desk while others are nearby; what should the CMA do next?

Move the conversation to a private area or lower voice and limit details to necessary scheduling/check-in information, because minimum necessary privacy applies and the trap is continuing in public “to be efficient.”
Question 693

A parent brings in a 16-year-old who asks to speak alone about sexual health concerns; the parent demands to stay; what is the CMA’s best action?

Politely pause and involve the provider/office manager to follow state minor-consent rules and clinic policy, because consent/privacy can differ for minors and the trap is automatically siding with the parent without checking legal requirements.
Question 694

A patient’s spouse calls asking for lab results and says, “I’m on the account and I pay the bills”; what should the CMA do?

Verify documented authorization for release first and, if not present, decline and offer to speak with the patient directly, because HIPAA requires permission and the trap is assuming relationship or payment equals access.
Question 695

A patient at check-out asks you to print “everything in my chart” and says they want it now to bring to a lawyer; the front desk is busy and the provider is still seeing patients—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Verify the patient’s identity and route the request through the standard medical records release process (have them complete a signed authorization or request form and provide the expected timeframe), because HIPAA requires controlled release and a common trap is printing the full EHR immediately without proper documentation and safeguards.
Question 696

A patient on the phone says they “moved and need their chart sent to my new doctor,” then asks you to “just email it to me” and sounds frustrated; what should the CMA do next to stay HIPAA-compliant and keep care moving?

Verify the caller’s identity and explain that records are released only with a signed authorization specifying what to send and to whom, then offer secure delivery (e.g., send directly to the new provider or provide a patient portal/pickup option); emailing records without verified identity and written authorization is a common HIPAA breach.
Question 697

At check-in, a patient asks to pick up her adult son’s lab results “because he’s at work,” and the chart shows no release of information on file; what should the CMA do next?

Decline to release the results and ask the son to authorize disclosure (e.g., written ROI or direct verification and permission) because HIPAA requires patient authorization for sharing PHI with family, and a common mistake is assuming a relative can receive results without documented consent.
Question 698

A provider gives a verbal order to “give 40 mg” but doesn’t specify the medication, and the patient is waiting; what should the CMA do?

Clarify the complete order (drug name, dose, route, frequency, indication) with the provider before acting, because incomplete orders risk medication error and the trap is guessing based on the patient’s usual meds.
Question 699

A patient schedules a physical but arrives stating it’s for an injury from a car accident and they need insurance forms completed; what is the best next step?

Verify the visit type and payer details, then reschedule/adjust appointment length and billing pathway as needed per clinic process, because MVA/third-party billing often differs and the trap is proceeding as a routine physical and misbilling.
Question 700

During documentation you notice the chief complaint field is blank, but the provider already saw the patient; what should the CMA do next?

Add the patient-stated complaint as an addendum with date/time and your initials and notify the provider if needed, because documentation must reflect what occurred and the trap is entering a new complaint that wasn’t actually reported.
