Questions 3101–3200

Question 3101

While taking vitals, an adult’s pulse feels irregular and the automated cuff shows HR 52; what should you do next?

Count the apical pulse for a full minute and report the irregular rhythm to the provider, because machines can misread irregular beats and the trap is documenting the device number without verifying.
Question 3102

A provider plans a lumbar puncture in an outpatient procedure suite; what positioning should the CMA assist with?

Help the patient into a lateral fetal position (or seated leaning forward as directed) to flex the spine, because this opens intervertebral spaces and the trap is leaving the patient flat, which makes the procedure harder and less safe.
Question 3103

A patient’s respirations increase when you announce “I’m counting your breathing”; what is the best technique to get an accurate RR?

Keep fingers on the radial pulse and silently count respirations without telling the patient, because awareness alters breathing and the trap is asking the patient to “breathe normally.”
Question 3104

You are rooming a patient for an exam and the provider requests a rectal exam; how should the CMA position the patient?

Place the patient in Sims’ position (left side-lying with right knee flexed), because it provides access and comfort and the trap is using dorsal recumbent, which is less appropriate for rectal exams.
Question 3105

You enter an exam room and see dried blood on the outside of a sharps container; what is the best action?

Don gloves and disinfect the exterior with an appropriate facility-approved disinfectant and perform hand hygiene, because the container can act as a fomite and the trap is ignoring it since it’s “just the outside.”
Question 3106

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint during standing height/weight; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a seated or supine position and assess responsiveness, because fall prevention is immediate safety and the trap is trying to “finish quickly” before helping them down.
Question 3107

After removing gloves, you plan to answer the phone at the nurses’ station; what must you do next?

Perform hand hygiene before touching the phone, because removing gloves does not eliminate contamination and the trap is assuming gloves alone prevent transmission.
Question 3108

You obtain a BP of 168/104 on a patient who is talking and has legs crossed; what should you do next?

Reposition (seated, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level) and repeat after a brief rest, because technique errors can falsely elevate BP and the trap is immediately escalating without confirming accurate measurement.
Question 3109

A coworker recaps a used needle “just for a second” before disposal; what is the CMA’s best response?

Stop the action and instruct immediate disposal into a sharps container without recapping, because recapping is a major needlestick risk and the trap is thinking careful recapping is acceptable.
Question 3110

Immediately after an injection, you realize you forgot to confirm the patient’s second identifier; what is the best next step?

Notify the provider and follow the clinic’s medication error process with documentation, because this is a patient-safety event and the trap is staying silent since “nothing happened.”
Question 3111

A patient asks you to inject a medication they brought from home in an unmarked syringe “pre-drawn by my spouse”; what should you do?

Refuse to administer it and notify the provider, because the contents, dose, and sterility cannot be verified and the trap is assuming home-prepared syringes are acceptable if the patient insists.
Question 3112

You spill a small amount of blood on the counter while setting up supplies; what should you do next?

Put on gloves and clean then disinfect with an appropriate bloodborne pathogen disinfectant using the correct contact time, because prompt decontamination breaks the chain of infection and the trap is wiping it with a dry paper towel only.
Question 3113

You are preparing a subcutaneous injection and the patient is very thin; what is the best technique adjustment?

Pinch the tissue and use a 45-degree angle (or per device guidance) to avoid IM delivery, because thin patients have less subcutaneous tissue and the trap is using 90 degrees by default.
Question 3114

You are about to irrigate a wound and the patient coughs repeatedly without a mask; what should you do first?

Provide the patient a mask (or tissues) and don appropriate PPE including eye protection before proceeding, because splash/respiratory exposure risk is high and the trap is starting the procedure “quickly” without source control.
Question 3115

You are preparing an IM vaccine and notice the vial label doesn’t match the EHR order even though the patient says “that’s what I always get”; what should you do first?

Stop and verify the medication against the order (right drug) with the provider/pharmacy before preparing it, because label-order mismatch is a safety stop and the trap is relying on patient history to override verification.
Question 3116

A patient with a draining wound requires dressing change and you also need to draw up an injection afterward; how should you sequence tasks?

Prepare and administer the injection first (if ordered and appropriate) and perform the wound care last with proper cleanup, because wound drainage increases contamination risk and the trap is moving from dirty to clean tasks.
Question 3117

During a dressing change, the patient suddenly reports itching and you notice hives and lip swelling after a recent injection; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per clinic protocol) and assess airway and breathing, because this suggests anaphylaxis and the trap is having the patient “wait to see if it passes.”
Question 3118

In the waiting room, a patient collapses and is unresponsive; staff say “someone already called 911”; what should the CMA do next?

Begin CPR and have someone retrieve and apply the AED as soon as it arrives, because early compressions and defibrillation save lives and the trap is waiting passively for EMS.
Question 3119

During intake, a 58-year-old reports new chest pressure and looks pale while you are about to take routine vitals; what should you do first?

Stop the intake and alert the provider/activate the clinic’s emergency response while positioning the patient seated/upright; chest pain is time-sensitive and the trap is finishing vitals or paperwork before escalation.
Question 3120

A patient becomes lightheaded when moving from lying to standing for orthostatic vitals; what should you do next?

Assist the patient back to a safe position (sitting/lying), ensure safety, then notify the provider with what you obtained; the trap is pushing through measurements and risking a fall.
Question 3121

A patient’s initial BP is 168/102 after rushing in late and talking on the phone; what should you do next to get an accurate reading?

Have the patient sit quietly 5 minutes with feet flat and arm supported at heart level, then repeat BP; the trap is accepting the first number without proper positioning/rest.
Question 3122

You obtain an oral temperature of 103.1°F on a patient who just drank hot coffee in the waiting room; what is the best next step?

Wait about 15 minutes and recheck (or use an alternate site per protocol) before reporting as a true fever; the trap is documenting/treating a falsely elevated reading without verifying.
Question 3123

A healthy adult’s respirations appear irregular because the patient is watching you count; what should you do to obtain the most accurate respiratory rate?

Count respirations immediately after the pulse without telling the patient you’re counting breaths; the trap is announcing it, which often changes the breathing pattern.
Question 3124

While taking vitals, you count a radial pulse of 44 and the patient feels dizzy; what is the CMA’s best action?

Reassess promptly (confirm apical pulse if trained) and notify the provider immediately while keeping the patient seated; the trap is documenting “low pulse” and moving on despite symptoms.
Question 3125

You spill a small amount of blood on the counter after a fingerstick; what is the best immediate response?

Don gloves and clean/disinfect with an appropriate disinfectant following contact time, then remove gloves and perform hand hygiene; the trap is wiping quickly with a dry towel or skipping contact time.
Question 3126

After removing gloves, you notice your hands look clean and you’re behind schedule; what should you do next before touching the computer keyboard?

Perform hand hygiene immediately; the trap is thinking glove removal replaces hand hygiene and contaminating shared equipment.
Question 3127

During rooming, a 54-year-old patient becomes pale and says “I feel dizzy” while you are halfway through taking a standing blood pressure after sitting vitals were normal; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the standing measurement and assist the patient back to a safe seated or supine position, then recheck vitals and notify the provider because preventing a fall and addressing possible orthostatic hypotension takes priority over completing the procedure, and a common mistake is trying to “finish the reading” while the patient is unstable.
Question 3128

Just before administering a medication injection, the label reads a different patient’s name than the chart you’re holding; what should you do first?

Stop and resolve the discrepancy by re-verifying the order and patient identifiers before giving anything; the trap is assuming it’s “probably fine” and administering a wrong-patient medication.
Question 3129

You are preparing an IM vaccine and the patient asks if it can be given “in the hip because it hurts less”; what is the best action?

Use the recommended site (usually deltoid for adults) and explain site choice briefly; the trap is using the dorsogluteal site, which has higher sciatic nerve injury risk.
Question 3130

A patient with a cough asks for a mask at check-in, but the front desk is crowded; what should the CMA do first to reduce transmission?

Provide a mask and instruct the patient to wear it and perform hand hygiene immediately; the trap is delaying source control until after registration.
Question 3131

During intake for a same-day sick visit, an adult patient looks pale and says they feel faint while you are about to obtain orthostatic vital signs; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the orthostatic measurements, assist the patient to a safe position (sit or lie down), and alert the provider/clinical team immediately because preventing a fall and responding to possible syncope is higher priority than completing vitals; a common mistake is trying to “finish the set” despite clear instability.
Question 3132

In the exam room, you accidentally touch the inside of a sterile dressing package with your bare hand; what is the correct next step?

Discard the contaminated sterile field and obtain a new sterile dressing setup; the trap is “being careful” and using it anyway, which breaks asepsis.
Question 3133

A patient arrives for suture removal and you notice redness with yellow drainage at the site; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Do not remove sutures—notify the provider and document findings because infection may require evaluation; the trap is proceeding with removal as scheduled despite signs of infection.
Question 3134

During intake a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 86/52, HR 118, RR 22; the patient says they “just need water” and asks to finish paperwork first—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Seat/lay the patient down and alert the provider immediately while rechecking BP manually; symptomatic hypotension with tachycardia is urgent, and the trap is delaying care to complete forms.
Question 3135

You draw up 0.5 mL of a vaccine but notice air bubbles and the dose line is slightly off; what should you do next?

Expel the air and adjust to the exact ordered dose at eye level before administration; the trap is giving an inaccurate dose to “save time.”
Question 3136

During a sterile wound dressing change, the patient reaches toward the open sterile field to point at the wound; what should you do next?

Instruct the patient to keep hands away and re-establish sterility if contamination occurs; the trap is continuing without addressing contamination, increasing infection risk.
Question 3137

After giving an injection, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while standing at the checkout desk; what should you do first?

Assist the patient to a safe seated or supine position and stay with them while calling for clinical help; the trap is letting them “walk it off,” risking a fall injury.
Question 3138

A patient says they had hives and throat tightness after a prior flu shot, and today’s order is for a flu vaccine; what should the CMA do next?

Hold the vaccine and notify the provider immediately for further direction; the trap is proceeding because the vaccine is “routine” despite a possible anaphylaxis history.
Question 3139

In the lobby, an adult suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what should you do first in an outpatient clinic?

Assess responsiveness and breathing, call for help/activate EMS and get the AED, and begin CPR if not breathing normally; the trap is searching for a pulse for too long instead of starting the chain of survival.
Question 3140

A 4-year-old is crying and kicking during vitals, and the temporal thermometer reads 101.9°F after the child had been wearing a winter hat—what is the BEST next step?

Remove the hat, wait briefly, and recheck temperature with the same method per device instructions; the trap is documenting a likely inaccurate fever without correcting the environmental factor.
Question 3141

A patient’s radial pulse feels irregular at 96 bpm when you are taking vitals; the automated BP cuff also shows an error—what is the BEST action?

Apically count the pulse for a full minute and document irregularity, then notify the provider; the trap is relying on a machine reading or a 15-second count when rhythm is irregular.
Question 3142

You are about to perform a fingerstick glucose and notice dried blood on the glucometer from a prior use—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Put on gloves and disinfect the device with approved cleaner/contact time before use (or remove from service), then perform hand hygiene; the trap is “just wiping it quickly” or using it anyway, risking cross-contamination.
Question 3143

You are cleaning an exam room after a patient with vomiting and diarrhea; what PPE is MOST appropriate to start with?

Gloves and a gown (and add face protection if splash risk) because contact with infectious body fluids is likely; the trap is using only gloves and contaminating clothing/skin.
Question 3144

While rooming a patient for a routine visit, you count RR 8 with shallow breaths and the patient is difficult to arouse—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (call for help/911 per protocol) and begin ABC assessment; the trap is trying to finish vitals or “let them sleep” instead of treating as a potential respiratory emergency.
Question 3145

A patient with a draining wound asks to shake your hand while you are not wearing gloves; what is the BEST response?

Decline politely and perform hand hygiene, then don gloves before any contact with drainage; the trap is thinking gloves are only needed for “procedures” and not for potential body fluid exposure.
Question 3146

While removing gloves after a procedure, you feel your bare finger touch the contaminated outer glove surface—what should you do NEXT?

Perform hand hygiene immediately and continue doffing safely; the trap is assuming “I wore gloves” so hand hygiene isn’t needed after potential contamination.
Question 3147

After walking from the lobby, a patient’s first BP is 168/102 but they are asymptomatic and talking; what should the CMA do before reporting it as the official reading?

Have the patient rest quietly with feet flat and arm supported, then repeat BP with correct cuff size; the trap is reporting an exertion-inflated value without proper positioning/rest.
Question 3148

A patient in a wheelchair needs an orthostatic BP series for lightheadedness; what is the correct sequence the CMA should follow?

Measure BP/pulse after resting supine, then sitting, then standing (or as tolerated) with timed intervals and close guarding; the trap is starting with standing first, increasing fall risk and skewing results.
Question 3149

You have an order for an IM vaccine, and the patient says they have a “bad latex allergy” and becomes anxious when they see the syringe—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop and verify the product and supplies are latex-free (vial stopper/syringe components) and notify the provider/pharmacist if uncertain; the trap is proceeding because the order is written, risking an allergic reaction.
Question 3150

A patient needs a posterior nasal swab and starts gagging and coughing forcefully during the procedure—what is the BEST immediate action?

Stop the procedure, withdraw the swab, allow the patient to recover while maintaining source control (mask/tissues) and hand hygiene/PPE, then reassess; the trap is forcing completion and increasing injury/exposure risk.
Question 3151

A provider asks you to “hold the sterile field” while they step out, and you notice the sterile drape edge hanging below table level—what should the CMA do?

Consider the hanging portion contaminated and tell the provider the sterile field is broken so it can be replaced; the trap is trying to “fix” sterility by tucking it back up.
Question 3152

The provider orders an injection, but the patient’s name and DOB on the chart do not match the wristband information—what is the CMA’s BEST action?

Pause the process and resolve patient identification using two identifiers before giving the injection; the trap is assuming the wristband or chart is correct and proceeding, risking wrong-patient medication.
Question 3153

You are drawing up medication for an IM injection and the needle touches the counter before use—what is the BEST action?

Discard the needle and replace it with a new sterile needle before administering; the trap is wiping the needle with alcohol, which does not restore sterility.
Question 3154

After giving a subcutaneous injection, the patient immediately reports itching and you see hives forming on the neck—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Call for help and initiate the clinic’s anaphylaxis/emergency protocol (notify provider, prepare for airway/breathing support, call 911 as directed); the trap is telling the patient to “wait and see” or only documenting.
Question 3155

A patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while sitting on the exam table after a dressing change—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Assist the patient to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if tolerated) and stay with them while notifying the provider; the trap is leaving them seated or walking them to the lobby, increasing fall risk.
Question 3156

While preparing a medication, you realize the label says 2 mg/mL and the order is for 1 mg; you already drew up 1 mL—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Discard and redraw the correct volume (0.5 mL) and re-verify the dose before administration; the trap is trying to “adjust later” or give it because it’s “close,” causing a dosing error.
Question 3157

In the waiting room, an adult collapses and is unresponsive; you do not feel a pulse within 10 seconds and another staff member brings an AED—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Begin high-quality chest compressions immediately while the AED is powered on and applied as soon as available; the trap is delaying compressions to look for the “perfect” pulse check or waiting for the AED first.
Question 3158

You prepare to enter an exam room and see the previous patient’s used gloves on the counter and paper on the table; what should you do before rooming the next patient?

Perform hand hygiene and clean/disinfect high-touch surfaces and the exam table per contact time before bringing the next patient in; the trap is just changing table paper, which does not remove pathogens.
Question 3159

A provider requests you position a patient for a rectal exam; what is the BEST position the CMA should assist with?

Left Sims’ position with the right knee flexed for optimal access and patient comfort; the trap is using dorsal recumbent, which is less appropriate for rectal exams.
Question 3160

An adult’s apical pulse is irregular during vitals, but the patient denies symptoms and wants to proceed quickly—what is the best action?

Count for a full 60 seconds and report the irregular rhythm to the provider; the trap is counting 15 seconds ×4, which misses irregularity and delays appropriate evaluation.
Question 3161

During intake, a 62-year-old patient becomes pale and says they feel faint while you are taking their blood pressure seated on the exam table; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the measurement and assist the patient to a safe position (lying supine with legs elevated if tolerated), stay with them and call for clinical help, because preventing a fall and addressing possible syncope is the priority over finishing vitals—a common mistake is trying to “get the reading” before ensuring safety.
Question 3162

A child is anxious and crying during BP; you get an unusually high reading with a cuff that “almost fits”—what should you do next?

Select the correct cuff size and repeat the BP after a brief rest; the trap is reporting a likely inaccurate reading from a too-small cuff, which overestimates blood pressure.
Question 3163

During intake, a seated adult patient says they feel dizzy; you obtain BP 86/54 and pulse 122 after they rushed in from the parking lot—what should you do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and call the provider/activate clinic emergency protocol while rechecking vitals; the trap is documenting it as “anxiety” or letting them sit upright, which can worsen hypotension.
Question 3164

During intake, a patient states, “I’ve had chest pressure since this morning,” and appears pale; what should the CMA do first?

Stop routine intake, stay with the patient, and alert the provider/activate emergency response per clinic protocol; the trap is finishing vitals or sending them back to the waiting room, which delays urgent care.
Question 3165

While cleaning an exam room, you find dried blood on the countertop—what is the correct approach?

Don gloves (and other PPE as needed) and disinfect with an appropriate EPA-registered product effective for bloodborne pathogens, observing the required wet contact time; the trap is wiping quickly with alcohol, which may not disinfect adequately.
Question 3166

During the medication “rights” check, the patient’s name matches, but the date of birth does not—what should the CMA do next?

Hold the medication and re-identify the patient using two identifiers and verify the order before proceeding; the trap is relying on name alone, which can lead to wrong-patient administration.
Question 3167

You remove gloves after assisting with a wound dressing change and immediately reach for the computer keyboard to document—what should you do next?

Perform hand hygiene before touching the keyboard or other clean surfaces; the trap is assuming glove removal alone prevents contamination, which can spread organisms to shared equipment.
Question 3168

After giving a vaccine, the patient says they feel itchy and you notice hives forming—what should you do first?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (get the provider, bring emergency kit/AED as directed) and monitor airway/breathing; the trap is having the patient “wait it out” or focusing only on documentation while anaphylaxis may be developing.
Question 3169

A patient with a persistent cough is checking in at the front desk without a mask—what is the CMA’s best first action?

Offer a mask and separate the patient from others while performing hand hygiene and following standard precautions; the trap is waiting until the back office to address source control, increasing exposure risk.
Question 3170

You are preparing an IM injection and notice the prefilled syringe label is partially smudged; the patient says, “That’s fine, just give it”—what should you do?

Stop and obtain a clearly labeled, verifiable medication before administering; the trap is giving an unverified drug, violating medication safety rights and risking a serious error.
Question 3171

You’re about to don PPE for a procedure with splash risk; what is the correct sequence for putting on PPE?

Put on gown, then mask/respirator, then eye protection, then gloves; the trap is putting gloves on first, which makes it harder to properly secure other PPE and increases contamination risk.
Question 3172

You draw up 0.5 mL for a vaccine, then the provider is delayed and you set the syringe on the counter for “a minute”—what is the best action?

Discard the syringe and prepare a new dose when ready to administer to maintain sterility and correct timing/documentation; the trap is leaving a prepared syringe unattended, increasing contamination and mix-up risk.
Question 3173

During intake, a 68-year-old reports dizziness; you obtain BP 88/54 and pulse 124, and the patient looks pale—what should you do first?

Have the patient sit or lie down safely and call the provider/activate clinic emergency protocol because these are signs of possible shock; the trap is finishing the rest of the intake “for completeness” while the patient deteriorates.
Question 3174

You are preparing a subcutaneous injection and the patient says they are on a blood thinner and bruise easily—what is the best technique adjustment?

Use gentle technique and apply firm pressure afterward without rubbing; the trap is massaging the site, which increases bleeding/bruising in anticoagulated patients.
Question 3175

During a pelvic exam setup, the patient asks to keep their underwear on “until the last second,” and the provider is waiting—what should the CMA do?

Protect privacy and dignity by providing draping, explaining the steps, and allowing the patient to undress when ready while maintaining the exam flow; the trap is rushing or exposing the patient unnecessarily, which harms trust and can be reportable.
Question 3176

A patient needs a dressing change on a simple sutured laceration; the provider ordered routine wound care—what is the CMA’s best practice for technique?

Use clean technique for routine dressing changes and keep the wound area from contacting contaminated surfaces; the trap is assuming sterile technique is required for all wound care, which can be unnecessary and inconsistently performed.
Question 3177

During intake, a 58-year-old patient becomes pale and lightheaded while standing for height/weight; before you finish vitals, what should the CMA do FIRST?

Assist the patient to sit or lie down safely and reassess (check responsiveness and then vital signs) while alerting the provider if symptoms persist, because preventing a fall/syncope injury takes priority over completing measurements and a common mistake is keeping the patient standing to “finish quickly.”
Question 3178

In the clinic lobby, a patient collapses and an AED arrives; what is the correct next step after turning it on?

Apply pads and follow AED prompts, ensuring no one touches the patient during rhythm analysis and before shock; the trap is continuing compressions during analysis, which can prevent accurate rhythm detection.
Question 3179

You are assisting with a fainting episode in the hallway; the patient is slumped and unresponsive—what should you do first?

Check responsiveness and breathing, call for help, and initiate CPR if not breathing normally while someone gets the AED; the trap is trying to sit the patient up or offering water, which delays life-saving actions.
Question 3180

You position a patient for a routine blood draw in an exam room chair without arm supports, and they mention they “sometimes pass out with needles”—what should you do next?

Have the patient lie down (or use a chair with arm support) and ensure safety measures before proceeding; the trap is proceeding seated without support, increasing fall injury risk if syncope occurs.
Question 3181

You are about to give a vaccine and the patient says, “I’m allergic to latex,” and you notice latex-containing gloves on your hands—what should you do first?

Stop, remove latex gloves, perform hand hygiene, and obtain non-latex supplies because preventing an allergic reaction is a safety priority; the trap is proceeding since “it’s just gloves,” ignoring exposure risk.
Question 3182

During vitals, a 24-year-old has respirations 28 and is using accessory muscles but insists it’s “just anxiety”—what should you do first?

Stop the routine intake and alert the provider immediately while positioning upright because visible respiratory distress is urgent regardless of the patient’s explanation; the trap is coaching breathing and delaying escalation.
Question 3183

While taking BP over a sweatshirt sleeve, you hear faint Korotkoff sounds and get 146/92—what is the best action?

Remove the sleeve/ensure correct cuff placement and repeat after the patient rests because clothing and poor technique can falsely elevate or distort readings; the trap is accepting a questionable value and prompting unnecessary escalation.
Question 3184

After removing gloves, you notice your hands look clean and you’re about to type in the EHR—what should you do next?

Perform hand hygiene immediately because glove removal can contaminate hands even when they appear clean; the trap is assuming gloves replace hand hygiene.
Question 3185

A patient’s radial pulse feels irregular during vitals, but they are otherwise stable—what should you do next?

Measure the apical pulse for a full 60 seconds and report/document the irregularity because apical assessment is more accurate for dysrhythmias; the trap is estimating for 15 seconds and missing clinically significant irregular beats.
Question 3186

In an exam room, you see a used needle left on the counter after an injection—what is the best action?

Without recapping, place it directly into the sharps container (using a safe handling method) and report the safety hazard per protocol because exposed sharps are an immediate injury risk; the trap is recapping “to make it safer,” which increases needlestick risk.
Question 3187

A patient becomes lightheaded during standing weight measurement—what is the best immediate response?

Assist the patient to sit (or lie down if needed) and stay with them to prevent a fall because safety takes priority over completing measurements; the trap is stepping away to “grab water” and leaving an unstable patient unattended.
Question 3188

You’re rooming a patient with suspected influenza who is coughing frequently—what PPE should you put on before close contact?

Don a surgical mask (and eye protection if splash risk) and perform hand hygiene because droplet precautions plus standard precautions reduce exposure; the trap is using only gloves, which don’t protect mucous membranes.
Question 3189

During a nosebleed in the clinic, the patient tilts their head back because “that’s what I was taught”—what should you instruct them to do?

Sit upright and lean forward while applying firm pressure to the soft part of the nose because this reduces swallowing blood and helps control bleeding; the trap is head-back positioning, which increases aspiration/nausea risk.
Question 3190

You are about to administer a medication, and the patient asks what it is for; the provider is with another patient—what should you do?

Pause administration and notify the provider to counsel/confirm indications because informed cooperation and safety require answering medication-purpose questions within scope; the trap is guessing or giving incomplete information and proceeding.
Question 3191

After giving an IM vaccine, the patient stands up quickly and says they feel faint—what should you do first?

Assist them back to a seated or supine position and monitor closely because syncope/fall prevention is immediate; the trap is sending them to the lobby or restroom where they can collapse unobserved.
Question 3192

You prepared a sterile dressing change setup, but you accidentally touched the sterile gauze with your bare hand—what is the best next step?

Discard the contaminated gauze and replace it with sterile gauze because the sterile field is breached; the trap is using it anyway since “it only touched for a second.”
Question 3193

A patient’s wound drainage saturates the dressing; while removing it, the outside of the soiled dressing brushes your scrub top—what should you do next?

Remove contaminated PPE/clothing as needed and perform hand hygiene, then replace barriers before continuing because you’ve transferred contamination and must break the chain of infection; the trap is continuing the procedure to “finish quickly.”
Question 3194

Question 3196

Question 3197

During intake, a 68-year-old ambulatory patient says they feel “lightheaded,” and you obtain BP 86/54, pulse 118, and they look pale while the provider is with another patient; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the intake, assist the patient to a safe position (sit or supine with legs elevated if tolerated) to prevent a fall, then immediately alert the provider/activate clinic emergency response and recheck vital signs—don’t leave the patient alone or continue routine screening when signs suggest possible shock.
Question 3198

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (alert provider/911 per protocol) and place the patient supine with legs elevated if tolerated because hypotension with concerning symptoms is unstable; the trap is finishing the rest of the intake or repeating vitals before escalating.
Question 3199

In the waiting room, a patient collapses and is unresponsive; you don’t know if they’re breathing normally—what should you do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per protocol) and begin assessment for breathing/pulse to start CPR and get an AED because early activation and CPR save lives; the trap is spending time searching for medical history before calling for help.
Question 3200

While rooming a patient, you note RR 8 with shallow breaths after they took a prescribed pain medication; what should the CMA do first?

Stay with the patient and immediately notify the provider/activate rapid assistance because bradypnea can signal respiratory depression; the trap is documenting and waiting for the provider to “see it later.”
