Question 1601

You are setting up a 12-lead EKG and the patient’s skin is oily with lotion; several leads keep popping off; what should you do next?

Clean and dry the skin (and lightly abrade if allowed) before reapplying electrodes because good skin contact reduces artifact and prevents lead failure; the trap is repeatedly replacing electrodes without prepping skin, leading to poor tracings.
Question 1602

During a morning draw, a CCMA collects blood cultures (sterile) and then fills a light blue tube but forgets to fill it completely; the patient has already left—what should the CCMA do next?

Notify the provider/lab and arrange a recollect before any PT/INR result is reported because an underfilled light blue (citrate) tube can invalidate coagulation testing due to the required blood-to-additive ratio; a common mistake is sending it anyway and assuming the lab will “adjust.”
Question 1603

Right after venipuncture, a patient develops a rapidly swelling, painful area at the site; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw, remove the needle, apply firm pressure, and then use a cold compress because this suggests a hematoma; the trap is continuing to fill tubes or “massaging” the site, which worsens bleeding and bruising.
Question 1604

During a venipuncture, blood stops flowing and you suspect the bevel is against the vein wall; what is the best action within CMA scope to re-establish flow?

Slightly reposition the needle (pull back a bit without removing completely) and recheck tourniquet and patient fist position because minor adjustment often restores flow; the trap is “digging” or probing widely, which increases patient injury.
Question 1605

A patient’s requisition and labels print correctly, but the patient says they recently changed their last name; before labeling the tubes, what is the best next step?

Verify identity using two identifiers and clarify/update registration per office workflow before labeling because specimen identification must match the patient’s legal identifiers; the trap is labeling with “what’s on the label” without resolving a mismatch, risking wrong-patient results.
Question 1606

You are performing a CLIA-waived rapid strep test and the internal control line fails to appear; the patient is waiting and the provider asks for the result; what is the best next step?

Do not report a result, repeat the test with a new device/reagents and verify QC per manufacturer instructions because a failed control means the test is invalid; the trap is reporting “negative” based on no test line, which can lead to missed treatment.
Question 1607

You drew a CBC in a lavender tube and are about to walk it to the lab; what tube-handling step is most important to do immediately after collection?

Gently invert the tube the recommended number of times to mix anticoagulant because inadequate mixing can cause clotting and inaccurate counts; the trap is shaking vigorously, which can hemolyze the specimen.
Question 1608

The EKG tracing shows a wandering baseline and the patient is talking and shivering; what should the CMA do first to reduce artifact?

Coach the patient to lie still and quiet and address shivering (warm blanket) before repeating because muscle movement causes artifact; the trap is immediately repeating the EKG without correcting the cause, producing the same poor-quality strip.
Question 1609

A glucometer QC check is due, but the clinic is behind and a patient needs an in-office glucose reading now; what should the CMA do?

Run the required QC (or use a device with current QC) before testing the patient because out-of-control equipment can give unsafe results; the trap is testing first “to save time” and documenting later, which defeats quality assurance and patient safety.
Question 1610

A patient hands the CMA a form authorizing the clinic to send records to an attorney but the form has no date or description of what to release; what should the CMA do?

Do not release records and request a complete, properly dated authorization specifying information and purpose, because incomplete authorizations are not valid and the trap is “sending everything” to be helpful.
Question 1611

While charting, a CMA realizes they documented the wrong vaccine lot number on today’s visit note; what is the best way to correct it in the EHR?

Enter an addendum/correction per EHR policy with the accurate lot number and reason, because records must remain auditable and the trap is deleting/overwriting the original entry.
Question 1612

A parent arrives with a 16-year-old for a sports physical and asks to read the teen’s portal messages at the front desk; what should the CMA do next?

Explain that access depends on state law and the clinic’s minor-consent policy and route the request to the supervisor/office manager, because minors may have confidential services and the trap is granting parental access automatically.
Question 1613

A provider asks the CMA to “just sign my name” on a prescription refill request because they are busy; what should the CMA do?

Refuse and route the request for the provider’s review/signature, because signing for a provider is falsification and outside CMA scope, and the trap is thinking it’s acceptable with verbal permission.
Question 1614

At check-in, a celebrity patient asks to be listed under a fake name in the schedule “for privacy”; what should the CMA do first?

Follow the clinic’s privacy workflow while maintaining accurate legal identification in the medical record (e.g., confidentiality flag/alias feature if available), because misidentifying the patient can cause safety and billing errors and the trap is changing the legal chart name informally.
Question 1615

During rooming, the patient says, “I don’t want my visit shared with insurance,” then asks the CMA to bill it anyway; what is the best CMA response?

Explain that billing insurance requires sharing necessary information for payment and offer self-pay options per clinic policy, because patients can’t demand insurance billing without disclosure and the trap is promising confidentiality that conflicts with billing.
Question 1616

A patient calls asking for their spouse’s lab results and correctly states the spouse’s date of birth but is not listed as an authorized contact in the EHR; what should the CMA do next?

Politely decline to release results and offer to speak with the patient directly or obtain documented authorization, because DOB alone is not HIPAA authorization and the trap is “helping” by sharing PHI to a family member.
Question 1617

The CMA is about to fax immunization records to a school and notices the fax number on the request differs from the school’s official number on file; what should the CMA do first?

Verify the recipient’s correct fax number using a trusted source (chart/school directory) before sending, because misdirected faxes are a common HIPAA breach and the trap is trusting the number on an unverified form.
Question 1618

A patient’s chart shows two similar names and the CMA is about to scan outside records into the EHR; what should the CMA confirm first?

Confirm patient identifiers (full name and DOB/medical record number) match the documents before scanning, because misfiling records can cause wrong-patient care and the trap is relying on room number or appointment time.
Question 1619

A patient signs a procedure consent but seems confused and asks the CMA, “Can you explain the risks again?”; what is the best CMA action?

Pause and notify the provider to re-explain and answer questions before proceeding, because informed consent requires provider discussion and the trap is the CMA trying to counsel on risks beyond their role.
Question 1620

In the waiting room, a patient loudly asks, “Why is my HIV test taking so long?” and others can hear; what should the CMA do best?

Lower voice and move the conversation to a private area before discussing any details, because HIPAA requires reasonable safeguards and the trap is answering publicly even if the patient brought it up.
Question 1621

Request the provider place/confirm the written order in the EHR before scheduling, because scheduling without an order risks wrong test/authorization problems and the trap is booking first “to save time.”
Question 1622

Question 1623

Question 1624

Question 1625

Question 1626

Question 1627

Question 1628

Question 1629

Question 1630

Question 1631

Question 1632

Question 1693

A patient on the phone starts describing new symptoms and asks, “Do you think this is serious?”; what is the CMA’s best communication approach?

Use therapeutic listening, ask approved clarifying questions within protocol, and escalate to the provider/triage process rather than giving an opinion, because CMAs don’t diagnose and the trap is reassuring the patient based on limited information.
Question 1694

At check-out, a spouse demands a printed medication list “because I’m the one who manages it,” and the patient is not present; what should the CMA do first?

Verify documented permission/legal authority (e.g., ROI, healthcare proxy) before releasing any information because HIPAA restricts disclosure; the trap is assuming family involvement equals authorization.
Question 1695

You enter a vaccine lot number in the EHR and realize you transposed two digits after you already saved it; what should you do next?

Use the EHR’s approved correction/amendment method to correct the lot number and add a brief note explaining the correction because traceability is required; the trap is deleting the entry or leaving the incorrect lot “since the patient got the vaccine anyway.”
Question 1696

At check-in, a patient says, “I moved and my insurance changed,” and hands you a new card while the EHR still shows last year’s plan; the provider is running on time and the patient wants to be roomed immediately—what should the CMA do first to prevent billing/claim problems?

Verify and update the patient’s demographics and insurance information in the EHR using the new card and confirm eligibility/required referrals before rooming, because outdated payer data commonly leads to claim denials and the trap is assuming “we’ll fix it after the visit.”
Question 1697

The schedule shows two patients booked into the same 15-minute slot with one provider, and one is a time-sensitive wound check; what should the CMA do first?

Notify the scheduler/provider and triage by visit urgency (e.g., wound check prioritized) to adjust the schedule because access and safety depend on proper prioritization; the trap is “first come, first served” without considering clinical need.
Question 1698

You overhear staff discussing a patient’s diagnosis in the elevator; what is the CMA’s best action?

Promptly stop the conversation and remind staff to discuss PHI only in private areas because incidental disclosure is preventable and reportable; the trap is ignoring it because it’s “coworkers only.”
Question 1699

A patient’s friend offers to pick up the patient’s printed lab results and says, “She told me it’s fine”; what should the CMA do first?

Require documented patient authorization and verify the requester’s identity before releasing results because verbal third-party permission is not adequate; the trap is accepting the friend’s statement as proof.
Question 1700

During check-in, a patient’s spouse steps to the front desk and asks, “Can you print his lab results and tell me what they mean? He said it was fine,” but the patient is still in the waiting room and no release is documented in the EHR; what should the CMA do next?

Verify the patient’s permission before any disclosure by speaking directly with the patient and obtaining/documenting a HIPAA-compliant authorization (or confirming an existing one) before releasing results; relying on the spouse’s statement or discussing results without documented permission is an avoidable privacy violation.
