Question 3701

You collected a throat swab for a rapid strep but the courier is delayed and it will sit at room temperature for hours; what should you do BEST?

Follow the test’s specimen handling requirements (e.g., place in appropriate transport medium and store as directed or recollect if stability is exceeded) because viability/stability affects accuracy, and the trap is “sending it whenever it leaves.”
Question 3702

During venipuncture, the patient reports sharp tingling down the forearm and you feel resistance; what should you do FIRST?

Immediately stop the draw and remove the needle, then apply pressure because this may indicate nerve involvement, and the trap is continuing “just to finish the tube.”
Question 3703

After venipuncture, a patient develops a rapidly swelling, painful lump at the site; what should you do NEXT?

Stop the draw (if still in progress), remove the needle, and apply firm pressure with a cold pack because this indicates hematoma, and the trap is applying heat immediately or “just bandaging and moving on.”
Question 3704

After collecting several tubes, you notice the tourniquet has been on for over 2 minutes while you searched for a vein; what is the BEST action?

Release the tourniquet and allow time before reapplying because prolonged stasis can hemoconcentrate and skew results, and the trap is leaving it on “so you don’t lose the vein.”
Question 3705

You collect a potassium level and shake the tube vigorously to mix it before it clots; what is the most appropriate corrective step?

Recollect the specimen and gently invert per tube requirements because vigorous shaking can hemolyze and falsely elevate potassium, and the trap is thinking “more mixing is better.”
Question 3706

A urine culture is ordered, and the patient hands you a sample collected in a nonsterile cup from home without instructions; what is the BEST next step?

Request a new specimen using a sterile container and provide clean-catch instructions because nonsterile collection compromises culture results, and the trap is accepting it “since urine is urine.”
Question 3707

While labeling tubes, you realize you forgot to confirm the patient’s date of birth and only used the name on the chart; what should you do FIRST?

Verify two identifiers with the patient and label in their presence (or discard and recollect if identity can’t be confirmed) because misidentification is a major lab error, and the trap is “asking the front desk later.”
Question 3708

During a venipuncture for a CMP and CBC, the CMA draws the lavender tube first and then the gold serum tube; before labeling, the provider adds an order for PT/INR—what should the CMA do NEXT to ensure valid results?

Stop and recollect using the correct order of draw starting with a light blue citrate tube (then serum, then lavender) and label at the bedside; drawing EDTA before coag/serum can contaminate specimens and falsely alter results, and a common mistake is trying to “add on” PT/INR from the wrong tube or continuing the draw sequence anyway.
Question 3709

A CLIA-waived urine pregnancy test shows no control line; what is the BEST action?

Treat it as an invalid test and repeat with a new device/sample per procedure because no control means results can’t be trusted, and the trap is reporting it as negative.
Question 3710

You perform a fingerstick glucose and the first drop of blood is used directly on the strip after the patient’s hands were just sanitized with alcohol; what should you do NEXT?

Repeat the test after the finger is fully dry and wipe away the first drop because alcohol/first drop can dilute or contaminate the sample, and the trap is trusting the number because it “looks reasonable.”
Question 3711

You place EKG limb electrodes on the wrists and ankles of a shivering patient, and the tracing has heavy baseline artifact; what should you do NEXT?

Reduce muscle tremor and improve electrode contact (warm/cover patient, have them relax, prep skin and reposition electrodes if needed) because tremor commonly causes artifact, and the trap is repeatedly re-running without fixing the cause.
Question 3712

At the start of the day, the POCT glucometer fails QC but patient testing is already scheduled; what should you do BEST?

Do not run patient tests, troubleshoot per manufacturer (new strips, control solution, cleaning, recalibration) and notify the supervisor/provider if unresolved because QC failure invalidates results, and the trap is testing anyway “to avoid delaying the clinic.”
Question 3713

A patient complains of chest pressure while you are about to start an ordered EKG for “palpitations,” and they look sweaty and anxious; what should you do FIRST?

Alert the provider immediately and initiate the clinic’s emergency response while obtaining the EKG as directed because symptoms may indicate an acute event and the trap is prioritizing “finish the test before telling anyone.”
Question 3714

You are setting up a 12-lead EKG and notice the patient’s chest is oily with lotion; what is the BEST next step before applying precordial leads?

Clean and dry the skin (and lightly abrade if needed) to improve adhesion and signal quality because poor contact creates artifact, and the trap is pressing harder on electrodes and assuming the machine is broken.
Question 3715

During a venipuncture, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint; what should you do FIRST?

Stop the procedure and assist the patient into a safe position (seated with head down or supine) because syncope risk is immediate, and the trap is trying to “finish the last tube quickly.”
Question 3716

After completing an EKG, the machine prints “AC interference,” and you see the patient’s phone charging cable near the leads; what should you do NEXT?

Remove/relocate potential electrical sources and ensure leads are secure, then repeat the tracing because electrical interference can distort readings, and the trap is sending the strip “since it printed.”
Question 3717

A patient at the front desk speaks loudly about having an STI and asks you to confirm their diagnosis in front of others—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Move the conversation to a private area and speak quietly using neutral language, because continuing at the desk risks an incidental disclosure even if the patient started the conversation.
Question 3718

While rooming a patient, you realize the provider documented the wrong medication dose in yesterday’s note and the patient is worried—what is the CMA’s BEST next action?

Notify the provider and follow the clinic’s amendment process rather than editing the provider’s note yourself, because altering another user’s documentation can be considered falsification.
Question 3719

You are drawing blood for PT/INR (light blue) and a lipid panel (serum) using a butterfly set, and you accidentally fill the serum tube first—what is the best next step?

Discard that collection and redraw using the correct order of draw (blood culture if ordered, then light blue before serum) because drawing serum first can contaminate the citrate tube and falsely alter coag results; a common trap is keeping the blue tube “since it filled,” but the error affects test accuracy.
Question 3720

A minor arrives alone for a sports physical and hands you a signed consent “from mom” on notebook paper—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Confirm legal consent per state/clinic policy (e.g., parent/guardian present or verified consent) before proceeding, because accepting informal notes without verification is a common consent error.
Question 3721

You entered a vaccine lot number in the EHR and then notice one digit is wrong—what is the BEST way to correct it?

Follow the EHR’s correction/addendum function with date/time and reason, because deleting or overwriting the original entry can compromise the legal integrity of the record.
Question 3722

Verify whether a valid authorization is on file before discussing any results and, if not, decline and offer to have the patient call back, because assuming a spouse has access is a common HIPAA breach.
Question 3723

A patient asks you to email their visit summary to a work email “because it’s easier”—what should the CMA do?

Question 3724

During a venipuncture for a CMP and CBC, the patient says “I feel hot and dizzy,” becomes pale, and starts sliding down the chair while the tourniquet is still on—what should the CMA do first?

Stop the draw by releasing the tourniquet and removing the needle, then assist the patient to a safe position and alert the provider per clinic protocol because preventing injury from syncope is the priority; a common trap is trying to “finish the tube” before addressing the patient’s reaction.
Question 3725

An insurance claim is denied for “invalid diagnosis code,” and the patient calls upset—what should the CMA do NEXT within scope?

Route the issue to the billing/coding workflow to review coding/coverage and inform the patient you will follow up, because a CMA should not guess codes or promise coverage.
Question 3726

A walk-in patient demands to be seen immediately for a “refill” and becomes angry when told there are no openings—what is the CMA’s BEST response?

Use calm, respectful communication and assess for urgent symptoms that require escalation while offering the next available appointment or provider message, because dismissing without screening can miss an emergency.
Question 3727

During documentation, you notice you charted vitals under the wrong patient with a similar name—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Immediately alert a supervisor/manager and follow the EHR’s misfiled documentation correction process for both charts, because simply re-entering in the correct chart without addressing the erroneous entry leaves a dangerous record.
Question 3728

Question 3729

A provider asks you to “just backdate” a note to match the visit date because they forgot to sign it—what is the CMA’s BEST action?

Question 3730

A patient says they never received a portal message about abnormal results and asks you over the phone what they were—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Verify identity and then follow the clinic protocol for communicating results (often provider review and documented communication) rather than improvising, because giving results without authorization/documentation is a common legal and safety trap.
Question 3731

Verify identity using approved identifiers before sharing any details, because confirming the visit reason to an unverified caller can disclose protected information.
Question 3732

Confirm the patient’s insurance requirements (in-network, prior authorization, referral form) and follow the referral process before scheduling, because booking without verifying can lead to denied coverage and delays.
Question 3733

Notify your supervisor/office manager and the provider immediately and follow policy to correct the author/attestation because misattribution is a legal documentation error; the trap is “just editing it” yourself, which can worsen audit risk.
Question 3734

Question 3737

Question 3739

Question 3794

While rooming a patient, the CMA notices the provider’s note in the EHR has auto-populated “left ear pain” from a prior visit, but today the patient reports right ear pain and the provider is waiting to sign the note; what should the CMA do next?

Notify the provider immediately and document the patient’s report accurately in the appropriate section without altering the provider’s note, because correcting copy-forward errors before the note is finalized prevents inaccurate documentation and the trap is “fixing” the provider’s entry yourself or ignoring it to save time.
Question 3795

A provider documents a procedure, but the CMA notices the note is missing the site (left vs right) and laterality affects coding and safety; what should the CMA do best?

Prompt the provider to add an addendum clarifying the site before the encounter is finalized, because incomplete documentation can cause billing errors and clinical risk, and the trap is trying to “fix” clinical details without provider confirmation.
Question 3796

A patient asks the CMA at checkout, “How much will today’s visit cost with my deductible?” and the CMA is unsure; what should the CMA do next?

Provide a general explanation and refer the patient to billing/financial counselor or run an eligibility/benefits check per workflow, because quoting exact amounts without verification can be inaccurate and the trap is guessing to sound helpful.
Question 3797

A patient wants you to email their visit summary to a personal Gmail address “right now”; what is the best response?

Use an approved secure patient portal or encrypted process (or offer printed pickup with ID verification) because standard email is typically not secure and “patient requested it” doesn’t remove HIPAA safeguards.
Question 3798

A patient asks for a copy of their lab results while checking out, but the printed report includes another patient’s name at the top; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Stop the release immediately, secure/shred the misprinted page, and alert the supervisor/privacy officer so the potential HIPAA breach is contained and documented; the common mistake is handing over “just the right page” or reprinting without reporting the improper disclosure risk.
Question 3799

A patient calls asking for their adult daughter’s lab results and says, “I’m on her HIPAA form somewhere,” but you don’t see any release on file; what should the CMA do first?

Verify whether a valid authorization is documented and, if not, decline to disclose and offer to speak with the patient directly or obtain written permission, because “family” requests are a common HIPAA trap.
Question 3800

While rooming a patient, the CMA hears the front desk confirm another patient’s appointment details over speakerphone in the waiting area; what should the CMA do FIRST to stay HIPAA-compliant?

Ask the staff member to take the call off speaker and move the conversation to a more private area because minimum-necessary privacy must be maintained; a common mistake is assuming appointment and demographic details are “not medical” and can be discussed publicly.
