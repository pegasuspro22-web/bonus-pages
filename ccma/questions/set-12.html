Question 1101

The chart note from a prior visit documents “edema” and today the patient points to swelling in both ankles and asks what system is most directly involved, what should the CMA say without diagnosing?

Explain that ankle swelling commonly relates to fluid balance involving the cardiovascular/renal systems and report it to the provider because the trap is naming a specific disease (outside CMA scope) instead of linking the symptom to systems.
Question 1102

While reconciling meds, a patient reports “dizzy and nauseated” after starting a new medication yesterday, and their BP in the room is 88/54; is dizziness a sign or symptom and what should the CMA do first?

Dizziness is a symptom, and the CMA should promptly notify the provider and keep the patient seated/monitored because the trap is treating it as a minor complaint instead of correlating it with an objective low BP.
Question 1103

A teen in the waiting room asks you what “NKA” on their intake form means because they saw it on the screen at check-in, what is the CMA’s best response?

Explain privately that NKA means “no known allergies” and then ensure the screen isn’t visible to others because the trap is discussing health information where other patients can overhear (privacy/HIPAA risk).
Question 1104

During rooming, the provider’s order says “qhs” for a new tablet and the patient says, “I’ll just take it three times a day like my vitamins,” what should the CMA do next?

Clarify and teach that qhs means “every night at bedtime” and document the education because the trap is letting the patient reinterpret abbreviations, leading to unsafe overuse.
Question 1105

During medication history, the patient reports an “itchy rash” after taking amoxicillin in the past; how should the CMA document this information and why?

Document it as a medication allergy/adverse reaction with the specific drug and reaction (e.g., “amoxicillin—rash/itching”) because the trap is recording only “penicillin allergy” or only “allergy: yes,” which can cause unsafe medication decisions later.
Question 1106

During intake, a 54-year-old patient reports new chest pressure and looks pale while you are about to take routine vitals; what should the CMA do first?

Stop the intake and activate the clinic’s emergency response (alert provider/911 per protocol) because possible cardiac symptoms require immediate escalation; the trap is continuing vitals and delaying care.
Question 1107

You obtain BP 188/112 on a seated patient who denies symptoms and says they “feel fine”; what is the best next CMA action?

Recheck manually after 5 minutes of rest with the correct cuff size and arm position, then promptly report if still elevated because technique error is common but severe hypertension must be escalated.
Question 1108

A patient calls saying their referral was “sent weeks ago” but the specialist never received it; what is the CMA’s best next step within scope?

Verify the referral details in the chart (correct specialist, fax number, authorization status) and re-send/confirm receipt, then document the contact because the trap is assuming it’s the specialist’s fault without checking transmission/insurance steps.
Question 1109

While preparing to administer an ordered IM injection, you notice the vial concentration is different than what you used last week and the calculated volume seems unusually large; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and re-check the order, concentration, and dose calculation (and ask for a second check per safety practice) before drawing up because the trap is proceeding based on habit, which risks a dosing error.
Question 1110

A patient’s oral temperature reads 101.9°F, and they admit taking acetaminophen an hour ago; what should the CMA do next?

Document the temperature and the antipyretic use/time and notify the provider per office process because medication can mask trends; the trap is recording only the number without context.
Question 1111

A prescription label reads “take 0.5 mg” but the tablets are 1 mg each; the patient asks how many tablets to take, what should the CMA instruct?

Instruct the patient to take one-half tablet if the tablet is scored and the provider/pharmacy directions allow it, and verify with the pharmacist/provider if not clearly splittable because the trap is rounding up to a whole tablet and doubling the dose.
Question 1112

A patient with asthma says they are using their “rescue inhaler” multiple times daily and asks if that’s normal; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Advise them to discuss the frequent rescue use with the provider today and document it because the trap is reassuring or adjusting therapy, and frequent rescue use can signal poor control needing provider evaluation.
Question 1113

A 6-year-old is fidgeting and talking during a BP measurement, and the reading is high; what should the CMA do to obtain the most accurate result?

Calm the child, ensure feet supported/arm at heart level, and repeat after quiet rest because movement/talking falsely elevates BP; the trap is accepting the first value as true.
Question 1114

You are cleaning an exam table after a patient leaves and notice dried blood on the paper; what is the correct first action?

Don gloves before handling contaminated materials because blood is a standard precautions risk; the trap is stripping paper bare-handed “because it’s dry.”
Question 1115

The provider requests sterile irrigation of a wound, but the sterile field is set up and you realize the outer package of a sterile tray is wet; what should you do?

Discard it and obtain a new sterile tray because moisture can wick contaminants through packaging; the trap is using it “since it’s still sealed.”
Question 1116

A patient is dizzy when standing for weight and says they “almost fainted in the parking lot”; what is the best positioning action first?

Assist them to a supine position with legs elevated and stay with them because this supports perfusion and prevents falls; the trap is insisting they remain standing to finish intake.
Question 1117

You’re about to take a pulse ox reading on a patient wearing dark nail polish with cold hands; what should you do first to avoid an inaccurate result?

Warm the hand and use an alternate site/remove polish if possible because poor perfusion/polish can cause false lows; the trap is reporting a low SpO₂ without troubleshooting.
Question 1118

You enter an exam room to collect a throat swab for suspected strep and the patient is actively gagging and coughing; what PPE is most appropriate?

Wear gloves, gown, and a surgical mask with eye protection because droplets/splashes are likely; the trap is using gloves only and risking mucous membrane exposure.
Question 1119

After removing gloves, you immediately answer the phone to schedule the next patient; what infection-control step is missing?

Perform hand hygiene right after glove removal because gloves are not a substitute and hands can be contaminated; the trap is assuming “no skin contact” means no handwashing.
Question 1120

A patient is in the waiting room coughing heavily and asks to sign in at the front desk; what should the CMA do first?

Provide a mask and perform hand hygiene/offer sanitizer and separate them from others because source control breaks transmission; the trap is completing paperwork first while exposing others.
Question 1121

After giving a subcutaneous injection, the patient begins wheezing and says their lips feel tingly; what is the CMA’s priority action?

Activate emergency response and stay with the patient (prepare epinephrine/AED per protocol) because this may be anaphylaxis; the trap is sending them to the lobby to “wait it out.”
Question 1122

You are assisting with a pelvic exam and the patient starts sliding down the table while in lithotomy position; what is the best CMA action?

Reposition and secure the patient safely (adjust buttocks to table edge, confirm stirrup support, provide draping) because preventing falls/injury is priority; the trap is continuing the exam while the patient is unstable.
Question 1123

During rooming, a 58-year-old reports dizziness; you obtain BP 86/52 sitting and 82/48 standing, HR 112, and they look pale—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Have the patient sit or lie supine with legs elevated and immediately alert the provider/activate emergency response as indicated, because these findings suggest symptomatic hypotension/possible shock and the trap is rechecking vitals or finishing intake before stabilizing and escalating.
Question 1124

While performing the 6 rights check, you find the vaccine name matches the order but the dose in the syringe is higher than ordered; what should you do next?

Stop and correct the dose by preparing a new syringe or adjusting per protocol before administration because dose errors are preventable; the trap is “just giving what’s drawn up.”
Question 1125

You are preparing an IM vaccine and draw it up, then notice the label on the vial expired last month; what is the best next step?

Discard the dose and obtain a non-expired vial because expired meds may be ineffective/unsafe; the trap is giving it anyway since it “looks fine.”
Question 1126

A patient says they had hives and throat tightness after a flu shot last year, and you are about to administer today’s ordered influenza vaccine; what should the CMA do first?

Hold the vaccine and immediately notify the provider because this suggests a severe prior reaction requiring clinical decision-making; the trap is proceeding because the vaccine is “ordered.”
Question 1127

You are about to administer a medication injection, and the patient asks, “What is this for?” but you’re not sure; what should you do?

Pause and obtain clarification from the provider/label before proceeding because informed, accurate administration is required; the trap is guessing or giving reassurance without verifying.
Question 1128

During a dressing change, the patient’s old gauze is stuck to the wound and starts bleeding when you pull; what should the CMA do next?

Moisten the dressing with sterile saline to loosen it before removal because tearing tissue increases bleeding and delays healing; the trap is pulling faster to “get it over with.”
Question 1129

A patient arrives for a routine visit and says they drank two large coffees and jogged up stairs; you get BP 156/94—what is the best next step before documenting it as “hypertensive”?

Have the patient rest quietly 5 minutes with feet flat and recheck BP with correct cuff/position because activity/caffeine and poor technique can falsely elevate readings; the trap is reporting a single rushed value as definitive.
Question 1130

In the hallway, you find a visitor collapsed and unresponsive, and a staff member says they’ll “go find the provider”; what should the CMA do first?

Call for help/activate EMS and begin CPR while someone retrieves the AED because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is waiting for the provider before starting lifesaving steps.
Question 1131

During intake, a 52-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 86/54, HR 118, RR 22, SpO₂ 92% on room air; what should the CMA do first?

Seat/lay the patient supine and alert the provider immediately while reassessing vitals because these are potentially unstable findings; the trap is continuing routine history questions or walking the patient to an exam room.
Question 1132

A patient with severe shortness of breath is brought to triage; what position should the CMA use while notifying the provider?

Place the patient in high Fowler’s (upright) to ease breathing while escalating promptly because positioning can quickly improve ventilation; the trap is lying the patient flat “to rest.”
Question 1133

While taking an oral temperature on a child, the caregiver states the child just drank ice water in the lobby; what should the CMA do next?

Wait about 15 minutes (or use an alternate appropriate site per clinic protocol) before retaking because recent hot/cold intake skews oral readings; the trap is charting an inaccurately low temperature.
Question 1134

During intake at an urgent care clinic, a 62-year-old becomes pale and says she feels faint while standing on the scale; what should the CMA do first before continuing vitals?

Assist her to sit or lie down safely and assess responsiveness (then obtain vitals and alert the provider if abnormal) because preventing a fall and checking for an acute change takes priority over “finishing the intake,” a common mistake that leads to injury.
Question 1135

A patient’s respirations increase when you tell them you’re counting; what is the best technique to obtain an accurate respiratory rate?

Count respirations discreetly immediately after the pulse while maintaining your hand position because awareness alters breathing pattern; the trap is announcing “I’m counting your breathing now.”
Question 1136

You obtain an adult’s radial pulse and it feels irregular; what is the best action for the CMA?

Auscultate the apical pulse for a full 60 seconds and report/document the irregularity because irregular rhythms require more accurate assessment; the trap is counting 15 seconds and multiplying.
Question 1137

After removing gloves used for a wound dressing change, what should the CMA do immediately next?

Perform hand hygiene right away because glove removal can contaminate hands and is required even if gloves were intact; the trap is thinking gloves replace handwashing.
Question 1138

A provider orders 0.5 mL of a medication, but the prefilled syringe is 1 mL and the gradations are hard to see; what is the safest CMA action?

Ask for a clearly marked syringe/second verification and measure carefully before administering because dose accuracy is a core medication right; the trap is “eyeballing” half.
Question 1139

You are about to administer an IM injection and notice the medication label on the vial does not match the provider’s order in the EHR; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and verify with the provider/pharmacy and the order before preparing the dose because medication-rights discrepancies must be resolved before administration; the trap is assuming it’s a “similar” drug and proceeding.
Question 1140

While drawing up a vaccine, you realize you forgot to check the expiration date and lot number; the syringe is already filled—what is the best next step?

Pause to check and document the lot number and expiration (and discard if expired/unknown) because vaccine tracking is required for safety/recalls; the trap is giving it and trying to find the box afterward.
Question 1141

While wearing gloves, you answer the phone at the nurses’ station and then return to the patient to adjust their dressing; what is the correct action?

Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene, and don new gloves before patient contact because gloves are single-use and become contaminated; the trap is keeping the same gloves since they “look clean.”
Question 1142

While cleaning an exam room, you notice dried blood on the exam table paper and the next patient is already waiting; what is the best action?

Don gloves and disinfect the surface with an approved bloodborne pathogen disinfectant, allowing proper contact time, before rooming the next patient because blood requires appropriate decontamination; the trap is just replacing paper to save time.
Question 1143

A patient asks why the CMA is cleaning hands even after wearing gloves during an injection visit; what is the best response?

Explain that hand hygiene is required before and after patient contact because gloves can have microscopic defects and removal can contaminate hands; the trap is saying gloves make handwashing unnecessary.
Question 1144

Discard it and obtain a new sterile pack because a compromised wrapper breaks sterility; the trap is using it because the contents “might still be clean.”
Question 1145

In the waiting area, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per clinic protocol) and begin CPR while someone retrieves the AED because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is searching for a pulse for an extended time or moving the patient unnecessarily.
Question 1146

Assist the patient to a safe position (sit/lie supine with legs elevated) and call for help/notify the provider while rechecking vitals, because preventing a fall and supporting perfusion is the priority and the common mistake is continuing the intake or walking the patient to the exam room.
