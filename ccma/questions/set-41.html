Question 4001

While rooming a patient, the CMA sees the EHR’s “reason for visit” is auto-filled as “annual physical,” but the patient states they are here for “worsening asthma symptoms and inhaler refill”; what should the CMA do next before the provider enters?

Update the EHR with the patient-stated chief complaint and verify key details (onset/severity/med list) in the appropriate fields so the provider has accurate documentation for care and coding; the common mistake is leaving incorrect auto-populated text that can mislead clinical decisions and create billing/record errors.
Question 4002

A patient checking in insists you fax her visit summary to her employer “right now” and offers to sign “whatever you need,” but she cannot name the exact fax number and says her manager will call with it later; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Verify the recipient and obtain a completed, specific written authorization (exact recipient/fax number, what records, purpose, expiration) before sending anything because HIPAA permits disclosure only with proper authorization—common mistake is faxing to an unverified number based on a verbal request.
Question 4003

A patient asks you to “change the diagnosis code so insurance will pay,” because the service was denied as non-covered; what should the CMA do?

Refuse and explain that codes must reflect the provider’s documentation, then refer billing/provider for appropriate review because altering codes is fraud; the trap is “helping” by selecting a payable code not supported by the record.
Question 4004

A patient leaves an angry voicemail with detailed symptoms and threats to sue, and you need to document it in the EHR; what is the best documentation approach?

Document objectively using direct quotes, time/date, and actions taken (who was notified) because neutral, factual charting is legally defensible; the trap is adding opinions like “patient is crazy” or omitting your follow-up steps.
Question 4005

A patient messages through the portal: “Please change my allergy list—I’m not allergic to penicillin”; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Route the request to the provider/clinical team for verification and update per protocol rather than independently removing an allergy because it impacts prescribing safety; the trap is editing allergies without confirmation.
Question 4006

At check-in, a patient states they are 17 and here alone for care; the parent calls demanding details of today’s visit; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Do not disclose any information and escalate to the supervisor/provider to determine consent and disclosure rights based on visit type and state law because minors’ confidentiality varies; the trap is automatically telling the parent because they are the guardian.
Question 4007

You are documenting an injection and can’t recall the exact lot number after discarding the vial box; what should you do?

Pause and obtain the correct lot/expiration from an approved source (e.g., inventory log or vaccine management record) before finalizing documentation because accuracy is legally and clinically critical; the trap is guessing or leaving it blank.
Question 4008

A provider asks you to “backdate” yesterday’s note so it matches the encounter date for billing; what is the CMA’s best response?

Refuse and inform the provider/supervisor that entries must reflect the actual documentation date/time with a late entry/addendum if needed because backdating is fraud; the trap is thinking it’s a harmless billing fix.
Question 4009

A law enforcement officer shows up requesting a patient’s address and last visit date “for an investigation”; what should the CMA do next?

Refer the request to the privacy officer/manager and require proper legal authorization (e.g., subpoena/court order or applicable exception) before releasing PHI because not all requests are permitted; the trap is assuming a badge equals permission.
Question 4010

In the waiting room, a patient asks loudly, “Why is my appointment taking so long—are you seeing someone with COVID?”; what is the best response?

Apologize for the delay and provide a general update without confirming any patient condition because acknowledging another patient’s diagnosis is a HIPAA disclosure; the trap is trying to reassure by sharing specifics.
Question 4011

A patient storms out after a dispute and says, “Fine, I’m never coming back,” and you think they may miss critical follow-up; what should the CMA do?

Document the interaction factually and promptly notify the provider/office manager so appropriate follow-up/outreach can be attempted because abandonment risk and continuity of care must be addressed; the trap is documenting emotional opinions or doing nothing.
Question 4012

A patient scheduled for a procedure says they never signed any consent forms and asks you to “just sign for me since you work here”; what is the CMA’s best action?

Do not sign; notify the provider so the procedure consent can be reviewed and signed by the patient (with proper witness if required) because consent must be voluntary and by the patient/legal rep; the trap is acting as a surrogate signer.
Question 4013

At check-in, a patient says, “I changed my address and insurance,” and hands you a new card while the provider is running 20 minutes behind; what should the CMA do first to prevent claim denial and HIPAA issues?

Verify the patient’s identity using two identifiers and then update the demographics/insurance in the EHR and scan the new card before the encounter is billed, because billing uses the registration data and relying on old information or discussing details where others can overhear is a common error.
Question 4014

You leave a voicemail to confirm tomorrow’s appointment and start to mention the patient’s diagnosis, then realize it’s a shared family phone; what should the CMA do BEST?

Keep the message minimal (name, clinic callback number, appointment time) without diagnosis or test details because voicemails can be accessed by others; the trap is leaving detailed clinical information for convenience.
Question 4015

At checkout, a patient loudly asks, “Why did my insurance deny my claim—was it because of my diagnosis?” while other patients are within earshot; what should the CMA do next?

Move the conversation to a private area and discuss only the minimum necessary billing details (or connect the patient with the billing specialist) because diagnosis/claim information is PHI and discussing it in a public space is a common HIPAA breach.
Question 4016

While scheduling, the patient says they can only come at 8 a.m. but the provider has no openings; the patient insists “just squeeze me in and skip my copay”; what is the CMA’s best action?

Offer the next available appropriate slot or waitlist option and follow financial policy (do not waive copays without authorization) because scheduling and billing must be consistent and non-discriminatory; the trap is making unofficial exceptions.
Question 4017

During checkout, a patient asks you to code the visit as “preventive” so insurance will pay, even though the provider documented a problem-focused visit; what should the CMA do?

Do not change coding; route the request to billing/provider for review because coding must reflect documented services and altering it is fraud; the trap is “helping” by changing codes to satisfy coverage.
Question 4018

A patient asks to “see what you wrote” in today’s visit note while you’re still rooming them and the provider hasn’t signed it yet; what should the CMA do next?

Direct the patient to the office’s medical records/release process (or designated portal access) and notify the provider/office manager as needed, because patients can request access but staff should not hand over unsigned/draft documentation; the trap is casually showing the screen in the exam room.
Question 4019

Politely explain you must verify identity before sharing any details and offer alternatives (call back, portal, come with ID) because disclosure without verification risks HIPAA breach; the trap is giving information to de-escalate.
Question 4020

Question 4021

Question 4022

Use neutral language and protect privacy (e.g., “We’re bringing patients back based on clinical need and appointment flow”) without referencing anyone else’s care, because even confirming another patient’s status can be a HIPAA disclosure; the trap is explaining the other patient’s reason for being seen.

Request the provider document the diagnosis supporting the code before billing because coding must be supported by documentation; the trap is adding a diagnosis to maximize reimbursement without chart support.
Question 4089

You receive a faxed referral request missing the patient’s date of birth and insurance information, and the patient is scheduled tomorrow—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Contact the referring office/patient to obtain the missing identifiers and insurance details before processing because incomplete demographics can cause misidentification and claim denial; the trap is creating a chart with guesses and “fixing later.”
Question 4090

A patient asks you to email their X-ray report to a personal Gmail account because the portal is “too hard”—what should the CMA do?

Use the approved secure method (patient portal, secure fax, or records process) and do not email PHI to an unsecured account because standard email is not HIPAA-secure; the trap is doing it “with the patient’s permission” without secure safeguards.
Question 4091

While entering immunization history, you notice the last Tdap date was typed as 2015 but the scanned record shows 2005—what should the CMA do BEST?

Correct the entry using the scanned source and document the source of verification because accurate records drive safe care; the trap is leaving a known discrepancy “for later,” which can lead to inappropriate treatment.
Question 4092

A coworker asks you to look up their neighbor’s medication list “because I’m worried about them”—what is the CMA’s correct action?

Decline and do not access the chart without a job-related need because accessing PHI out of curiosity is a HIPAA violation; the trap is thinking good intentions justify chart access.
Question 4093

You receive a subpoena by fax requesting a patient’s full record and the requester is pressuring you for immediate release—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Forward it to the office manager/privacy officer/provider for legal review before releasing any records because subpoenas require verification and proper process; the trap is sending records immediately because it “looks official.”
Question 4094

A patient asks, “What does my pathology report mean—do I have cancer?” immediately after you scanned it into the EHR—what should the CMA do BEST?

Tell the patient you can’t interpret results and promptly route the message to the provider for explanation because interpretation is outside CMA scope; the trap is paraphrasing the report or offering reassurance based on assumptions.
Question 4095

A patient asks for a printed copy of their lab results at checkout, but their spouse is standing at the desk and answers questions for them; what is the BEST action?

Verify the patient’s identity and ask the patient privately whether they authorize release to the spouse because assuming “family = okay” is a common HIPAA mistake.
Question 4096

While documenting a procedure, you realize you forgot to note the patient’s tolerance and post-procedure instructions—what should the CMA do NEXT?

Add a timely addendum with objective details (tolerance, teaching, and patient response) because complete documentation supports continuity and legal defense; the trap is leaving it out because “it went fine.”
Question 4097

A parent calls asking for their 17-year-old’s pregnancy test result and says, “I’m the parent, I have a right to know,” but your state often protects minors’ reproductive privacy—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Follow clinic policy/state law by not disclosing and escalate to the supervisor/provider to verify minor-consent confidentiality rules because minors may control certain results; the trap is assuming parent access always overrides confidentiality.
Question 4098

While rooming a patient, you notice yesterday’s progress note in the EHR lists the wrong medication dose entered under your login; what should you do FIRST?

Notify the provider/office policy lead and correct the entry using the EHR’s approved amendment process (date/time, reason, initials) because altering or deleting the original is a legal trap and can look like falsification.
Question 4099

You are scheduling a follow-up and the patient says they can only come on certain days; the provider ordered a 2-week recheck for blood pressure—what is the CMA’s BEST scheduling action?

Offer the nearest appropriate appointment within the ordered timeframe and notify the provider/team if the patient cannot meet the timeframe because timing affects clinical safety; the trap is booking far out without alerting anyone.
Question 4100

A patient’s insurance denies a claim stating “patient not covered on date of service,” and the patient insists they gave the card at check-in—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Verify eligibility and the insurance information entered (member ID, group number, effective dates) and correct any registration errors before resubmitting because denials are often data/coverage issues; the trap is immediately blaming the payer or resubmitting unchanged.
