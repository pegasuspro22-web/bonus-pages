Question 2301

You are about to put on PPE to irrigate a wound that may splash—what PPE is most appropriate?

Gloves, gown, and eye/face protection because splashes to mucous membranes are a key exposure route; the trap is wearing only gloves and risking eye contamination.
Question 2302

You are preparing an IM vaccine, and the patient says they had a severe allergy to a previous dose but “it was years ago”; the provider hasn’t seen them yet—what should you do next?

Hold the injection and immediately inform the provider because a history of severe reaction requires clinical decision-making; the trap is proceeding because the order exists or because the reaction was “in the past.”
Question 2303

A coworker asks you to recap a used needle “just this once” so they can carry it to the sharps container—what should you do?

Do not recap; activate the safety device and place the needle directly into a sharps container because recapping increases needlestick risk; the trap is prioritizing convenience over exposure prevention.
Question 2304

After removing your gloves, you plan to sanitize your hands but notice a visible smear of blood on your finger—what is the best action?

Wash with soap and water immediately because visible soil requires washing, not sanitizer alone; the trap is relying on alcohol gel when hands are visibly contaminated.
Question 2305

After an IM injection, the patient becomes pale, diaphoretic, and says they feel like they will faint—what is the best immediate action?

Assist them to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if tolerated) and stay with them while calling for help because vasovagal reactions can lead to falls; the trap is sending them to the lobby or leaving them alone.
Question 2306

In the exam room, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; you don’t see normal breathing—what should you do first in the outpatient clinic?

Activate the emergency response (call for help/911 per protocol) and start CPR while someone retrieves the AED because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is checking vitals or searching for a pulse for too long before starting compressions.
Question 2307

During intake, a 58-year-old reports chest pressure and looks pale; before finishing the health history, what should the CMA do first?

Stop the intake, seat/position the patient safely and alert the provider/activate the clinic’s emergency response immediately because chest pain is potentially life-threatening and the trap is “finishing the forms/vitals first” and delaying escalation.
Question 2308

You draw up an ordered injection but the patient’s name and date of birth on the wristband do not match the chart—what should you do first?

Pause and re-identify the patient using two identifiers and resolve the discrepancy before giving anything because wrong-patient errors are preventable; the trap is relying on room number or verbal confirmation alone.
Question 2309

While preparing to administer an injection, you notice the medication label concentration does not match what the order in the chart states—what is the best action?

Stop and clarify with the provider/pharmacist/clinical lead before preparing/administering because concentration mismatches can cause dosing errors; the trap is “doing the math” and giving it anyway without verification.
Question 2310

While taking an adult’s blood pressure, the cuff is placed over a long-sleeve sweater and you get 182/96; what is the best next step?

Recheck BP with the correct cuff size on a bare arm after a brief rest because improper technique can falsely elevate readings; the trap is documenting/acting on an artifact value without confirmation.
Question 2311

A teen arrives for a sports physical and says they feel dizzy when standing; which patient position is best before obtaining vital signs?

Have the patient lie supine (or sit with support if supine not possible) before repeating vitals because safety and fall prevention come first; the trap is standing them for orthostatic vitals before stabilizing.
Question 2312

During a dressing change, the provider uses sterile supplies and asks you to open the sterile kit—what is the best technique to avoid contamination?

Open the outer flap away first, then side flaps, then the flap toward you, keeping your hands above waist and out of the sterile field because crossing over or reaching in contaminates; the trap is leaning over the field to “help.”
Question 2313

You record a pulse of 48 bpm in an adult who denies athletic training and appears lightheaded; what should the CMA do next?

Reassess the pulse for a full minute and notify the provider promptly because symptomatic bradycardia needs evaluation; the trap is assuming it’s normal or only documenting without reporting symptoms.
Question 2314

During intake in an urgent care clinic, a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 86/52 with a manual cuff after walking from the lobby; the patient is pale but talking—what should the CMA do next?

Have the patient sit or lie supine with legs elevated, recheck the BP promptly, and immediately notify the provider while staying with the patient because symptomatic hypotension can signal instability; the common trap is documenting and continuing the intake without addressing positioning and escalation first.
Question 2315

You are about to enter an exam room to remove a dressing and notice your hands are visibly soiled after cleaning a spill; what should you do next?

Wash with soap and water before patient contact because visibly soiled hands require washing (not just sanitizer); the trap is using alcohol rub when it won’t remove organic material effectively.
Question 2316

After removing gloves, you realize you still need to adjust the patient’s glasses and open the door; what is the correct next step?

Perform hand hygiene immediately after glove removal before touching anything because gloves don’t replace hand hygiene; the trap is touching “clean” items right after doffing gloves.
Question 2317

A provider requests help setting up a sterile field for a minor procedure; what is the CMA’s best practice when placing sterile packages on the field?

Open packages without reaching over the sterile field and keep hands above waist level because reaching over/low hands contaminate the field; the trap is “quickly” leaning across the field and breaking sterility.
Question 2318

A patient with uncontrolled coughing is checking in for a routine visit; what should the CMA do first to reduce infection spread?

Provide a mask and perform hand hygiene (and place them away from others) because source control interrupts transmission; the trap is rooming them first without addressing respiratory hygiene.
Question 2319

You obtain respirations immediately after telling a patient you’re going to “count their breathing,” and it reads 24; what is the best action?

Recount respirations discreetly (without alerting the patient) because awareness changes breathing pattern; the trap is accepting an anxiety-influenced rate as baseline.
Question 2320

You enter a room wearing gloves and the patient asks you to silence their phone; what is the best action?

Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene, then handle the phone (or ask the patient to do it) because personal items are contaminated surfaces and you must prevent cross-contamination; the trap is touching multiple surfaces with the same gloves.
Question 2321

Immediately after an injection, the patient becomes flushed, wheezes, and says their throat feels tight; what should the CMA do first?

Activate the emergency response/notify the provider STAT and stay with the patient while positioning for breathing because this suggests anaphylaxis; the trap is sending them to the waiting room or “getting water” first.
Question 2322

A patient with a known latex allergy is scheduled for an injection; what should the CMA do before preparing supplies?

Select latex-free gloves and equipment and verify the product packaging is latex-free because exposure can trigger a reaction; the trap is assuming “non-latex gloves alone” are enough while using latex-containing vial stoppers/supplies.
Question 2323

The provider orders a subcutaneous injection and the patient is very thin; what technique helps avoid injecting into muscle?

Pinch the skin and use the appropriate needle/angle (commonly 45° if minimal tissue) because proper technique ensures correct route; the trap is using a 90° angle automatically regardless of body habitus.
Question 2324

A patient is on contact precautions for suspected scabies and you must take vitals; what PPE should the CMA use at minimum?

Gown and gloves (plus hand hygiene) because contact precautions target skin-to-surface spread; the trap is using only gloves and contaminating clothing that then spreads organisms.
Question 2325

A patient returns after a blood draw with a growing bruise at the site and reports tenderness; what should the CMA do next?

Apply firm pressure and a cold compress (if available) and reassess because this is consistent with a hematoma and early compression limits bleeding; the trap is ignoring it or only offering a bandage without pressure.
Question 2326

While changing a wound dressing, you notice the outer glove touched the bed rail; what should you do to maintain asepsis?

Remove contaminated gloves, perform hand hygiene, and don new gloves before continuing because touching non-sterile surfaces contaminates care; the trap is continuing “carefully” with contaminated gloves.
Question 2327

You’re preparing an IM vaccine and notice the vial label doesn’t match the EHR order even though the patient says “that’s the shot I always get”; what should you do first?

Stop and verify the medication against the order (and clarify with the provider/pharmacist) before drawing it up because the “right medication” must be confirmed; the trap is relying on patient memory and proceeding.
Question 2328

During rooming, a 68-year-old patient says they feel “lightheaded,” then becomes pale and slumps in the chair while you are about to take blood pressure; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a safe position (supine with legs elevated if tolerated) and call for help/activate the clinic’s emergency response, because protecting airway/circulation and preventing a fall is the priority; a common mistake is trying to finish vital signs before stabilizing the patient.
Question 2329

During intake a 58-year-old reports dizziness; you obtain BP 84/52, HR 118, and the patient is pale but awake—what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and call for the provider/activate clinic emergency response because these are shock-like vitals; the trap is finishing the rest of the intake or rechecking later instead of immediately supporting perfusion and escalating.
Question 2330

You are about to enter an exam room for a patient with uncontrolled coughing and you’ll be within 3 feet to take vitals; what PPE should you use first?

Perform hand hygiene and don a mask (and eye protection if splash risk) because droplet exposure is likely at close range; the trap is relying on gloves alone, which don’t protect mucous membranes.
Question 2331

A patient’s oral temperature is 101.9°F after drinking hot coffee in the waiting room; what is the best next step before reporting fever?

Wait 15–20 minutes and recheck using the correct method because recent hot liquids can falsely elevate oral readings; the trap is documenting and alerting the provider based on an inaccurate measurement.
Question 2332

While taking vitals, a 24-year-old becomes lightheaded and says they might faint; what should the CMA do next?

Stop the procedure, assist the patient to a safe position (sit/lie down), and assess responsiveness because preventing a fall is priority; the trap is “pushing through” to finish vitals.
Question 2333

A patient’s pulse feels irregular during routine vitals, but they deny symptoms; what is the best CMA action?

Reassess apical pulse for a full minute and report/document the irregularity because an irregular radial pulse can be misleading; the trap is estimating for 15 seconds or ignoring it because the patient feels fine.
Question 2334

A patient arrives for a wound check and you notice visible dried blood on your last-used stethoscope diaphragm; what is the best next step before continuing care?

Clean and disinfect the stethoscope with an approved disinfectant because it’s a noncritical item requiring disinfection between patients; the trap is wiping with a dry tissue or proceeding because it “looks mostly clean.”
Question 2335

You find an unresponsive patient in the lobby and another staff member says they “might have just fainted”; what should the CMA do first?

Check responsiveness and breathing and activate emergency response/911 (and get the AED) immediately because early recognition and activation saves time in cardiac arrest; the trap is spending time searching for history or moving the patient before assessment.
Question 2336

You obtain BP 168/104 in a patient here for a refill who says they “feel normal”; what should the CMA do next?

Recheck in 5 minutes with correct cuff size and positioning, then notify the provider of the confirmed elevated reading because technique errors can falsely elevate BP; the trap is documenting one reading without confirmation.
Question 2337

After giving an IM injection, the patient says, “I feel itchy and my throat feels tight”; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response, stay with the patient, and be ready to assist with epinephrine per standing orders/provider direction because this suggests anaphylaxis; the trap is having them “wait in the lobby” to see if it passes.
Question 2338

A patient is scheduled for an IM injection but states they have a severe latex allergy; what should the CMA do next?

Stop and verify that all supplies (gloves, syringe components, vial stopper, bandage) are latex-free and alert the provider if alternatives are needed because exposure can trigger anaphylaxis; the trap is proceeding with “just one bandage” that may contain latex.
Question 2339

You draw up a vaccine and then realize you did not clean the rubber stopper of a multi-dose vial; what is the best action?

Discard the syringe, disinfect the stopper with alcohol, and prepare a new dose because contamination risk can’t be undone once accessed; the trap is injecting anyway since the needle was “sterile.”
Question 2340

The provider orders 0.5 mL of a medication; you accidentally draw up 0.7 mL and notice before entering the room—what is the best next step?

Expel the excess using aseptic technique to the correct dose (or discard and redraw if sterility is compromised) because accurate dosing is part of medication safety; the trap is “rounding up” since it seems close.
Question 2341

During intake, a 54-year-old patient seated for vitals says “I’m feeling dizzy,” looks pale, and begins sliding down in the chair as you finish the blood pressure reading. What should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient into a safe supine position with legs elevated and stay with them while calling for the provider/clinic help, because preventing a fall and supporting perfusion is the immediate priority; a common mistake is continuing the vitals or sending the patient to the exam room unassisted.
Question 2342

You are about to administer a vaccine and the patient says they are unsure what it is for and looks hesitant; what should the CMA do next?

Pause and provide the Vaccine Information Statement/required education and confirm consent/understanding before giving it because informed consent is required; the trap is proceeding because the patient “showed up for the appointment.”
Question 2343

While preparing an injection, you notice the medication label doesn’t match the provider’s order in the chart; what should the CMA do first?

Hold the medication and clarify the discrepancy with the provider before administration because giving the wrong drug is a preventable error; the trap is assuming the pharmacy label is correct and administering anyway.
Question 2344

After removing gloves, your hands look clean and you’re heading to the next room; what should you do next?

Perform hand hygiene immediately because glove removal can contaminate hands; the trap is skipping hand hygiene because gloves were worn.
Question 2345

Stop the intake and alert the provider/activate emergency response immediately while keeping the patient seated or supine and monitoring airway/breathing/circulation, because hypotension with chest pressure is potentially life-threatening and the common trap is continuing routine vitals/history instead of escalating.
Question 2346

Keep the patient seated, recheck BP manually after a brief rest, and immediately alert the provider/activate urgent protocol because this may be a hypertensive emergency; the trap is documenting it and letting the patient wait or sending them to the lobby.
Question 2347

Question 2348

Question 2349

Question 2350

Place the patient in Sims’ (left lateral) position because it provides safe access and comfort; the trap is using supine, which increases discomfort and risk of improper insertion.
Question 2351
