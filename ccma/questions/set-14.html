Question 1301

The provider orders 0.5 mL of a medication, but the syringe you grabbed is a 3 mL syringe with wide markings and you’re unsure of precision; what should the CMA do next?

Use an appropriately sized syringe (e.g., 1 mL) to measure accurately, because dosing errors often occur from using the wrong syringe and guessing at markings.
Question 1302

You prepare to administer a vaccine and the patient says they feel lightheaded with shots; what positioning is best before giving the injection?

Have the patient seated or supine with support, because syncope and falls are common after injections and standing is a frequent safety mistake.
Question 1303

Right before an injection, the patient says, “I’m allergic to latex,” and you already opened supplies; what is the best action?

Stop and replace with latex-free equipment and alert the provider if needed, because proceeding can trigger a reaction and “the needle is latex-free” doesn’t address gloves/bandages.
Question 1304

After removing gloves, you plan to answer the phone “just for a second” before washing your hands; what should you do instead?

Perform hand hygiene immediately after glove removal, because gloves are not a substitute for handwashing and touching shared equipment is a common way pathogens spread.
Question 1305

You are positioning a patient for an abdominal exam and they say they are short of breath when lying flat; what is the best positioning adjustment?

Raise the head of the table (semi-Fowler’s) and notify the provider as needed, because forcing supine can worsen breathing and patient comfort/safety comes first.
Question 1306

During CPR in the clinic, an AED arrives and another staff member says, “Keep compressions going while I analyze”; what should the CMA do?

Stop compressions during AED rhythm analysis and ensure no one touches the patient, because movement can interfere with analysis and touching the patient risks safety during shock delivery.
Question 1307

During intake, an adult’s BP reads 188/112 and they say they feel “fine” and want to proceed with their sports physical; what should the CMA do first?

Recheck the BP manually after 5 minutes of rest with the correct cuff size and arm at heart level, because an initial high reading may be technique-related and the trap is immediately “clearing” the patient without confirming a potentially urgent value.
Question 1308

During a wound dressing change, the patient winces and says the pain is suddenly much worse than earlier; what should the CMA do next?

Pause the procedure, assess for bleeding/changes, and notify the provider, because new or escalating pain can signal a complication and continuing is a common mistake.
Question 1309

A provider asks you to administer an IM injection, but you notice the medication label doesn’t match the order (different concentration); what should the CMA do first?

Hold the medication and clarify with the provider/pharmacist per clinic policy before preparing the dose, because giving it “as usual” is a common trap that leads to wrong-dose errors.
Question 1310

While taking an oral temperature, the patient says they just drank hot coffee in the waiting room; what should the CMA do next?

Wait about 15 minutes and retake (or use a tympanic/temporal method per clinic practice), because recent hot liquids falsely elevate oral readings and the trap is documenting an inaccurate fever.
Question 1311

A patient stands up from the exam table after vitals and becomes pale and dizzy; what is the CMA’s best next action?

Assist them back to a safe position (sit or supine) and check BP/pulse, because preventing a fall is the immediate priority and the trap is continuing the visit or sending them to the lobby.
Question 1312

In the waiting area, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per protocol) and start CPR if no pulse/breathing, because early activation and compressions save lives and searching for the chart is a common delay.
Question 1313

A 6-year-old’s respirations are irregular and the child is crying during counting; how should the CMA obtain the most accurate respiratory rate?

Count respirations unobtrusively for a full minute after the child calms (or while pretending to take the pulse), because crying skews the rate and the trap is announcing you’re counting breaths and worsening distress.
Question 1314

An older adult’s pulse is irregular on initial check; what should the CMA do next before reporting it?

Count the apical pulse for a full minute, because irregular rhythms require longer assessment and the trap is charting a 15-second estimate that can miss clinically significant irregularity.
Question 1315

After removing gloves, the CMA notices a small tear on one fingertip that may have contacted the patient’s skin; what should the CMA do first?

Perform hand hygiene immediately and assess for any exposure to blood/body fluids, because hand hygiene is required even after glove removal and the trap is assuming gloves alone prevent contamination.
Question 1316

A patient arrives for a wound check and the dressing is saturated with drainage; how should the CMA handle the used dressing during removal?

Remove and discard it directly into an appropriate biohazard container without placing it on the counter, because contaminated materials spread organisms and the trap is setting it down “just for a second.”
Question 1317

A provider requests sterile dressing supplies but the outside of a sterile package is wet from the sink area; what should the CMA do?

Discard the package and obtain a new sterile set, because moisture wicks microorganisms through packaging and the trap is opening it anyway because the inside “looks clean.”
Question 1318

You enter the room to room a patient with a frequent cough and visible nasal drainage; what PPE should the CMA apply first?

Perform hand hygiene and don a surgical mask (then eye protection if splash risk), because respiratory source control reduces transmission and the trap is skipping the mask because it’s “just a cold.”
Question 1319

A patient in a wheelchair is due for orthostatic vitals but cannot safely stand; what is the CMA’s best action?

Notify the provider and document that standing measurements could not be safely obtained, because patient safety and accurate reporting come first and the trap is forcing standing and risking a fall.
Question 1320

The CMA is preparing to irrigate a wound and expects splashing; what is the best PPE choice?

Gloves plus gown and eye/face protection (goggles/face shield), because irrigation creates splash risk and the trap is wearing only gloves and contaminating clothing or mucous membranes.
Question 1321

Immediately after an IM injection, the patient reports tingling and sharp pain radiating down the arm; what should the CMA do next?

Stop the injection (if still injecting), assess, and notify the provider promptly, because this may indicate nerve irritation/injury and the trap is telling the patient it’s “normal” and sending them out.
Question 1322

A patient scheduled for a vaccine says they feel lightheaded when they see needles and previously fainted; what should the CMA do before giving the injection?

Seat or place the patient in a supine position and ensure a safe environment, because preventing syncope-related injury is priority and the trap is vaccinating them standing at the counter.
Question 1323

The provider orders a subcutaneous medication, but the CMA is preparing a 1.5-inch needle because it’s what’s available; what should the CMA do?

Obtain the correct needle/syringe for the ordered route before administering, because wrong equipment increases tissue injury and absorption changes and the trap is “making it work” to save time.
Question 1324

While cleaning an exam room, the CMA finds a used needle left on the tray; what should the CMA do next?

Place it immediately into a sharps container without recapping, because sharps disposal prevents needlestick injuries and the trap is recapping “to be safe,” which increases injury risk.
Question 1325

During a dressing change, the CMA cleans a surgical incision from the dirtiest area outward to “get the worst part first”; what should be done instead?

Clean from the cleanest area to the dirtiest using a new gauze each stroke, because this prevents dragging microbes into the incision and the trap is starting where drainage is heaviest.
Question 1326

In the waiting room, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what is the CMA’s first action?

Check responsiveness and call for help/activate emergency response (have someone call 911 and bring the AED), because rapid activation and AED access saves time and the trap is running for supplies before initiating the response.
Question 1327

The provider orders an IM injection; the patient says they are allergic to latex and you notice the syringe tip cap packaging indicates latex; what should the CMA do first?

Stop and obtain latex-free supplies (and alert the provider if needed), because preventing an allergic reaction is critical and the trap is proceeding since the latex “won’t touch the patient.”
Question 1328

While rooming a patient for an annual physical, you measure a blood pressure of 186/112 mm Hg; the patient says they feel “fine” and asks to proceed with the visit—what should the CMA do next?

Recheck the BP after the patient rests quietly for about 5 minutes with proper cuff size/arm position, and if it remains severely elevated notify the provider immediately because this level can indicate a hypertensive urgency/emergency and the common trap is documenting it as “high” and moving on without confirmation and escalation.
Question 1329

During intake, a 58-year-old reports “pressure in my chest” and looks pale; you haven’t taken vitals yet and the provider is with another patient—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Activate the clinic’s emergency response (get help/notify provider immediately and prepare AED) because possible cardiac event takes priority over completing vitals; the trap is delaying escalation to “finish intake.”
Question 1330

You’re rooming a patient and need to palpate the abdomen; which position is BEST to place the patient in?

Supine with knees flexed (or pillow under knees) because it relaxes abdominal muscles for comfort and accuracy; the trap is leaving the patient sitting upright, which increases guarding.
Question 1331

A seated BP reads 182/110 on an adult who denies symptoms and says they “rush-stressed” to the appointment; what is the BEST next step before reporting it?

Recheck BP after 5 minutes of quiet rest with correct cuff size and arm at heart level because technique/anxiety can falsely elevate readings; the trap is reporting a single potentially inaccurate reading as confirmed.
Question 1332

During intake at an urgent care clinic, a 58-year-old reports sudden dizziness and looks pale; you obtain vital signs showing BP 84/52, pulse 118, RR 22, and they say they might faint—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Assist the patient to a supine position with legs elevated and immediately alert the provider/activate emergency response while reassessing vitals, because suspected shock/syncope requires rapid perfusion support and escalation; a common mistake is leaving them seated to finish paperwork or walking them to an exam room, increasing fall risk.
Question 1333

A patient becomes lightheaded and diaphoretic immediately after standing for a weight measurement; what should the CMA do FIRST?

Assist the patient to a safe position (sit or lie down) and assess responsiveness because fall prevention and airway/breathing/circulation come before continuing measurements; the trap is trying to “finish the weight” while the patient is unstable.
Question 1334

A 6-year-old’s respirations are 34/min at rest with nasal flaring during intake; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Stop routine intake and notify the provider promptly while keeping the child upright because signs of respiratory distress require immediate escalation; the trap is waiting until all vitals are completed.
Question 1335

A patient is pulseless and not breathing and the AED arrives; what should the CMA do next?

Turn on the AED, apply pads, and follow prompts while minimizing interruptions in CPR, because early defibrillation is key and the trap is delaying pad placement to continue compressions without integrating the AED.
Question 1336

While taking a radial pulse, you note it is irregular and you count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4; what is the BEST action?

Count for a full 60 seconds and report/document irregularity because short counts are unreliable with irregular rhythms; the trap is using a standard shortcut that under/overestimates the rate.
Question 1337

You are preparing an IM injection and the medication label reads “10 mg/mL,” order is “5 mg IM”; what should the CMA do BEFORE preparing the syringe?

Calculate the volume (0.5 mL) and double-check the math/label against the order because dose-to-volume conversion errors cause overdoses; the trap is drawing up “5 mL” by confusing mg with mL.
Question 1338

A patient with a cough asks you to lower your mask so they can “hear you better” at check-in; what is the BEST response?

Keep your mask on and offer communication alternatives (speak clearly, move to a private area, use written instructions) because source control protects patients and staff; the trap is removing PPE for convenience.
Question 1339

In an exam room, you remove gloves after cleaning a small blood spill and then reach for the doorknob; what should you do NEXT?

Perform hand hygiene before leaving the room because contaminated glove removal can transfer pathogens to hands; the trap is assuming glove removal alone prevents spread.
Question 1340

During room turnover, the previous patient used the exam table and BP cuff; what should be cleaned FIRST to reduce cross-contamination?

Clean/disinfect high-touch items the next patient will contact (exam table surface, BP cuff, stethoscope) because these are common vectors; the trap is focusing on low-touch areas while missing patient-contact surfaces.
Question 1341

After drawing up medication for an injection, you accidentally touch the sterile needle to the countertop; what is the BEST next step?

Discard the contaminated needle/syringe appropriately and prepare a new dose using aseptic technique because sterility is broken; the trap is “wiping it off” with alcohol and using it anyway.
Question 1342

Right before administering a vaccine, the patient says, “I faint every time I get shots”; what should the CMA do BEST to prevent injury?

Have the patient seated or supine and observe after administration because syncope risk makes positioning a safety priority; the trap is giving the injection while the patient stands.
Question 1343

You’re told to place a patient with suspected shingles rash on the trunk into a room; what is the BEST infection-control action within CMA scope?

Apply standard precautions and ensure the rash is covered and the patient is roomed promptly while notifying the provider for isolation guidance because transmission risk is reduced by covering lesions; the trap is treating every rash as requiring sterile technique instead of appropriate precautions.
Question 1344

You are preparing to give an IM injection and realize you didn’t perform hand hygiene after removing your jacket; what should you do immediately BEFORE touching supplies?

Perform hand hygiene because it is required before any clean/aseptic task; the trap is thinking gloves alone replace hand hygiene.
Question 1345

A patient’s oral temperature reads 95.9°F after they were chewing ice in the waiting room 2 minutes ago—what is the best next step?

Wait the recommended interval (about 15 minutes) and retake the temperature (or use an alternate route per clinic policy) because recent cold intake falsely lowers oral readings; the trap is documenting a false hypothermia.
Question 1346

During intake, a 58-year-old reports dizziness and you obtain BP 86/52 with HR 122; the patient is pale but awake—what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a supine position with legs elevated and notify the provider immediately because this suggests possible shock/hypotension; the trap is continuing the interview or rechecking multiple times while the patient deteriorates.
Question 1347

A patient arrives for suture removal but the wound edges look separated with yellow drainage; what should the CMA do NEXT?

Do not remove the sutures and notify the provider for evaluation because dehiscence/infection needs provider assessment; the trap is proceeding because “they’re due for removal.”
Question 1348

After giving an IM injection, you notice a small drop of blood and the patient asks you to massage the site vigorously; what should you do?

Apply gentle pressure with gauze and place a bandage if needed because vigorous massage can increase bleeding/bruising and alter absorption for some meds; the trap is massaging automatically for every injection.
Question 1349

In the lobby, an adult collapses and is unresponsive; a coworker is calling 911—what should the CMA do FIRST?

Start CPR and send someone to bring the AED immediately because early CPR/AED use improves survival; the trap is searching for a pulse for too long instead of beginning compressions when unresponsive and not breathing normally.
Question 1350

You prepared a subcutaneous injection, but the patient says they already received the same medication today at another clinic; what is the BEST next step?

Hold the injection and verify with the provider and documentation/records because preventing duplicate dosing is critical; the trap is administering anyway because “it’s ordered.”
Question 1351

While taking an adult’s blood pressure you hear intermittent “thumps” that disappear and then reappear as you deflate—what should you do to avoid an inaccurate reading?

Inflate 20–30 mmHg above the palpated systolic and deflate slowly, recording systolic at first sound and diastolic at disappearance because an auscultatory gap can cause underestimation; the trap is starting too low and missing true systolic.
Question 1352

While changing a simple dressing, you accidentally drop the sterile gauze onto the patient’s gown; what is the BEST action?

Discard it and use new sterile gauze because a dropped item is contaminated even if it “looks clean”; the trap is using it to avoid wasting supplies.
Question 1353

A sharps container is 3/4 full and a coworker tries to push down used needles to make space—what should the CMA do?

Stop them and replace/close the container at the fill line because pushing contents increases needlestick risk; the trap is compressing sharps to “save supplies.”
Question 1354

An adult arrives for a routine visit with RR 8/min and appears difficult to arouse—what is the CMA’s priority action?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per protocol) and begin basic life support assessment because this is a potentially life-threatening respiratory depression; the trap is trying to finish vitals or leaving the patient unattended.
Question 1355

A patient with a latex allergy needs a dressing change—what is the best way to prevent a reaction?

Use non-latex gloves and supplies and verify the allergy is clearly flagged before starting because exposure can occur through gloves and adhesive products; the trap is assuming “small contact won’t matter.”
Question 1356

You are about to obtain orthostatic vital signs on a patient who nearly fainted earlier—what is the safest sequencing?

Measure BP/HR after 5 minutes supine, then sitting, then standing while staying with the patient because position changes can trigger syncope; the trap is leaving them standing alone or skipping the supine baseline.
Question 1357

You remove gloves after cleaning a small blood spill on the counter—what must you do immediately next?

Perform hand hygiene because gloves are not a substitute and removal can contaminate hands; the trap is putting on new gloves or touching the chart before washing.
Question 1358

You realize you touched the outside of your clean gloves to a used tissue in the room while setting up for an injection—what is the best action?

Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene, and don new gloves because the gloves are contaminated; the trap is continuing because “the needle is still sterile.”
Question 1359

A patient with a productive cough is placed in an exam room and asks for water; before entering you plan to do vitals—what PPE is most appropriate?

Wear a surgical mask (and add gloves if anticipating contact with secretions) because droplet protection reduces transmission risk; the trap is using no mask because it’s “just vitals.”
Question 1360

A patient’s pulse is irregular and you count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4—what should you do next before documenting?

Recount the apical pulse for a full 60 seconds because irregular rhythms require a full minute for accuracy; the trap is using a shortened count that misrepresents the rate.
Question 1361

While preparing an IM injection, you accidentally touch the needle to the counter—what should you do?

Discard the needle in sharps and restart with a new sterile needle because sterility is broken; the trap is wiping the needle with alcohol and proceeding.
Question 1362

After an IM injection, a patient becomes lightheaded and diaphoretic while seated on the exam table—what should you do next?

Assist them to a safe position (supine or seated with support), monitor, and notify the provider because vasovagal syncope can lead to falls; the trap is having them stand to “walk it off.”
Question 1363

A patient returns from the restroom and you are about to clean an abrasion; you’ve washed your hands and donned gloves—what is the correct skin-prep direction?

Clean from the center of the wound outward using a new swab each pass because it moves contaminants away from the area of highest risk; the trap is wiping back and forth and dragging bacteria into the wound.
Question 1364

You are changing a simple wound dressing and the patient reaches toward the sterile gauze; what is the best immediate response?

Ask the patient to keep hands away and replace any supplies that were contaminated because patient contact breaks clean technique; the trap is using the same gauze since it “looks clean.”
Question 1365

A patient says, “I’m nervous—what are you injecting me with?” and you notice the syringe is unlabeled on your tray—what is the best action?

Do not administer it; discard and redraw/relabel after verifying the medication and dose because unlabeled syringes create high-risk errors; the trap is trying to “remember what it was.”
Question 1366

You are about to administer a medication injection and notice the EHR shows “penicillin allergy,” but the order is for an antibiotic injection—what should you do first?

Stop and clarify with the provider before giving the medication because allergy verification is part of safe administration; the trap is assuming it’s unrelated or relying on the patient “looking fine.”
Question 1367

The provider orders 0.5 mL of a vaccine; the vial label reads 1 mL single-dose and you draw up 0.5 mL—what is the correct next step?

Give only the ordered 0.5 mL and discard the remaining vaccine per single-dose guidance because leftover should not be saved; the trap is storing the remainder for another patient.
Question 1368

A provider requests help positioning a patient for a rectal exam—what position should the CMA assist with?

Place the patient in Sims’ (left lateral) position with the right knee flexed because it provides optimal access and comfort; the trap is using supine which limits exposure and stability.
Question 1369

During intake, a 54-year-old patient says he feels “fine,” but you obtain BP 198/112 with a regular pulse and no acute symptoms; what should the CMA do next?

Recheck BP manually after 5 minutes with the correct cuff and arm supported, then immediately report the confirmed critical reading to the provider because severe hypertension requires prompt escalation and the trap is charting a single automated value as “true” without confirmation.
Question 1370

Auscultate the apical pulse for a full 60 seconds and report the irregularity to the provider because irregular rhythms can be missed or misread by machines and the trap is documenting a pulse rate without assessing rhythm.
Question 1371

Question 1372

Question 1373

Assist the patient to sit or lie down safely and check vital signs before continuing intake because preventing a fall is the priority and the trap is “finishing vitals” while the patient is actively unstable.
Question 1374
