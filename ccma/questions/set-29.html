Question 2801

In the lobby, an adult collapses and is unresponsive; a coworker is calling 911—what should the CMA do next?

Start CPR immediately and send someone to retrieve the AED because early compressions/AED use improves survival; the trap is checking vital signs for an extended period before beginning compressions.
Question 2802

During vitals you count respirations and the patient suddenly starts watching you and breathing slower; what is the best technique to get an accurate rate?

Keep your fingers on the radial pulse and unobtrusively count respirations, because patients alter breathing when aware and the trap is announcing “I’m counting your breaths.”
Question 2803

A patient’s pulse oximeter shows 88% but they are talking in full sentences with cold fingers and dark nail polish; what should the CMA do first?

Warm the hand/remove polish if possible and recheck with a good waveform (or alternate site), because poor perfusion/artifact can cause false lows and the trap is treating the number without confirming accuracy.
Question 2804

A teen arrives for a sports physical and says they “faint with shots”; where should the CMA position the patient before vaccines?

Place the patient supine (or seated with support) and observe after injection, because preventing fall injury is priority and the trap is vaccinating while standing.
Question 2805

A patient comes for suture removal and you see dried blood on your hands from a paper cut; what is the correct action before starting care?

Perform hand hygiene and cover the cut, then don gloves, because non-intact skin increases transmission risk and the trap is relying on gloves without protecting broken skin.
Question 2806

After removing gloves, you realize you touched the outside of the glove with your bare wrist while doffing; what should you do next?

Perform hand hygiene immediately and avoid touching clean surfaces until done, because contamination can occur during removal and the trap is assuming “gloves off” automatically means “clean.”
Question 2807

During a sterile dressing change, the sterile field becomes wet from saline; what should the CMA do next?

Consider the field contaminated and set up a new sterile field, because moisture allows strike-through contamination and the trap is continuing because “it’s only saline.”
Question 2808

A patient asks why you’re wearing gloves to check their blood sugar; what is the best explanation within CMA scope?

State that gloves are standard precautions to protect both patient and staff from blood exposure, because blood is a potential biohazard even when infection status is unknown and the trap is mentioning a specific diagnosis or implying the patient is “infectious.”
Question 2809

The provider orders 0.5 mL of a vaccine, but your syringe contains visible air bubbles; what should you do next?

Remove the bubbles and re-verify the dose at eye level before administration, because air can alter delivered volume and the trap is “it’s fine for IM” without ensuring accurate dosing.
Question 2810

You’re cleaning an exam room after a draining wound visit and the provider asks for a “quick wipe”; what is the best response?

Use the approved disinfectant with the correct wet contact time on high-touch surfaces, because killing pathogens requires proper dwell time and the trap is wiping dry immediately, which doesn’t disinfect.
Question 2811

A patient with a persistent cough checks in and removes their mask to talk; what should the CMA do first?

Ask the patient to put on a mask/cover cough and separate them from others as able, because source control reduces droplet spread and the trap is focusing on paperwork before infection control.
Question 2812

You prepare an IM injection and notice the medication vial label looks similar to another vial on the counter; what should you do before drawing up?

Stop and verify the medication name/strength against the order using the label (and barcode if available), because look-alike packaging is a common error and the trap is relying on vial color/shape.
Question 2813

In an exam room a patient suddenly becomes unresponsive and you see no normal breathing; another staff member is nearby—what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response/ask the staff member to call 911 and get the AED while you begin CPR, because early CPR and AED retrieval are time-critical and the trap is searching for a pulse for too long.
Question 2814

During a wound dressing change, the patient complains of increasing pain and you see new redness spreading beyond the wound edge; what is the CMA’s best next step?

Stop and promptly report the findings to the provider before proceeding, because worsening pain and spreading erythema may indicate infection needing evaluation and the trap is finishing the dressing and mentioning it later.
Question 2815

A patient becomes pale and diaphoretic after an injection and says they feel faint; what should the CMA do next?

Assist them to a supine position with legs elevated as tolerated and monitor, notifying the provider, because this is consistent with vasovagal syncope and the trap is having them “sit up and drink water” before preventing a fall.
Question 2816

A patient scheduled for an injection says they had hives and throat tightness after the same medication last year; what should the CMA do first?

Hold the medication and notify the provider immediately, because this suggests a prior severe allergic reaction and the trap is administering first and “watching them afterward.”
Question 2817

During intake, a 64-year-old patient says, “I feel a little dizzy,” and you obtain BP 82/54 and pulse 118 after they hurried from the parking lot; what should the CMA do first?

Sit/lay the patient down safely and recheck BP manually after 1–2 minutes, because immediate fall prevention and confirming an abnormal reading comes before documenting it as “true hypotension” or sending them back to the lobby.
Question 2818

A patient’s oral temperature is 101.8°F, but they just finished a hot coffee in the waiting room; what is the best next step?

Wait about 15 minutes and retake the temperature (or use an alternate site per clinic protocol), because recent hot liquids can falsely elevate oral temps and the trap is reporting fever without controlling for intake.
Question 2819

You’re about to give a deltoid IM injection and the patient’s sleeve is tight; they offer to “just pull it up a little”; what is the best action?

Have the patient remove the arm from the sleeve or change into a gown to fully expose the site, because injecting through or near tight clothing risks contamination and poor landmarking and the trap is injecting with limited visibility.
Question 2820

After a subcutaneous injection, the patient rubs the site vigorously and asks if that helps; what should the CMA do?

Instruct them not to rub and apply gentle pressure if needed, because rubbing can increase bruising or alter absorption and the trap is encouraging rubbing to “spread the medicine.”
Question 2821

You get a pulse ox reading of 84% on a patient who is talking comfortably but has dark nail polish and cold hands; what should you do first?

Reposition the probe/warm the hand or use an alternate site and recheck, because poor perfusion and nail polish cause false lows and the trap is treating the number without confirming accuracy.
Question 2822

You need to suction a small amount of drainage from a fresh surgical wound per provider order; which technique is required?

Use sterile technique, because fresh surgical sites are high risk for introducing pathogens and the trap is using clean technique because it’s an outpatient setting.
Question 2823

You obtain BP 168/102 on a patient who is anxious and talking during the measurement; what is the best next step before reporting it?

Have the patient rest quietly with feet flat and arm supported at heart level, then repeat the BP with correct cuff/technique, because poor technique can falsely elevate readings and the trap is charting an “abnormal” without a proper repeat.
Question 2824

A patient stands up from the exam table after a blood draw and says they feel faint; what should the CMA do first?

Assist them back to a seated or supine position and assess for responsiveness, because preventing syncope-related falls is the immediate priority and the trap is offering water or sending them to the lobby.
Question 2825

A patient with suspected tuberculosis is coughing in the waiting room at check-in; what should the CMA do first?

Provide a mask and quickly place the patient in a separate room, because source control and separation break the chain of infection and the trap is completing paperwork first.
Question 2826

After removing gloves used for a wound dressing change, what should the CMA do next before touching the computer keyboard?

Perform hand hygiene immediately, because gloves are not a substitute and contamination occurs during removal; the trap is assuming “I wore gloves” makes hands clean.
Question 2827

While rooming a patient, you notice their respirations are 28/min with audible wheezing and they can only speak in short phrases; what should you do next?

Stop the routine intake and alert the provider immediately while keeping the patient upright, because respiratory distress is a priority finding and the trap is continuing vitals/history as if stable.
Question 2828

You are about to enter an exam room for a routine sore throat visit and notice your hands are visibly soiled from setting up equipment; what is the correct hand hygiene action?

Wash with soap and water before patient contact, because visible soil requires soap-and-water (sanitizer is less effective) and the trap is using gel as a shortcut.
Question 2829

A patient becomes pale, sweaty, and shaky in the exam room shortly after a procedure and says they have diabetes; what should the CMA do first?

Check their blood glucose per protocol and alert the provider while keeping the patient seated/ सुरक्षित, because hypoglycemia is a common urgent cause and the trap is giving food/juice without confirming level or notifying.
Question 2830

The provider orders 0.5 mL of vaccine IM, but the syringe you grabbed is a 3 mL syringe without 0.1 mL markings; what should you do?

Replace it with an appropriate syringe (e.g., 1 mL) to measure accurately, because dose accuracy is part of medication safety and the trap is estimating volume on an imprecise syringe.
Question 2831

You are preparing an IM injection and notice the medication label is smudged and you cannot clearly read the concentration; what should you do next?

Stop and obtain a clearly labeled vial (or verify with pharmacy/stock and replace), because you must confirm the right drug/strength and the trap is “guessing” based on vial shape or color.
Question 2832

When drawing up medication for an injection, you realize you forgot to perform the final label check and have two syringes on your tray; what is the best next step?

Discard and redraw with one medication prepared and labeled at a time, because unlabeled/uncertain syringes are an error risk and the trap is trying to “figure it out” by appearance.
Question 2833

A patient requests you “just recap this needle” after you give a vaccine because the sharps container is across the room; what is the best action?

Do not recap; activate the safety device and immediately dispose in a sharps container, because recapping increases needlestick risk and the trap is prioritizing convenience over safety.
Question 2834

You are asked to position a patient for a rectal exam; what is the correct position to use?

Place the patient in Sims’ position (left side-lying with right knee flexed), because it provides optimal access and safety; the trap is using dorsal recumbent, which is less appropriate.
Question 2835

You are about to administer a medication injection and the patient says, “I’m allergic to that one,” but the chart shows no allergies documented; what should the CMA do first?

Hold the medication and immediately verify the allergy details and notify the provider, because patient-reported allergy requires confirmation before administration and the trap is relying only on the chart.
Question 2836

While cleaning an exam room, you see dried blood on the exam table paper and nearby surface; what should you do?

Don gloves and clean then disinfect with an EPA-registered disinfectant appropriate for blood/body fluids, because blood requires proper disinfection and the trap is wiping with alcohol or changing paper only.
Question 2837

A teen faints immediately after standing for weight and height—what position is best right now?

Assist to a supine position with legs elevated and maintain airway, then notify the provider because it supports cerebral perfusion; the trap is sitting them upright quickly which can worsen syncope.
Question 2838

A patient’s pulse is irregular on the pulse oximeter at check-in—what is the best next action by the CMA?

Verify by counting the apical pulse for a full minute and report the irregular rhythm to the provider because equipment can misread and irregularity may be clinically significant; the trap is charting the machine rate without confirmation.
Question 2839

During intake, a 58-year-old reports dizziness; vitals are BP 84/52, HR 118, RR 22, skin cool/clammy—what should the CMA do first?

Have the patient lie supine with legs elevated and call for the provider/activate clinic emergency response while staying with the patient because possible shock requires immediate support, not finishing paperwork or rechecking later.
Question 2840

In the lobby, a patient suddenly collapses and is unresponsive; what should the CMA do first?

Activate emergency response (call for help/911 per clinic protocol) and start CPR while someone retrieves the AED, because early CPR/AED saves lives and the trap is checking vitals or searching for the provider first.
Question 2841

You obtain BP 178/112 in an asymptomatic patient who says they “ran up the stairs”—what should you do next?

Recheck manually after 5 minutes of seated rest with proper cuff size and arm at heart level, then report if still elevated because technique/rest affect accuracy; the trap is documenting a single high automated reading as final.
Question 2842

During a wound dressing change, the old gauze is stuck to the wound bed and begins to pull tissue when removed; what should the CMA do next?

Moisten the dressing with sterile saline and gently remove, because dry removal can damage tissue and the trap is pulling harder to “get it off quickly.”
Question 2843

A patient with suspected meningitis arrives coughing and febrile in the waiting room—what should the CMA do first regarding infection control?

Place a surgical mask on the patient and move them to a private room promptly because source control reduces droplet spread; the trap is putting on your PPE first while leaving the patient unmasked among others.
Question 2844

A 6-year-old is anxious and moving during an oral temperature; the reading is 102.9°F—what is the best next step before reporting?

Recheck using a more reliable route for the situation (e.g., tympanic/temporal per clinic device) because motion can falsely elevate oral readings; the trap is accepting a questionable measurement without confirmation.
Question 2845

While preparing an IM injection, you draw up the medication but notice the vial label doesn’t match the MAR order—what should you do?

Do not administer; re-verify the order and medication (and ask the nurse/provider/pharmacist as appropriate) because a mismatch is a medication error risk; the trap is assuming “same drawer” means correct drug.
Question 2846

While rooming a patient, you notice dried blood on the exam table paper from the prior visit—what is the correct next step?

Don gloves and disinfect the surface with an approved agent for the required contact time and replace the paper because visible soil requires cleaning/disinfection before reuse; the trap is just changing paper without disinfecting.
Question 2847

During intake, a 64-year-old becomes pale and says he feels faint while standing on the scale after reporting he skipped breakfast; what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a seated or supine position and assess airway/breathing and vital signs while calling for help as needed, because preventing a fall and checking for acute instability comes before continuing intake or offering food/drink (a common mistake is trying to “push through” vitals while the patient is still standing).
Question 2848

You enter a room to perform a wound dressing change and the patient begins coughing forcefully—what PPE change is most appropriate?

Add a surgical mask with eye protection (and keep gloves) because splash/droplet risk increases during coughing; the trap is proceeding with only gloves and risking mucous membrane exposure.
Question 2849

You accidentally stick yourself with a used lancet after a glucose check—what is the first action?

Wash the area immediately with soap and water, then report and follow exposure protocol because prompt cleansing and reporting reduces risk and ensures timely evaluation; the trap is finishing tasks before reporting.
Question 2850

You are preparing to administer a vaccine and the patient says they had an anaphylactic reaction to a prior dose—what should the CMA do next?

Stop and notify the provider before administering because history suggests a contraindication/need for medical decision-making; the trap is proceeding because the order exists.
Question 2851

Assist the patient to a safe supine position with legs elevated and immediately notify the provider/activate clinic emergency response as needed because symptomatic hypotension with tachycardia is potentially unstable; the common trap is repeating the BP or continuing intake instead of prioritizing safety and rapid escalation.
Question 2852

Question 2853

After an IM injection, the patient becomes pale and says they feel faint—what should the CMA do first?

Assist the patient to a safe seated or supine position and assess airway/breathing while calling for help because preventing a fall and rapid assessment come before documentation; the trap is telling them to “take deep breaths” while they remain standing.
Question 2854

In the lobby, a patient collapses and is unresponsive; you don’t feel a pulse within 10 seconds—what should you do first?

Activate emergency response (call 911/get AED) and begin CPR immediately because early CPR/AED saves lives; the trap is spending extra time rechecking pulse or searching for history.
Question 2855

Question 2856

Question 2857

Question 2858

Question 2859

You are about to give a subcutaneous injection; the patient’s abdomen has multiple bruises from recent injections—what is the best site-selection action?

Choose a different appropriate site/area and rotate away from bruised tissue because bruising alters absorption and increases discomfort; the trap is injecting into bruised areas because they’re “already sore.”
Question 2860

You bring the AED to an unresponsive patient and a bystander is touching the patient’s shoulder while you are about to deliver a shock—what is the best action?

Loudly direct “clear” and ensure no one is touching the patient before delivering the shock because contact can injure rescuers and reduce effectiveness; the trap is shocking quickly without visual confirmation.
Question 2861

While taking vitals, you count a respiratory rate of 10 with shallow breaths in a patient who is very sleepy after a procedure; what is the best action?

Stay with the patient and immediately alert the provider/clinical lead because slow, shallow respirations can signal respiratory depression; the trap is assuming they are “just tired” and leaving them alone.
Question 2862

A patient with severe shortness of breath arrives for a routine follow-up and cannot speak full sentences; what should the CMA do first?

Question 2863

Question 2864

Question 2865

Question 2866

Question 2867

Question 2868

Question 2869

Question 2870
