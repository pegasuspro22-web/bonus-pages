Reading and Writing

PassageMarine biologists studying deep-sea vent communities have long assumed that such ecosystems are largely self-contained, but recent tracking data reveal a surprising ______: larvae from vent-dwelling species have been found drifting hundreds of miles away, settling in entirely different vent fields.
Q1Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: C. "Connectivity" matches the idea that larvae travel between distant vent fields, linking populations once assumed to be separate. "Certainty" and "decline" don't relate to the movement described. "Isolation" is the opposite of what the data show — vent communities turn out NOT to be self-contained.
PassageDespite the committee's initial ______ about funding an untested teaching method, pilot classroom results were compelling enough that the program was expanded district-wide within a year.
Q2Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: A. "Reluctance" fits "despite... initial [X]" being overcome by compelling results — the committee was hesitant at first. "Enthusiasm" wouldn't need to be overcome by good results. "Indifference" and "familiarity" don't create the contrast the sentence needs, since "despite" signals a shift from doubt to acceptance.
PassageA city planner proposed closing Elm Street to car traffic on weekends. Local shop owners worried that reduced car access would hurt sales. The planner responded by citing data from three other cities where similar closures had increased foot traffic and, within a year, raised nearby retail revenue by an average of 18%.
Q3Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the function of the last sentence in the passage?
Correct answer: D. The last sentence gives concrete data — revenue increases in other cities — directly responding to the shop owners' worry about lost sales; that's evidence addressing their concern. It doesn't undermine the proposal (choice A; no flaw is mentioned), doesn't shift topics (choice B), and doesn't merely restate the objection (choice C) — it answers it.
PassageThe essay opens with a vivid description of a single abandoned factory, then gradually widens its focus to the economic decline of the entire town, and finally ends by connecting that decline to national manufacturing trends.
Q4Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the overall structure of the essay?
Correct answer: B. The essay explicitly moves from one factory (specific/local) to the town (broader) to national trends (broadest) — specific to general. Choice D reverses that order. Choices A and C describe structures (repetition, argument/counterargument) that aren't present in the description given.
PassageA 2024 study tracked 400 commuters who switched from driving to cycling for their daily commute. Researchers measured self-reported stress levels before and after the switch. On average, participants reported a 22% drop in stress within two months, though the effect was smaller for commuters whose routes included heavy traffic intersections.
Q5Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
Which choice best states the main finding of the study?
Correct answer: B. Choice B captures both parts of the finding: an overall stress reduction and the qualification that busy intersections lessened the effect. Choice A overstates the finding by ignoring that qualifier ("always," "regardless of route"). Choice C introduces walking, which the passage never mentions. Choice D contradicts the reported 22% drop.
PassageCoral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor, yet they support roughly a quarter of all known marine species. This concentration of biodiversity makes reefs a priority for conservation, even though their small physical footprint might suggest otherwise.
Q6Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
According to the passage, why are coral reefs considered a conservation priority?
Correct answer: C. The passage's central contrast is between small area (under 1%) and large species share (a quarter) — that disproportion is why reefs matter. Choice A is factually wrong; the passage says reefs cover very little area. Choice B overstates the claim with "only." Choice D isn't stated or implied anywhere in the passage.
PassageA researcher claims that home tutoring in mathematics improves standardized test scores more than group tutoring does. Two groups of 50 students each received 10 weeks of tutoring; the home-tutoring group's average score rose from 68 to 79, while the group-tutoring group's average rose from 67 to 74.
Q7Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best supports the researcher's claim?
Correct answer: A. The claim is a comparison of effectiveness, so the relevant evidence is the size of each group's improvement: home tutoring gained more points (11 vs. 7). Choices B, C, and D describe conditions that were equal between the two groups — useful for a fair comparison, but they don't by themselves show that one method worked better.
PassageA city council member argues that a new bike-lane network reduced traffic congestion downtown. Average commute times downtown were 34 minutes before the bike lanes were installed and 31 minutes one year after installation. During the same year, the city also synchronized several downtown traffic lights.
Q8Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best describes a weakness in using the commute-time data to support the council member's claim?
Correct answer: D. Because the traffic-light synchronization happened at the same time as the bike-lane installation, the 3-minute drop could be caused by either change, or both — the data alone can't isolate the bike lanes' effect. Choices A and C are factually wrong according to the passage (commute times were measured downtown, and they decreased, not increased). Choice B isn't addressed by the passage at all.
PassageThe museum's new wing features a recreated dig site ______ visitors can search for artifacts themselves.
Q9Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: C. "Where" correctly introduces a clause describing the dig site (a place). "Were" (choice A) and "we're" (choice B) are homophones that don't fit grammatically — the sentence needs a relative pronoun referring to a location, not a verb or contraction. Choice D adds an unnecessary comma that incorrectly separates the clause from the site it describes.
PassageThe collection of rare manuscripts, donated by three separate alumni over the past decade, ______ housed in a climate-controlled room on the library's top floor.
Q10Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: B. The subject of the sentence is "the collection" (singular), not "manuscripts" or "alumni," which sit inside a modifying phrase. A singular subject takes "is." Choices A, C, and D all agree with a plural noun instead of the true singular subject.
PassageBy the time the results were announced, the two ______ methods had already been adopted by several other labs studying the same phenomenon.
Q11Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: D. "Researchers'" is the correct plural possessive — since there are two researchers, the apostrophe goes after the "s." Choice A is singular possessive, but the sentence describes two researchers. Choice B ("researchers's") isn't a standard English form. Choice C has no apostrophe at all, leaving "methods" without an owner.
PassageOriginally designed for military communication, engineers eventually adapted the technology for civilian use in hospitals and schools.
Q12Standard English Conventions
Which choice best revises the sentence to correct a misplaced modifier?
Correct answer: A. The opening phrase "Originally designed for military communication" describes the technology, not the engineers, so "the technology" must immediately follow the comma — which only choice A does. The original sentence (and choice C) incorrectly implies the engineers were "originally designed for military communication." Choice B still doesn't place "the technology" right after the modifier.
PassageThe novel's final chapter, which many critics consider the strongest part of the book ______ resolves every major plot thread introduced in the first half.
Q13Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: B. The clause "which many critics consider the strongest part of the book" is nonessential information inserted into the main sentence ("The novel's final chapter... resolves every major plot thread") — nonessential clauses need a comma on both sides. Since the clause already opens with a comma, it must close with one too (choice B). Ending it with a semicolon, period, or no punctuation instead breaks the sentence's grammar.
PassageThe new library wing was completed six months ahead of schedule. ______, the project came in nearly 12% under its original budget.
Q14Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: C. Both sentences describe positive outcomes of the same project — early completion and under-budget spending — so "Additionally" (choice C) correctly signals that a second positive fact is being added. "However" (A) and "In contrast" (D) wrongly signal a contradiction where none exists. "For example" (B) wrongly implies the budget fact is an instance of the schedule fact, when they're two separate facts.
PassageMany species of migratory birds rely on magnetic fields to navigate. Increasing electromagnetic interference from urban areas, ______, may be disrupting these natural navigation systems.
Q15Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: A. "Researchers suspect" (choice A) works as a parenthetical attribution — the sentence reads naturally as "interference..., researchers suspect, may be disrupting...," signaling this is a hypothesis rather than a proven fact. "For instance" (B) and "in other words" (C) don't fit that parenthetical slot. "Therefore" (D) would need to signal a logical conclusion, but nothing earlier in the text establishes a cause requiring "therefore."
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A local nonprofit distributed 4,000 winter coats to families in need last year. • The nonprofit relies entirely on volunteer labor and donated materials, with no paid staff. The student wants to emphasize how the nonprofit achieved a large impact with minimal resources.
Q16Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: D. Choice D directly connects the minimal resources (all-volunteer, no paid staff) to the large impact (4,000 coats) with "despite," which is exactly the emphasis the student wants. Choice B lists both facts but without framing them as impact-despite-limited-resources. Choice A omits the specific coat count. Choice C is vague ("thousands") and drops the volunteer-only detail entirely.
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A community garden project converted 12 vacant lots into growing spaces over three years. • A survey found that residents near the converted lots reported a 30% increase in sense of neighborhood safety. The student wants to present the connection between the garden project and residents' sense of safety.
Q17Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: B. Choice B links both facts together — the conversion project, then the resulting survey finding — in a single connected statement, exactly the connection the student wants to present. Choices C and D each state only one fact alone. Choice A is a general claim not drawn from the specific notes at all.

Math

Q18Algebra
If 5x − 3 = 2x + 15, what is the value of x?
Correct answer: A. 5x − 3 = 2x + 15 → 5x − 2x = 15 + 3 → 3x = 18 → x = 6. Choice B would result from combining the constants incorrectly; choices C and D don't satisfy the original equation when substituted back in.
Q19Algebra
The system of equations 2x + y = 11 and x − y = 1 has solution (x, y). What is the value of x + y?
Correct answer: D. Adding the two equations directly eliminates y: (2x + y) + (x − y) = 11 + 1, so 3x = 12 and x = 4. From x − y = 1, y = 3. So x + y = 4 + 3 = 7. Choice A would come from an arithmetic slip solving for y; choices B and C don't satisfy both original equations.
Q20Algebra
A parking garage charges $4 for the first hour and $2.50 for each additional hour. If Maya has at most $16.50 to spend, what is the maximum number of whole hours she can park?
Correct answer: C. The cost for h hours (h ≥ 1) is 4 + 2.5(h − 1). Setting 4 + 2.5(h − 1) ≤ 16.50 gives 2.5(h − 1) ≤ 12.5, so h − 1 ≤ 5 and h ≤ 6. At h = 6 the cost is exactly $16.50, within budget; at h = 7 (choice D) the cost would be $19.00, over budget. Choices A and B don't use the full amount available.
Q21Advanced Math
What are the solutions to x² − 5x − 14 = 0?
Correct answer: B. x² − 5x − 14 factors as (x − 7)(x + 2), since −7 × 2 = −14 and −7 + 2 = −5. Setting each factor to zero gives x = 7 and x = −2. The other choices come from sign errors made while factoring.
Q22Advanced Math
For x > 0, (x^(4/3))² = x^k. What is the value of k?
Correct answer: A. Raising a power to a power multiplies the exponents: (4/3) × 2 = 8/3. Choice B results from dividing instead of multiplying the exponents; choices C and D come from other common exponent-rule errors.
Q23Advanced Math
The function f(x) = 2(x − 3)² + 1. What is f(5)?
Correct answer: D. Substituting x = 5: 2(5 − 3)² + 1 = 2(2)² + 1 = 2(4) + 1 = 9. Choice B would result from squaring before subtracting inside the parentheses incorrectly; choices A and C come from other order-of-operations slips.
Q24Advanced Math
For P(x) = 3x² − 5x + 4, what is the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x − 2)?
Correct answer: C. By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x − 2) equals P(2): 3(2)² − 5(2) + 4 = 12 − 10 + 4 = 6. The other choices come from arithmetic slips evaluating P(2).
Q25Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
A backpack's price was reduced from $80 to $68. By what percent was the price reduced?
Correct answer: B. The dollar decrease is $80 − $68 = $12. As a percent of the original price: 12/80 = 0.15 = 15%. Choice A mistakes the dollar amount for the percent; choices C and D come from dividing by the wrong base.
Q26Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
In a random sample of 180 students, 120 reported owning a bicycle. Based on this sample, how many students out of a school of 900 would be expected to own a bicycle?
Correct answer: A. The sample proportion is 120/180 = 2/3. Applying that proportion to 900 students: 900 × 2/3 = 600. Choice D would come from using 3/5 instead of 2/3; choices B and C come from other proportion errors.
Q27Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
Five students scored 72, 85, 90, 68, and 80 on a quiz. What is the median score?
Correct answer: D. Sorting the scores gives 68, 72, 80, 85, 90. With five values, the median is the middle (third) value: 80. Choices A, B, and C are close to the mean (79) or other miscalculations, not the median.
Q28Geometry and Trigonometry
A right triangle has legs of length 9 and 12. What is its area?
Correct answer: C. The area of a right triangle is half the product of its two legs: (1/2)(9)(12) = 54. Choice B omits the factor of 1/2; choices A and D come from other arithmetic errors.
Q29Geometry and Trigonometry
A circle has a diameter of 14. What is its area, in terms of π?
Correct answer: B. The radius is half the diameter: 14/2 = 7. Area = πr² = π(7)² = 49π. Choice A confuses diameter with area; choice C squares the diameter instead of the radius.
Q30Geometry and Trigonometry
In right triangle ABC, angle C is the right angle, the length of side BC (opposite angle A) is 6, and the length of the hypotenuse AB is 10. What is sin(A)?
Correct answer: A. sin(A) is the ratio of the side opposite angle A to the hypotenuse: 6/10 = 3/5. Choice B (4/5) would be cos(A), using the other leg (8, found via the Pythagorean theorem) over the hypotenuse. Choices C and D invert the correct ratios.
