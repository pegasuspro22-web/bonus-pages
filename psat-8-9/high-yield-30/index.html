Reading and Writing

PassageAfter weeks of practicing the same three chords, Malik finally felt ready to write his own song instead of just copying others. His confidence, once so ______, had grown steadily with every rehearsal.
Q1Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: D. "Fragile" (choice D) fits because it contrasts with "grown steadily" — his confidence started weak/easily shaken and became stronger over time. "Permanent" (A) contradicts the idea that it changed. "Widespread" (B) and "unnecessary" (C) don't describe a personal quality that can "grow."
PassageThe new student was surprisingly ______ about joining the debate team, showing up early to every practice and volunteering to speak first.
Q2Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: A. "Enthusiastic" (choice A) matches the evidence — showing up early and volunteering to speak first are signs of eagerness. "Reluctant" (B) is the opposite. "Confused" (C) and "forgetful" (D) don't match the described actions at all.
PassageA student council proposed adding a recycling bin to every classroom. Some teachers worried it would create more work. The council responded by sharing results from a nearby school where the same program cut classroom trash by half within two months.
Q3Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the function of the last sentence in the passage?
Correct answer: B. The last sentence provides real data (cutting trash by half) that responds directly to teachers' worry about extra work — that's evidence addressing their concern (choice B). It doesn't criticize anyone (A), doesn't change topics (C), and doesn't just restate the worry (D) — it answers it.
PassageThe article begins by describing one student's struggle to find a quiet place to study at home, then broadens to discuss the lack of study spaces across the whole school district, and ends by connecting the issue to a statewide funding debate.
Q4Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the overall structure of the article?
Correct answer: C. The article explicitly widens from one student, to the district, to the state — specific to broad (choice C). Choice D reverses this order. Choices A and B describe structures (comparison, repetition) that don't match the description.
PassageA 2024 survey asked 500 middle school students about their sleep habits. Students who reported going to bed after 11 p.m. were more likely to describe their mood as "irritable" the next day, though the pattern was weaker among students who took naps after school.
Q5Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
Which choice best states the main finding of the survey?
Correct answer: A. Choice A captures the whole finding: an association between late bedtime and irritability, plus the qualifier that naps weakened it. Choice B overstates with "always" and "no matter what." Choice C claims something absolute the passage never says. Choice D contradicts the reported association.
PassageCommunity gardens take up a small fraction of total city land, yet a recent study found they produce a large share of the fresh vegetables grown within city limits. This makes them valuable to food access programs despite their modest size.
Q6Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
According to the passage, why are community gardens considered valuable to food access programs?
Correct answer: D. The passage's key contrast is small land use vs. large vegetable output — that disproportion is why they're valuable (choice D). Choice A is factually wrong. Choice B overstates with "only." Choice C isn't discussed in the passage.
PassageA teacher claims that students who review notes within a day of a lesson remember more than students who wait a week. In a class experiment, one group of 20 students reviewed notes the next day and averaged 85% on a later quiz, while another group of 20 waited a week and averaged 71%.
Q7Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best supports the teacher's claim?
Correct answer: C. The claim compares memory retention, so the relevant evidence is the score gap between groups — 85% vs. 71% directly supports it (choice C). Choices A, B, and D describe equal/neutral conditions that make the comparison fair but don't by themselves prove one method worked better.
PassageA principal argues that a new morning announcement system reduced students being late to first period. Late arrivals dropped from 40 per day to 25 per day after the system started. During the same month, the school also moved the start of first period 10 minutes later.
Q8Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best describes a weakness in using this data to support the principal's claim?
Correct answer: B. Because the later start time happened during the same month, the drop in late arrivals could be caused by either change — the data alone can't isolate the announcement system's effect (choice B). Choices A and D are factually wrong per the passage. Choice C isn't addressed by the passage at all.
PassageThe classroom has a reading corner ______ students can pick a book during free time.
Q9Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: A. "Where" (choice A) correctly introduces a clause describing the reading corner (a place). "Were" (B) and "we're" (C) are homophones that don't fit grammatically. Choice D adds an unnecessary comma separating the clause from what it describes.
PassageThe set of science projects, submitted by four different classes this semester, ______ displayed in the school lobby.
Q10Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: D. The subject is "the set" (singular), not "projects" or "classes," which sit inside a modifying phrase. A singular subject takes "is" (choice D). The other choices agree with a plural noun instead of the true subject.
PassageBy the end of the tournament, the two ______ trophies had already been engraved with their team names.
Q11Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: B. "Winners'" (choice B) is the correct plural possessive — there are two winning teams, so the apostrophe goes after the "s." Choice A is singular possessive. Choice C isn't a standard form. Choice D has no apostrophe, leaving "trophies" without an owner.
PassageOriginally built as a train station, the city eventually converted the building into a public library.
Q12Standard English Conventions
Which choice best revises the sentence to correct a misplaced modifier?
Correct answer: C. "Originally built as a train station" describes the building, not the city, so "the building" must follow the comma — only choice C does this. The original sentence (and choice D) wrongly implies the city was "originally built as a train station." Choice A still doesn't place "the building" right after the modifier.
PassageThe school's spring concert, which many parents consider the highlight of the year ______ features every grade level performing together.
Q13Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: D. "Which many parents consider the highlight of the year" is nonessential information inserted into the main sentence, so it needs a comma on both sides. Since it already opens with a comma, it must close with one too (choice D). Ending with a semicolon, period, or nothing (choices A, B, C) breaks the sentence's grammar.
PassageThe school play was performed two nights in a row. ______, both performances sold out within a day.
Q14Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: A. Both sentences describe positive facts about the same event, so "Additionally" (choice A) correctly adds a second positive fact. "However" (B) and "In contrast" (D) wrongly suggest a contradiction. "For example" (C) wrongly implies the second fact is an instance of the first.
PassageMany students struggle to focus during long study sessions. Short breaks every 25 minutes, ______, can noticeably improve concentration.
Q15Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: C. "Teachers report" (choice C) works as a parenthetical attribution, making the sentence read naturally as a claim attributed to a source rather than a stated fact. "For instance" (A) and "in other words" (D) don't fit that slot. "Therefore" (B) would need an established cause, which the text doesn't provide.
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A school's peer-tutoring program matched 60 students with tutors last year. • The program relies entirely on student volunteers, with no paid staff. The student wants to emphasize how the program achieved a large reach with minimal resources.
Q16Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: B. Choice B directly connects the minimal resources (all-volunteer, no paid staff) to the large reach (60 students) with "despite" — exactly the emphasis wanted. Choice A lists both facts without that framing. Choice C omits the specific number. Choice D is vague and drops the volunteer detail.
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A school garden club planted vegetables in 8 raised beds over two years. • A survey found that students who visited the garden reported feeling calmer during the school day. The student wants to present the connection between the garden club and students' sense of calm.
Q17Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: D. Choice D links both facts in one connected statement — the planting, then the resulting survey finding — exactly the connection wanted. Choices A and B each state only one fact alone. Choice C is a general claim not drawn from the notes.

Math

Q18Algebra
If 3x + 4 = x + 14, what is the value of x?
Correct answer: C. 3x + 4 = x + 14 → 3x − x = 14 − 4 → 2x = 10 → x = 5 (choice C). Choice A would come from a sign error; choices B and D don't satisfy the equation.
Q19Algebra
The system of equations x + y = 9 and x − y = 3 has solution (x, y). What is the value of x?
Correct answer: A. Adding the equations eliminates y: (x + y) + (x − y) = 9 + 3 → 2x = 12 → x = 6 (choice A). Choice B would be the value of y, not x; choices C and D don't satisfy both equations.
Q20Algebra
A school club charges $3 to join plus $2 for each event ticket. If Priya spent exactly $15, how many event tickets did she buy?
Correct answer: D. 3 + 2t = 15 → 2t = 12 → t = 6 (choice D). Choices A and B don't use the full $15 exactly; choice C would cost more than $15.
Q21Algebra
A parking lot charges $5 for the first hour and $1.50 for each additional hour. If Andre has at most $11 to spend, what is the maximum number of whole hours he can park?
Correct answer: B. The cost for h hours (h ≥ 1) is 5 + 1.5(h − 1). Setting 5 + 1.5(h − 1) ≤ 11 gives 1.5(h − 1) ≤ 6, so h − 1 ≤ 4 and h ≤ 5 (choice B). At h = 5 the cost is exactly $11; at h = 6 (choice D) it would be $12.50, over budget.
Q22Advanced Math
What are the solutions to x² − x − 6 = 0?
Correct answer: A. x² − x − 6 factors as (x − 3)(x + 2), since −3 × 2 = −6 and −3 + 2 = −1. Setting each factor to zero gives x = 3 and x = −2 (choice A). The other choices come from sign errors made while factoring.
Q23Advanced Math
The function f(x) = 3x² − 2. What is f(3)?
Correct answer: D. f(3) = 3(3)² − 2 = 3(9) − 2 = 27 − 2 = 25 (choice D). Choice B would come from squaring after multiplying incorrectly; choices A and C come from other arithmetic slips.
Q24Advanced Math
What is the value of (2³)(2⁴)?
Correct answer: C. When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2^(3+4) = 2⁷ = 128 (choice C). Choice A comes from adding the bases instead of the exponents; choice B comes from multiplying the exponents instead of adding; choice D is 2⁶, one power too few.
Q25Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
A jacket's price was reduced from $60 to $48. By what percent was the price reduced?
Correct answer: B. The dollar decrease is $60 − $48 = $12. As a percent of the original price: 12/60 = 0.20 = 20% (choice B). Choice A mistakes the dollar amount for the percent; choices C and D come from dividing by the wrong base.
Q26Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
In a random sample of 150 students, 90 said they walk to school. Based on this sample, how many students out of a school of 600 would be expected to walk to school?
Correct answer: D. The sample proportion is 90/150 = 3/5. Applying that proportion to 600 students: 600 × 3/5 = 360 (choice D). Choice A would come from using 1/2 instead of 3/5; choices B and C come from other proportion errors.
Q27Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
Five students scored 62, 78, 85, 70, and 90 on a project. What is the median score?
Correct answer: A. Sorting the scores gives 62, 70, 78, 85, 90. With five values, the median is the middle (third) value: 78 (choice A). The other choices are close to the mean or other miscalculations, not the median.
Q28Geometry and Trigonometry
A rectangle has a length of 11 and a width of 6. What is its area?
Correct answer: C. Area of a rectangle = length × width = 11 × 6 = 66 (choice C). Choice A resembles the perimeter (2(11 + 6) = 34), not the area. Choice B comes from a multiplication error. Choice D comes from subtracting instead of multiplying.
Q29Geometry and Trigonometry
A circle has a radius of 6. What is its area, in terms of π?
Correct answer: B. Area = πr² = π(6)² = 36π (choice B). Choice A confuses radius with area. Choice D doubles the radius instead of squaring it. Choice C doubles the correct answer.
Q30Geometry and Trigonometry
A right triangle has legs of length 9 and 12. What is the length of its hypotenuse?
Correct answer: A. By the Pythagorean theorem, hypotenuse² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, so the hypotenuse = √225 = 15 (choice A). Choice B adds the legs instead of applying the theorem. Choice D is 9 × 12, the area of a rectangle with those side lengths, not the hypotenuse.
