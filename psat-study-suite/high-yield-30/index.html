Reading and Writing

PassageRestoration ecologists had long assumed that a burned forest needs decades to recover its full range of species, but new survey data reveal a striking ______: within just five years, understory plant diversity in the burn zone matched levels found in untouched stands nearby.
Q1Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: B. "Anomaly" (choice B) matches a surprising deviation from the expected slow-recovery pattern — five years matching untouched levels is unexpected. "Decline" (A) contradicts the positive recovery described. "Consensus" (C) and "delay" (D) don't fit a finding that contradicts prior assumptions.
PassageAlthough the internship paid nothing, Dana described the experience as ______, citing the mentorship and hands-on skills she gained as worth far more than a paycheck.
Q2Craft and Structure — Words in Context
Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?
Correct answer: D. "Invaluable" (choice D) matches "worth far more than a paycheck" — despite no pay, she valued it highly. "Worthless" (A) is the opposite. "Ordinary" (B) and "brief" (C) don't connect to the mentorship/skills reasoning given.
PassageA city debated banning plastic bags at grocery stores. Some small business owners worried about extra costs for reusable alternatives. The city council responded by pointing to a neighboring city where a similar ban led retailers to report a 10% drop in packaging expenses within a year.
Q3Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the function of the last sentence in the passage?
Correct answer: A. The last sentence provides real data (a drop in packaging expenses elsewhere) that responds directly to business owners' worry about extra costs — evidence addressing their concern (choice A). It doesn't undermine the ban (B; no hidden cost is revealed), doesn't shift topics (C), and doesn't just restate the worry (D) — it answers it.
PassageThe documentary opens with a close-up interview of a single factory worker describing a shift change, then widens to show the entire plant's production line, and finally cuts to national employment statistics tied to the manufacturing sector.
Q4Craft and Structure — Text Structure and Purpose
Which choice best describes the overall structure of the documentary?
Correct answer: C. The documentary explicitly widens from one worker, to the plant, to national statistics — specific to broad (choice C). Choice D reverses this order. Choices A and B describe structures (comparison, repetition) that don't match the description.
PassageA 2025 study tracked 600 office workers who began taking short walking breaks every 90 minutes. Researchers measured self-reported focus levels before and after adopting the habit. On average, participants reported an 18% improvement in focus within three weeks, though the improvement was smaller among workers whose jobs required frequent video calls.
Q5Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
Which choice best states the main finding of the study?
Correct answer: D. Choice D captures the whole finding: an association between walking breaks and improved focus, plus the qualifier that video calls weakened it. Choice A overstates with "always" and "regardless." Choice B claims something absolute the passage never says. Choice C contradicts the reported 18% improvement.
PassageUrban rooftop farms occupy a tiny fraction of a city's total land area, yet a recent audit found they supply a disproportionately large share of the leafy greens sold at nearby farmers markets. This makes them a valuable piece of local food systems despite their limited footprint.
Q6Information and Ideas — Central Ideas and Details
According to the passage, why are urban rooftop farms considered valuable to local food systems?
Correct answer: B. The passage's key contrast is small land use vs. large output — that disproportion is why they're valuable (choice B). Choice A is factually wrong. Choice C overstates with "only." Choice D isn't discussed in the passage.
PassageA trainer claims that athletes who stretch before AND after practice recover faster than athletes who only stretch before. In a team study, one group of 24 athletes stretched both before and after practice and reported average soreness scores of 3.2 out of 10 the next day, while another group of 24 who stretched only before reported average soreness scores of 5.6.
Q7Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best supports the trainer's claim?
Correct answer: A. The claim compares recovery, so the relevant evidence is the soreness-score gap between groups — 3.2 vs. 5.6 directly supports it (choice A). Choices B, C, and D describe equal/neutral conditions that make the comparison fair but don't by themselves prove one method worked better.
PassageA gym manager argues that a new class-booking app reduced no-shows. No-shows dropped from 18 per week to 9 per week after the app launched. During the same period, the gym also began charging a small late-cancellation fee.
Q8Information and Ideas — Command of Evidence
Which choice best describes a weakness in using this data to support the manager's claim?
Correct answer: C. Because the cancellation fee began during the same period, the drop in no-shows could be caused by either change — the data alone can't isolate the app's effect (choice C). Choices A and D are factually wrong per the passage. Choice B isn't addressed by the passage at all.
PassageThe lab has a specimen archive ______ researchers can request samples for approved studies.
Q9Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: B. "Where" (choice B) correctly introduces a clause describing the archive (a place). "Were" (A) and "we're" (C) are homophones that don't fit grammatically. Choice D adds an unnecessary comma separating the clause from what it describes.
PassageThe row of display cases, restored by volunteers over the past three summers, ______ located just inside the museum's main entrance.
Q10Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: A. The subject is "the row" (singular), not "cases" or "summers," which sit inside a modifying phrase. A singular subject takes "is" (choice A). The other choices agree with a plural noun instead of the true subject.
PassageBy the time the ceremony ended, the two ______ medals had already been polished and displayed.
Q11Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: D. "Winners'" (choice D) is the correct plural possessive — there are two winners, so the apostrophe goes after the "s." Choice A is singular possessive. Choice B isn't a standard form. Choice C has no apostrophe, leaving "medals" without an owner.
PassageOriginally designed as a warehouse, the developers eventually converted the building into loft apartments.
Q12Standard English Conventions
Which choice best revises the sentence to correct a misplaced modifier?
Correct answer: C. "Originally designed as a warehouse" describes the building, not the developers, so "the building" must follow the comma — only choice C does this. The original sentence (and choice B) wrongly implies the developers were "originally designed as a warehouse." Choice A still doesn't place "the building" right after the modifier.
PassageThe observatory's main telescope, which astronomers consider the most precise instrument on site ______ has tracked the same comet for over a decade.
Q13Standard English Conventions
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
Correct answer: A. "Which astronomers consider the most precise instrument on site" is nonessential information inserted into the main sentence, so it needs a comma on both sides. Since it already opens with a comma, it must close with one too (choice A). Ending with a semicolon, period, or nothing (choices B, C, D) breaks the sentence's grammar.
PassageThe community pool reopened two weeks ahead of schedule. ______, membership renewals rose by 15% that same month.
Q14Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: B. Both sentences describe positive facts about the same event, so "Additionally" (choice B) correctly adds a second positive fact. "However" (A) and "In contrast" (D) wrongly suggest a contradiction. "For example" (C) wrongly implies the renewal fact is an instance of the reopening fact.
PassageMany commuters struggle to stay alert during long train rides. Brief standing stretches every 20 minutes, ______, can noticeably reduce fatigue.
Q15Expression of Ideas — Transitions
Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
Correct answer: D. "Commuters report" (choice D) works as a parenthetical attribution, making the sentence read naturally as a claim attributed to a source rather than a stated fact. "For instance" (A) and "in other words" (B) don't fit that slot. "Therefore" (C) would need an established cause, which the text doesn't provide.
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A school's robotics club recruited 45 new members last year. • The club operates on a budget funded entirely by bake sales, with no school funding. The student wants to emphasize how the club achieved strong growth with minimal resources.
Q16Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: C. Choice C directly connects the minimal resources (bake-sale funding only, no school funding) to the strong growth (45 members) with "despite" — exactly the emphasis wanted. Choice B lists both facts without that framing. Choice A omits the specific number. Choice D is vague and drops the funding detail.
PassageA student has researched two facts: • A neighborhood tool-lending library loaned out equipment 900 times in its first year. • A survey found that residents who borrowed tools reported completing more home repair projects. The student wants to present the connection between the tool library and residents completing more projects.
Q17Expression of Ideas — Rhetorical Synthesis
Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?
Correct answer: A. Choice A links both facts in one connected statement — the loans, then the resulting survey finding — exactly the connection wanted. Choices C and D each state only one fact alone. Choice B is a general claim not drawn from the notes.

Math

Q18Algebra
If 4x − 7 = x + 8, what is the value of x?
Correct answer: B. 4x − 7 = x + 8 → 4x − x = 8 + 7 → 3x = 15 → x = 5 (choice B). Choice A would come from a sign error; choices C and D don't satisfy the equation.
Q19Algebra
The system of equations 3x + y = 14 and x − y = 2 has solution (x, y). What is the value of x + y?
Correct answer: D. Adding the equations eliminates y: (3x + y) + (x − y) = 14 + 2 → 4x = 16 → x = 4. From x − y = 2, y = 2. So x + y = 4 + 2 = 6 (choice D). Choices A, B, and C don't satisfy both original equations.
Q20Algebra
A rental car costs $40 for the first day and $25 for each additional day. If Elena has at most $190 to spend, what is the maximum number of whole days she can rent the car?
Correct answer: C. The cost for d days (d ≥ 1) is 40 + 25(d − 1). Setting 40 + 25(d − 1) ≤ 190 gives 25(d − 1) ≤ 150, so d − 1 ≤ 6 and d ≤ 7 (choice C). At d = 7 the cost is exactly $190; at d = 8 (choice D) it would be $215, over budget.
Q21Advanced Math
What are the solutions to x² + 2x − 15 = 0?
Correct answer: A. x² + 2x − 15 factors as (x + 5)(x − 3), since 5 × (−3) = −15 and 5 + (−3) = 2. Setting each factor to zero gives x = −5 and x = 3 (choice A). The other choices come from sign errors made while factoring.
Q22Advanced Math
For x > 0, (x^(3/5))⁵ = x^k. What is the value of k?
Correct answer: D. Raising a power to a power multiplies the exponents: (3/5) × 5 = 3 (choice D). Choice A comes from adding the numerator and the outer exponent incorrectly; choice B comes from multiplying 3 × 5 instead of (3/5) × 5; choice C comes from dividing instead of multiplying.
Q23Advanced Math
The function f(x) = 2(x + 4)² − 5. What is f(−1)?
Correct answer: B. f(−1) = 2(−1 + 4)² − 5 = 2(3)² − 5 = 2(9) − 5 = 18 − 5 = 13 (choice B). Choice A would come from an arithmetic slip inside the parentheses; choices C and D come from other order-of-operations errors.
Q24Advanced Math
For P(x) = 2x² + 3x − 1, what is the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x − 3)?
Correct answer: C. By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x − 3) equals P(3): 2(3)² + 3(3) − 1 = 18 + 9 − 1 = 26 (choice C). The other choices come from arithmetic slips evaluating P(3).
Q25Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
A tablet's price was reduced from $250 to $200. By what percent was the price reduced?
Correct answer: D. The dollar decrease is $250 − $200 = $50. As a percent of the original price: 50/250 = 0.20 = 20% (choice D). Choices A, B, and C come from dividing by the wrong base or misreading the dollar amount as the percent.
Q26Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
In a random sample of 220 commuters, 132 said they use public transit. Based on this sample, how many commuters out of a population of 1,100 would be expected to use public transit?
Correct answer: A. The sample proportion is 132/220 = 3/5. Applying that proportion to 1,100 commuters: 1,100 × 3/5 = 660 (choice A). Choice B would come from using 6/11 instead of 3/5; choices C and D come from other proportion errors.
Q27Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
Six students scored 55, 82, 91, 68, 77, and 84 on an exam. What is the median score?
Correct answer: C. Sorting the scores gives 55, 68, 77, 82, 84, 91. With six values, the median is the average of the two middle values (3rd and 4th): (77 + 82)/2 = 79.5 (choice C). The other choices are individual scores or other miscalculations, not the median.
Q28Geometry and Trigonometry
A right triangle has legs of length 10 and 24. What is its area?
Correct answer: B. The area of a right triangle is half the product of its two legs: (1/2)(10)(24) = 120 (choice B). Choice A omits the factor of 1/2. Choices C and D resemble the perimeter or half the perimeter, not the area.
Q29Geometry and Trigonometry
A circle has a diameter of 18. What is its area, in terms of π?
Correct answer: D. The radius is half the diameter: 18/2 = 9. Area = πr² = π(9)² = 81π (choice D). Choice A confuses diameter with area. Choice C uses the diameter squared incorrectly. Choice B doubles the radius instead of squaring it.
Q30Geometry and Trigonometry
In right triangle DEF, angle F is the right angle, side DE (the hypotenuse) has length 13, and side EF (opposite angle D) has length 5. What is cos(D)?
Correct answer: A. cos(D) is the ratio of the side adjacent to angle D to the hypotenuse. The side adjacent to D is DF = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12, so cos(D) = 12/13 (choice A). Choice B (5/13) would be sin(D), using the opposite side over the hypotenuse. Choices C and D invert the correct ratios.
