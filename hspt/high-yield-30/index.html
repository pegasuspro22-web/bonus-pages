Verbal Skills

Q1Synonyms
Which word means the same as “meticulous”?
Correct answer: C. “Meticulous” means showing great care and attention to detail, which matches “thorough.” Choice A, “careless,” is essentially the opposite of meticulous. Choice B, “hurried,” implies rushing, which contradicts the careful, unhurried nature the word describes. Choice D, “confused,” describes a mental state unrelated to attentiveness or precision.
Q2Synonyms
Which word means the same as “candid”?
Correct answer: A. “Candid” means open and truthful, which matches “honest.” Choice B, “secretive,” is nearly the opposite — hiding information rather than sharing it openly. Choice C, “cautious,” describes carefulness or wariness, not truthfulness. Choice D, “formal,” describes tone or style, not honesty.
Q3Analogy
Sunrise is to morning as sunset is to _____.
Correct answer: D. Sunrise marks the beginning of morning, and sunset marks the beginning of evening — the relationship is event to the time period it begins. Choice A, “noon,” refers to midday and has no direct link to sunset. Choice B, “midnight,” occurs many hours after sunset, not at its start. Choice C, “dawn,” is essentially another word for sunrise, making it the wrong half of the pair.
Q4Analogy
Thermometer is to temperature as scale is to _____.
Correct answer: B. A thermometer measures temperature, and a scale measures weight — the relationship is instrument to the quantity it measures. Choice A, “balance,” is tempting because scales achieve balance when reading a weight, but balance itself is not the quantity displayed. Choice C, “numbers,” describes the format of the readout, not what is actually being measured. Choice D, “metal,” may describe what some scales are made of, which is unrelated to their function.
Q5Verbal Classification
Which word does NOT belong with the others?
Correct answer: D. Trumpet, violin, and drum are all musical instruments, sharing the category “objects used to make music.” Painting is a visual artwork, not an instrument, so it does not belong with the other three.
Q6Logic
Statement 1: All the students in Mr. Reyes’s class play an instrument.
Statement 2: Sofia is a student in Mr. Reyes’s class.
If the first two statements are true, the third statement is: “Sofia plays an instrument.”
Correct answer: A. Since every student in the class plays an instrument, and Sofia is a student in that class, it necessarily follows that Sofia plays an instrument — a valid deductive conclusion, so the statement is true. Choice B is incorrect because nothing in the premises contradicts the conclusion. Choice C, “uncertain,” would only apply if the first statement described just some students rather than all of them, leaving Sofia’s status ambiguous — but “all” removes that ambiguity entirely.

Quantitative Skills

Q7Number Series
What number should come next in the series?
4, 9, 14, 19, 24, ___
Correct answer: C. Each term increases by 5 (4+5=9, 9+5=14, and so on), so the next term is 24+5=29. Choice A (26) reflects a +2 step instead of +5. Choice B (27) would fit a +3 pattern, not the actual pattern shown. Choice D (30) overshoots, as if the step were +6.
Q8Number Series
What number should come next in the series?
2, 6, 5, 9, 8, 12, 11, ___
Correct answer: B. The series alternates between adding 4 and subtracting 1: 2→6 (+4) →5 (−1) →9 (+4) →8 (−1) →12 (+4) →11 (−1) →15 (+4). Choice A (14) results from adding only 3 instead of 4. Choice C (10) reverses the final step, subtracting instead of adding. Choice D (13) results from adding only 2 instead of 4.
Q9Number Manipulation
A number is tripled, then 4 is added, giving 31. What is the original number?
Correct answer: D. Let the unknown number be x. Tripling gives 3x, and adding 4 gives 3x+4=31. Subtracting 4 from both sides gives 3x=27, so x=9. Choice A (27) is the value of 3x itself — the step before dividing by 3 to isolate x, mistakenly left as the final answer. Choice B (10) results from dividing 31 by 3 directly (≈10.3, rounded to 10) without first subtracting 4 to undo the addition step. Choice C (35) results from adding 4 to 31 instead of subtracting it, reversing the correct operation.
Q10Geometric Comparison
Quantity A is the area of a triangle with base 12 and height 5.

Quantity B is the area of a rectangle with length 6 and width 5.

Which quantity is greater?
Correct answer: C. Quantity A = (1/2)(12)(5) = 30. Quantity B = 6×5 = 30. The two areas are equal. Choice A may result from forgetting the 1/2 factor in the triangle-area formula, which would make Quantity A appear to be 60. Choice B reverses the correct comparison. Choice D is incorrect because both quantities can be calculated exactly from the information given — nothing is missing.
Q11Non-Geometric Comparison
Quantity A is 3/5 of 40.

Quantity B is 45% of 50.

Which quantity is greater?
Correct answer: A. Quantity A = (3/5)×40 = 24. Quantity B = 0.45×50 = 22.5. Since 24 > 22.5, Quantity A is greater. Choice B reverses the correct comparison. Choice C is incorrect because, while close, the values are not equal. Choice D is incorrect because both quantities can be calculated exactly from the given information.

Reading

PassageFor most of the 1800s, lighthouse keepers lived beside the towers they tended, climbing hundreds of steps each night to keep the lamp burning. The job demanded constant attention: a few hours of neglect could let the light go dark, putting nearby ships at risk. When the first automatic lighthouse lamps were introduced in the early 1900s, many keepers doubted the new technology, fearing it would fail during storms when it mattered most. Decades of reliable performance slowly changed their minds. By the 1960s, most lighthouses had been automated, and the profession that once required a keeper’s constant presence had nearly disappeared.
Q12Comprehension — Main Idea
What is the main idea of the passage?
Correct answer: B. The passage traces how lighthouse keeping shifted from requiring constant human presence to being automated by the 1960s, which is its central thread. Choice A describes only the keepers’ initial doubt, a supporting detail rather than the main idea. Choice C states a possible consequence of neglect but is not something the passage says actually happened. Choice D refers to a detail about when lighthouses required keepers, not the passage’s overall point about automation.
Q13Comprehension — Inference
Based on the passage, why were keepers initially skeptical of automatic lamps?
Correct answer: A. The passage directly states keepers “fear[ed] it would fail during storms when it mattered most,” supporting this choice. Choice B introduces cost concerns never mentioned in the passage. Choice C assumes a motive (job protection) the passage never states, even though it might seem plausible. Choice D is too literal — the passage specifies a fear about storm performance, not simple unfamiliarity.
Q14Vocabulary in Context
As used in the passage, the word “demanded” most nearly means:
Correct answer: D. In context, “the job demanded constant attention” means the job required or necessitated constant attention. Choice A softens the meaning too much — “demanded” implies necessity, not a polite request. Choice B is nearly the opposite of the intended meaning. Choice C does not fit — the job wasn’t “questioning” attention, it was requiring it.
PassageWhen Marcus joined the debate team, he assumed the hardest part would be memorizing facts and statistics. He spent hours each week building a library of evidence for every possible topic. During his first real match, though, his opponent calmly dismantled his strongest argument in under a minute, exposing a flaw Marcus had never noticed. Stunned, Marcus lost that round badly. Rather than quitting, he began attending every practice early to study how experienced debaters questioned each other’s logic, not just their facts. Within a season, Marcus was known less for what he memorized and more for how sharply he could think on his feet.
Q15Comprehension — Main Idea
What is the main idea of the passage?
Correct answer: C. The passage shows Marcus shifting his focus from memorization to sharpening his reasoning after a loss revealed its limits — the passage’s central arc. Choice A directly contradicts the passage, which says he did not quit. Choice B is the opposite of what the passage ultimately concludes. Choice D introduces an idea the passage never suggests — the opponent’s skill was simply stronger in the moment, not unfair.
Q16Comprehension — Inference
It can be inferred that Marcus’s opponent won the first match mainly because that opponent:
Correct answer: A. The passage says the opponent “dismantled his strongest argument … exposing a flaw,” which points to a strength in logical analysis rather than raw fact recall. Choice B is not supported — the passage never compares how much each competitor memorized. Choice C introduces a detail never mentioned. Choice D assumes prior knowledge of Marcus’s specific evidence, which the passage does not indicate.
Q17Vocabulary in Context
As used in the passage, the word “dismantled” most nearly means:
Correct answer: B. “Dismantled” in this context describes systematically taking apart and defeating the argument, matching “completely disproved.” Choice A undersells the strength of the action — the passage describes a decisive defeat, not a gentle question. Choice C contradicts the passage, since the opponent actively engaged with and defeated the argument rather than ignoring it. Choice D is nearly the opposite of the intended meaning.

Mathematics

Q18Percent
A jacket originally priced at $80 is on sale for 25% off. What is the sale price?
Correct answer: D. 25% of $80 is 0.25×80=$20; subtracting the discount gives $80−$20=$60. Choice A ($55) subtracts too much, as if the discount were about 31%. Choice B ($65) reflects subtracting only $15, about a 19% discount. Choice C ($70) reflects subtracting just $10, about a 12.5% discount instead of the correct 25%.
Q19Ratio & Proportion
A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar. If a baker uses 9 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar are needed to keep the same ratio?
Correct answer: C. Since 9 cups of flour is 3 times as much as the original 3 cups, the sugar must also be scaled by 3: 2×3=6 cups. Choice A (4) results from dividing 9 by 2 (the sugar's original amount) instead of by 3 (the flour's original amount) to find the scale factor: 9÷2=4.5, rounded down to 4. Choice B (5) results from adding the scale factor (9÷3=3) to the original sugar amount instead of multiplying by it: 2+3=5. Choice D (7) results from adding the ratio's total parts (3+2=5) to the original sugar amount instead of scaling proportionally: 2+5=7.
Q20Order of Operations
What is the value of 6 + 4 × (9 − 5) ÷ 2?
Correct answer: A. Following order of operations: first the parentheses (9−5=4), then multiplication (4×4=16), then division (16÷2=8), and finally addition (6+8=14). Choice B (10) results from adding 6 and 4 first (violating order of operations) and stopping there, without carrying out the remaining × and ÷ steps. Choice C (16) stops after the multiplication step and ignores the division and addition. Choice D (20) results from that same left-to-right slip carried further: 6+4=10 first, then ×(9−5)=40, then ÷2=20 — order of operations skipped throughout.
Q21Algebra
Solve for x: 5x − 3 = 2x + 12
Correct answer: D. Subtracting 2x from both sides gives 3x−3=12. Adding 3 to both sides gives 3x=15, so x=5. Choice A (3) results from dividing 15 by 5 instead of by 3 — using the original coefficient from “5x” rather than the simplified coefficient from “3x.” Choice B (12) results from subtracting 3 from 15 instead of dividing by 3, treating the coefficient as if it were an added term rather than a multiplier: 15−3=12. Choice C (15) is the value of 3x itself, mistakenly left un-divided by 3.
Q22Geometry
A trapezoid has parallel sides of length 6 and 10, and a height of 4. What is its area?
Correct answer: B. The area of a trapezoid is (1/2)(b₁+b₂)(h) = (1/2)(6+10)(4) = (1/2)(16)(4) = 32. Choice A (20) comes from using only the base of 10 and forgetting to add the base of 6 before halving: (1/2)(10)(4)=20. Choice C (24) comes from mistakenly doubling the smaller base instead of adding both different bases: (1/2)(6+6)(4)=24. Choice D (64) comes from forgetting the 1/2 factor entirely: (6+10)(4)=64.
Q23Geometry — Pythagorean Theorem
A right triangle has legs of length 9 and 12. What is the length of the hypotenuse?
Correct answer: C. By the Pythagorean theorem, hypotenuse² = 9²+12² = 81+144 = 225, so the hypotenuse = √225 = 15. Choice D (21) comes from simply adding the two legs (9+12), ignoring the Pythagorean relationship entirely. Choices A (13) and B (18) do not correspond to any calculation using the given legs — they are plausible-looking numbers designed to tempt someone unsure of the correct formula.
Q24Numbers & Estimation
Which of the following is closest to the value of 597 × 31?
Correct answer: A. Rounding 597 to 600 and 31 to 30 gives an estimate of 600×30=18,000, which is closest to the actual product. Choice B (1,800) is off by a factor of 10 — likely from a misplaced decimal or dropped zero. Choice C (6,000) reflects an underestimate, as if only one of the two numbers were rounded appropriately. Choice D (60,000) overestimates by a factor of about 3, as if 597 were rounded up to roughly 2,000 instead of 600.

Language

Q25Capitalization
Identify the segment that contains an error. If there is no error, choose “No mistakes.”
Correct answer: B. Segment B incorrectly capitalizes “Summer.” Seasons (spring, summer, fall, winter) are not proper nouns and should not be capitalized unless they start a sentence or are part of a proper title. Segment A is correctly punctuated, with commas properly setting off the nonessential clause “who lives in Denver.” Segment C contains no errors. Since an actual error exists in segment B, “No mistakes” is not correct.
Q26Usage
Identify the segment that contains an error. If there is no error, choose “No mistakes.”
Correct answer: C. Segment C incorrectly uses “they're” (a contraction of “they are”) where the possessive form “their” is required to describe notes belonging to the students. Segment A is correctly punctuated with a comma before the coordinating conjunction “so.” Segment B contains no errors. Because segment C contains an error, “No mistakes” is incorrect.
Q27Usage
Identify the segment that contains an error. If there is no error, choose “No mistakes.”
Correct answer: B. Segment B contains a subject-verb agreement error. “Neither” is a singular pronoun, so the verb should be “has finished,” not “have finished.” Segment A correctly introduces the singular subject “Neither of the boys.” Segment C is correct. Since segment B contains a genuine error, “No mistakes” is incorrect.
Q28Spelling
Identify the segment that contains an error. If there is no error, choose “No mistakes.”
Correct answer: A. Segment A misspells “committee,” which requires a double “m,” double “t,” and double “e” (c-o-m-m-i-t-t-e-e). Segments B and C contain no spelling errors. Because segment A contains a clear spelling error, “No mistakes” is incorrect.
Q29Composition
Which is the best way to combine these two sentences?
“The team practiced every day after school. They wanted to win the championship.”
Correct answer: B. Choice B combines the two ideas clearly and logically using “because” to show the cause-and-effect relationship between practicing and wanting to win. Choice A creates a comma splice by joining two independent clauses with only a comma. Choice C uses “but,” a contrast word, which misrepresents the relationship between the two ideas — there is no contrast here, only a cause and its motivation. Choice D is needlessly wordy and repetitive (“it was because … so that's why”), which weakens clarity rather than improving it.
Q30Punctuation
Identify the segment that contains an error. If there is no error, choose “No mistakes.”
Correct answer: D. The sentence is correctly punctuated throughout: segment A closes the introductory clause with a comma, segment B connects the infinitive phrase “to stay focused” to the verb without an unnecessary comma, and segment C completes the sentence with the second parallel infinitive “play as a unit,” correctly joined by “and” without a comma. Since no segment contains an error, “No mistakes” is the correct choice — a reminder that not every real-test sentence has a planted error, and confidently choosing “No mistakes” when a sentence truly is correct is just as important as spotting real mistakes elsewhere.
