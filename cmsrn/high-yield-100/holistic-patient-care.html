Holistic Patient Care

Q33Spiritual distress
A patient with newly diagnosed pancreatic cancer says, "I wonder why God is punishing me." Which response by the RN is most therapeutic?

Why C is correct

Open reflection invites the patient to share spiritual distress and shows presence without judging beliefs.

Why the others are wrong

A. Redirecting avoids the patient's expressed need.

B. Correcting the patient's belief closes communication and dismisses the concern.

D. A generic reassurance minimizes a genuine spiritual crisis.

Q34End-of-life communication
A patient with end-stage heart failure who is comfort-focused asks quietly, "Am I dying?" Which response is best?

Why D is correct

Exploring the question honors the patient's readiness for information before the RN or team gives details.

Why the others are wrong

A. False reassurance undermines trust at the end of life.

B. Deflecting leaves the patient alone with a direct and important question.

C. A blunt prognosis without exploring readiness or involving the provider is not appropriate.

Q35Refusal of blood products
A competent adult who is a Jehovah's Witness is scheduled for surgery and signs a refusal of blood transfusion. Which nursing action is most appropriate?

Why C is correct

A competent adult has the right to refuse treatment, and the team must know to plan alternatives.

Why the others are wrong

A. Coercion violates the patient's autonomy.

B. Giving blood against a valid refusal is a serious ethical and legal violation.

D. The patient's own informed decision cannot be overridden by relatives.

Q36Interpreter use
A patient who speaks very little English needs discharge teaching about anticoagulants. The patient's 12-year-old child offers to translate. What should the RN do?

Why C is correct

Trained interpreters give accurate medical translation and protect confidentiality, and teach-back confirms understanding.

Why the others are wrong

A. A child cannot reliably interpret medical terms and should not carry this burden.

B. Written English does not help a patient with limited English.

D. A new anticoagulant requires teaching before the patient goes home.

Q37Health literacy
A patient nods during teaching about a new inhaler but looks confused when asked to repeat the steps. What is the best next action?

Why D is correct

A return demonstration shows what the patient can truly do and reveals knowledge gaps.

Why the others are wrong

A. Nodding is not evidence of understanding.

B. More text does not solve a comprehension problem.

C. The patient should be supported to learn, and the decision is not the nurse's alone.

Q38Pain in dementia
A patient with advanced dementia cannot report pain but is grimacing, guarding the hip, and moaning during turns. Which action is best?

Why D is correct

Behavior is the most reliable indicator of pain in patients who cannot self-report.

Why the others are wrong

A. Sedating untreated pain hides the cause and harms the patient.

B. Patients with advanced dementia often cannot ask.

C. A numeric self-report is not usable when the patient cannot answer reliably.

Q39Intimate partner violence
An RN notices bruises in different stages of healing on a patient's arms and a partner who answers every question. Which approach to screening is best?

Why B is correct

Privacy protects the patient and increases honesty, and the safety screen should follow policy.

Why the others are wrong

A. This may endanger the patient and obtain a false account.

C. Documentation without screening ignores an opportunity to protect the patient.

D. Confrontation can escalate the risk to the patient.

Q40Fasting and medications
A patient who takes twice-daily oral medication says, "I will fast from dawn to sunset for Ramadan and will not take pills in the daytime." What should the RN do?

Why C is correct

Respecting religious practice while ensuring safe therapy is the best balance, and a timing change may satisfy both.

Why the others are wrong

A. Doubling doses can be unsafe for many drugs.

B. A planned change is not noncompliance and labeling is inappropriate.

D. This dismisses the patient's values and may lead to nonadherence.

Q41Comfort care dyspnea
A patient with end-stage COPD on comfort-focused care has distressing shortness of breath. Which intervention is most appropriate?

Why D is correct

Opioids relieve the sensation of dyspnea, and a fan and positioning help comfort without aggressive intervention.

Why the others are wrong

A. Withholding appropriate opioids causes needless suffering.

B. Intense exercise exhausts a patient at the end of life.

C. Intubation is not part of comfort-focused care.

Q42Grief
After an expected death, the patient's spouse states, "He is just sleeping," and refuses to leave. Which RN response is best?

Why A is correct

Grief takes many forms, and compassionate presence allows the spouse to move toward acceptance.

Why the others are wrong

B. Pressuring someone in shock harms the relationship and the grieving process.

C. Sedation is not routine and delays natural grieving.

D. This criticizes a normal reaction.

Q43Portable medical orders
A patient with advanced lung disease wants their wishes about resuscitation to be followed by emergency personnel at home. Which document is most appropriate?

Why C is correct

A POLST is a portable medical order for seriously ill patients that emergency staff follow.

Why the others are wrong

A. Verbal statements are not enforceable instructions for responders.

B. A living will gives guidance but is not an immediate medical order for emergency crews.

D. An informal note is not a recognized medical order.

Q44Organ donation
A family member of a patient with brain death asks the RN whether organ donation is possible. What is the RN's best response?

Why D is correct

Trained requesters speak with families to avoid conflict of interest and ensure informed choices.

Why the others are wrong

A. Notification of the organization is required by policy and should not wait.

B. Dismissing the question leaves the family without information.

C. The bedside nurse should not request consent.

Q45Inclusive care
A patient admitted for heart failure introduces their same-sex spouse and asks that the spouse stay for all discussions. What should the RN do?

Why A is correct

Patient-centered care honors identity and the patient's choice of who is involved.

Why the others are wrong

B. Requiring proof is discriminatory and unnecessary.

C. This ignores the patient's own designation.

D. A different visitor policy for this couple is unjustified.

Q46Motivational interviewing
A patient admitted with pancreatitis says, "I know I drink too much, but I do not think I can stop." Which response best uses motivational interviewing?

Why D is correct

Open questions elicit the patient's own reasons for change and support readiness.

Why the others are wrong

A. Scare tactics increase defensiveness.

B. Imposing a plan skips the patient's readiness.

C. A dismissive reply discourages honesty.

Q47Anger
A patient who has waited a long time for pain medication shouts at the RN and demands another nurse. Which response is most effective?

Why B is correct

Acknowledging feelings lowers tension and keeps the focus on solving the problem.

Why the others are wrong

A. Skipping assessment ignores a safety standard.

C. Defensiveness escalates the conflict.

D. Leaving without a plan increases anger and delays care.
