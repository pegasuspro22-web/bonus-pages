Domain 4 of 5 · 15 questions

Professional Concepts

Q65Privacy
Two RNs discuss a patient's diagnosis in a hospital elevator crowded with visitors. Which action by a nearby RN is most appropriate?

Why D is correct

Protected health information must not be discussed where others can hear.

Why the others are wrong

A. Waiting leaves the privacy breach to continue in the meantime.

B. Silence allows the breach to continue and models poor practice.

C. Details can still identify the patient.

Q66Scope of practice
A charge nurse asks an RN to perform a procedure the RN has never been trained to do and is not credentialed to perform. What should the RN do?

Why D is correct

Nurses are accountable for practicing within competence and scope.

Why the others are wrong

A. Acting outside competence puts patients at risk.

B. Delegating an unsafe task is also unsafe.

C. Self-teaching does not establish validated competence.

Q67Incident reports
A patient falls but is unharmed. How should the RN handle documentation?

Why B is correct

The record states facts, and the incident report is an internal quality tool not referenced in the chart.

Why the others are wrong

A. Reports should contain facts, not opinions or assigned fault.

C. Referencing the report in the chart is discouraged.

D. All events need documentation.

Q68Impaired colleague
An RN smells alcohol on a coworker who is slurring speech before medication administration. What should the RN do?

Why A is correct

Patient safety comes first, and impaired practice must be reported through proper channels.

Why the others are wrong

B. Waiting allows possible errors to occur.

C. Covering up risks harm to patients.

D. Public confrontation is unprofessional and unsafe.

Q69Social media
An RN wants to post a photo of a rare wound on social media without a name or face visible. What is the best response?

Why D is correct

Patient images are protected, and even unnamed photos can lead to identification.

Why the others are wrong

A. Private groups still breach privacy.

B. Details alone may allow recognition.

C. Discharge does not remove the duty of confidentiality.

Q70Mandated reporting
During a home visit, an RN suspects that a dependent older adult is being neglected by a caregiver. What is the RN's duty?

Why C is correct

Nurses are mandated reporters and need reasonable suspicion, not proof.

Why the others are wrong

A. Confrontation can endanger the patient.

B. Waiting delays protection.

D. Documentation alone does not meet the legal duty.

Q71Informed consent
A patient asks the RN to explain the risks of a procedure before signing the consent form. What is the RN's best action?

Why C is correct

The provider is responsible for informing, while the RN verifies understanding and advocates.

Why the others are wrong

A. A competent patient signs for themselves.

B. Consent must be informed and voluntary.

D. Informing is the provider's role.

Q72Restraints
A confused patient keeps pulling at a urinary catheter. The RN has tried reorientation and a family sitter. What must happen before using a restraint?

Why C is correct

Restraints are a last resort with orders and close monitoring.

Why the others are wrong

A. That is excessive and unsafe.

B. Patients in restraints need regular checks.

D. Restraints need an order and proper assessment.

Q73Disaster triage
During a mass casualty event using START triage, a victim is breathing at 36/min. How should the victim be tagged?

Why D is correct

A rate above 30 breaths per minute indicates a life-threatening problem needing immediate care.

Why the others are wrong

A. Delayed is for patients who can wait with a stable respiratory rate.

B. Black is for those who are not breathing after airway repositioning.

C. Green is for walking wounded.

Q74Just culture
An RN makes a medication error because of a confusing label that looks like another product. Which approach reflects a just culture?

Why B is correct

Just culture separates human error from reckless behavior and fixes system causes.

Why the others are wrong

A. Punishing unintentional error discourages reporting.

C. Ignoring prevents learning.

D. Public shaming damages safety culture.

Q75Right to refuse
A competent patient declines a recommended blood draw. What should the RN do?

Why B is correct

Patients have the right to refuse and need information to make an informed refusal.

Why the others are wrong

A. Forced treatment violates rights.

C. That would be battery.

D. Threats are coercive.

Q76Evidence-based question
Which format best organizes a clinical question for evidence-based practice?

Why D is correct

PICO structures a searchable question about patient groups and results.

Why the others are wrong

A. That is a communication format.

B. That is an improvement cycle.

C. That is a documentation format.

Q77Professional boundaries
A patient's family offers the RN an expensive gift certificate on the day of discharge. What should the RN do?

Why A is correct

Valuable gifts can blur boundaries, while a card is acceptable.

Why the others are wrong

B. Gifts of value should not be accepted.

C. Secrecy suggests impropriety.

D. Discharge does not remove the boundary concern.

Q78Unsafe staffing
An RN is assigned patients that exceed safe limits, and the manager says there is no one else. What should the RN do?

Why C is correct

Escalation and documentation protect patients and the nurse.

Why the others are wrong

A. Abandonment is unsafe and illegal.

B. Silence leaves the hazard unaddressed.

D. Shortcuts compromise care and leave the unsafe assignment unaddressed.

Q79Ethical principles
A competent patient chooses a treatment the care team does not prefer. The RN supports the choice. Which ethical principle is being applied?

Why D is correct

Autonomy is the right to make informed decisions about one's own care.

Why the others are wrong

A. Justice concerns fair distribution of resources.

B. Beneficence means acting for the patient's good as the team sees it.

C. Nonmaleficence means avoiding harm.
