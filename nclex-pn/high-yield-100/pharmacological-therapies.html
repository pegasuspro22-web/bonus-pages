Pharmacological Therapies

Q66Medication rights
A practical nurse finds a labeled syringe of insulin on the counter, drawn up by a nurse who left for lunch, with the client's name and today's date on the label. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: B. A nurse should give only medications that the nurse prepared, because the contents, dose, and time cannot be verified otherwise. The unverified syringe is discarded per policy and a new dose is prepared after checking the order. A label with the client's name does not prove what is in the syringe, and holding it for later adds delay and risk.
Q67Medication reconciliation and MAR use
A newly admitted client's home medication list includes metoprolol, but no beta-blocker was ordered on admission. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: C. Any difference between home medications and admission orders must be resolved by the prescriber. Abruptly stopping a beta-blocker can cause rebound tachycardia and hypertension, but the nurse cannot start a medication without an order. Using the client's own supply, assuming the omission was intended, or adding it without an order all bypass the required clarification.
Q68Routes of administration
A client with a nasogastric tube is prescribed several oral medications. Which medication should the nurse question before crushing it?
Correct answer: A. Extended-release tablets are designed to release the drug slowly, and crushing them delivers the whole dose at once, which can cause toxicity. The nurse should ask the provider or pharmacist for a liquid or immediate-release alternative. The three immediate-release tablets can be crushed and given through the tube.
Q69Blood products
Ten minutes into a transfusion of red blood cells, a client reports low back pain and chills and has dark urine and a falling blood pressure. What should the nurse do first?
Correct answer: C. Back pain, chills, hypotension, and dark urine suggest an acute hemolytic reaction, a life-threatening emergency. The transfusion is stopped immediately, and normal saline is run through new tubing to keep the vein open without pushing more blood into the client. Slowing or continuing the blood delays treatment, and flushing the same tubing sends the remaining incompatible blood to the client.
Q70Blood products
A client receiving a red blood cell transfusion develops cough, dyspnea, crackles, and a rising blood pressure. After stopping the transfusion, which action should the nurse take next?
Correct answer: B. These findings point to transfusion-associated circulatory overload. Sitting the client upright eases breathing, and oxygen and a diuretic treat the fluid overload. Lying flat worsens breathing, increasing IV fluids adds to the overload, and restarting the transfusion without provider direction adds more volume.
Q71IV flow
A provider orders 1,000 mL of IV fluid to infuse over 8 hours. The tubing delivers 15 drops per mL. At how many drops per minute should the nurse set the infusion?
Correct answer: B. Drops per minute equals volume times drop factor divided by time in minutes: 1,000 mL × 15 drops/mL ÷ 480 minutes = 31.25, which rounds to 31 drops/min. Using a 10 drops/mL set gives 21, using 20 drops/mL gives 42, and 125 is the rate in mL per hour, not drops per minute.
Q72Controlled substances
A practical nurse prepared 10 mg of morphine from a vial, but the order is for 6 mg. What is the correct way to handle the remaining 4 mg?
Correct answer: B. Wasting a controlled substance requires a witness, and both nurses document and sign it so the narcotic count stays accurate. Returning drug to a vial contaminates it, wasting alone leaves no verification, and giving more than the ordered dose is a medication error.
Q73Pre-administration data review
Before giving digoxin to a client with heart failure, the nurse reviews the client's data. Which finding should cause the nurse to hold the dose and notify the provider?
Correct answer: D. Low potassium increases the heart's sensitivity to digoxin and raises the risk of digoxin toxicity and dysrhythmias. A pulse of 62/min is above the usual hold threshold of 60/min, the blood pressure is acceptable, and mild ankle edema is common in heart failure and is not a reason to hold the drug.
Q74Therapeutic response monitoring
One hour after a client receives IV morphine for postoperative pain, which finding best shows a therapeutic response with a safe effect?
Correct answer: A. A good response is meaningful pain relief with a normal respiratory rate and alertness. Complete pain relief with slow respirations and difficulty waking signals oversedation and respiratory depression. Unchanged pain means the drug is not effective, and a fast respiratory rate with moderate pain may signal another problem.
Q75Adverse response monitoring
A client taking lisinopril reports sudden swelling of the lips and tongue. Which action should the nurse take first?
Correct answer: D. Angioedema is a serious adverse effect of ACE inhibitors and can rapidly close the airway. The airway is the priority, and emergency help is called at once. Waiting after an antihistamine delays treatment, the dry cough is a different and less dangerous effect, and elevating the legs does not address the airway.
Q76Drug interactions
A client taking sertraline says she has started taking St. John's wort for low mood. Which response by the nurse is best?
Correct answer: B. St. John's wort increases serotonin activity, and combining it with an SSRI can cause serotonin syndrome, a dangerous condition. The provider must be told so the combination can be reviewed. Herbal products are not automatically safe, spacing the doses does not remove the interaction, and increasing the SSRI dose raises the risk.
Q77Critical labs
Which laboratory result should the nurse report to the provider first?
Correct answer: C. A potassium of 6.4 mEq/L with peaked T waves is a life-threatening hyperkalemia that can cause fatal dysrhythmias. Mildly low sodium, the mild anemia expected in chronic kidney disease, and a slightly high BUN are abnormal but not immediately dangerous.
Q78Medication calculations
A provider orders acetaminophen 15 mg/kg by mouth for a child who weighs 44 lb. The suspension contains 160 mg per 5 mL. How many mL should the nurse give?
Correct answer: C. The child weighs 44 ÷ 2.2 = 20 kg. The dose is 15 mg/kg × 20 kg = 300 mg. With a concentration of 160 mg per 5 mL, the volume is 300 ÷ 160 × 5 = 9.4 mL. Forgetting to multiply by 5 mL gives 1.9 mL, using half the weight gives 4.7 mL, and using pounds instead of kilograms gives 20.6 mL.
