Reduction of Risk Potential

Q79Risk identification
A client on bed rest after hip replacement has a swollen right calf and suddenly reports sharp chest pain on inspiration and shortness of breath. The heart rate is 118/min. Which complication should the nurse suspect?
Correct answer: A. Immobility after orthopedic surgery, a swollen calf suggesting a deep vein thrombosis, and sudden pleuritic chest pain with dyspnea and tachycardia point to a pulmonary embolism. A myocardial infarction typically causes pressure-like pain not related to breathing, pneumonia develops more gradually with fever and cough, and a pneumothorax is not linked to a swollen calf.
Q80Specimen collection
A practical nurse is teaching a client how to collect a sputum specimen for culture. Which instruction is correct?
Correct answer: D. An early-morning specimen contains secretions that have collected overnight, and rinsing with water removes food and bacteria from the mouth without killing the organisms to be cultured. A deep cough brings up sputum from the lungs. Eating first increases the risk of contamination and vomiting, antiseptic mouthwash can prevent growth of organisms, and saliva is not sputum.
Q81Diagnostic and lab interpretation
A client with diabetic ketoacidosis has arterial blood gas results of pH 7.29, PaCO2 29 mm Hg, and HCO3- 13 mEq/L. How should the nurse interpret these results?
Correct answer: A. The pH is low, so the client is acidotic. The bicarbonate is low, which identifies a metabolic cause. The PaCO2 is also low because the lungs are blowing off carbon dioxide to compensate, but the pH is still abnormal, so compensation is partial. Respiratory acidosis would show a high PaCO2, and both alkalosis options do not fit a low pH.
Q82Prenatal complications
A client with severe preeclampsia is receiving IV magnesium sulfate. Which finding requires the nurse to stop the infusion and notify the provider?
Correct answer: D. Magnesium sulfate toxicity depresses the central nervous system, causing slowed respirations and loss of deep tendon reflexes. The infusion is stopped and the provider is notified, and calcium gluconate is the antidote. Urine output of at least 30 mL/hr is adequate, warmth and flushing are common effects, and a still-elevated blood pressure is expected in preeclampsia.
Q83Point-of-care tests
A client who takes insulin has a capillary blood glucose of 52 mg/dL. The client is alert and able to swallow. Which action should the nurse take first?
Correct answer: A. An alert client who can swallow is treated with 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, such as 4 oz of juice, and the glucose is rechecked in 15 minutes. A sandwich contains fat and protein that slow absorption, IV dextrose is reserved for clients who cannot swallow or are unresponsive, and more insulin would lower the glucose further.
Q84Vital signs
A pulse oximeter shows 84% on a client who is talking comfortably, has pink skin, and has a respiratory rate of 16/min. The client is wearing dark nail polish. What should the nurse do first?
Correct answer: B. Dark nail polish can interfere with the light sensors and give a falsely low reading. The client looks and acts well, so the nurse first checks the accuracy of the measurement by changing the site or removing the polish. Applying oxygen or calling the provider for an unconfirmed value is premature, and waiting an hour leaves a possible true problem unchecked.
Q85Procedure and diagnosis precautions
Which finding in a client's history is the greatest safety concern before a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan?
Correct answer: B. The strong magnet of an MRI can interfere with or damage many pacemakers, so the provider and radiology staff must be told before the scan. Dental fillings and most joint replacements are not usually a hazard. Fear of enclosed spaces can be managed with support or medication but is not a safety hazard from the magnet.
Q86ECG
A nurse finds a client on telemetry slumped in bed, unresponsive, and without a pulse. The monitor shows a rapid, regular, wide-complex rhythm at 220/min. What is the priority action?
Correct answer: B. An unresponsive client without a pulse and a rapid, wide-complex rhythm has pulseless ventricular tachycardia, which is treated like ventricular fibrillation with immediate CPR and defibrillation. Checking the leads is appropriate for a suspicious rhythm in a client who looks well, not for one who is pulseless. Medication and observation delay lifesaving treatment.
Q87Perioperative care
Two hours after abdominal surgery, which finding is the earliest sign of hemorrhage?
Correct answer: D. Restlessness and rising heart rate are early compensatory signs of blood loss. A falling blood pressure is a late sign. A stable, mildly low blood pressure, adequate urine output, and a slightly elevated temperature are not signs of hemorrhage.
Q88Tubes and IV access
A client's IV site is cool, pale, and swollen, and the infusion is running slowly. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: D. A cool, pale, swollen site with a slow infusion indicates infiltration, in which fluid leaks into the tissue. The infusion is stopped, the catheter removed, and the IV restarted elsewhere. Flushing forces more fluid into the tissue, and waiting or slowing the rate allows further tissue damage, especially with irritating solutions.
Q89Focused data collection
A client with cellulitis of the right lower leg reports that the redness is spreading. Which focused assessment is best?
Correct answer: B. Marking the border of the redness allows the team to see whether the infection is spreading, and temperature, pulses, and pain show its severity and effect on circulation. Massaging inflamed tissue can spread infection, and weight or walking does not measure the progress of cellulitis.
Q90Monitoring response to treatment
A client receiving a continuous heparin infusion has an aPTT of 92 seconds. The therapeutic range for this client is 60 to 80 seconds. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: A. An aPTT above the therapeutic range means an increased risk of bleeding, so the nurse notifies the provider, expects an order to lower the rate, and checks for bleeding. Continuing or increasing the rate adds risk, and vitamin K is the antidote for warfarin, not for heparin, for which protamine sulfate is used.
