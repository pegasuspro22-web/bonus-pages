Safety and Infection Prevention and Control

Q22Client identification
A confused client's identification band was removed when an IV was restarted, and a new band has not been applied. The nurse must give the 0900 medications. What should the nurse do first?
Correct answer: C. A confused client cannot reliably state identifiers, and a room or bed sign is not an identifier. Without a band, the nurse confirms identity through the method in policy, usually involving another staff member, and applies a new band before any medication is given. Giving the medications first defeats the purpose of identification.
Q23Safe equipment use
While setting up an electric bed for a client, a practical nurse notices a frayed power cord. What is the best action?
Correct answer: C. A frayed cord is an electrical shock and fire hazard. Damaged equipment is taken out of service, tagged so no one else uses it, and reported for repair. Taping the cord, avoiding contact, or delaying the report all leave a hazardous device in use.
Q24Allergy checks
A client who had anaphylaxis after taking penicillin last year is prescribed IV cefazolin, a cephalosporin. What should the practical nurse do before giving the first dose?
Correct answer: D. Clients with a history of penicillin allergy can react to cephalosporins because of similar chemical structure. A history of anaphylaxis makes this especially serious, so the nurse withholds the dose and clarifies with the provider first. Giving the drug slowly or only documenting the allergy still exposes the client to a possibly life-threatening reaction.
Q25Environmental and disaster safety
A fire starts in a wastebasket in the room of a client who is in bed receiving oxygen. Which action should the nurse take first?
Correct answer: B. The first step in fire response is rescue: remove anyone in immediate danger, especially a client with oxygen in the room. After that the nurse activates the alarm, contains the fire by closing doors, and extinguishes it if it is small and safe to do so.
Q26Least-restrictive restraints
A client with dementia keeps pulling at an indwelling urinary catheter, and the nurse is considering restraints. Which action reflects the least restrictive approach?
Correct answer: C. Restraints are a last resort after less restrictive measures fail. The best first step is to find and remove the cause, and a catheter that is no longer needed can be removed, which ends the behavior. Wrist restraints, mittens, and enclosure beds all limit the client's movement and carry their own risks.
Q27Incident and near-miss awareness
A practical nurse takes the wrong insulin vial from the medication cart, notices the mistake before drawing up any insulin, and puts the vial back. No dose was given. Which action is most appropriate?
Correct answer: B. Near misses show weaknesses in the system, such as look-alike labels or cart layout, and should be reported through the facility's event-reporting system even though no harm occurred. Event reports are not part of the client's medical record, so the chart is not the place for them. Ignoring the event or reporting only verbally prevents the facility from finding and fixing the problem.
Q28Transfer devices
A client who weighs 145 kg (320 lb) cannot bear weight on either leg and needs to move from the bed to a chair. Which device should the nurse use?
Correct answer: C. A client who cannot bear weight needs a full-body lift, and a person of this size requires more than one staff member for safe handling. A gait belt and a sit-to-stand lift both require the client to bear weight through the legs. A transfer board requires the client to help with the slide and is unsafe without adequate help for a client of this size.
Q29Infection-control practices
After removing gloves following care of a client with Clostridioides difficile infection, which hand hygiene method should the nurse use?
Correct answer: D. Alcohol-based sanitizers do not kill C. difficile spores. Washing with soap and water removes the spores by friction. Sanitizer, with or without new gloves, will not remove them, and rinsing with water alone without soap is not effective.
Q30PPE
A nurse is leaving the room of a client on contact and droplet precautions. Which sequence for removing personal protective equipment is correct?
Correct answer: C. PPE is removed from most contaminated to least contaminated: gloves first, then the goggles or face shield, then the gown, and the mask last, after leaving the room, followed by hand hygiene. Removing the mask or gown early exposes the nurse's face and clothing to contaminated surfaces.
Q31Asepsis
A practical nurse is setting up a sterile field for a dressing change. Which action indicates a break in sterile technique?
Correct answer: B. Reaching across a sterile field can drop contaminated particles onto it and brushes clothing against it, so it breaks technique. Keeping the field at waist level and in sight, opening the far flap away from the body first, and treating the outer 1 inch (2.5 cm) as contaminated are all correct.
Q32Isolation precautions
A client is admitted with suspected meningococcal meningitis. Which protective equipment is required for close contact with this client?
Correct answer: D. Meningococcal meningitis spreads through large respiratory droplets, so droplet precautions with a surgical mask are used until the client has received 24 hours of effective antibiotic therapy. An N95 and a negative-pressure room are for airborne diseases such as tuberculosis, measles, and chickenpox. Gown and gloves are contact precautions.
Q33Isolation precautions
A client is admitted with chickenpox (varicella), and airborne and contact precautions are started. Which nurse should not be assigned to this client?
Correct answer: D. A nurse with no immunity can catch chickenpox and should not care for the client. Immunity comes from having had the disease or from two doses of the vaccine. A pregnant nurse with documented immunity is protected, so pregnancy alone is not a reason to exclude her.
Q34Standard precautions
A practical nurse has just given a subcutaneous injection with a safety needle. What is the correct next action?
Correct answer: A. Used needles are never recapped, bent, or carried. The safety device is activated and the needle is placed in the puncture-resistant sharps container immediately, where it was used. Recapping, bending, and carrying used needles are the main causes of needlestick injuries.
