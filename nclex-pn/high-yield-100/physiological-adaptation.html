Physiological Adaptation

Q91Acute conditions
A client with right lower quadrant pain, nausea, and a low-grade fever is suspected of having appendicitis. Which intervention is contraindicated?
Correct answer: B. Heat increases blood flow to an inflamed appendix and can lead to rupture. Keeping the client NPO in case surgery is needed, positioning for comfort, and monitoring for changes are all appropriate.
Q92Chronic conditions
A client with diabetes is being taught foot care. Which statement by the client shows correct understanding?
Correct answer: B. Clients with diabetes often have reduced sensation and circulation in the feet, so daily inspection and protective footwear prevent unnoticed injuries. Hot water can burn feet that cannot feel heat, cutting corns can cause infections, and walking barefoot exposes the feet to injury.
Q93Life-threatening conditions
A client develops sudden wheezing, lip swelling, and a drop in blood pressure shortly after an IV antibiotic is started. Which action should the nurse take first?
Correct answer: A. These findings suggest anaphylaxis, a life-threatening reaction. The first step is to stop the drug and call for help so epinephrine and airway support can be given quickly. Antihistamines act too slowly as the only response, slowing the infusion still delivers the drug, and delaying action for a full set of vital signs wastes critical time.
Q94Drainage devices
A practical nurse has emptied a closed-suction drain (Jackson-Pratt) and cleaned the port. Which action should the nurse take next?
Correct answer: B. A closed-suction drain works by vacuum. After emptying, the bulb is squeezed to remove air and the port is closed while it is compressed, which restores suction. An open port breaks the vacuum, saline should not be added, and clamping the tubing blocks drainage.
Q95Thermoregulation
An older adult is found confused in a hot apartment with hot, dry skin and a temperature of 105°F (40.6°C). What should the nurse do first?
Correct answer: D. Confusion, hot dry skin, and a temperature this high indicate heat stroke, a medical emergency that requires rapid cooling. Antipyretics do not work in heat stroke, and a confused client may not swallow safely. Warm blankets would raise the temperature further.
Q96Wound care
A client has a stage 2 pressure injury with a small amount of drainage. Which dressing is most appropriate?
Correct answer: C. Partial-thickness wounds heal best in a moist environment, which hydrocolloid or film dressings maintain while protecting the site. Dry gauze sticks and damages new tissue, wet-to-dry dressings with antiseptic can injure healthy cells, and leaving the wound open allows it to dry out and become infected.
Q97CPR and emergency response
A nurse is performing chest compressions on an adult. Which technique is correct?
Correct answer: D. Effective adult compressions are at least 2 inches deep, but not more than about 2.4 inches, at 100 to 120 per minute, with complete recoil so the heart can refill. Shallow and slow compressions do not circulate blood, compressions deeper than recommended can injure the chest, and long pauses reduce blood flow to the brain.
Q98Respiratory interventions
A client with COPD has an oxygen saturation of 82% on room air and is short of breath. Which initial oxygen order does the nurse anticipate?
Correct answer: A. Clients with chronic COPD may depend on a low oxygen level to stimulate breathing, and high concentrations can suppress it and cause carbon dioxide retention. Low-flow oxygen, typically 1 to 2 L/min, aims for a saturation of about 88% to 92%. The other options deliver much higher oxygen concentrations.
Q99Fluid and electrolyte imbalance
Twenty-four hours after a thyroidectomy, a client reports tingling around the mouth and in the fingers. When the nurse taps the facial nerve in front of the ear, the corner of the mouth twitches. Which imbalance does the nurse suspect?
Correct answer: A. Tingling around the mouth and fingers and a twitch when the facial nerve is tapped (Chvostek sign) are signs of low calcium, which can occur when the parathyroid glands are injured or removed during thyroid surgery. Hyperkalemia causes muscle weakness and dysrhythmias, hyponatremia causes confusion and headache, and hypermagnesemia decreases reflexes.
Q100Dialysis
A client on peritoneal dialysis has cloudy outflow fluid and abdominal pain. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: C. Cloudy effluent with abdominal pain suggests peritonitis, a common and serious complication of peritoneal dialysis. The provider is notified and a sample is sent for culture so treatment can begin. Continuing the exchange as normal, lengthening the dwell time, or repeating the exchange delays treatment.
