Health Promotion and Maintenance

Q35Antepartum care
A client who is planning a pregnancy asks how to reduce the risk of neural tube defects. Which instruction is best?
Correct answer: D. The neural tube closes in the first weeks of pregnancy, often before a client knows she is pregnant, so folic acid must be taken before conception and through early pregnancy. Waiting for a positive test is too late for full protection. High-dose vitamin A can harm the fetus, and iron prevents anemia but does not prevent neural tube defects.
Q36Labor care
A client in labor is 8 cm dilated. She becomes irritable, trembles, vomits, and says, 'I can't do this anymore.' Which phase of labor is she in?
Correct answer: A. Irritability, trembling, nausea and vomiting, and loss of coping at 8 to 10 cm of dilation are characteristic of the transition phase, the last part of the first stage. The latent phase is 0 to 3 cm and the active phase is about 4 to 7 cm. The second stage begins at full dilation, when the urge to push develops.
Q37Stable postpartum care
One hour after a vaginal birth, a client's fundus is boggy, above the umbilicus, and displaced to the right. What should the nurse do first?
Correct answer: D. A boggy fundus that is high and pushed to one side is typically caused by a full bladder, which prevents the uterus from contracting and raises the risk of hemorrhage. Emptying the bladder comes first, followed by reassessment and massage if the fundus stays soft. Massaging first is less effective with a distended bladder, and a cold pack or a routine recheck delays care for a possible hemorrhage.
Q38Developmental milestones
The mother of a 2-year-old says her child answers 'no' to everything at mealtime. Which teaching is most appropriate?
Correct answer: A. Toddlers are developing autonomy, and saying 'no' is a normal part of that. Offering limited choices gives the child a sense of control while keeping the parent in charge of what is offered. Forcing food creates power struggles, unlimited choice leads to poor nutrition, and distraction feeding teaches unhealthy eating habits.
Q39Screening and prevention
Which activity is an example of tertiary prevention?
Correct answer: A. Tertiary prevention limits disability and prevents complications in people who already have a disease, as in cardiac rehabilitation after a heart attack. Blood pressure screening and mammograms detect disease early and are secondary prevention. Vaccination prevents disease before it occurs and is primary prevention.
Q40Immunizations
Which client should not receive the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine today?
Correct answer: A. MMR is a live vaccine and is not given during pregnancy because of a theoretical risk to the fetus. Mild illness, breastfeeding, and a penicillin allergy are not contraindications.
Q41Care needs from newborn to older adult
A practical nurse is teaching new parents about safe infant sleep. Which statement by a parent shows correct understanding?
Correct answer: B. Placing the infant on the back on a firm, flat surface with no soft items lowers the risk of sudden infant death and suffocation. Healthy infants do not choke more often on their backs, side positioning is unstable, and blankets, pillows, and positioners increase the risk of suffocation.
Q42Learning barriers
A client with limited health literacy is learning to give insulin injections. Which teaching approach is best?
Correct answer: D. Clients with limited health literacy learn best through simple language, demonstration, and teach-back, in which the client shows the skill. A long booklet may be too hard to read, and asking whether the client understands often gets a polite yes without confirming the skill. A single video with unsupervised practice does not check for errors.
Q43Health history
A client scheduled for surgery says, 'I don't take any medications.' Which follow-up question is most likely to reveal a risk of bleeding or drug interactions?
Correct answer: C. Many clients do not consider aspirin, pain relievers, vitamins, or herbal products such as ginkgo, garlic, or ginseng to be medications, yet these can increase bleeding or interact with anesthesia. A direct question about over-the-counter and herbal products uncovers this information. The other questions are useful but would not identify this specific risk.
