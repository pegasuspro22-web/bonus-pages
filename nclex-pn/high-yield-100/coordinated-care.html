Coordinated Care

Q1Prioritization
At change of shift, a practical nurse on a surgical unit is told about four clients. Which client should the nurse assess first?
Correct answer: D. New confusion with fast breathing and a low oxygen saturation soon after orthopedic surgery suggests a pulmonary or fat embolism, which threatens airway, breathing, and circulation and needs assessment right away. Severe pancreatitis pain is important but is an expected, treatable problem. Urgency to void after furosemide is an expected drug effect. A dark pink, moist stoma with serous drainage is a normal finding in a new colostomy.
Q2Prioritization
Four clients are assigned to a practical nurse. Which client's finding requires the nurse's immediate follow-up?
Correct answer: B. A cold, numb foot after femoral artery access suggests arterial occlusion or a clot in the affected limb, which can cause tissue loss within hours, so it is the only unexpected, limb-threatening finding. A low-grade temperature and mild tenderness on postoperative day 3 are expected. A saturation at a documented COPD baseline is expected. A glucose of 210 mg/dL needs treatment at the scheduled time but is not an immediate threat.
Q3Assignment and delegation
A practical nurse is deciding which vital-sign checks to assign to an assistive personnel (AP). Which client's vital signs should the nurse keep rather than delegate?
Correct answer: D. A client shortly after surgery with a low blood pressure is unstable. Interpreting the trend and deciding what to do next requires nursing assessment, so the nurse keeps this task. Routine vital signs on stable, predictable clients, such as steady hypertension, a healing fracture, or improving pneumonia, can be delegated with clear instructions about which values to report.
Q4Assignment and delegation
Which task is most appropriate for a practical nurse to assign to an assistive personnel (AP)?
Correct answer: B. Repositioning on a schedule set by the care plan is a routine, predictable task within the AP's training. Assessing the skin around a tracheostomy, giving initial teaching, and monitoring the response to a new medication all require nursing knowledge and judgment, so they cannot be delegated to an AP.
Q5Client rights and advocacy
A client who is deaf communicates in American Sign Language and needs wound-care teaching before discharge. The client's teenage daughter says she can sign for the client. What should the practical nurse do?
Correct answer: C. Clients have the right to accurate communication in their own language, which for many deaf clients is sign language rather than written English. A qualified interpreter keeps the teaching complete and confidential. A family member, especially a minor, may filter or leave out information and should not interpret medical content. Written notes alone may not match the client's reading level, and a signature does not prove understanding.
Q6Client rights and advocacy
An alert, oriented client refuses a scheduled oral antihypertensive, saying, 'I don't want that one anymore.' Which action should the practical nurse take?
Correct answer: C. A client with decision-making capacity has the right to refuse treatment. The nurse's role is to find out why, make sure the client understands the risks, document the refusal, and notify the provider so the plan can be reviewed. Hiding the medication in food, pressuring the client with a witness, or threatening a delayed discharge all override the client's right to make the decision.
Q7Confidentiality and privacy
A practical nurse notices a coworker from another unit opening the electronic record of a well-known local athlete who is not in the coworker's assignment. What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: A. Opening a record without a work-related need violates client privacy even if nothing is shared afterward. The nurse reports it through the channel set by policy. Questioning the coworker alone can compromise an investigation, opening the record adds a second unnecessary access, and having system access does not give staff the right to view any client's record.
Q8Confidentiality and privacy
A practical nurse wants a colleague's opinion about a client's wound and considers taking a photo with a personal phone. Which action best protects the client's privacy?
Correct answer: A. A wound image is part of the client's health information. Personal phones and consumer apps are not secure and cannot be audited. Only approved devices and systems, with consent when policy requires it, protect privacy. Leaving out the face or deleting the photo later does not make a personal device acceptable, and a verbal request for confidentiality is not a safeguard.
Q9Advance directives
A client with a valid do-not-resuscitate (DNR) order and a signed advance directive becomes unresponsive. The client's daughter arrives and demands, 'Do everything to save my mother.' What should the practical nurse do?
Correct answer: C. A valid DNR order and advance directive record the client's own wishes and stay in effect when the client cannot speak. The nurse follows them and involves the charge nurse and provider so the daughter receives explanation and support. Starting CPR contradicts the client's wishes. A family member cannot cancel a DNR by signing a form; only the provider can revise the order, based on the client's or a legally authorized decision-maker's wishes. Refusing to discuss it leaves the daughter unsupported and does not resolve the conflict.
Q10Informed consent
A Spanish-speaking client is handed an English surgical consent form and says, 'The doctor said it was fine,' then starts to sign. What should the practical nurse do?
Correct answer: C. Consent must be informed and voluntary, which requires the provider's explanation in a language the client understands. Witnessing a signature on a form the client cannot read confirms nothing. An untrained interpreter can introduce errors, and explaining surgical risks is the provider's responsibility, not the nurse's.
Q11Informed consent
A practical nurse is asked to witness a client's signature on a surgical consent form. Which statement best describes what the nurse is confirming?
Correct answer: D. A witness attests that the person who signed is the client and that the signature was given voluntarily, without pressure. The provider is responsible for explaining risks, benefits, and alternatives and for deciding that surgery is indicated. If the client seems confused or asks questions during the signing, the nurse should stop and notify the provider.
Q12Discharge and referrals
A client recovering from a stroke coughs when drinking thin liquids and has slurred speech. Which referral is most appropriate for the swallowing problem?
Correct answer: B. The speech-language pathologist evaluates swallowing, recommends food and liquid consistencies, and teaches safe swallowing techniques, which is what a client who coughs with thin liquids needs. Occupational therapy focuses on daily activities such as dressing and bathing, physical therapy on walking and balance, and respiratory therapy on breathing treatments. None of these is the specialist for evaluating and treating a swallowing disorder.
Q13Discharge and referrals
A 78-year-old client with heart failure who lives alone has been admitted twice this month after missing medication doses and not reporting weight gain. Which referral is best to reduce readmission?
Correct answer: A. The readmissions came from missed medications and unreported weight gain, so the referral that directly addresses those gaps is home health nursing, which can set up medication routines and watch weights and symptoms. Physical therapy, low-sodium meals, and pastoral care may help in other ways but do not address the causes named in the question.
Q14Quality improvement
A unit is working to reduce client falls. Which measure is an outcome measure for this project?
Correct answer: A. An outcome measure shows the result the project is trying to change, here how often clients actually fall. The other three measure whether steps in the process were carried out (assessments, education, rounds) and are process measures. Improving them may reduce falls, but they do not show the result itself.
Q15Interdisciplinary information sharing
A practical nurse calls a provider about a client with new chest pain. Which statement is the best example of the recommendation portion of SBAR?
Correct answer: A. In SBAR, the recommendation states what the nurse wants or suggests, such as an evaluation, a test, or an order. Current vital signs belong to the situation, the admission history is the background, and the nurse's concern about a heart attack is the assessment.
Q16Legal and ethical scope
Which action is within the scope of practice of a practical nurse?
Correct answer: C. Practical nurses collect data, report findings, perform care under supervision, and reinforce teaching that a registered nurse or provider began. The comprehensive assessment, developing the plan of care, initial teaching about a new medication, and independent evaluation and revision of the plan are registered nurse or provider responsibilities.
Q17Legal and ethical scope
A practical nurse floated to another unit is assigned a client on a ventilator and has never received training to care for ventilated clients. What is the best action?
Correct answer: B. Nurses are accountable for working within their competence. The safe response is to state the limitation to the charge nurse and request an appropriate assignment or supervision. Relying on alarms or handing nursing care to another discipline does not meet the nurse's obligations, and leaving without notice risks abandonment of the assignment.
Q18Mandatory reporting
During a home visit, a practical nurse finds an older adult with bruises in different stages of healing, poor hygiene, and no food in the house. The caregiver says the client 'falls a lot.' What should the nurse do?
Correct answer: C. Nurses are mandated reporters and must report reasonable suspicion, not proof. The nurse documents what was seen and heard objectively and reports to the designated agency. Waiting for confirmation or another visit delays protection, and confronting the caregiver can endanger the client and interfere with an investigation.
Q19Management of assistive personnel
A practical nurse assigns an assistive personnel (AP) to feed a client who has mild dysphagia and is on aspiration precautions. Which instruction is most important?
Correct answer: B. Feeding a stable client with known precautions can be delegated if the AP knows the safe technique and exactly what to report. Upright positioning and small, slow bites reduce aspiration, and coughing or a wet voice are signs of aspiration that need nursing assessment. Rushing, giving thin liquids first, and reclining between bites all increase aspiration risk.
Q20Management of assistive personnel
A practical nurse is observing an assistive personnel (AP) care for a client with right-sided weakness. Which action requires the nurse to intervene?
Correct answer: D. Pulling on a weak or paralyzed arm can injure or dislocate the shoulder. The client should be moved with a draw sheet or lift device and support under the shoulders. Using a gait belt, holding the cane on the stronger left side, and standing at the weaker side while walking are all correct techniques.
Q21Equitable access to care
A client with end-stage kidney disease misses hemodialysis sessions because the client cannot afford transportation. Which nursing action best promotes equitable access to care?
Correct answer: D. Equitable access means removing the barriers that keep clients from care. Social services or case management can connect the client with transportation resources. Labeling the client noncompliant blames the client for a system barrier, a list of distant centers does not solve the transportation problem, and skipping or randomly rescheduling dialysis is unsafe.
