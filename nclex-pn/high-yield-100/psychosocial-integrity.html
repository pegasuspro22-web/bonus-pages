Psychosocial Integrity

Q44Emotional support
A client who recently lost a spouse says quietly, 'I just want it all to end.' Which response by the practical nurse is best?
Correct answer: B. A statement that may signal suicidal thoughts must be clarified directly. Asking directly does not put the idea in the client's mind and lets the nurse assess for a plan and means so the client can be kept safe. Reassurance and cliches dismiss the client's feelings, and an open invitation to talk is therapeutic but does not find out whether the client is at risk.
Q45Coping
Which client behavior is an example of displacement?
Correct answer: B. Displacement transfers feelings from a threatening person to a safer target, as when anger at the physician is directed at the nursing assistant. Doubting the results is denial, childlike behavior under stress is regression, and blaming the test and the instructor is rationalization or projection.
Q46Violence risk
A nurse is interviewing a client who is agitated and has a history of assault. Which nurse action best protects safety?
Correct answer: A. The nurse should keep an unblocked route out and stay at a safe distance so the client does not feel trapped and the nurse can leave if the situation escalates. Blocking the door or standing too close can provoke the client, and closing the door removes the nurse's escape route.
Q47Substance misuse and withdrawal
A client hospitalized for pneumonia had the last alcoholic drink 3 days ago. The client is now disoriented, sees insects on the wall, and has a heart rate of 128/min and a temperature of 101.4°F (38.6°C). What should the nurse suspect?
Correct answer: C. Disorientation, visual hallucinations, tachycardia, and fever 48 to 96 hours after the last drink are typical of delirium tremens, a life-threatening complication that needs immediate medical treatment. Wernicke encephalopathy causes confusion with an unsteady gait and eye movement abnormalities, not this pattern. The client has not been drinking for 3 days, so this is not intoxication, and the severity rules out mild withdrawal.
Q48Behavior management
A client in the manic phase of bipolar disorder paces constantly and has eaten very little for two days. Which intervention is best?
Correct answer: A. A client in a manic state burns many calories and cannot sit long enough to eat a full meal. Portable, high-calorie finger foods meet nutritional needs without demanding that the client stay still. Requiring the client to sit, withholding food, or serving a formal meal are unrealistic and can increase agitation and weight loss.
Q49Therapeutic communication
A client scheduled for surgery says, 'I'm afraid I won't wake up.' Which response by the practical nurse is most therapeutic?
Correct answer: C. Reflecting the feeling and inviting the client to say more encourages expression of the fear and gives the nurse information to address it. Reassurance, generalizing that other clients feel the same, and 'why' questions all block communication, because they dismiss the concern or put the client on the defensive.
Q50Therapeutic communication
A client with heart failure has stopped taking prescribed medications. Which statement by the practical nurse is nontherapeutic?
Correct answer: C. 'Why' questions and statements that suggest blame put the client on the defensive and discourage honest answers. Restating what the client did, sharing an observation, and reflecting a possible change use open-ended, nonjudgmental communication that invites the client to explain.
Q51De-escalation
A client in the dayroom begins to pace, speak loudly, and clench both fists. Which action should the nurse take first?
Correct answer: D. De-escalation starts with the least restrictive measures: a calm approach, a low voice, and reduced stimulation. Medication, threats, and seclusion are used only when verbal strategies fail or there is imminent danger. Starting with a threat or seclusion can increase the client's agitation.
Q52Cognitive impairment
A client with Alzheimer disease becomes agitated and confused every evening. Which intervention is most appropriate?
Correct answer: C. Evening agitation, often called sundowning, is reduced by good lighting, low stimulation, and a consistent routine. Extra visitors add stimulation, confining the client to bed is a form of restraint, and arguing or correcting a client with dementia increases distress.
Q53Cultural considerations
A client who is a Jehovah's Witness has a hemoglobin of 6.8 g/dL and refuses a red blood cell transfusion. Which action is appropriate?
Correct answer: C. A client with decision-making capacity has the right to refuse treatment, including blood products, based on religious beliefs. The nurse respects the decision, documents the refusal, and notifies the provider, who can discuss alternatives. Family members cannot override a capable client's refusal, giving blood under drowsiness violates the client's wishes, and withholding other care is punitive.
Q54Sensory and perceptual problems
A client tells the nurse, 'The voices are talking to me again.' Which response is best?
Correct answer: A. The priority is to find out whether the voices give commands to harm the client or others, because command hallucinations are a safety risk. Dismissing the experience, ignoring it, or distracting the client without assessing it may miss a dangerous command and does not build trust.
Q55End-of-life psychosocial care
The daughter of a dying client cries, 'He won't eat anything. We have to start a feeding tube.' Which response is best?
Correct answer: B. As death approaches, the body needs less food and fluid, and forced feeding can cause discomfort or aspiration. The nurse explains this and offers comfort measures the family can provide. Saying a tube is the only way to prevent suffering is inaccurate, pushing food against the client's wishes causes distress, and promising the appetite will return gives false reassurance.
