Basic Care and Comfort

Q56Enteral feeding and site care
A practical nurse is giving a bolus feeding through a nasogastric tube. Which action reduces the risk of aspiration?
Correct answer: D. An upright position uses gravity to keep formula in the stomach and lowers the risk of reflux and aspiration, so the head of the bed stays raised during the feeding and for a period afterward. Lying flat, pushing formula quickly, and reclining after the feeding all promote reflux.
Q57Mobility
A client with a fractured left leg who is non-weight-bearing is learning to climb stairs with crutches. Which instruction is correct?
Correct answer: C. When going up stairs the stronger leg leads, then the crutches and the injured leg follow ('up with the good'). When going down, the crutches and injured leg go first ('down with the bad'). Leading with the injured leg or the crutches first is unstable and can cause a fall.
Q58Nutrition
A client with iron-deficiency anemia is prescribed oral ferrous sulfate. Which instruction best improves absorption of the iron?
Correct answer: D. Vitamin C increases iron absorption, so taking iron with orange juice on an empty stomach is best. Calcium in milk and antacids binds iron and reduces absorption, and the tannins in coffee also interfere with it.
Q59Nonpharmacologic pain relief
A client twisted an ankle 2 hours ago, and the ankle is swollen and painful. Which nonpharmacologic measure should the nurse teach?
Correct answer: A. In the first 24 to 48 hours after an injury, cold and elevation reduce swelling and pain. Heat and warm soaks increase blood flow and swelling early on, and keeping the ankle below the heart while wrapped tightly increases swelling and can impair circulation.
Q60Activities of daily living
A client has weakness of the left arm after a stroke. Which technique is correct when helping the client dress?
Correct answer: D. When dressing, the affected limb goes in first while there is the most room to move it, and when undressing, the unaffected side comes off first. Doing the strong side first makes the weak arm harder to dress. Rushing the weak arm risks injury, and the weak hand cannot hold the garment reliably.
Q61Bowel and bladder protocols
A nurse is about to remove stool digitally from a client with a fecal impaction. Which finding should cause the nurse to stop the procedure?
Correct answer: C. Digital stimulation of the rectum can stimulate the vagus nerve, causing bradycardia and a drop in blood pressure. A pulse falling to 52/min is a sign to stop and notify the provider. Mild discomfort, some liquid stool, and mild cramping are expected during the procedure.
Q62Skin and tissue integrity
A client at high risk for pressure injuries is on bed rest. Which position best reduces pressure over bony areas?
Correct answer: D. A 30-degree tilt keeps weight off the bony trochanter and sacrum, and keeping the head of the bed low reduces shearing. A 90-degree side-lying position puts full pressure on the hip bone, a high head of bed increases pressure and shear over the sacrum, and donut cushions concentrate pressure around the ring and reduce blood flow.
Q63Intake and output
A client ate 1 cup (240 mL) of ice chips and 4 oz (120 mL) of gelatin. How much should the nurse record as oral intake?
Correct answer: B. Ice chips are counted as half their volume, so 240 mL of ice chips equals 120 mL of fluid. Gelatin turns to liquid at body temperature and is counted at full volume, 120 mL. The total is 120 mL plus 120 mL, or 240 mL. Counting ice at full volume gives 360 mL, and counting only one item or doubling the total gives the other values.
Q64Care of immobilized clients
A client is in Buck's skin traction for a hip fracture. Which observation by the nurse indicates correct care?
Correct answer: A. Traction works only when weights hang freely, creating a constant pull. Weights that rest on the floor or bed, weights removed for turning, and a heel pressed against the footboard all reduce the pull and can cause misalignment, pain, or pressure injury.
Q65Sleep and rest
An older client reports trouble falling asleep. Which teaching is most likely to help?
Correct answer: A. A consistent schedule and limited, early naps support the body's sleep-wake rhythm. Alcohol may cause drowsiness but disrupts sleep later in the night, vigorous exercise close to bedtime is stimulating, and a long late nap makes it harder to fall asleep at night.
