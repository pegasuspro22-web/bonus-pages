Hazard and Risk Identification and Control

Q1Prioritizing corrective action
A site manager walking a parking structure finds a pallet of concrete blocks stored on a deck edge above a pedestrian walkway, and a faded exit sign in a stairwell. Which condition should be corrected first?

Why B is correct

A load hanging at an unprotected edge above people can cause a fatal struck-by event, so it takes priority over a faded sign.

Why the others are wrong

A. A sign does not stop blocks from falling.

C. The sign needs attention, but waiting a week leaves workers under a serious dropped-object hazard.

D. Hard hats are a last-line protection and do not control the source of the hazard.

Q2Atmospheric monitor condition
Before an inspector enters a manhole to check a sewer connection, the four-gas meter shows no record of the function check the manufacturer requires before each day's use. What should the competent person do?

Why D is correct

A meter that has not been verified can give false readings, so it must be confirmed to work before it is relied on to clear an entry.

Why the others are wrong

A. Atmospheres change, so old readings do not show current conditions.

B. Smell is not a reliable test for dangerous atmospheres.

C. A start-up screen does not replace the manufacturer's function check.

Q3Biological hazards
A laborer clearing brush behind a vacant building is stuck by a needle hidden in the debris. Workers were assigned to this cleanup and sharps were expected. What should the employer do right away?

Why A is correct

A puncture from a discarded needle is an exposure incident that calls for prompt, confidential post-exposure evaluation and follow-up.

Why the others are wrong

B. A clean look does not rule out contamination.

C. Infection can be present before symptoms show, so waiting is not acceptable.

D. A delayed review leaves the exposed worker without timely care.

Q4Electrical hazards
A painting crew plans to repaint a building's fascia just below an overhead service drop and wants to use an aluminum extension ladder near the wires. Which hazard and control fit best?

Why A is correct

A metal ladder that touches or nears a live conductor can become an electrical path, so clearance or a different access method removes the exposure.

Why the others are wrong

B. Weather delay does not address the electrical contact hazard.

C. A barrier on the ground does nothing about contact with the wires.

D. Footing the ladder helps stability but does not keep it away from the live conductors.

Q5Asbestos hazards
While pulling cable above a ceiling in a 1960s school, electricians find crumbling insulation on a steam pipe. Nobody knows what it contains, and classes are running in the next room. What should happen first?

Why C is correct

Damaged material of unknown composition in an older building should not be disturbed further, and occupants must be protected until it is evaluated.

Why the others are wrong

A. Taping does not evaluate the material and the work may disturb it again.

B. Work nearby can still disturb the material and expose the occupants.

D. Ordinary vacuuming can spread fibers and is not an approved cleanup.

Q6Chemical hazards
Plumbers are gluing PVC joints in a six-foot-deep pit. The competent person sees open cement cans and a vapor haze settling at the bottom where two workers are kneeling. What is the best action?

Why A is correct

Solvent vapors collect in low areas and can harm lungs or ignite, so the exposure must stop while vapor control is put in place.

Why the others are wrong

B. Moving the cans inside the pit does not remove the vapor already collecting.

C. A cloth mask does not protect against solvent vapors.

D. Hand fanning is not reliable ventilation for a low, partly enclosed space.

Q7Concrete hazards
A laborer screeding a slab kneels in fresh concrete without waterproof boots. By lunch he reports burning on his knees. Which hazard and control fit best?

Why D is correct

Wet concrete is alkaline and can burn skin, so keeping it off the body and washing quickly is the right control.

Why the others are wrong

A. The reported burning comes from the material, not from posture.

B. Extra breaks do not stop skin contact with the wet concrete.

C. Noise does not cause burning of the skin.

Q8Rigging inspection
A rigger preparing to set a rooftop fan notices that a clip on the wire-rope sling has slid and the sling eye is crushed. The fan is still on the truck. What is required?

Why C is correct

A shifted clip and crushed eye show damage or poor assembly, so the sling must not be used for the lift.

Why the others are wrong

A. Rigging must be inspected before it is used.

B. A defective sling in the load path is still a risk.

D. Field tightening does not make a damaged assembly safe, and slow lifting does not remove the risk of failure.

Q9Excavation protective systems
A crew must enter a six-foot-deep pit to connect a storm drain. The soil is mixed clay and sand, not solid rock, and no shoring has been installed. What is required before anyone enters?

Why D is correct

Excavations 5 feet or deeper that are not entirely in stable rock need cave-in protection before entry under 29 CFR 1926.652.

Why the others are wrong

A. A sign and reduced occupancy do not protect against a collapse.

B. Short duration and head protection do not stop a cave-in.

C. A visual check does not replace a protective system.

Q10Fall arrest equipment
At a water treatment plant project, an inspector finds a harness whose webbing is stiff and faded after being splashed with an acid cleaner. What corrective action is best?

Why C is correct

Chemical damage can weaken webbing, so the harness cannot be trusted to arrest a fall.

Why the others are wrong

A. A second lanyard still relies on the damaged harness.

B. A lower height does not make weakened webbing safe.

D. Rinsing does not restore webbing strength.

Q11Power tool defects
During pipe-support fabrication, a welder uses a bench grinder with its wheel guard missing. The wheel is chipped and the machine vibrates strongly. What action is required?

Why B is correct

A missing guard, a damaged wheel, and abnormal vibration signal an unsafe tool that can throw fragments.

Why the others are wrong

A. A lighter touch does not remove the hazard.

C. Adding a person near a defective tool increases exposure.

D. Protective gear does not correct the defects that can make the wheel break.

Q12Synthetic slings
A crew lifting paint pails to a roof uses a polyester sling with a nick through its cover and solvent staining. What should the rigger do?

Why C is correct

A cut cover and chemical exposure can reduce the strength of a synthetic sling in ways that cannot be seen.

Why the others are wrong

A. Changing the hitch does not make a damaged sling acceptable.

B. A lighter load does not establish that the damaged sling is safe.

D. Tape does not restore capacity or address chemical damage.

Q13Hot work precautions
A carpentry crew plans to use a cutting torch to remove a bent steel strap beside a pile of plywood sheets. What must be done before the torch is lit?

Why B is correct

Hot work near combustibles needs immediate fire-control capability, which the construction hot work provisions require.

Why the others are wrong

A. Gloves do not control ignition of the plywood.

C. A fire watch helps, but exposed combustibles still need to be moved or protected.

D. The combustibles must be handled before sparks or heat are produced.

Q14Housekeeping and egress
During a walkthrough of an electrical room, the technician sees conduit scraps and cardboard blocking the aisle that leads to the only exit. What action is best?

Why D is correct

A blocked exit route is a life-safety problem, and a routine prevents the condition from returning.

Why the others are wrong

A. Small scraps and packaging still block the route and create trip hazards.

B. Tape leaves the route obstructed.

C. Footwear does not keep an exit clear.

Q15Lead exposure
Crew members scraping old lead paint from steel railings in an enclosed work area take their lunch inside the same area. What corrective action is best?

Why D is correct

Eating where lead dust settles creates an ingestion path, so meals must be taken in a clean, separate place.

Why the others are wrong

A. Moving inside the same space does not remove the contamination.

B. Brushing does not remove fine lead dust and can spread it.

C. Gloves do not make a dusty area clean and can carry dust to the food.

Q16Material handling
In a materials yard, a forklift operator sees that the boards under a pallet of electrical panels are cracked and the stack leans to one side. The pallet is about to be raised to a loading dock. What is the right action?

Why A is correct

A damaged pallet and shifted load can fail during the lift, so the condition must be corrected before handling.

Why the others are wrong

B. A worker beside a leaning load is exposed to it falling.

C. A low lift still moves a weak pallet with a leaning load.

D. Straps do not repair the cracked boards that carry the load.

Q17Material storage near cranes
A crane will pick a bundle of rebar from a lay-down area. Loose steel bars lie on a slight slope right beside the swing path with nothing to stop them rolling. What should the competent person require before the pick?

Why D is correct

Loose bars on a slope can roll into people or equipment during the lift, so they must be controlled first.

Why the others are wrong

A. The hazard exists during the lift and must be controlled before it starts.

B. A faster lift does not stop rolling and adds load-control risk.

C. A person downhill can be struck or crushed.

Q18Noise exposure
A crew runs a jackhammer and an air compressor inside a concrete tunnel section, and measurements show noise above the level at which controls are required. What is the best employer response?

Why B is correct

Reducing noise at the source or along the path comes first, with hearing protection covering what is left.

Why the others are wrong

A. Rotation helps only when it is planned and shown to lower each person's dose.

C. Short jobs can still cause harmful exposure, and protection alone skips feasible controls.

D. Removing protection during loud work raises exposure.

Q19Eliminating tool hazards
In a hospital parking garage retrofit, installers must attach metal brackets to a concrete column next to a walkway used by staff. A powder-actuated tool would create a projectile hazard, while a practical mechanical anchor method is available. What is the best control?

Why C is correct

When a practical alternative exists, removing the tool removes the projectile and misfire hazard next to the walkway.

Why the others are wrong

A. Hearing protection does not control a projectile hazard.

B. Skill does not stop a fastener or fragment from reaching people on the walkway.

D. A warning does not protect people from an unexpected fastener.

Q20Radiation hazards
A paving crew finds a portable nuclear moisture-density gauge left on the shoulder beside a staging area, its transport case cracked. Workers are about to unload material nearby. What should the supervisor do?

Why A is correct

A damaged, unattended gauge may expose people to radiation, so access is limited and qualified people take control of it.

Why the others are wrong

B. Handling a damaged device can increase exposure and is not appropriate for untrained workers.

C. Moving it can raise exposure or cause more damage.

D. A tarp does not control the source and leaves workers close to it.

Q21Silica dust control
Laborers are dry-cutting concrete pavers with a handheld saw beside a crosswalk. Visible dust drifts toward nearby workers and pedestrians. Which action best applies the required silica dust control?

Why D is correct

Dry cutting concrete creates respirable silica, so the dust must be controlled at the source before work continues.

Why the others are wrong

A. This leaves the operator and others exposed to uncontrolled dust.

B. A paper mask is not the preferred control and does not protect against respirable silica.

C. Wind direction is not a reliable control for fine dust.

Q22Hand-arm vibration
A concrete finisher who has used a handheld breaker for most of the shift reports tingling and white fingertips. What should the foreman do first?

Why D is correct

Numbness and whitening of the fingers point to a harmful vibration exposure, so exposure stops before more work is assigned.

Why the others are wrong

A. Both hands would still be exposed to the same vibration.

B. Delay leaves the worker exposed to the same tool now.

C. Gloves do not justify continued exposure after symptoms.

Q23Fall arrest anchorage
A utility crew is replacing street lights from a bucket truck. The inspector sees a worker clip his lanyard to the basket's removable gate bar instead of the manufacturer's anchorage point. What should be done?

Why A is correct

A removable gate or rail is not designed to be a fall arrest anchorage, so the worker must use the intended anchor.

Why the others are wrong

B. A person on the ground cannot provide a dependable anchorage.

C. Knots can damage a lanyard and do not fix the unsuitable anchor.

D. A closed street does not make a gate bar strong enough to arrest a fall.

Q24Confined space atmosphere
Before a technician enters a below-grade pump vault, the meter shows low oxygen. Which control addresses the hazard?

Why C is correct

A low-oxygen atmosphere impairs breathing and judgment, so entry waits until the air is corrected and verified.

Why the others are wrong

A. A retrieval line helps rescue but does not fix the atmosphere.

B. An attendant does not make a low-oxygen vault safe to enter.

D. More people means more exposure to the same atmosphere.

Q25Scaffolds in freezing weather
After an overnight sleet storm, a painting crew plans to use a rolling scaffold for exterior work. Ice coats the platform and the wheel locks. What is required before use?

Why D is correct

Ice can affect footing, components, and the work surface, and a competent person's inspection decides whether it is safe.

Why the others are wrong

A. Melting ice does not remove the need to inspect components and footing.

B. Using workers as the test exposes them to a possible failure or slip.

C. Warm clothing does not show that the scaffold is safe.

Q26Backup alarms
A telehandler moving drywall pallets on a busy floor has a backup alarm that no longer sounds. Workers on foot are behind the machine. What should happen first?

Why C is correct

A failed warning device creates a backing struck-by hazard, so operation under the same conditions must stop.

Why the others are wrong

A. More speed raises the likelihood and severity of a backing incident.

B. Engine noise is not a reliable substitute for the alarm.

D. Steering and braking do not warn workers in the backing path.

Q27Struck-by exclusion zones
While erectors assemble a scaffold bay beside a loading dock, a forklift driver starts carrying pallets through the area under the workers installing cross braces. What should the competent person do first?

Why A is correct

Workers above are exposed to the moving forklift and falling components, so the activities must be separated at once.

Why the others are wrong

B. A horn warns but does not keep a forklift out of the work zone.

C. Visibility does not stop a forklift from entering the area.

D. Rushing adds errors and leaves the vehicle hazard in place.

Q28Job hazard analysis
A flooring contractor is preparing a job hazard analysis for refinishing floors in an occupied office building. The draft lists only 'floor refinishing' as the task. Which revision improves it most?

Why A is correct

A step-by-step breakdown reveals hazards such as dust, fumes, slips, and occupant interaction that a single task name hides.

Why the others are wrong

B. A general reminder does not identify specific hazards.

C. Hazards must be analyzed before workers and occupants are exposed.

D. Material choice is one part and does not cover steps, access, or occupants.

Q29Elimination in the hierarchy of controls
At the pre-task meeting, a superintendent learns that carpenters plan to dry-cut fiber-cement siding on site. The supplier can still deliver boards pre-cut to the plan dimensions. Which action best applies the hierarchy of controls?

Why A is correct

Removing the dust-generating task during planning is more protective than controlling exposure after cutting begins.

Why the others are wrong

B. These are useful engineering controls, but eliminating the cutting ranks higher.

C. Respirators rank lower and depend on fit, use, and a program.

D. Rotation is administrative and leaves the task in place.

Q30Ergonomics
During demolition in an older apartment building, workers carry sealed bags of plaster debris down four flights by hand, twisting through a narrow door. What is the best ergonomic correction?

Why D is correct

Mechanical handling and lighter loads reduce force, awkward posture, and repeated twisting.

Why the others are wrong

A. Speed does not reduce load or twisting and can lead to loss of control.

B. The hazardous lifting process stays the same.

C. Gloves help grip but do not fix forceful, awkward lifting.

Q31Eye and face protection
A pipefitter uses an abrasive cut-off saw to trim threaded rod in a mechanical room while wearing only safety glasses. Which control best addresses the flying-particle hazard?

Why B is correct

The saw throws metal particles toward the eyes and face, so primary eye protection plus face protection matches the hazard.

Why the others are wrong

A. A sign does not protect the operator from particles at the point of cutting.

C. A hard hat protects against head impacts, not flying particles to the face.

D. Better housekeeping does not stop particles hitting the operator.

Q32Multiple trades
In a stairwell, electricians are pulling wire on the upper landing while painters work directly below on the lower flights. Dropped tools and overspray could reach the lower crew. Which control best follows the hierarchy of controls?

Why B is correct

Separating the work removes the simultaneous exposure more effectively than relying on protective equipment.

Why the others are wrong

A. Speed does not remove the conflict and can add errors.

C. Radios help coordination but leave the exposure in place.

D. Respiratory protection does not stop dropped tools.

Q33Safety through design
During design review for a new warehouse, the team knows maintenance workers will service rooftop units near the roof edge for decades. Which design decision best applies safety through design?

Why D is correct

Building safe access and fall protection into the design reduces exposure for builders and future maintenance crews.

Why the others are wrong

A. Decals communicate a hazard but do not engineer it out.

B. Ladders alone do not address travel near edges.

C. Reminders are helpful but do not build protection into the layout.

Q34Head protection
A pipe layer enters a trench where loose clods and small stones can drop from the walls during cleanup. The competent person has confirmed the trench protections are in place. Which apparel protects the worker from the remaining falling-object hazard?

Why D is correct

Soil and stones can strike the head, and a properly selected hard hat protects against that residual hazard.

Why the others are wrong

A. Visibility does not protect the head from falling material.

B. The hazard here is falling material, not chemical splash.

C. Hearing protection does not address falling objects.

Q35Monitoring instruments
During tile work in an occupied office, installers apply a solvent-based adhesive in a room that shares the building ventilation system. The safety technician wants to screen whether vapors are reaching the hallway. Which instrument is most suitable?

Why A is correct

A photoionization detector is commonly used to screen many volatile organic vapors and can show whether they are spreading.

Why the others are wrong

B. It assesses flammability and may not respond properly to adhesive vapors.

C. Oxygen readings do not identify organic vapors.

D. Tubes are compound-specific, so an unrelated tube is not reliable.

Q36Hazard communication labeling
At a bridge project, a painter pours thinner into an unlabeled squeeze bottle to carry up a ladder and keep in a toolbox for several days. Which action best addresses the hazard communication issue?

Why C is correct

An unlabeled container can lead to misuse or a wrong emergency response, so identity and hazards must be communicated.

Why the others are wrong

A. Color does not communicate identity or hazards.

B. Locking it away does not inform the next person who uses it.

D. Initials do not identify the chemical or its hazards.

Q37Damaged extension cord
On a roof project, an inspector finds a temporary extension cord running across a metal deck with a deep gash through the outer jacket near the plug. What corrective action is most appropriate?

Why D is correct

A damaged jacket can expose workers to shock, especially when the cord lies on metal, so it must be taken out of use.

Why the others are wrong

A. Tape over a deep gash is not a reliable repair.

B. Moving it does not fix the damaged insulation.

C. Gloves do not replace removing a damaged cord from service.
