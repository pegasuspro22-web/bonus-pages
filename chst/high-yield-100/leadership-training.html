Domain 4 of 4 · 21 questions

Leadership, Communication, and Training

Q80Training for a new product
A roofing contractor switches to a hot-applied membrane that has different heating, ventilation, and burn protection needs than the products the crew used before. Several workers have limited English and learn best by doing. What is the most effective training method?

Why D is correct

Current information with demonstration and hands-on practice tests whether workers can handle the new material safely.

Why the others are wrong

A. Practice must come before exposure.

B. A handout for a different product does not cover the new requirements.

C. A reply shows delivery, not skill or understanding.

Q81Motivating correction
On a shared site, one subcontractor keeps leaving packaging scraps in a common corridor. Its foreman says other trades make more scraps, so the correction is unfair. What is the best way to motivate correction?

Why B is correct

A fact-based talk at the location with clear expectations and follow-up focuses the foreman on his own contribution without escalating blame.

Why the others are wrong

A. Skipping the resisting crew leaves the cause unaddressed.

C. Public blame without specifics does not guide correction.

D. Waiting leaves the hazard in a shared route.

Q82Training for recognizing conditions
A crew will lay a sewer line in a trench where soil conditions change and equipment works near the edge. The competent person wants to train new laborers to recognize unsafe conditions before they go in. Which approach is best?

Why B is correct

A demonstration at the trench links training to the real conditions and entry decisions the laborers will face.

Why the others are wrong

A. A general video does not address the site's conditions.

C. Unrelated pictures do not prepare workers for this trench.

D. A quiz without discussion does not build field recognition.

Q83Briefing before a complex lift
A crane contractor adds new riggers for a complicated pick of steel trusses. The lift plan has multiple rigging components, changing hand signals, and a small landing area. Which training method is most effective before the pick?

Why A is correct

Review, roles, signal practice, and a dry run prepare new riggers and let understanding be checked before the pick.

Why the others are wrong

B. Pairing alone leaves the crew without a shared plan.

C. Independent reading does not check understanding or practice signals.

D. Training after the lift is too late to help.

Q84Communicating an overhead hazard
On a block wall project, a foreman sees laborers sweeping near a material hoist while bricklayers work above on the scaffold. What communication approach best completes the risk reduction?

Why A is correct

Two crews share a space with an immediate falling-object exposure, so a focused huddle with a clear boundary addresses it now.

Why the others are wrong

B. A posted notice does not reach the two crews at the point of exposure.

C. Waiting leaves the exposure in place during the work.

D. The laborers below remain exposed.

Q85Site-specific training
A contractor starts renovating a dental clinic floor that stays open to patients. Crews will work near patients, staff, public corridors, and active building systems. Which training approach best fits these conditions?

Why C is correct

Walking the real space with scenarios ties the training to the occupied environment and the tasks near patients and staff.

Why the others are wrong

A. Rules without context do not teach practical responses.

B. Interior finishing in an occupied building has different hazards.

D. Crews need the information before they start work.

Q86Correcting an at-risk behavior
A grader operator keeps backing toward an open trench while laborers measure elevations nearby. He says his alarm works and the laborers should watch out. What is the best way for the CHST to correct the behavior?

Why C is correct

The behavior creates a foreseeable struck-by hazard, so the fix involves both parties and a physical separation control.

Why the others are wrong

A. Discipline alone leaves the exposure in place.

B. Speed does not remove the conflict.

D. Reliance on noise and attention is a weak control.

Q87Confidentiality
While upgrading the ventilation in an older school, a laborer tells the safety technician that an air-quality report mentions possible hazardous material in the ceiling. The laborer asks him to hand copies of the full report to the whole crew. What should the technician do first?

Why B is correct

The report may contain confidential details, so the responsible people decide how to share the hazard information and controls.

Why the others are wrong

A. Unrestricted sharing can release confidential information.

C. Affected workers must be told about hazards and the controls.

D. Posting everything publicly can disclose sensitive information.

Q88Near-miss reporting culture
At a new-hire orientation, several subcontractor workers say their previous employer disciplined people who reported close calls. The project manager wants more reporting without fear of retaliation. What is the best approach?

Why C is correct

Clear expectations, visible follow-up, and protection from retaliation show that reporting is valued.

Why the others are wrong

A. Without feedback, workers see no value in reporting.

B. Forced reports produce noise, not trust.

D. Limiting reporters reduces information and trust.

Q89Training for a leading approach
A crane crew will make repeated picks of prefabricated column cages near an active foundation. Observation shows tag lines used inconsistently and spotters standing in different places. Which training method best supports a leading risk management approach?

Why A is correct

A simulation lets the real crew find weak points before production work exposes anyone.

Why the others are wrong

B. Statistics and signatures do not train field practice.

C. A lecture after delivery does not test the crew's practice.

D. Enforcing on the first production pick exposes workers while they learn.

Q90Project-specific onboarding
A mechanical subcontractor sends a crew that completed a general orientation at another company. This site has one-way haul roads, two separate assembly points, and a written procedure for work near live equipment. What should the site safety representative do?

Why C is correct

Workers need this project's information before entering, and documentation and confirmation show it was understood.

Why the others are wrong

A. General training does not cover this site's routes, assembly points, or procedures.

B. A posted map does not confirm understanding.

D. A late, secondhand briefing leaves workers uninformed at the start.

Q91Worker participation
At a pre-task meeting, a veteran ironworker keeps talking over two apprentices who are trying to warn about a delivery route that now crosses a work area. The foreman suspects the apprentices have seen real conflicts. What is the best approach?

Why B is correct

Giving everyone a voice brings out hazards and confirms the controls for the changed route.

Why the others are wrong

A. Concerns are needed before work begins.

C. Experience does not replace current information about a changed route.

D. This removes the information and leaves the conflict unaddressed.

Q92Correcting a communication gap
While cutting rebar inside a pump station, a worker keeps stepping out to grab consumables without telling the person assigned as fire watch, who then cannot tell whether the cutting has stopped or when the post-work observation should start. How should the CHST correct the behavior?

Why B is correct

The missing handoff prevents proper monitoring, so work stops until the process is corrected and demonstrated.

Why the others are wrong

A. Waiting leaves the danger in place.

C. This does not fix the handoff.

D. Guessing is not a reliable way to know hot work has stopped.

Q93Consulting the manufacturer
A rigging crew wants to use a proprietary plate clamp to hoist stacked steel plates. The clamp has sat in the yard for years, and nobody can find its instructions or the inspection checklist. What should the safety technician do?

Why D is correct

Without current instructions and criteria, the crew cannot confirm the clamp is suitable or safe after long storage.

Why the others are wrong

A. This does not address the clamp's suitability.

B. A test lift exposes workers to a possible failure.

C. Appearance does not show capacity or condition.

Q94Consulting for a new hazard
A contractor plans to enter a below-grade pump pit. Its entry procedure does not address a newly installed sodium hypochlorite feed line that may alter the air. Whom should the safety technician consult before authorizing entry?

Why C is correct

The new line may create hazards the procedure does not cover, so people with the right information must evaluate it first.

Why the others are wrong

A. Experience and smell are not reliable for atmospheric hazards.

B. Routine knowledge does not cover the new hazard.

D. A concrete supplier cannot assess chemical hazards.

Q95Motivating lasting compliance
A drywall crew has started carrying board through an active forklift lane instead of the marked staging route. The foreman wants a communication strategy that motivates lasting compliance without humiliating experienced workers. What should the supervisor do?

Why A is correct

Involving the crew builds ownership, and recognition reinforces the new behavior.

Why the others are wrong

B. Threats without understanding the reason rarely last.

C. The shortcut and its exposure remain.

D. Public shaming can damage trust and does not fix the cause.

Q96Communicating across languages
A paving crew works in extreme heat, and members speak several different primary languages. The superintendent must communicate the heat illness response plan before the afternoon shift. Which strategy is most effective?

Why B is correct

Visuals, demonstration, and teach-back reduce language barriers and confirm that workers understand before conditions worsen.

Why the others are wrong

A. Everyone needs to know the signs and the response.

C. A reminder and a late explanation leave workers unprepared.

D. An English email does not reach all workers or show understanding.

Q97Controls that get defeated
At a culvert replacement, a crew sets up temporary fencing to keep walkers away from a loading zone. The next morning the technician finds a panel dragged aside so an excavator's air hose can pass. What is the best next risk reduction action?

Why B is correct

The fence controlled the walker exposure and the hose change defeated it, so the work must be adjusted.

Why the others are wrong

A. Convenience does not justify removing the control.

C. A verbal warning is a weak substitute for separation.

D. Waiting for closeout leaves people exposed now.

Q98Communicating a foreseeable exposure
An electrical crew is hanging cable tray in an active boiler room. Workers regularly walk under suspended tray sections while another worker adjusts chain hoists above. What communication approach is most effective for this foreseeable exposure?

Why A is correct

A direct briefing with a defined exclusion area and route communicates the required behavior and confirms understanding.

Why the others are wrong

B. Waiting leaves the exposure in place.

C. Those walking below also need the information.

D. A general text does not give a specific control.

Q99Equipment compatibility
A crew will mount a hydraulic breaker on a skid steer type the contractor does not usually operate. The operator says the quick coupler looks like it fits. What communication approach is best before the work begins?

Why B is correct

A specific compatibility question is best settled with approved information and a crew discussion before work starts.

Why the others are wrong

A. Concerns must be addressed before the attachment is used.

C. Appearance is not verification and the crew stays uninformed.

D. A general newsletter does not address this attachment.

Q100Leadership response to cold stress
A superintendent wants to keep erecting precast panels through a sudden freeze. Workers report numb fingers, and the project has no written cold weather plan. What leadership action is most appropriate?

Why A is correct

Reported numbness calls for immediate protection first, and a qualified, participatory plan prevents repeats.

Why the others are wrong

B. A fixed rule ignores the factors that determine exposure.

C. Waiting for severe symptoms is too late.

D. Different tolerance does not remove the hazard.
