Emergency Preparedness, Incident Investigation, and Response

Q38First aid before investigation
At a new-hire orientation day, a carpenter slices his palm deeply with a utility knife while opening a delivery. Blood is flowing steadily, and the orientation leader asks him to explain how the cut happened so the form can be filled out. What should happen first?

Why B is correct

The worker's medical needs come before interviews or paperwork, which begin once care is under way and he is stable.

Why the others are wrong

A. Paperwork must not delay care.

C. Questioning a bleeding worker delays treatment.

D. Documentation waits until the injured worker is being treated.

Q39Spill response priority
During a rainstorm, a valve on a diesel storage tank opens partly and fuel runs across gravel toward a storm inlet. While starting the incident investigation, what should the supervisor direct first?

Why B is correct

The release is active and moving toward a drain, so controlling the source and protecting the inlet comes before fact gathering.

Why the others are wrong

A. Estimating volume can wait until the release is stopped.

C. Interviews follow control of the release.

D. Documentation must not delay containment.

Q40Updating the emergency action plan
On a townhome project, the superintendent updates the emergency action plan because the crew has moved from foundation work to second-floor decking. Which item should be added or verified for the new work area?

Why B is correct

A change in work location changes access and reporting, so responders need to know where to go.

Why the others are wrong

A. Equipment lists do not tell responders where the emergency is.

C. A ground-level map does not help responders reach the upper floor.

D. This is a housekeeping rule and does not address emergency reporting.

Q41Trench emergency
A pipe crew sees a trench wall slough while a coworker is still near the bottom. What is the immediate response?

Why B is correct

Sloughing soil signals a possible cave-in, so work stops and no one else enters while the worker exits or rescue is called.

Why the others are wrong

A. Leaving him in an unstable trench increases the risk.

C. Work must stop immediately, not after a call.

D. Equipment near an unstable wall adds to the danger.

Q42Work zone intrusion
In a highway work zone, a car drives through the closed lane and knocks down several cones beside a flagger. Traffic keeps moving in the adjacent lanes. What should the site leader do first?

Why A is correct

The first priorities are preventing a second strike, checking people for injury, and getting help if needed.

Why the others are wrong

B. Paperwork comes after safety.

C. Sending a worker into live traffic repeats the exposure.

D. Moving signs does not account for injured people.

Q43Emergency washing equipment
A crew will batch concrete sealers and acid cleaners in a shed. The emergency plan must identify equipment for chemical splashes to the eyes or skin. Which plan element is most appropriate?

Why C is correct

Splash exposure needs flushing equipment that works, is within reach, and is understood by workers.

Why the others are wrong

A. A tap is not emergency washing equipment and may be out of reach.

B. Pads do not flush eyes or skin.

D. A bottle may not flush long enough and may be empty when needed.

Q44Incident command
At a shared construction yard, a fuel truck catches fire near a common exit. Each contractor's foreman starts moving his people toward a different gate. What should happen immediately under an incident command approach?

Why A is correct

Separate evacuations create confusion, and unified command with one assembly point coordinates the response.

Why the others are wrong

B. Waiting leaves workers near a growing fire.

C. Different routes make accounting for people impossible.

D. Sending supervisors toward a fire exposes them and delays evacuation.

Q45Drill versus real emergency
During a planned evacuation drill on a multi-floor project, a worker reports that a coworker on the third floor is truly dizzy and short of breath. What should the drill facilitator do immediately?

Why B is correct

A possible medical emergency needs real response, not continued exercise play.

Why the others are wrong

A. A discussion later does not help a person in distress now.

C. An ill worker should not be left to manage alone.

D. Treating a real event as an exercise delays care.

Q46Unconscious worker in enclosed space
A worker spraying a coating inside a closed pump room slumps to the floor. A coworker starts reading the product label to identify the solvent before making any calls. What should be done first?

Why D is correct

An unconscious worker in an enclosed area may be affected by vapors or low oxygen, so help and protecting rescuers come first.

Why the others are wrong

A. No emergency call means no medical response is under way.

B. An unprotected rescuer can become a second victim.

C. Identifying the product must not delay the call.

Q47Electrical shock response
An electrician is shocked while testing a live panel and has a visible burn on his hand. He is awake, and nobody has called for help yet. What should happen first, before details of the scene are collected?

Why A is correct

An electrical shock with a burn needs prompt medical evaluation, and documentation follows once help is on the way.

Why the others are wrong

B. Interviews follow emergency actions.

C. A statement is not a priority while he needs care.

D. Data collection can wait until medical needs are addressed.

Q48Accounting for workers
During a large deck pour, a laborer sees a form brace buckle and warns the crew. The superintendent is writing the emergency action plan for the next pour. Which element best ensures all workers are accounted for if the area must be evacuated?

Why C is correct

An assembly point with a way to verify attendance shows whether anyone is still in the unstable area.

Why the others are wrong

A. Inspections are useful but do not account for people during an evacuation.

B. A kit does not tell anyone who has left the area.

D. Repair supplies do not help account for workers.

Q49Gas odor in an occupied building
Plumbers cutting into a wall in a retail store smell natural gas. Shoppers are inside the building and a stockroom light is on. What is the best immediate action?

Why A is correct

A suspected gas release near people calls for clearing the area, avoiding anything that could ignite the gas, and calling the utility and emergency services.

Why the others are wrong

B. Covering the opening does not control a gas release.

C. A fading smell does not show the gas is gone, and work may ignite it.

D. A light can be an ignition source and delays moving people away.

Q50Fire response
A concrete crew's propane heater flares at its hose connection beside a stack of plastic sheeting. The fire is growing, and the crew has a clear exit. What should the foreman direct immediately?

Why D is correct

A growing fire beside combustibles calls for alerting others and evacuating, with extinguishers used only by trained people who are not cut off.

Why the others are wrong

A. Delaying the decision to evacuate puts the crew near a growing fire.

B. Alerting and evacuating come before moving materials.

C. Watching leaves the crew close to a fire that may spread.

Q51Reentry after evacuation
At the start of the shift, a superintendent reviews yesterday's evacuation caused by a refrigerant leak. The leak is repaired and responders have cleared the building. Before crews return, what should be emphasized?

Why C is correct

Work resumes through a defined process that confirms conditions and tells the affected crews.

Why the others are wrong

A. Every worker should be able to report a suspected release.

B. Return should follow one coordinated process.

D. Lack of odor does not confirm safe conditions.

Q52Universal precautions
At a toolbox talk, a laborer mentions that a coworker cut his hand yesterday and left blood on the handle of a grinder that is still in the tool crib. What action best follows universal precautions?

Why C is correct

Blood must be treated as potentially infectious, so access is limited and cleaning is done with protective measures.

Why the others are wrong

A. Gloves do not remove the contamination from the handle.

B. Delay leaves the contaminated tool available for use.

D. Precautions apply regardless of anyone's history.

Q53Rescue in a trench
A worker in a deep utility trench reports dizziness and trouble breathing. Coworkers at the edge are debating whether to climb down and pull him out. What is the best immediate action?

Why C is correct

The symptoms may point to a hazardous atmosphere, and untrained entry can create more victims.

Why the others are wrong

A. Fall protection does not protect against a bad atmosphere.

B. The coworker is exposed to the same hazard before help is called.

D. Waiting delays rescue for a worker who may be in danger.

Q54Solvent exposure
A floor installer spreading a solvent-based adhesive in a small windowless storeroom becomes lightheaded, nauseated, and unsteady on his feet. What is the best immediate response?

Why D is correct

These symptoms point to a solvent vapor exposure, so removing him from it, stopping the source, and arranging care come first.

Why the others are wrong

A. The source of the exposure continues and care is delayed.

B. Sitting beside the source keeps him exposed.

C. A fan does not protect him and the vapors remain in the room.

Q55Water rescue coordination
During bridge pier work, a laborer falls from a floating platform into a river. A rescue boat is on station and radios work. Which plan element is most critical to coordinate the response?

Why C is correct

Several responders are involved, so one coordinator keeps rescue, notification, and headcount together.

Why the others are wrong

A. Separate counts delay shared information.

B. Uncoordinated action can conflict with other responders.

D. One person cannot direct rescue, calls, and accountability alone.

Q56Fuel release
A water truck backs into a drum rack at a staging yard, splitting a drum of hydraulic oil that begins to run across the gravel. The driver stops and no one is hurt. What should the site technician do right away?

Why D is correct

An active release near vehicles needs the response plan started promptly, the area controlled, and containment by trained people when safe.

Why the others are wrong

A. A release must be reported and controlled promptly.

B. Driving through the spill spreads it and delays the response.

C. Washing oil into a drain spreads contamination.

Q57Evacuation from a fire
While grinding in a basement, sparks drop into a concealed ceiling void and smoke keeps thickening after the crew empties a portable extinguisher. Which emergency plan element matters most?

Why D is correct

Thickening smoke in a hidden void may be a fire the crew cannot control, so evacuation and accountability come first.

Why the others are wrong

A. Records do not manage an active fire.

B. A list does not help get people out.

C. This is a planning item for later work.
