Safety Program Development, Implementation, and Sustainment

Q58Employer responsibility
A concrete subcontractor's crew will pour columns from a mobile work platform supplied by another company. The superintendent asks who must make sure the crew understands the fall hazards of that platform. What is the best response?

Why B is correct

Each employer is responsible for its own employees' training and protection, and coordination by the controlling contractor does not remove that duty.

Why the others are wrong

A. A supplier does not take over the employer's training duty.

C. Delivery does not create responsibility for training the crew.

D. Employees are not left to decide whether they need training.

Q59Confined space plan component
A mechanical contractor will enter an underground pump chamber to replace a gate valve. Which site-specific safety plan component is essential before entry begins?

Why D is correct

Entry into a permit space needs a complete process, not a single element.

Why the others are wrong

A. A signature alone does not evaluate the air or arrange rescue.

B. Ventilation alone leaves out testing, roles, and rescue.

C. Housekeeping outside does not manage entry hazards.

Q60Suspect hazardous material
During renovation of a 1970s factory, workers uncover cracked wrap on a steam line that may contain hazardous material. The subcontractor wants to keep removing it while a sample goes to the laboratory. What is the best risk management decision?

Why C is correct

Continuing before the material is identified could expose workers and spread contamination.

Why the others are wrong

A. Workers should not decide hazard status on their own.

B. Gloves do not control airborne exposure or contamination.

D. Controls must be based on the result, not on sample collection.

Q61Scaffold plan components
A contractor will erect and use tubular welded frame scaffolds for brick repair on a six-story building. Which component is essential in the site-specific safety plan?

Why A is correct

A scaffold program needs defined responsibility from erection through use and changes.

Why the others are wrong

B. Unauthorized changes can compromise the structure.

C. Conditions and configuration change, so inspections must continue.

D. Training must also cover defects, capacity, and reporting changes.

Q62Records responsibility
Two contractors are installing pipe in a deep underground structure. The safety manager asks who must keep the entry, atmospheric monitoring, and training records. What is the best response?

Why D is correct

Employers remain responsible for records about their own people and operations, while coordination supports safe work in a shared space.

Why the others are wrong

A. Records are needed regardless of duration.

B. Employees do not carry the employer's recordkeeping duty.

C. Each employer still has duties for its own employees' records.

Q63Night work planning
A contractor will replace conduit overnight in an operating airport terminal. Which site-specific safety plan component best addresses the safety effect of this work method?

Why C is correct

Night work in an active facility raises visibility, access, communication, fatigue, and operational interface hazards, which must be addressed together.

Why the others are wrong

A. Lighting alone leaves other night-work hazards unmanaged.

B. Shift limits do not cover lighting, access, or coordination.

D. Uncontrolled selection of areas increases conflicts with operations.

Q64Trending incidents
At a pipeline project, the contractor logs five events in two months where workers stepped into a bell hole before the spoil and exit points were checked. No cave-in occurred. What is the best program action?

Why B is correct

Analysis shows where and when the process breaks down so action can be targeted before an injury.

Why the others are wrong

A. A broad reminder does not address specific patterns.

C. Looking at one crew misses patterns across shifts and tasks.

D. Repeated events without harm still signal a weakness.

Q65Hazardous materials program
A contractor will gut the interior of a mid-century hospital annex, removing wall finishes and ceilings. Which program must be in the site-specific plan before suspect materials are disturbed?

Why B is correct

Demolition can disturb hidden hazardous materials, so identification and controls must come before removal.

Why the others are wrong

A. Disposal rules do not protect workers while the material is disturbed.

C. Sampling after disturbance exposes workers first.

D. Visual checks miss hidden materials.

Q66Post-incident responsibilities
A laborer suffers a deep cut from a power saw during a deck installation. After first aid, the employer begins the post-incident process. Which assignment of responsibility is most appropriate?

Why B is correct

The employer owns the reporting and follow-up system, and the injured worker is a key source of facts.

Why the others are wrong

A. The employer is responsible for the system, not the employee.

C. A work-related injury is documented regardless of tool ownership.

D. Without review and corrective action, the process is incomplete.

Q67Manufacturer instructions
A masonry contractor keeps damaging the guardrails of a suspended platform when stacks of bricks are set down on it. Which program improvement best addresses proper equipment use?

Why A is correct

Manufacturer directions show how loads may be placed without exceeding limits or damaging protective parts.

Why the others are wrong

B. A clear walkway does not control capacity or damage.

C. Removing railings exposes workers to falls.

D. Damaged protective parts must not stay in service.

Q68Determining causation
A drywall contractor's records show three finger injuries over two weeks while hanging board. Each report names a different immediate event: a knife slipped, a sheet shifted, and a worker touched a sharp screw tip. What should the safety professional do next to determine causation?

Why C is correct

The events share the same activity, so looking across conditions can reveal contributing causes that single reports miss.

Why the others are wrong

A. Different mechanisms can still share underlying causes.

B. Blame does not identify causes.

D. Manual work does not make injuries unavoidable.

Q69Scaffold after impact
A telehandler bumps a finished scaffold bay shortly before the morning crew arrives. The foreman sees no obvious damage and wants painters to start while erectors work on another side. What is the best action?

Why C is correct

A vehicle impact is an event that could affect integrity, so the scaffold is evaluated before anyone uses it.

Why the others are wrong

A. The earlier inspection does not cover the impact.

B. Experience and observation do not replace an inspection.

D. Defects must be found before use, not after.

Q70Excavation monitoring in the plan
A storm-sewer contractor will excavate a deep trench for several weeks. The project manager wants the site-specific plan to say how trench conditions are watched over time. Which component should be included?

Why D is correct

A trench plan needs competent person inspections that address the conditions that can change and create a hazard.

Why the others are wrong

A. Inspections are the employer's regular duty.

B. Workers exposed to the hazard are not the inspection authority.

C. Pipe quality does not cover trench conditions.

Q71Auditing onboarding
A general contractor wants to know whether new subcontractor crews are consistently getting the same safety orientation. Which audit approach is most effective?

Why D is correct

Records plus interviews test both the documentation and whether the message reached workers.

Why the others are wrong

A. Managers' impressions do not show what workers learned.

B. A complete deck does not show how orientation was delivered.

C. Early sheets miss later arrivals and show nothing about understanding.

Q72Task-specific plan content
A contractor is writing a site-specific plan for reroofing a strip mall. Crews will remove membrane, work near parapet edges, and carry materials up fixed and portable ladders. Which component makes the plan relevant to that work?

Why B is correct

Reroofing creates predictable fall exposures, so the plan has to describe the controls, exposure locations, and responsible people for those tasks.

Why the others are wrong

A. Meetings on general topics do not address these tasks.

C. A general statement does not describe how the exposures are controlled.

D. A roster does not set out task controls.

Q73Risk control planning
A contractor will place a large suspended slab for a parking deck. The site plan names falls from the edge and form collapse as the most severe risks. Which component must be included to manage them?

Why C is correct

A high-consequence operation needs defined oversight, checks, controls, emergency planning, and the power to stop.

Why the others are wrong

A. A roster does not assign safety responsibilities or emergency coverage.

B. Delivery dates do not manage fall or collapse risk.

D. Rewarding speed can work against safe practice.

Q74Addressing a trend
A contractor's records show three forklift near misses in one month: blocked sightlines, pedestrians in the travel path, and loads carried too high. The manager asks who should ensure the trend is addressed in the powered industrial truck program. Who is most appropriate?

Why A is correct

The trend concerns equipment use and conditions, so the person responsible for the program must lead corrective action with management support.

Why the others are wrong

B. Repeated near misses call for a program-level response.

C. One person cannot correct field conditions and practices without the program owner.

D. The employer controls its own equipment program.

Q75Learning from an event
While drilling for fence posts at a substation, a crew's auger breaks an unmarked water line. No one is hurt, but work stops and emergency response is needed. What is the best action to improve the program after the event?

Why D is correct

The event may reflect several system failures, so a fact-based review supports corrections that prevent a repeat.

Why the others are wrong

A. A strike with response needs documentation and review.

B. Slower work does not fix errors in locating or verification.

C. Blame skips the review of system causes.

Q76Authority and responsibilities
On a stadium project, records show repeated delays because riggers wait to learn who may approve changes to a planned crane pick. What is the best next step?

Why A is correct

The delays show a gap in responsibility, which a clear approval process closes.

Why the others are wrong

B. One role alone should not decide all changes.

C. Lift plan changes need review and approval.

D. Changes should be coordinated, not decided alone.

Q77Sustaining a program
A hoisting contractor has had no incidents for months, but weekly checks show that sling inspection records are inconsistent and damaged tag lines are sometimes pulled and not replaced. Which improvement best sustains the lifting program?

Why C is correct

This fixes both weaknesses: inconsistent records and missing replacement of damaged gear.

Why the others are wrong

A. Filing alone does not remove or replace damaged gear.

B. A lack of incidents does not remove the need for records.

D. This is wasteful and does not fix the inspection process.

Q78Systemic cause
On a multi-employer site, an electrician is struck by a falling bundle of conduit from an upper deck. Investigators find that a laborer had removed a barricade to move supplies, and the area had no handoff process between trades. Which improvement best addresses the likely system cause?

Why B is correct

The event involved a removed barricade and a missing cross-trade process, so a coordinated control system addresses the wider conditions.

Why the others are wrong

A. Blame ignores the conditions that allowed exposure.

C. Limited attendance does not create cross-trade control.

D. A lighter load does not fix the control and communication gap.

Q79Verifying a change
A paving contractor reviews a month of records. Most vehicle intrusions into the work area happened while traffic control was being set out and taken in at dawn. After procedures are changed and crews briefed, what should the safety coordinator do to confirm the changes work?

Why A is correct

Observation and follow-up data show whether the controls are used and whether the exposure is dropping.

Why the others are wrong

B. Near misses are the data needed to verify the change.

C. Hours do not measure the target exposure.

D. Waiting leaves the exposure unverified.
