Reading

PassageNew teachers often assume that classroom management is primarily about discipline — correcting misbehavior after it happens. Experienced teachers, however, tend to describe it differently: as the design of routines and expectations that prevent most disruptions before they start. A veteran teacher who spends the first weeks of school explicitly teaching how to line up, pass in papers, and ask for help is not wasting instructional time; she is investing it.
Q1Main Idea
What is the main idea of the passage?
Correct answer: B. The passage contrasts a reactive view of classroom management (correcting misbehavior) with a proactive one (designing routines that prevent disruptions), and argues the proactive approach is more effective — that contrast is the main idea. Choice A overgeneralizes; the passage compares two views of classroom management, not teachers' overall effectiveness. Choice C directly contradicts the passage, which says this time is “invested,” not wasted. Choice D describes the view the passage argues against, not its main point.
Q2Inference
Based on the passage, what can be inferred about the veteran teacher's use of the first weeks of school?
Correct answer: D. The passage explicitly frames her time spent teaching routines as an investment tied to preventing disruptions, supporting the inference that she sees early routine-building as essential. Choice A overstates the passage, which never claims discipline is unnecessary — only that prevention is emphasized. Choice B contradicts the passage's description of her proactive approach. Choice C contradicts the passage, which says she “explicitly teaches” routines rather than leaving students to learn them alone.
Q3Vocabulary in Context
As used in the passage, the word “investing” most nearly means:
Correct answer: A. “Investing” here means using the time productively in a way that pays off later (preventing future disruptions), matching “using productively for future benefit.” Choice B is the opposite of the intended meaning — the passage explicitly says this time is not wasted. Choice C introduces a meaning (giving something away) unrelated to the sentence. Choice D suggests delay, which does not match the meaning of actively and productively using the time now.
PassageA district considering a longer school day pointed to research showing that added instructional minutes correlate with higher test scores. Critics countered that correlation is not the same as causation: districts that can afford longer days often have more resources overall, which could explain the score differences just as easily as the extra time itself. The district ultimately piloted the change in only a few schools before deciding whether to expand it further.
Q4Main Idea
What is the main idea of the passage?
Correct answer: C. The passage centers on the district's consideration of a longer day, the correlation-versus-causation objection critics raised, and the cautious pilot approach taken in response — that overall arc is the main idea. Choice A overstates the passage, which raises doubt about causation rather than affirming the claim. Choice B introduces a broader claim about test scores the passage never makes. Choice D contradicts the passage, which says the district piloted the change, not that it rejected it outright.
Q5Detail
According to the passage, what specific concern did critics raise about the research on longer school days?
Correct answer: B. The passage states the critics' specific concern directly: better-resourced districts could explain the score differences “just as easily as the extra time itself.” Choice A introduces a claim never made in the passage. Choice C introduces a student-preference concern the passage never mentions. Choice D misstates the critics' argument — they questioned the cause of the improvement, not whether scores improved at all.
Q6Craft & Purpose
The author's discussion of the district's decision to pilot the change in only a few schools primarily serves to:
Correct answer: A. Mentioning the small-scale pilot right after the correlation-versus-causation objection shows the district responding to that uncertainty with a cautious, evidence-gathering approach — supporting choice A. Choice B introduces a critical tone the passage does not take; the passage presents the decision neutrally. Choice C overstates the passage, which never concludes longer days “do not work.” Choice D introduces an opinion about pilot programs the passage never expresses.
Q7Vocabulary in Context
In the sentence “The committee's decision was arbitrary, based on no clear criteria or consistent reasoning,” the word “arbitrary” most nearly means:
Correct answer: D. “Arbitrary” describes a decision made without a clear reason or system, matching the sentence's own description (“no clear criteria or consistent reasoning”). Choice A is close to the opposite — a carefully researched decision would have clear criteria. Choice B introduces an unrelated meaning about group agreement. Choice C introduces an unrelated legal meaning.
Q8Craft & Purpose
A reviewer writes: “The novel's pacing drags in the middle chapters, though the ending redeems much of what came before.” What is the reviewer's overall attitude toward the novel?
Correct answer: C. The sentence contains both a criticism (“pacing drags”) and praise (“the ending redeems much of what came before”), making the reviewer's attitude mixed rather than one-sided. Choice A ignores the praise for the ending. Choice B ignores the criticism of the pacing. Choice D is contradicted by the sentence, which expresses clear, specific opinions on both counts.
Q9Integration of Ideas
“Because Employee A has more years of experience than Employee B, Employee A should be promoted instead of Employee B.” Which unstated assumption does this argument rely on?
Correct answer: A. The argument moves directly from “more experience” to “should be promoted” without stating why experience should determine the outcome — it relies on the unstated assumption that experience is the most important factor for promotion. Choice B introduces a claim about performance reviews that the argument never relies on. Choice C introduces a detail that isn't necessary for the argument's logic to function. Choice D introduces a scarcity assumption the argument doesn't depend on — it would work the same whether one or several promotions were available.
PassageCommunity gardens were once considered a niche hobby, but city planners increasingly treat them as infrastructure. Choice A reverses the comparison the passage draws, which treats garden space as being taken just as seriously as parking, not less seriously. Choice C directly contradicts the passage, which credits gardens with lowering nearby summer temperatures. Choice D introduces a preference claim the passage never makes.

As used in the passage, the word “niche” most nearly means:

Choice A is nearly the opposite of “niche.” Choice B and Choice D introduce meanings unrelated to the word.

Mathematics

Q12Percent
A school district's budget is $2,400,000. If 35% of the budget is allocated to teacher salaries, how much money is allocated to salaries?
Correct answer: D. 0.35 × 2,400,000 = $840,000. Choice A results from using 30% instead of 35%: 2,400,000×0.30=720,000. Choice B results from misreading the total budget as $2,500,000 instead of $2,400,000: 2,500,000×0.35=875,000. Choice C is 65% of the budget (the remaining, non-salary portion) instead of the 35% allocated to salaries: 2,400,000×0.65=1,560,000.
Q13Rate
A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours at a constant speed. At this same speed, how many miles will it travel in 5 hours?
Correct answer: A. The rate is 150÷3=50 mph, so in 5 hours the car travels 50×5=250 miles. Choice B results from adding only one extra hour's worth of distance (150+50=200) instead of the full two additional hours needed to reach 5 hours. Choice C results from misreading the original time as 2 hours instead of 3, giving a rate of 75 mph: 75×5=375. Choice D results from using 6 hours instead of 5: 50×6=300.
Q14Unit Conversion
A recipe requires 2.5 quarts of water. How many cups is this? (1 quart = 4 cups)
Correct answer: B. 2.5 × 4 = 10 cups. Choice A results from mistakenly using the conversion for pints (1 quart = 2 pints) instead of cups (1 quart = 4 cups): 2.5×2=5. Choice C results from rounding 2.5 down to 2 quarts before converting: 2×4=8. Choice D results from rounding 2.5 up to 3 quarts before converting: 3×4=12.
Q15Algebra
Solve for x: 4x − 7 = 3x + 5
Correct answer: A. Subtracting 3x from both sides gives x−7=5; adding 7 to both sides gives x=12. Choice B results from subtracting 7 (instead of adding it) when isolating x: 5−7=−2. Choice C results from subtracting the two constant terms directly (7−5=2) instead of correctly isolating x. Choice D is simply the constant term from the right side of the equation (5), mistakenly treated as the answer without solving.
Q16Algebra Word Problem
A tutoring center charges a $20 registration fee plus $15 per session. If a family paid $110 total, how many sessions did they purchase?
Correct answer: D. Subtracting the fee: $110−$20=$90. Dividing by the per-session rate: 90÷15=6 sessions. Choice A results from mistakenly subtracting the $20 fee twice: (110−20−20)/15≈4.67, rounded to 5. Choice B results from forgetting to subtract the $20 registration fee first and dividing the full $110 by $15: 110/15≈7.33, rounded to 7. Choice C results from using $10 per session instead of $15 on the post-fee amount: 90/10=9.
Q17Geometry
A triangular garden has a base of 10 feet and a height of 6 feet. What is its area?
Correct answer: B. Area = (1/2)(10)(6) = 30 sq ft. Choice A forgets the 1/2 factor: 10×6=60. Choice C adds the base and height instead of multiplying: 10+6=16. Choice D results from computing 2×(10+6)=32, a perimeter-style calculation, instead of the triangle area formula.
Q18Geometry — Angles
In a triangle, two of the angles measure 50° and 65°. What is the measure of the third angle?
Correct answer: A. The angles of a triangle sum to 180°, so the third angle is 180−50−65=65°. Choice B is the sum of the two given angles (50+65=115), mistakenly treated as the third angle instead of subtracting that sum from 180°. Choice C results from subtracting the two given angles from each other (65−50=15) instead of subtracting their sum from 180°. Choice D results from an arithmetic slip adding 50+65 as 125 instead of 115, giving 180−125=55.
Q19Data Analysis
A survey asked 40 teachers which grade level they prefer to teach: 12 chose elementary, 10 chose middle school, and the rest chose high school. What percent of teachers chose high school?
Correct answer: C. High school: 40−12−10=18 teachers. 18/40=0.45=45%. Choice A is 12/40=30%, the percentage that chose elementary, not high school. Choice B is 10/40=25%, the percentage that chose middle school, not high school. Choice D results from adding the elementary and middle school counts (12+10=22) and reporting that as the high school percentage: 22/40=55%, instead of subtracting them from the total first.
Q20Statistics
A tutor's five students scored 82, 88, 91, 75, and 84 on a quiz. What is the mean score?
Correct answer: D. Sum = 82+88+91+75+84 = 420. Mean = 420÷5 = 84. Choice A mistakenly reports the first score listed (82) instead of calculating the mean. Choice B is simply one of the five individual scores (88), not the mean of all five. Choice C is the sum of all five scores (420), not divided by the number of students to find the mean.
Q21Order of Operations
What is the value of (6 + 4) × 2 − 8 ÷ 2?
Correct answer: C. Parentheses first (6+4=10), then multiplication (10×2=20), then division (8÷2=4), then subtraction (20−4=16). Choice A results from treating the entire expression as one left-to-right chain, including the final division: ((6+4)×2−8)÷2 = 12÷2 = 6. Choice B results from ignoring the parentheses around (6+4) and applying standard order of operations to 6+4×2−8÷2: 4×2=8, 8÷2=4, so 6+8−4=10. Choice D results from computing (6+4)×2−8=12 but forgetting the final ÷2 entirely.
Q22Estimation
Which of the following is the best estimate of 812 × 48?
Correct answer: B. Rounding 812 to 800 and 48 to 50 gives 800×50=40,000, close to the actual product (38,976). Choice A results from rounding 48 down to 40 instead of up to 50: 800×40=32,000. Choice C results from rounding 48 up to 500 by mistake (an extra zero): 800×500=400,000. Choice D results from rounding 812 down to 80 instead of 800 (a misplaced zero): 80×50=4,000.

Q27Sentence Structure

“The study had a small sample size. Its conclusions may not apply to the general population.”
Correct answer: D. Choice D uses “because” to correctly show the cause-and-effect relationship between the small sample size and the limited applicability of the conclusions. Choice B is a run-on that adds an unnecessary, vaguely connected clause (“this is important to note”) on top of a comma splice. Q30Research & Citation
A student writing a research paper wants to use a statistic from a source but is not quoting it word for word. What is the most appropriate way to handle this in the paper?
Correct answer: B. When using information from a source without quoting it directly, the correct approach is to paraphrase it in one's own words and still credit the original source with a citation — this avoids plagiarism while properly crediting the source of the data. Choice A is incorrect on two counts: copying text exactly requires quotation marks, and any borrowed information still requires a citation. Choice C is an overly cautious non-solution that avoids using legitimate source material unnecessarily. Choice D is incorrect because even factual statistics and data drawn from a specific source require a citation to credit that source and allow readers to verify it.
