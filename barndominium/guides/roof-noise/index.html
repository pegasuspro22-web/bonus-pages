Roof Noise & Sound Control

Noise in a barndominium comes from four places: rain and hail on the roof, echo in big rooms with hard surfaces, sound passing between rooms, and machines. Each has its own cure, and most of them cost little if they are chosen before the drywall goes up. Use this guide during design, then again during framing, when every upgrade is still cheap.

Rain and hail on the roof

A bare panel behaves like a drum skin. Quiet comes from three moves: damp the panel, add mass, and put a finished ceiling between the roof and the room. Pick the line in the table that fits your budget for each part of the house; bedrooms and the office deserve the quietest assembly you can afford.

Roof assemblies, from loud to quiet
AssemblyHow it soundsWhere it fits
Bare panels, foil onlyEvery drop is heard; hail is very loudOpen sheds only
Panels + closed-cell foamRain becomes a dull patterShops, garages, great rooms with a budget
Foam + finished ceiling with insulation aboveA soft hiss; conversation unaffectedLiving space
Solid deck + underlayment + panels, ceiling belowClose to a shingle roofBedrooms, office, media room

Echo in big rooms

Concrete floors, tall drywall and large windows reflect almost all sound. A great room needs soft and broken-up surfaces to sound comfortable. You do not need studio panels: the everyday materials of a furnished home do most of the work if you plan for enough of them.

What softens a room most
SurfaceEffectNotes
Large rug under seating and diningHighWool or dense synthetic, with a pad
Upholstered sofa and chairsHighFabric absorbs far more than leather
Wood slat or board ceiling with insulation behindHighWorks where sound collects, high in the room
Heavy curtainsMediumSoften glass, which reflects nearly everything
Full bookshelvesMediumScatter sound in every direction
Slat acoustic panels on one wallMediumAbsorption and a design feature

Sound between rooms

Layout protects quiet rooms for free; construction finishes the job. Upgrade the walls around bedrooms, baths and the office while the framing is open.

WallApprox. STCNext door

Machines and the shop

Two tests you can do yourselfThe clap test: clap once in the empty great room; a long ringing tail means it needs soft surfaces. The hose test: before the ceiling is finished, have someone spray a garden hose on the roof above a bedroom while you listen inside. You will hear what rain will sound like while there is still time to add a deck or more insulation.
