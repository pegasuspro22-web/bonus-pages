Floor Plan Evaluation

A plan that looks clean on a screen can hide a kitchen everyone walks through to reach the bedrooms or a great room that bakes in the afternoon sun. Use the scorecard below on every plan you consider: the book's plans, a catalog plan or your designer's draft. Score each question honestly from 0 to 2. The total tells you whether the plan is ready, needs changes, or should be dropped.

Walk the plan first

Put your finger on the front door and move through an ordinary day: bring in groceries, go to bed, get up at night for water, let the dog out, carry the laundry, come in from the shop with dirty boots. Note every time you pass through a room you have no business in, squeeze past furniture or open a door into something. Then score.

The scorecard

ZonesBedrooms grouped away from the great room and the shop, with closets, baths or halls as buffers.
ZonesService rooms (mudroom, laundry, mechanical) between the outside entry and the living space.
FlowNo daily path passes through a bedroom or bathroom to reach somewhere else.
FlowThe route from the back door to the bedrooms does not cut through the kitchen work area.
FlowWalkways at least 36 in, kitchen aisles 42–48 in, and no door opening into another door, a toilet or a walkway.
LightMain living spaces face roughly south; west-facing glass is modest or shaded.
LightEvery bedroom has a window large enough for emergency exit.
AirMost rooms have windows on two walls; tall rooms have ceiling fans planned.
AirEvery bedroom has a return-air path back to the air handler with the door closed.
ComfortRooms are in the comfortable range of the size table, not just the code minimum.
StorageA coat closet near each entry, linen storage near the baths, a pantry near the kitchen.
CostKitchen, baths and laundry grouped so drain runs are short; the building is a simple rectangle.
