Contractor Communication Checklist

Projects rarely fail because a crew cannot build a wall. They fail because the owner and the builder understood the job differently and found out too late. This checklist follows the order of a real project: choosing the builder, signing, running the site and closing out. Tick each item as it is done, and keep a printed copy in your binder for every meeting.

Before you choose a builder

Every bid must state

Red flagsA large deposit before any work, reluctance to write details down, no references you can visit, pressure to sign quickly, a bid far below the others with no explanation, or a suggestion that permits are unnecessary. One is a reason to slow down; two are a reason to walk away.

In the contract

Running the job

A short weekly meeting and a follow-up email prevent most misunderstandings. Use this agenda every week; it takes fifteen minutes.

  • ProgressWhat was finished this week, and what starts next week.
  • Decisions dueThe selections and approvals needed from you in the next two weeks, with dates.
  • ChangesAny change requested or discovered, with its written price and schedule effect.
  • MoneyThe next payment, the milestone it is tied to, and lien waivers received.
  • InspectionsThe next inspection, its date, and what must be ready.
  • Follow-up emailSend it the same day: decisions, open questions, next steps.
