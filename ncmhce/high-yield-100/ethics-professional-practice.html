Professional Practice and Ethics

Q1Boundaries and invitations
Priya, a client in treatment for anxiety for six months, invites her counselor to her wedding in the small town where both live. She says it would mean a great deal to her. What is the counselor's best response?

Why D is correct

Declining while exploring the meaning of the invitation protects the boundary and the alliance at the same time.

Why the others are wrong

A. Even a brief appearance creates a dual relationship and risks exposing the client's treatment to guests.

B. Small-town practice calls for more careful boundary management, not less; a kind explanation protects the alliance.

C. Skipping the discussion misses the clinical meaning of the invitation and can feel like rejection.

Q2Mandated reporting
A 10-year-old tells her school counselor that her uncle hits her with a belt when he babysits, and the counselor sees fresh welts on the girl's arm. What must the counselor do first?

Why C is correct

Reasonable suspicion of abuse triggers a prompt report; investigating belongs to the child protection agency, not the counselor.

Why the others are wrong

A. Contacting family first can endanger the child and delays a legally required report.

B. The law requires a report on reasonable suspicion, not on certainty, and delay leaves the child exposed.

D. The duty to report belongs personally to the counselor and cannot be handed to someone else.

Q3Telehealth across state lines
A counselor licensed in one state is asked by a long-term client to continue video sessions while the client works for three months in another state. What is the most ethical action?

Why B is correct

Authority to practice depends on where the client is physically located, and telehealth consent must cover the added risks.

Why the others are wrong

A. Many jurisdictions regulate practice by the client's physical location, so this assumption can break the law.

C. The medium does not remove licensure rules, and the telehealth risks still have to be addressed.

D. Abrupt termination without exploring options abandons a client in treatment.

Q4Disability access
Dev, who is deaf, asks for his sessions to include a sign language interpreter. The agency has no interpreter on staff. What is the most appropriate action?

Why A is correct

A qualified interpreter provides effective access, and documenting the request and explaining confidentiality protect the client.

Why the others are wrong

B. Written exchange is slower and is not the effective communication the client requested.

C. A referral is not the first step when an accommodation is feasible and the counselor is otherwise competent.

D. A family member is not a qualified interpreter and changes what the client can safely say.

Q5Release of information
A client's employer phones the counselor to ask whether the client is attending sessions, saying the answer will affect a promotion. What is the best response?

Why C is correct

Even confirming that someone is a client discloses protected information, which requires the client's written authorization.

Why the others are wrong

A. Good intentions do not replace authorization, and the client has not agreed to any disclosure.

B. Billing records are equally protected, so this routes the same improper disclosure through another door.

D. Confirming that a person is in counseling is itself confidential information.

Q6Social media boundaries
The day after a productive session, a client sends the counselor a friend request on a social media platform. What is the most ethical response?

Why A is correct

Declining and explaining keeps the boundary clear and respects the client's feelings about the request.

Why the others are wrong

B. Blocking without explanation can feel punitive and damages trust.

C. Silence leaves the client uncertain and misses the chance to explain the boundary.

D. A personal connection creates a dual relationship and exposes both parties' information.

Q7Billing and documentation
A counselor completes an intake evaluation for a client whose insurer requires documentation of medical necessity before it pays. What is the best way to handle the claim?

Why B is correct

Accurate documentation, payer verification, and consultation with billing staff protect the client and keep the claim truthful.

Why the others are wrong

A. Records must be accurate before submission, not repaired after a query.

C. Inflating findings is falsification and a serious ethical and legal violation.

D. Choosing a code to avoid scrutiny rather than to describe the service is improper billing.

Q8Fees and agency policy
A client says she can no longer pay the full fee after losing her job. The agency has a sliding-scale policy. What should the counselor do?

Why D is correct

Following the agency's published procedure keeps fee decisions fair, transparent, and documented.

Why the others are wrong

A. Unrecorded side agreements violate agency policy and create billing and ethical problems.

B. Abrupt termination ignores available options and risks abandoning the client.

C. Allowing debt to build without a plan puts both the client and the agency at risk.

Q9Referral limits
A college counselor finds that a student's severe bulimia, with daily purging and fainting, exceeds what a six-session limit can address. What should the counselor do first?

Why A is correct

When a service cannot meet the need, the counselor explains its limits and actively facilitates a safe referral.

Why the others are wrong

B. A list alone does not facilitate the referral for a client at medical risk.

C. Fainting and daily purging signal medical risk that cannot wait for deterioration.

D. A policy limit does not remove the duty to arrange appropriate care for a medically risky condition.

Q10Advocacy and access
A client who uses a wheelchair cannot reach the counseling office because the building has no accessible entrance. What is the best action?

Why B is correct

The counselor secures access for the client immediately and advocates for the structural change.

Why the others are wrong

A. Reducing the client's service format avoids the barrier instead of addressing it.

C. This is unsafe and places the burden of access on the client.

D. Delay denies the client services now, when an accessible alternative can be arranged.

Q11Duty to warn
A client tells his counselor that he plans to harm his former business partner and names the parking lot where he will wait for him tonight. What is the counselor's best action?

Why C is correct

A serious, specific threat to an identifiable person is an exception to confidentiality that calls for protective action.

Why the others are wrong

A. A specific plan with a victim and time requires action now, not at the next appointment.

B. A promise does not reduce a serious, imminent danger.

D. Families are not the persons the law requires protecting, and the intended victim remains unwarned.

Q12Supervision and risk
A client describes thoughts of ending her life and asks the counselor not to tell her husband. The counselor is unsure about disclosure. What is the best first step?

Why C is correct

Risk assessment comes first because it determines whether an exception to confidentiality is needed.

Why the others are wrong

A. Waiting leaves a possible acute risk unassessed.

B. Promising secrecy before assessing risk can trap the counselor between a promise and a duty.

D. Disclosure before assessing risk may be unnecessary and breaches trust.

Q13Limits of competence
A counselor trained in adult depression is asked to see a 15-year-old with severe self-harm and substance use, together with her parents. What must the counselor do first?

Why D is correct

Counselors practice within the boundaries of their competence and seek help or refer when a case exceeds it.

Why the others are wrong

A. Disclosure of inexperience does not replace competence or supervision.

B. Adolescent self-harm and substance use require specific training that adult work does not provide.

C. Learning on the client while treating risks harm.

Q14Group confidentiality
In the first meeting of a men's support group, a prospective member asks whether anything said there stays private. What is the best answer?

Why B is correct

Group leaders explain confidentiality accurately, including that they cannot control what members repeat.

Why the others are wrong

A. The code binds the leader, not the members, so this promise cannot be kept.

C. Confidentiality limits belong in informed consent before participation.

D. The leader sets the expectation and explains the limits of protection.

Q15Dual roles
A counselor who also volunteers as a youth leader at her church learns that one of her counseling clients has joined the youth group. What is the most appropriate action?

Why B is correct

Overlapping roles risk impaired judgment and confidentiality, so the counselor addresses them openly and removes the conflict.

Why the others are wrong

A. Disclosing the client's treatment without authorization breaches confidentiality.

C. Acting without the client's knowledge harms the alliance and is not collaborative.

D. Mixing roles creates a dual relationship and blurs the purpose of treatment.
