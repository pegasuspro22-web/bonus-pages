NCMHCE HIGH-YIELD 100
Domain 3 of 5 · 15 questions

Treatment Planning

Q41Termination
After 14 sessions, a client with panic disorder has had no panic attacks for two months, uses her breathing and exposure skills, and feels ready to finish. What is the best plan?

Why B is correct

Goals have been met, so termination is planned, with relapse prevention and a route back if symptoms return.

Why the others are wrong

A. Adding goals for the counselor's convenience serves the counselor, not the client.

C. Continuing after goals are met builds dependence and lacks a clinical rationale.

D. Termination should be planned and should include relapse prevention.

Q42Termination and ongoing needs
Parents bringing their 8-year-old for school anxiety report that the family conflict that prompted counseling has settled, but the child still avoids school. What is the best decision?

Why A is correct

The family goal has improved, but the child's anxiety remains, so treatment continues and is adjusted to that need.

Why the others are wrong

B. Referral needs a reason such as competence or scope.

C. This shifts focus away from the child's unresolved symptoms.

D. The child's symptoms persist, so the original goals are not met.

Q43Reviewing progress
A client in treatment for depression has had little change in her scores after eight weeks, and she says that homework tasks feel too demanding. What is the best response?

Why D is correct

Reviewing progress with the client and adjusting the plan together keeps treatment responsive and collaborative.

Why the others are wrong

A. Lack of progress calls for review, not ending services.

B. A plan that is not working should be reviewed and adjusted.

C. Raising demands ignores her stated barrier.

Q44Writing treatment goals
A client discharged from a psychiatric unit after a suicide attempt is starting outpatient counseling. Which goal is best written as a measurable objective?

Why A is correct

This goal is specific, measurable, time-limited, and tied directly to his safety and recovery.

Why the others are wrong

B. An absolute outcome is unrealistic and unmeasurable.

C. This describes the counselor's activity, not a client outcome.

D. This goal is vague and cannot be measured.

Q45Goals and graded exposure
A college student with severe social anxiety has been skipping seminars for a month. Which goal is most appropriate for the treatment plan?

Why A is correct

A graded, behaviorally specific goal addresses avoidance and links to what matters to the student.

Why the others are wrong

B. Withdrawal reinforces avoidance and delays recovery.

C. Insight alone does not address ongoing avoidance.

D. Immediate full exposure is unrealistic and may cause dropout.

Q46Conceptualizing missed work
A mother caring for her ill father has missed two sessions and did not complete the practice tasks. Which conceptualization is most helpful?

Why D is correct

Seeing missed tasks as a barrier to explore keeps the alliance intact and leads to a workable plan.

Why the others are wrong

A. Labeling her as resistant overlooks real constraints.

B. Attributing hidden motives to the client is speculative.

C. Her missed tasks show a barrier, not a lack of understanding.

Q47Relapse prevention
The parents of a teenager recovering from a first psychotic episode are near the end of a treatment phase. Which goal best supports relapse prevention?

Why C is correct

A specific, time-limited task builds a concrete plan that the family can use.

Why the others are wrong

A. This is a counselor action and has no client outcome.

B. An absolute standard sets the client up to fail.

D. Attitudes are not measurable behaviors.

Q48Referral and coordination
A client in counseling for depression has lost his housing and says he cannot focus on therapy while sleeping in his car. What is the best action?

Why D is correct

Basic needs affect progress, so the counselor coordinates with services and adjusts the goals.

Why the others are wrong

A. Unmet basic needs undermine treatment, so ignoring them is poor planning.

B. Stepping outside the role creates a dual relationship.

C. Withholding services until needs are met abandons the client.

Q49Concurrent treatment
A client sees an outside psychiatrist for medication while in counseling. What is the best way to coordinate care?

Why A is correct

Releases, clear roles, and shared monitoring keep concurrent treatment coordinated and ethical.

Why the others are wrong

B. Relayed messages are unreliable and may omit risk information.

C. Contact without authorization breaches confidentiality.

D. Isolated providers risk contradictory or unsafe care.

Q50Using results with clients
A client's outcome measures and coping log show some gains but not on his main goal, which was returning to work. What is the best approach?

Why B is correct

Using data with the client supports shared decisions and keeps the plan responsive.

Why the others are wrong

A. Changing the client's goal without him ignores his values.

C. Withholding results undermines collaboration.

D. Planned measurement is part of evidence-based practice.

Q51Revising goals
A client's plan includes a goal to be in bed by 10 p.m., but his sleep log shows that work messages arrive late most nights. What is the best response?

Why B is correct

Data show a barrier, so the objective is revised to address it.

Why the others are wrong

A. A stricter bedtime does not address late-night messages.

C. Abandoning the goal ignores a solvable barrier.

D. Pushing harder ignores the barrier shown by the data.

Q52Conceptualizing a case
A man mandated to counseling for coercive control tells the counselor he only needs better communication with his partner. Which conceptualization is most accurate?

Why B is correct

Coercive control is about power, so the plan must address accountability and safety.

Why the others are wrong

A. Accepting his framing ignores the behavior that led to the referral.

C. Treating coercive control as a skills problem may increase risk.

D. Attributing coercion to stress minimizes responsibility.

Q53Barriers to goals
A client's goal is weekly counseling, but his shared home means he cannot talk privately and his work schedule changes constantly. What is the best decision?

Why A is correct

The plan is adapted to remove the barrier so that treatment can continue.

Why the others are wrong

B. A rigid approach penalizes the client for a practical barrier.

C. Barriers call for adjusting the plan, not ending treatment.

D. Referring without discussion neglects the client's choice.

Q54Continuity during a leave
A counselor expects to be away on medical leave for three months and has a client who is stable but still in treatment. What is the best plan?

Why A is correct

Planning for a disruption protects continuity of care and respects client choice.

Why the others are wrong

B. A gap in care without a plan can harm a client still in treatment.

C. Short notice without a plan risks abandonment.

D. Informal contact during a medical leave is unsafe and unprofessional.

Q55Anxiety after an accident
After a minor car accident, a 15-year-old avoids riding in cars and has stopped attending her art club. Which goal is best?

Why D is correct

A graded, behavioral goal addresses avoidance and restores valued activities.

Why the others are wrong

A. Repeated retelling without a plan does not address avoidance.

B. Continued avoidance maintains and strengthens the fear.

C. Giving up valued activities shrinks her life and reinforces avoidance.
