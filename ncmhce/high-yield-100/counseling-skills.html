Domain 4 of 5 · 30 questions

Counseling Skills and Interventions

Q56Faith and coping
A woman from a devout Muslim family says that her daily prayer is the one thing that calms her anxiety. How should the counselor build a coping skill with her?

Why A is correct

Using the client's own source of calm respects her values and makes the skill more likely to last.

Why the others are wrong

B. Avoiding the topic overlooks a strength that the client herself named.

C. Dismissing a core coping resource disregards her values.

D. Directing her religious practice imposes the counselor's views.

Q57Grief work
A man whose brother died three months ago says he cannot stop thinking of him and feels he should be “moving on.” Which intervention is most appropriate?

Why D is correct

Continuing bonds approaches help clients keep a healthy connection with the person who has died.

Why the others are wrong

A. Forcing separation can intensify grief and ignores his pace.

B. There is no fixed timeline, and this message adds shame.

C. Avoidance blocks the grieving process.

Q58Alliance rupture in addiction work
During an alcohol assessment, a client says angrily, “You think I'm just a drunk.” What is the best response?

Why C is correct

Reflecting the concern and showing curiosity repairs the alliance and keeps him engaged.

Why the others are wrong

A. Confronting denial early raises resistance.

B. Avoiding the topic abandons the purpose of the assessment.

D. This defends the counselor and dismisses his feelings.

Q59Couples and intense emotion
A couple's discussion about money escalates into shouting in session. Which intervention is best?

Why B is correct

Structure slows the escalation and lets each partner be heard, which lowers the intensity.

Why the others are wrong

A. Permanent avoidance leaves the real issue unresolved.

C. Unstructured venting often reinforces conflict patterns.

D. Separating them abruptly leaves one partner out and does not teach skills.

Q60Grounding
A client begins to hyperventilate while describing a car accident. Which response is most appropriate?

Why D is correct

Grounding and mindful awareness help the client stay present without fighting the sensations.

Why the others are wrong

A. Reassurance dismisses the client's experience.

B. Pressing for details can increase the overwhelm.

C. Suppression usually increases distress.

Q61Motivational interviewing
A client says of a new coping skill, “That will never work for me.” Which response best fits motivational interviewing?

Why B is correct

Asking permission and exploring ambivalence respects autonomy and invites change talk.

Why the others are wrong

A. Arguing for change tends to evoke resistance.

C. Dropping the skill gives up without exploring the barrier.

D. This sounds accusing and invites defensiveness.

Q62Repairing the alliance by telehealth
In a video session, a client says, “This feels like talking to a screen.” What is the best response?

Why B is correct

Acknowledging the feeling and inviting collaboration addresses the rupture.

Why the others are wrong

A. Reassurance without exploration ignores the concern.

C. This dismisses the client's experience.

D. Blaming the client damages the alliance.

Q63Immediacy
A client goes silent after being asked about her father. Which response is best?

Why D is correct

Naming what is happening in the room, gently, keeps the client in control.

Why the others are wrong

A. Labeling the client as resistant is judgmental.

B. Interpreting silence as refusal closes off exploration.

C. Pressure can intensify withdrawal.

Q64Summarizing strengths
A client in recovery says that after a fight with his partner he wanted to drink, bought a bottle, then poured it out and called his sponsor. Which response best uses reflecting and summarizing?

Why D is correct

A summary that highlights his coping shows strengths and invites him to name what worked.

Why the others are wrong

A. The goal of avoiding every risk situation is unrealistic and misses his skills.

B. He did not drink, so this mislabels the event.

C. Criticism ignores his success in coping.

Q65Cognitive restructuring
A client says, “I always fail at everything.” Which response best fits cognitive behavioral therapy?

Why A is correct

Examining evidence for and against a thought is the core of cognitive restructuring.

Why the others are wrong

B. A “why” question is likely to evoke defensiveness.

C. Disputing without evidence invites argument.

D. An equally absolute thought is unrealistic.

Q66Couples communication
A couple repeatedly interrupts each other when discussing chores. Which intervention is most appropriate?

Why B is correct

A structured exchange with reflection teaches listening and reduces interruptions.

Why the others are wrong

A. Avoidance does not build communication skills.

C. A week of silence leaves the pattern unchanged.

D. This favors one partner and does not teach listening.

Q67Teaching a DBT skill
A client with urges to self-harm is learning paced breathing as a distress tolerance skill. What is the best way to teach it?

Why D is correct

Skills are taught through explanation, modeling, rehearsal, and linking to the client's real cues.

Why the others are wrong

A. Willpower alone is not a skill and may lead to failure.

B. Reading alone does not build a skill she can use under stress.

C. Skills should be rehearsed before they are needed at high distress.

Q68Solution-focused work
A parent says she usually shouts at her teenager, but one evening she stayed calm. Which question best fits solution-focused brief therapy?

Why B is correct

An exception question highlights what already works and builds on it.

Why the others are wrong

A. This frames the success as an oddity.

C. This focuses on the problem rather than on exceptions.

D. This explores history, not solutions.

Q69Acceptance and commitment therapy
A client's thought “I am a burden” keeps stopping him from asking friends for help. Which response best fits acceptance and commitment therapy?

Why B is correct

Defusion plus values-based action helps the client act even while the thought is present.

Why the others are wrong

A. Avoidance maintains the struggle.

C. Arguing with thoughts is a cognitive restructuring method, not defusion.

D. Repeated reassurance keeps the focus on the thought.

Q70Person-centered couple work
In session, Carlos says he feels criticized every time a mistake is pointed out, and Ana says she feels alone with all the household tasks. Which response is most person-centered?

Why C is correct

Reflecting both partners' feelings with empathy builds understanding without taking sides.

Why the others are wrong

A. Telling one partner what to think takes sides.

B. A directive does not reflect either partner's feelings.

D. This directs and blames one partner.

Q71Safety planning
A 15-year-old who has urges to self-harm agrees to create a safety plan. Which approach is best?

Why A is correct

A written, personalized plan gives concrete steps she can use in a crisis.

Why the others are wrong

B. A number alone does not guide her through a crisis.

C. A verbal promise is not a safety plan.

D. She needs access to her own plan.

Q72Crisis response with a child
A 9-year-old tells the counselor she is afraid to go home because her father was shouting and throwing things last night. What is the best response?

Why C is correct

Immediate safety planning comes first and is delivered in language the child can use.

Why the others are wrong

A. Pressing for details can distress the child and delays safety.

B. This leaves the child alone with the danger.

D. Reassurance ignores the safety concern.

Q73Narrative family therapy
A family has been afraid and tense since a house fire. Which intervention fits narrative therapy?

Why A is correct

Externalizing the problem lets the family unite against it and notice their own strengths.

Why the others are wrong

B. A skill alone does not use the family's own stories.

C. Avoiding the experience prevents meaning-making.

D. This blames individuals instead of separating the person from the problem.

Q74Psychoeducation
A client with bipolar disorder brings his family to a session so they can learn about the illness. What is the best way to deliver the psychoeducation?

Why B is correct

Psychoeducation works best when it builds on what the family knows and includes a practical skill.

Why the others are wrong

A. This puts the burden on the client and may lead to errors.

C. A lecture overloads them and ignores what they already know.

D. A pamphlet alone does not support practice or understanding.

Q75Rational emotive behavior therapy
After failing an exam, a client says, “I am a total failure.” Which response best fits rational emotive behavior therapy?

Why D is correct

This disputes the irrational belief by linking it to the client's own goals.

Why the others are wrong

A. A “why” question without a path invites defensiveness.

B. REBT holds that beliefs, not events, drive the feeling.

C. Reassurance does not challenge the belief.

Q76Rupture and risk
After the counselor responds in a way that seems dismissive, the client says, “Maybe it doesn't matter anymore.” What is the best response?

Why C is correct

The counselor repairs the rupture and asks about suicide directly.

Why the others are wrong

A. Ending the session leaves a possible risk unassessed.

B. Exploring annoyance first delays assessing safety.

D. This avoids the possible risk statement.

Q77Starting telehealth
A counselor begins video sessions with a new client who lives with several housemates. What is the best approach?

Why D is correct

These steps cover consent, privacy, and safety before any clinical work.

Why the others are wrong

A. Encryption does not protect against overhearing at home.

B. An emergency plan is needed whatever the living situation.

C. Privacy problems should be addressed before they occur.

Q78Values and family expectations
A young woman feels torn between her grandparents' strict household rules and her wish for independence. Which intervention is most appropriate?

Why A is correct

Clarifying values and rehearsing a response respects both her culture and her goals.

Why the others are wrong

B. Judging her culture damages trust.

C. This imposes the counselor's values on her.

D. Directing the choice ignores her own values.

Q79Defenses and grounding
A client smiles and jokes whenever she talks about her mother's illness. Which response best invites exploration?

Why D is correct

A skill plus gentle curiosity helps the client stay with feelings and notice the defense.

Why the others are wrong

A. Staying with avoided topics keeps the pattern in place.

B. Dismissing the pattern misses the chance to explore.

C. Labeling and ordering her to stop is judgmental.

Q80Group crisis
In a group session, one member is blamed by others and says, “Then I will just end it.” What should the leader do?

Why B is correct

The leader interrupts the process to assess risk right away.

Why the others are wrong

A. Waiting leaves possible danger unaddressed.

C. Peer responses are not a substitute for a risk assessment.

D. Dismissing the statement is unsafe.

Q81Resistance in a young client
A 13-year-old refuses an art task, saying “I am not doing that.” Which response best fits an Adlerian or psychodynamic approach?

Why B is correct

Naming the possible meaning of the refusal respects his autonomy and opens exploration.

Why the others are wrong

A. Changing tasks avoids the meaning of the refusal.

C. Labeling the refusal damages the alliance.

D. Insisting raises resistance.

Q82Building supports
A man with depression says that he has no one to turn to. Which intervention best builds his support system?

Why A is correct

Mapping, choosing, and rehearsing turns the goal into a concrete step.

Why the others are wrong

B. Contacting family without his agreement breaches his autonomy.

C. The group may not fit him, and the advice skips his choices.

D. Relying on the counselor builds dependence.

Q83Rupture in couple work
In couple counseling, the husband says to the counselor, “You always take her side.” Which response is best?

Why C is correct

The counselor acknowledges his feeling and invites discussion, which repairs the rupture.

Why the others are wrong

A. Telling him not to feel it dismisses his experience.

B. Separate sessions are not the first response to a rupture in the room.

D. This confirms his concern that the counselor takes sides.

Q84Career counseling
A 38-year-old is unsure whether to change careers and says that his father and grandfather both changed jobs often. Which technique fits best?

Why C is correct

A career genogram links family patterns to present career decisions.

Why the others are wrong

A. Friends' jobs do not explain his own pattern.

B. This ignores his values.

D. A test score alone should not decide a career.

Q85Trauma-focused therapy
A survivor of an assault says, “I should have fought back.” Which intervention is most appropriate?

Why B is correct

Cognitive processing therapy targets self-blaming stuck points after trauma.

Why the others are wrong

A. Starting exposure without preparation can overwhelm her.

C. Repeated reassurance does not change the belief.

D. Avoiding the belief leaves it unexamined.
